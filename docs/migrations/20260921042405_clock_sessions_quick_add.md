# 20260921042405_clock_sessions_quick_add.sql (2026-09-20, v2.3655)

Quick time add, PR 1 (`to-dos/quick-time-add`). **Push in a quiet moment**: step 1 takes a brief `ACCESS EXCLUSIVE` on `clock_sessions`, the busiest table in the app. It is metadata-only and `SET lock_timeout = '3s'` makes it fail fast rather than queue every clock query behind it — retry later if it times out.

- **`clock_sessions.quick_add_minutes smallint NULL`** — `NULL` = an ordinary punch; `5…30` in fives = a quick add (its length). The CHECK is added **`NOT VALID`** and then **`VALIDATE`d** in a second statement: a CHECK inside `ADD COLUMN` would scan the table under ACCESS EXCLUSIVE, while `VALIDATE CONSTRAINT` scans under `SHARE UPDATE EXCLUSIVE`, which blocks no reader or writer. Partial index `(user_id, work_date) WHERE quick_add_minutes IS NOT NULL` for the day total. `origin` is untouched — a quick add is `'user_punch'`.
- **`clock_sessions_guard_quick_add`** (BEFORE INSERT OR UPDATE OF the mark and the two times) — the insert policy lets a person insert their own finished session, so without this the rules could be skipped from a console. It refuses a direct insert carrying the mark, the owner changing the mark, and the owner stretching a quick add past its length (moving it, or editing the note, is fine). A dev, a pay-approved master or an assistant may correct **someone else's** entry — never their own. `add_quick_time()` passes by a transaction-local flag (`app.quick_add_rpc`); a no-JWT / service-role writer is not bound.
- **`app_settings.quick_add_daily_ceiling_minutes`** = 120 (`ON CONFLICT DO NOTHING`).
- **`add_quick_time(p_minutes int, p_note text, p_ended_at timestamptz default now())`** → `jsonb` — `SECURITY DEFINER`, EXECUTE to `authenticated` only. In order: signed in → an office role (assistant, controller, estimator, dev), not archived, not a twin → not read-only → not salaried (unless `record_hours_but_salary`, via `self_salary_clock_state()`) → 5…30 in fives → a note of 3+ characters (trimmed, capped at 200) → the window is today on the company calendar, start and end, and not in the future → a per-person advisory lock → no open session → no overlap with a live finished session (the clash is named in company time) → the day's live quick adds plus this one within the ceiling. Then one row: `work_date = app_today()`, the window, the note, the **Office job** from `app_settings.overhead_office_job_ledger_id_v1` (NULL when unset or stale), `origin = 'user_punch'`, the mark. **No job argument, on purpose**: a session on a real job promotes it waiting → working, moves its last work date, fills the customer's date met and joins the crew.

Run before merge on a throwaway Postgres 15 with stub tables: applied twice (idempotent); a helper, a salaried controller, a read-only assistant and `anon` refused, a salaried-but-records-hours controller allowed; 7 and 35 minutes, an empty note, tomorrow and yesterday refused; 10 minutes ending now landed as one pending `user_punch` row on the Office job with the trimmed note; a second one over it refused naming the first's times; refused while clocked in; the ceiling held and freed up again when the office rejected an entry; a direct insert with the mark, a self-stretch to 3 h and clearing one's own mark refused, while an ordinary My Time insert, a note edit, a same-length move, an assistant correcting someone else's entry and a service-role insert all passed; two simultaneous calls wrote one row.
