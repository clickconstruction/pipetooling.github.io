# 20261127052337_site_secrecy.sql (2026-11-27, v2.8692)

Secrecy on a site, the ground (punch list #439, piece 4: `to-dos/site-nda-for-vendors/README.md`). It feeds the gate of punch list #389, *Papers before a job opens*, from a site. Its first screen is a development's page (v2.8693, **Secrecy on this site**); the two other doors that turn secrecy on are client PRs that follow.

## The shape

The gate is one row a job (`job_required_papers`), one copy a person a row, and one question, `job_closed_to_me`. Turning secrecy on for a site writes that gate's own row on every job at the site. A trigger adds the row to a job made at, or moved to, a site that is on. The lock counts a person as signed for every job of a site once they signed for one. So the chip, the prompt, clock-in, Needs you, the sub's page and the emails work as they do, and nothing new decides who is shut out.

One site is on once: two partial unique indexes, one over developments and one over address keys. The office and the estimator read it (`is_office_or_estimator`). No policy lets anyone write it, and the grants for insert, update and delete are revoked: the two presses are the only way in. The four fence calls close the file.

## A job is at a site when

- the site is a development and the job is in it, or
- the site is an address and the job's own address cleans to one of the site's keys. The site's keys are its own key and every key a **Same place** answer joins to it, chains followed, as `samePlaceKeys` does in the app.

A job that already requires the same Contract Book paper by itself keeps its own row and takes no second one.

## The two address rules, and what holds them together

The rule is written twice: in the app and here. Nothing imports one into the other. One list of forty addresses with their keys sits in the bed (`supabase/tests/site_secrecy/20_scenario.sql`, between two markers). The bed proves the database writes every key of the list. `src/lib/addresses/addressKeyParity.test.ts` reads the same list and proves the app writes the same keys, proves the two tables of street words are one table, and proves every short word of that table is on the list. A change to either rule that the other does not follow turns one of the two red.

Where the two could part, and do not on anything a keyboard types:

- **Letters outside the plain alphabet.** Both turn them into a space. The app lowercases first with JavaScript, the database with Postgres. A character that lowercases to a plain letter in one and not the other would part them: the Kelvin sign is one, a capital dotted I another. Neither is a letter anyone types in an address.
- **Spaces that are not the space bar's.** The app keeps them as spaces and the database turns them into spaces. The key is the same.

## What it costs a read

`jobs_shut_to_me()` sits in the fence's policies inside a sub-select, so it is asked once a statement, in an InitPlan. This file does not restate it. It calls `jobs_closed_to_me()`, which gains one condition. For a row with no site that condition is a null test and nothing more, so a database with no site on does the work it did. For a row a site wrote it is one call that probes two indexes. No read of a job works out an address key: `address_key` runs when a site is turned on, when an answer changes, and for the one job a trigger is fired for.

The trigger on `jobs_ledger` fires after an insert, and after an update that names `job_address` or `development_id`. It returns at once when neither changed, and after one look at `site_secrecy` when no site is on.

## Locks

The file runs as one transaction, so each lock is held until its last line and a failure leaves nothing behind. In the order they are taken:

- `CREATE TABLE site_secrecy` with its foreign keys: a lock that holds back writes, never reads, on `users`, `developments`, `contract_template_documents` and `contract_form_templates`.
- `CREATE TRIGGER` on `jobs_ledger`, then on `address_place_answers`: a lock on each that waits for writes in flight and holds back new ones. It lets every read through. This is the one wait that can be long, so the file makes the two triggers before the column.
- `ALTER TABLE job_required_papers ADD COLUMN` with its foreign key: an exclusive lock on a small table. The column is nullable with no default, so no row is rewritten. **This is the lock a read of a job feels:** `jobs_shut_to_me()` looks at `job_required_papers` for every signed-in read of a fenced table, so those reads wait from this line to the file's end. What is left to run by then is two small indexes, the functions and the four fence calls, with no lock left to wait for.
- Everything else is new, or a `CREATE OR REPLACE FUNCTION`.

Made in that order, a job save in flight cannot deadlock with the file: the save is waited for before the exclusive lock is asked for.

`SET lock_timeout = '3s'` opens the file, so a push that cannot take a lock fails fast. Push it in a quiet moment.

## Its bed

`supabase/tests/site_secrecy/20_scenario.sql` (`npm run test:pg:site-secrecy`), which applies this file a second time first. By name:

- **A job with no site row reads exactly as before.** For the office, a master, a lead, a helper, a sub and an estimator, each of the gate's five questions answers what the rule of before answers, written out in the bed. Asked before any site is on, and again with two sites on.
- **A person who signed for one job of a site reads every job of that site**, with one copy, and their own papers read signed on the job they never signed for.
- **A person who has not is shut out of all of them**, and of no job outside the site. The office owes and keeps its reads.
- **A sub with a login is not shut out by a site row**, is shown no paper of the site, and still owes a job's own paper.
- **Turning off opens the jobs and keeps the signatures.** Turning on again brings the same row back and asks only those who never signed.
- **A new job at a site that is on gets its row by the trigger.** A job made elsewhere, or while the site is off, takes nothing.
- **A job moved to and away from a site**: by its development and by its address. Moved back, it gets the same row again.
- An address is a site by its key, two spellings are one site, a **Same place** answer joins a neighbour and Undo takes it off.
- A site's paper is not taken off one job. A job that requires the same paper by itself keeps its own row.
- Who may press, what a press would reach, the table's one policy and its grants.
- A helper's plain read of jobs asks the fence once, in an InitPlan, and never calls `address_key`. The bed prints those lines of the plan.

The five older job-paper beds run in the same CI job over the same migrated copy.
