# 20261114070000 — on_site_carry_job carries the four tables of 2026-11-13 (v2.7265)

Materials on site (punch list #209): a move or a combine leaves nothing of a job's on-site records behind. `on_site_carry_job` ([`20261109160000`](./20261109160000_on_site_move_job.md)) carries a job's places, cages and boxes, ledger, box moves and photos to another job, and both the whole-job combine (`migrate_job_ledger_costs_and_delete`) and the dev seat's move (`dev_on_site_move_job`) call it. It was written before the tables of 2026-11-13, and a read of the code on 2026-11-14 found that a move or a combine left those rows on the source job: a `job_part_renames` row names the ledger rows that moved, so its undo against the target failed the judge's `job_id` match; a tie or a price scoped to one job on no site (`on_site_part_ties`, `on_site_prices`) was stranded, and a combine deleted it with the source (`ON DELETE CASCADE`); a `sticker_runs` row printed for the source kept naming it, so its blank stickers were read by the old job's team, or lost their job when a combine deleted it (`ON DELETE SET NULL`). Three functions restated, `CREATE OR REPLACE`; no table, column, index, policy or trigger is made, so no fence call is owed.

1. **`on_site_carry_job(p_from uuid, p_to uuid)` → jsonb**: the same body with four tables more, after the photos. `job_part_renames` and `sticker_runs` take the target `job_id`. A tie or a price with `job_id = p_from` goes to the target's scope: `job_id = p_to` when the target is on no site; `development_id = <the target's development>, job_id = null` when it is on one, since the site's screen is the one reader of a job on a site and the store prices a job's lines through its site. One answer per pair per scope: where both scopes answered the same pair, the newer `at` stands and the older row is deleted first. Every price moves, since the newest row of a key is the one read. A tie or a price scoped to the source's development is the site's, shared with every job on it, and stays. A placed sticker, its events and a box's old names hang on their container and move with it, as before. The reply gains `part_renames`, `sticker_runs`, `ties`, `prices` (the rows that moved), `ties_into` (`'job'` or `'site'`) and `ties_dropped` (`[{ a, b, kept: 'source' | 'target' }]`). Rights unchanged: `SECURITY INVOKER`, `EXECUTE` revoked from `PUBLIC`, `anon` and `authenticated`; its two callers are definers that gate first. `on_site_part_ties` and `on_site_prices` are insert-and-delete tables for every client; this is their one update, made by the carry alone, as the ledger's `job_id` is.
2. **`on_site_job_counts(p_job_id uuid)` → jsonb**: gains `part_renames`, `sticker_runs`, `ties` and `prices` (the job-scoped rows) and `site_ties`, `site_prices` (the rows of the job's development, 0 for a job on no site), so a plan can hold the fold into a site to the same arithmetic. `SECURITY INVOKER`, `STABLE`, granted to `authenticated` as before: the dev counts under the tables' own RLS (a price is read by the roles that may write one, which a dev is).
3. **`dev_on_site_move_job(p jsonb, p_dry_run boolean DEFAULT true)` → jsonb**: a source with no place, cage, box, ledger row, photo, part rename, sticker run, Same part? answer or price is refused, in those words (a job with a run alone moves it). The add-up check holds every table: the source ends empty of all nine counts; the target holds both jobs' renames and runs; when `ties_into` is `job`, the target's ties are both jobs' less the pairs that lost an answer and its prices both jobs'; when it is `site`, the target's own job-scoped ties and prices are as they were and its site's are the site's before plus the source's, less the pairs. A failed check raises and writes nothing. The words and the note on both jobs name the four tables only when the job has rows in them, so a plain move reads as it did. The reply gains `ties { into, dropped, words }`; `words` names the site by its name when the answers fold into one and lists each pair that kept the newer answer with whose it was. `moved` carries the four counts and neither `ties_into` nor `ties_dropped`.

The combine is not restated: it calls the carry and gets the four tables with it. The combine's own note on the target (*Combined … into this job*, a contract with `jobCombineNote.ts`) is unchanged.
