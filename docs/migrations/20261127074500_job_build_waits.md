## The table, `job_build_waits`

| Column | Type | What it holds |
|---|---|---|
| `id` | `uuid`, primary key | |
| `job_id` | `uuid` → `jobs_ledger(id)`, `ON DELETE CASCADE` | The job. |
| `reason` | `text`, one of `gc_not_ready`, `materials`, `inspection`, `customer_hold`, `weather`, `other` | The reason the person picked. |
| `note` | `text`, default `''`, at most 300 characters | The person's words. Required when the reason is `other` (`job_build_waits_other_has_words`). |
| `marked_by`, `marked_by_name`, `since_at` | `uuid` → `users(id)` set null on delete, `text`, `timestamptz` | Who marked the job waiting, their name as it read that day, and when. Set by the function. |
| `lifted_by`, `lifted_by_name`, `lifted_at` | `uuid` → `users(id)` set null on delete, `text`, `timestamptz` | Who lifted the wait and when. NULL while the job still waits. |

`job_build_waits_one_open` is a unique index on `job_id` over the rows not lifted: a job holds at most one wait at a time. A lifted wait stays as a row, so the record keeps when each wait began and ended.

**Not a status change.** The job's status stays Working. The app's Waiting means not started, and the Pipeline's counts keep meaning what they mean today.

## The function, `set_job_build_wait(p_job_id uuid, p_waits boolean, p_reason text, p_note text)` → `jsonb`

`SECURITY DEFINER`, `search_path = public`. It answers `{ok, waits, changed}`, or `{error}` in words.

- **`p_waits = true`** marks the job waiting. The reason must be one of the six, and `other` needs words. A job that already waits is left as it is and answers `changed: false`.
- **`p_waits = false`** lifts the wait: who and when are stamped on the open row. A job that does not wait answers `changed: false`.
- **Each change writes one line on the job's Activity** (`job_activity_events`), as a status change does: `build_wait_marked` with *Marked the job waiting: the GC is not ready for us.*, and `build_wait_lifted` with *Lifted the wait. The job counts on the Build desk again.* A press that changed nothing writes no line.

## Who reads and who writes

- **Read: whoever reads the job.** The one policy, `job_build_waits_select_with_job`, asks `jobs_ledger` under the reader's own policies. So the chip on a job's card will show to the people who see that card and to nobody else.
- **Write: the office**, through the function alone: dev, owner, assistant, controller (`is_office_staff()`). An archived caller and a training account change nothing. The table has no INSERT, UPDATE or DELETE policy and `authenticated` holds SELECT only, so nobody writes it by hand.
- `anon` reads nothing and may not execute the function.
