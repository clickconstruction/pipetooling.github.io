# 20261125223610_bid_day_ground.sql (2026-11-25, v2.8605)

The ground for the bids of the day (punch list #445, `to-dos/clock-out-bids-of-the-day/README.md`): the beat by bid, the list a person keeps at clock out and the read of a person's own day. No client calls any of it yet.

## What it makes

Every statement is safe to run twice. It ends with the four fence calls, since it makes two tables.

- **`user_bid_activity`**: the seconds a bid was in front while the person was at work on it. One row a person, a bid, a Bids tab and a five-minute bucket (`bucket_at`). A bucket holds at most 300 seconds.
- **`bid_day_entries`**: the list a person left at clock out. One row a person, a company day (`work_date`) and a bid, with `state` *kept* or *off* and `source` *app* or *person*. It also keeps the rough size, the tabs and the count of changes the sheet showed.
- **`bump_user_bid_activity(p_bid, p_tab, p_seconds)`**: the beat, `SECURITY DEFINER`. It adds at most 60 seconds a call.
- **`get_my_bid_day(p_work_date)`**: the caller's own day as JSON, `SECURITY INVOKER`.
- **`save_my_bid_day(p_work_date, p_entries)`**: keeps the list, `SECURITY INVOKER`. It answers the rows kept.

## Who reads and writes

| Table | Reads | Writes |
|---|---|---|
| `user_bid_activity` | The person, the owner, a dev, anyone on `user_app_activity_viewers` | Nobody by hand. The beat function writes as the caller. |
| `bid_day_entries` | The person, a dev, payroll access, the office | The person, their own rows, for today or yesterday, on a bid they can read. No delete. |

The first rule is the People page's Activity tab's. The second is the clock sessions' own, since the kept list is what a person hands in with the session.

## The beat

`bump_user_bid_activity` never raises. A lost beat costs a few seconds of a rough size. It writes nothing when:

- nobody is signed in, or no bid is given
- the session was opened by someone else as the person (`session_opened_by_imitation()`)
- the caller is in training mode or is a digital twin
- the seconds are zero
- the bid is gone

The tab is lower-cased and cut to letters, digits, `_` and `-`, at most 40. The bucket is the five-minute floor of `now()`. The day a bucket belongs to is worked out at read time in the company's zone, so evening work stays on its own day.

## The read

`get_my_bid_day` answers one JSON object:

| Key | Holds |
|---|---|
| `day`, `from`, `to`, `now` | The company's day, its two bounds and the server's time |
| `changes` | The caller's changes in five-minute buckets by bid: `own` (the person's), `app` (the app's own writes, such as the Labor tab's sync on open) and the `tables` touched |
| `beats` | The caller's rows of `user_bid_activity` for the day |
| `kept` | The caller's rows of `bid_day_entries` for the day |
| `sessions` | The day's clock sessions, less a revoked or rejected one |
| `bids` | The number, name and trade of every bid named above that the caller can read |

The changes come from `bid_changes`, `bids_submission_entries` and `bid_rfqs`. The ledger has no index on its time on purpose, so the day's rows are found by walking its primary key backwards to the last row before the day. The ledger's id grows with time, so for today that is a walk over today's rows.

## The save

`save_my_bid_day` raises in words:

| Refusal | When |
|---|---|
| *Sign in to keep your list.* | Nobody is signed in |
| *The list is the person's own. It is not kept while you look as them.* | View as |
| *This account cannot keep a list of bids.* | Training mode, or a twin |
| *Only today's list and yesterday's can be kept.* | Any other day |
| *The list did not arrive as a list.* | The entries are not a JSON array |
| *A day holds at most 200 bids.* | A longer list |

An entry that is not an object, or names no bid, is left out. A bid the caller cannot read is left out. A bid named twice keeps its last entry.
