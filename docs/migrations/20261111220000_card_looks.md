# 20261111220000_card_looks.sql (2026-11-11, v2.6951)

The looks the company offers for a business card and an email footer, and a person's pick among them (punch list #313; `to-dos/cards-and-footers/README.md` → *Other looks, and offering approved ones*). A look is the card and the footer in other colours over the same layouts (`src/lib/brandKit/looks.ts`). A developer draws one in code. Nobody is offered it until the owner switches it on.

## What it does

1. **`users.card_look text`**: the look a person picked, by its name in the code (`classic`, `navy-orange`, `navy-yellow`). Null means the company's default. `users_card_look_short` holds it to 40 characters.
2. **`GRANT UPDATE (card_look)`** to `authenticated`, by name, as the card's other columns were.
3. **One row in `app_settings`, `card_looks_v1`**: a JSON object in `value_text`, `{"approved": [...], "default": "..."}`. It is seeded with the three looks on and the look in use as the default, since the owner approved the two navy looks as alternatives on 2026-11-11. `ON CONFLICT DO NOTHING`: a later push never overwrites what he has switched since.

## Why the shape

- **The pick is not held to a list in the database.** A pick the owner has since switched off, or a look that was retired, draws as the default (`resolveLook`). So switching a look off needs no write to anyone's row, and no person is left on a look that cannot be drawn.
- **No new policy.** A person writes their own row and the office anyone's, through the two policies that carry a name and a phone. Any signed-in person reads `app_settings` and only a dev writes it ("Devs can manage app settings"), which is who the owner's card is for.
- **The looks themselves are code, not rows.** A look is colours over layouts that a test holds (`looks.test.ts`: the same bones, and every pair of colours reads). A row could not carry that promise.

**Tables**: none created, so no fence call is owed. **Data**: one settings row is added when absent. **Lock**: a nullable column and one CHECK take a brief `ACCESS EXCLUSIVE` lock on `users`; the CHECK scans a small table whose new column is all null. With `lock_timeout = '3s'` a busy moment fails fast and the push is retried.

**Apply**: `supabase db push` from a clean checkout of `main` after this merges. The client reads the column and the setting fail-soft, so before the push nobody is offered a look and the cards draw as they did. Client first, then the push.

## Verify

Tried before the PR on a local Postgres 15 with stand-ins for `users` and `app_settings`: the file applied twice; with the setting changed between the two runs, the second left it as changed; the column took `navy-yellow` and refused a 41 letter name.

**Check** on prod after the push:

```sql
select column_name from information_schema.columns where table_schema = 'public' and table_name = 'users' and column_name = 'card_look';
select value_text from public.app_settings where key = 'card_looks_v1';
```

One column, and the setting with three looks and `classic` as the default.
