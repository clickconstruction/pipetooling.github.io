# 20261019060000_job_people.sql (2026-10-19, v2.5646)

The job's people (punch list #128 PR 1, `to-dos/estimator-money-word/README.md`): the customer's word on paying opens to the people who hear it. On a job an estimator bid, the estimator often hears first when the customer will pay, and today phones the assistant to have it marked: the promise gates name roles, and an estimator is none of them. The client works before and after the push (the per-job read falls back to the company-wide one until the function is there), so either order; push after the PR merges.

## What it does

1. **`is_job_people(p_job_id uuid)`** (STABLE, SECURITY DEFINER): true for the office (`is_office_staff()`: dev, master, assistant, controller), or for a signed-in **estimator or primary** who is the job's account manager (`jobs_ledger.account_manager_user_id`) or the estimator or account manager of the bid the job came from (`bids.estimator_id`, `bids.account_manager_id` through `jobs_ledger.bid_id`). False with nobody signed in, for a job that is not there, and for every other role, a helper on the job's team included.
2. **`add_job_payment_promise`**, **`void_job_payment_promise`** and **`set_job_promised_pay_date`**: each gate reads `can_write_payment_promises() OR is_job_people(job)` (the void looks up the promise's job first). Bodies otherwise as before: `heard_by` is `auth.uid()`, `source` is `office`, the payer follows the bill-to rule, the board's one-per-job date follows. A primary who is a job's account manager writes on it now; one who is not stays refused.
3. **`list_job_payment_promises_for(p_job_id uuid)`**: one job's live promises in the shape of `list_job_payment_promises()`, NULL outside `can_read_payment_promises() OR is_job_people(job)`. The company-wide read is unchanged, for the office's readers (the Lien desk, the legal packet, Owner Billing); the per-job sheet and the job's activity feed read this one.

Nothing on `jobs_ledger`'s policies changes: the estimator still reads no job row and reads the money through SECURITY DEFINER functions, as the job-accounts strip does.

## Why the shape

- The scope is the job's, not the role's: the field that means *whose customer this is* already exists (`account_manager_user_id`, the one `primary_scope` runs on), and a bid's estimator is the other person who sold the job. One predicate serves the estimator today and primary's own gap (they see their jobs but no promise chip) later.
- The role check inside the predicate (`estimator`, `primary`) keeps a stale `estimator_id` on a bid from opening a job to someone who has since become a helper or a sub.
- A new per-job function rather than an overload of `list_job_payment_promises`: PostgREST resolves overloads by argument names, and a new name keeps the client's fallback plain.
