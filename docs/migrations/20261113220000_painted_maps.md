# 20261113220000_painted_maps.sql (2026-11-13, v2.7202)

A painted map (punch list #345, PR 1). A street address cannot say which gate, where to park or where the meter is. A painted map can: lines somebody walked, drove or drew by hand on the view from above, and named places with a word and a photo. It hangs on a job, on a development, or on nothing yet. This migration is the thing itself. No screen reads or writes it yet.

## What it does

Three tables, two functions and one storage bucket.

1. **`painted_maps`**: a name (1 to 80 characters), a line of `words`, and its home: `job_id` or `development_id`, never both (`painted_maps_one_home_check`), or neither. Both are set null when the job or the development goes, so a map is kept for its maker and the office. `created_by` defaults to the caller.
2. **`painted_map_lines`**: one stroke. `how` is `walked`, `driven` or `drawn`. `points` is a list of `[latitude, longitude]` pairs, 2 to 5000 of them. `poor_parts` is a list of `[from, to]` indexes into the points: the stretches the phone was not sure of, drawn faint and left out of the length. `fixed_by_hand` marks a walked line with a part redrawn by hand. `length_m` and `margin_m` are the length and its give or take. `measured_ft` with `measured_how` (`wheel`, `tape`, `other`) is a length somebody measured and typed in, both or neither.
3. **`painted_map_places`**: a named dot: a name, a line of words, a point on the globe, `accuracy_m` (null when placed by hand) and `photo_path`, an object in the bucket.
4. **`painted_map_on_development_team(development)`**, SECURITY DEFINER: is the caller on the team of a job in this development? A helper or a subcontractor cannot read the ledger, so this one question is answered for them. It says yes or no about the caller and nothing else, and is closed to anon.
5. **`painted_map_can_touch(job, development, created_by)`**, SECURITY INVOKER: the gate. See below.
6. **The bucket `painted-map-photos`**: private, 2 MB a file, JPEG, PNG and WebP. Objects are named `<map id>/<file>`.

**A saved line is a shape.** `points` holds pairs of numbers and nothing else. There is no clock along a line, so a line cannot say how fast anyone walked or how long they stood. Nothing in this migration follows a phone.

## Who may do what

- **Read a map and add to it** (a stroke, a place, a photo): whoever passes the gate for the map's home.
  - A map on a job: `on_site_can_touch_job`, Materials on site's own gate. The caller sees the job in the ledger under their own RLS, or is on its team.
  - A map on a development: the caller can see the development, or can see a job in it, or is on the team of a job in it.
  - A map on nothing: its maker, and the office (`is_office_staff`).
- **Make a map**: the same gate, and `created_by` must be the caller.
- **Rename a map, move it to another home, throw it away**: its maker or the office, and they must pass the gate for the new home too.
- **Change or remove a line or a place**: whoever made it, or the office. A line and a place are signed by the caller (`made_by`, `set_by`) and cannot be signed for someone else.
- **Remove a photo from the bucket**: whoever uploaded it, or the office.
- Nobody signed out reads anything. The page for a shared link is a later migration with a door of its own.

The four fence calls close the three tables to an account in training, a digital twin and anyone archived.

## Locks

Three `CREATE TABLE`s on names nothing uses. The foreign keys take a brief lock on `jobs_ledger`, `developments` and `users`. The fence calls create only what is missing, so they lock the new tables alone. `SET lock_timeout = '3s'` makes a busy moment fail fast.

## Checked on a database

- **Locally, on a throwaway Postgres 15 with stub tables** (Docker was not available on this Mac): the file applied twice with no change the second time, and the scenario's 53 assertions that do not need the fences passed. That run found a real fault before it shipped: the bucket's policies read `split_part(name, '/', 1)` inside an `EXISTS` over `painted_maps`, and a map has a `name` of its own, so the policy compared a map's id with its own name and refused every photo. The policies now write `objects.name`.
- **The bed** `supabase/tests/painted_maps/20_scenario.sql` (`npm run test:pg:painted-maps`, in `scripts/sql-beds.txt`) runs the whole scenario on the real schema in CI, the fences included: an account in training and a twin write nothing, and someone who left reads nothing.
