# 20261113040000_on_site_part_ties.sql (2026-11-12, v2.7076)

Two names a person says are one part (punch list #330, PR 3 of 5). A site's materials screen folds two typed part names into one row only when their words are the same. Two names that are nearly the same are a person's call. The screen asks **Same part?** once, and this table holds the answer.

## What it does

One table, `on_site_part_ties`, one row per answer:

1. **`development_id`** or **`job_id`**: where the answer belongs. A site is a development. A job on no site carries its own. One of the two, never both and never neither (`on_site_part_ties_scope_check`, which names `IS NOT NULL` in both branches because a check passes on an unknown answer). Both cascade from their parent.
2. **`a_key`, `b_key`**: the screen's two row keys, `id:<part id>` for a parts-book part or `words:<the same-words fold>` for a typed name (`rollKey` in `src/lib/materials/siteRollup.ts`). The two differ and each starts `id:` or `words:` (`on_site_part_ties_keys_check`).
3. **`a_name`, `b_name`**: the names as they read when the answer was given, for the screen's *Names joined here*.
4. **`verdict`**: `same` or `different`. Both are kept, so a pair is asked about once.
5. **`by_user_id`** (the caller by default), **`by_name`**, **`at`**.

Two partial unique indexes hold one answer per pair per scope, whichever way round the pair was written (`LEAST` and `GREATEST` of the two keys).

Two helpers, both `SECURITY INVOKER`:

- `on_site_can_read_ties(development_id, job_id)`: the on-site gate, `on_site_can_touch_job`, on the job itself or on any job of the development that the caller's own `jobs_ledger` RLS shows.
- `on_site_can_tie_parts()`: the roles that read the Materials desk with its money: dev, master_technician, assistant, controller, primary.

**A tie never touches the ledger.** It is a reading of the finished lines. Each spelling keeps its own line and its own count in its box in `job_material_movements`, and the screen's row adds the lines. A count resets its line, so joining two spellings in the ledger would let one count wipe the other's pieces and report them missing.

**A tie belongs to its site.** `2" 90` is PVC on one site and may be copper on the next.

## Who may do what

- **Read**: `on_site_can_read_ties(development_id, job_id)`. A person on a job's team who cannot see the ledger row reads a job's answers and no site's, so their site screen shows the same-words rows alone.
- **Insert**: in the caller's own name, by a role `on_site_can_tie_parts()` names, on a scope they may read.
- **Delete**: the same roles on the same scopes. Undo is a delete.
- **Update**: nobody. `authenticated` holds `SELECT`, `INSERT` and `DELETE`; `anon` holds nothing.

The migration ends with the four fence calls, since it creates a table: training mode's write blocks and statement trigger, the digital-twin fence and the archived caller's read block.

## Locks

A new table takes no lock on a live one beyond the brief ones its two foreign keys take on `developments` and `jobs_ledger`. `SET lock_timeout = '3s'` makes a busy moment fail fast.

## Release order

The client reads the table when a site's screen opens, and treats a refused read as no answers, so the screen draws either way. Merge, then `bash scripts/db-push.sh` right after. Until the push, **Same part?** is not offered.

## Checked on a database

Docker was not running, so the repo's SQL beds could not run here. The migration was applied twice to a throwaway local Postgres 15 with stub tables and the real gate's body, then exercised as eight made-up people: 36 checks. Each role's insert, the pair refused a second time and the other way round, the same pair on another site, a job on no site, every shape check, who reads what, no update for anyone, who may delete, and the cascade. The stub is not the app's schema and Postgres 15 is not the app's 17, so this proves the SQL's logic, not its fit; the dry run at the push still matters.
