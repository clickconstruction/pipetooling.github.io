# 20261123160430_my_bids_hidden_at.sql (2026-11-23, v2.8369)

`user_bid_notes_read_state` can hold the bids a person hid on the Dashboard's My Bids. Step 6 of punch list #426.

## Why

A bid hidden on My Bids was kept in one browser's localStorage, under `dashboard_my_bids_hidden_<user>`. A bid hidden on the phone was back on the desk, and a cleared browser brought every hidden bid back. The owner picked *Hidden follows you* on 2026-11-23.

## What changes

- **`hidden_at timestamptz`**, nullable, on `user_bid_notes_read_state`. A hidden bid is the person's row with `hidden_at` set. Show again sets it back to null.
- A comment on the column says so.

Nothing else. The table already holds one row per person and bid, and the baseline's four own-row policies open each row to its person alone, for select, insert, update and delete. The new column rides on them.

## Rows with no read marks

Hiding a bid that has no row yet makes one with only `hidden_at`. Its read marks are null. Both readers of the marks read a null mark as they read no row (`isMyBidStreamUnread` on My Bids, `isUnreadVersusWatermark` in the Bid Board's unread counts), so a hide changes no unread count. **Mark read** upserts the two marks and `updated_at` alone, so it never clears a hide.

## Who reads it

Nobody yet. My Bids reads and writes it from the next PR of punch list #426, which also moves the ids kept in the browser into it once.

## Order

Additive and idempotent: a second run finds the column and adds nothing. Adding a nullable column with no default rewrites nothing, and the `SET lock_timeout` keeps the brief lock from queueing the app. The client on prod reads the table with `select('*')` or by named columns and never names `hidden_at`. Push any time after the merge, then regenerate the types.
