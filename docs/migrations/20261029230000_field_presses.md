# 20261029230000_field_presses.sql (2026-10-29, punch list #163 PR 2)

The server side of the queue a phone keeps when a press cannot reach the office ([`to-dos/offline-field-queue/README.md`](../../to-dos/offline-field-queue/README.md); the client in the PR 2 fragment).

**`field_presses`**: one row per press the phone queued and sent later, keyed by the id the phone minted (`id uuid PRIMARY KEY`), with `user_id`, `kind` (`clock_in`, `clock_out`, `job_note`, `field_report`), `pressed_at` (the phone's clock at the press), `landed_at`, `late` (landed more than the typed-hours slack of ten minutes after it was pressed) and `result` (what the first landing answered: `{"session_id": …}`). RLS: a person reads and inserts their own rows; a dev, payroll access and the assistant-like roles read everyone's. No UPDATE or DELETE policy. `anon` has nothing. The three fences (`apply_read_only_write_blocks`, `apply_read_only_stmt_blocks`, `apply_digital_twin_write_blocks`) close the file.

**`land_field_press(p_id uuid, p_kind text, p_pressed_at timestamptz, p_payload jsonb) RETURNS jsonb`**, `SECURITY INVOKER`, `search_path = public`: the one RPC for every queued press. It refuses with `42501` when nobody is signed in and when the id belongs to someone else's press, and with `22023` for a missing id or a kind this version does not land (job_note and field_report come with PR 3's `CREATE OR REPLACE`). **A second landing of the same id answers the first landing's `result` again with `repeat: true`**, which is how a send that lands twice (a timeout whose request landed, a resend after a lost answer) counts once; a race between two tabs on one id is settled by the primary key, the loser answering the winner's result. The pressed time is bounded: a phone clock ahead of the server reads as now, and a press older than 36 hours is refused (*That press is more than a day old. Report the day instead.*). Every write runs under the caller's own policies, exactly as the phone's direct inserts do today, so the RPC widens nothing.

- **`clock_in`**: refused with *You were already clocked in at 7:30 AM.* when an open session exists. Otherwise one `clock_sessions` row **at the pressed time** (`work_date` from it in the company calendar; `travel_started_at` or `arrived_at` from `payload.travel`). **Rule B** (decided 2026-10-29): when the press is late, the existing typed-hours trigger (v2.4242, `clock_sessions_typed_record`) files the stretch older than its ten-minute slack as a typed entry by the person, which waits for approval like any typed hours, and the session's notes carry the sent-late mark *Sent late from the phone: pressed 7:58 AM, reached the office 9:42 AM.*; the live session runs from the moment the press lands. One row, not two: a clock-out pressed before its clock-in landed still closes it at its own time.
- **`clock_out`**: the session is `payload.session_id`, or the one the queued clock-in press made (found by `payload.session_press_id` in this table). Refused with its reason when that clock-in never reached the office, when the session is gone, or when the office already closed it (*The office closed that session at 8:05 AM.*). Otherwise `clocked_out_at` is the pressed time, never before the clock-in plus a minute and never after now, with the notes, job and bid from the payload; a late clock-out carries the mark too.
- Training mode: a `users.read_only` person's own punch is allowed (v2.1041's rule) while the fences refuse the record, so the function catches `insufficient_privilege` on the record and answers `recorded: false`; the punch stands, as it does today.

`REVOKE ALL … FROM PUBLIC, anon`; `GRANT EXECUTE … TO authenticated`. Idempotent (`IF NOT EXISTS`, `DROP POLICY IF EXISTS`, `CREATE OR REPLACE`).
