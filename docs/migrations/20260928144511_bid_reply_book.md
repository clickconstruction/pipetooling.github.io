# 20260928144511_bid_reply_book.sql (2026-09-28, v2.4025)

The table behind Bid Board → Reply book (owner ask, 2026-09-28): wording the estimators reuse when they answer a GC. One table, two functions, one trigger. Additive; nothing reads it until the client that follows.

## `bid_reply_book_entries`

One row per reply: `title` (what it is for, 1–120 characters), `kind` (`declining` · `following_up` · `asking` · `after_decision` · `other`), `body` (the wording, 1–4,000 characters, without the sender's name), `sign_with_sender` (a copy ends with "Thank you," and the copier's name), `created_by` + `created_by_name`, `created_at`, `updated_at`, `updated_by`.

`created_by` and `updated_by` are `ON DELETE SET NULL`: a reply outlives its author's account, under the name it was posted with. After that only a dev can change or delete it.

## Who may do what (RLS)

| | Who |
|---|---|
| **SELECT**, **INSERT** | the roles that open the Bids page: dev · master · assistant · controller · estimator · primary · superintendent (`can_open_bid_reply_book()`) |
| **UPDATE**, **DELETE** | the person who posted it (while their role still opens Bids), or a dev |

`can_open_bid_reply_book()` mirrors `canOpenBids` in `src/lib/bids/bidsTabAccess.ts`. A role added there is added here in a new migration.

## The stamp trigger

`bid_reply_book_entries_stamp` (BEFORE INSERT OR UPDATE → `stamp_bid_reply_book_entry()`):

- **Insert** — the author is the caller, whatever the row says; the author's name is read from `users`; the posting time is the server's. The title and the wording are trimmed.
- **Update** — the author, the author's name and the posting time cannot be changed. `updated_at` and `updated_by` move only when the title, the kind, the wording or the sign-off changed.
- **An account removal** reaches the row as an UPDATE (`SET NULL`): the author may be cleared, never handed to someone else, and it is not counted as an edit.

## House rules

Opens with `SET lock_timeout = '3s'`; ends with `apply_read_only_write_blocks()`, `apply_read_only_stmt_blocks()` and `apply_digital_twin_write_blocks()`. It touches no existing table.

## Tried before the push

`supabase/tests/bid_reply_book/` — a stand-in schema and one scenario, run by hand against a throwaway Postgres 15:

```bash
psql -v ON_ERROR_STOP=1 -f supabase/tests/bid_reply_book/00_schema.sql
psql -v ON_ERROR_STOP=1 -d bed -f supabase/migrations/20260928144511_bid_reply_book.sql
psql -v ON_ERROR_STOP=1 -d bed -f supabase/tests/bid_reply_book/20_scenario.sql
```

The migration ran twice (idempotent). The scenario ends `bid_reply_book PASSED`: a post naming someone else as its author is stamped with the poster; a blank title, an unknown kind, a title over 120 and wording over 4,000 are refused; another estimator reads a reply and can neither change nor delete it; the author changes and deletes her own; a dev changes and deletes anybody's, and the author stays the author; a helper and a caller with no user read nothing and cannot post; a primary, a superintendent and a controller read and post; removing an account leaves its replies and is not an edit; a save that changes nothing is not an edit.

The first run of the scenario failed on the account removal — the trigger put the author back after `SET NULL` cleared it. The trigger as shipped is the fix.
