# 20261120120000_builder_lookups.sql (2026-11-20, v2.8118)

Permits, Market: what was looked up about a builder, and a person's check of it (punch list #405, step three, Ask for; `to-dos/permits-market-page/`). Two tables, both append-only. They ship alone, ahead of every line of client code that names them, so the merge holds no deploy.

## What it does

- **`builder_lookups`** (id, builder_key, fact, value, detail, source_url, looked_up_on, looked_up_by, looked_up_how, city, created_at): one fact looked up about a name.
  - `fact` is `kind`, `phone` or `person`.
  - A `kind`'s `value` is `builder`, `owner`, `design_firm`, `permit_service` or `trade`, or `unknown` when no public page said it in plain words. `detail` is a few words on the kind.
  - A `phone`'s `value` is the office phone as the page wrote it.
  - A `person`'s `value` is the name and `detail` the title.
  - `source_url` is the page the fact was read on, and `looked_up_on` the day. A check refuses a fact with no web address. The one row that may have none is a kind of `unknown`.
  - `looked_up_by` is the account that wrote the row and `looked_up_how` says an agent or a person did the looking. The account is set null when its user is deleted, so the fact stands.
  - `builder_key` is the name's key, as `permits.builder_key` holds it. No foreign key to `builders`: most names on permits have no row there. `city` records whose call list the name was looked up for.
  - The newest row per key and fact is the fact. One index, on `(builder_key, fact, created_at)`.
- **`builder_lookup_checks`** (id, lookup_id, verdict, said_by, said_at): a person's press on one fact.
  - `verdict` is `checked` (it held), `wrong` (it did not, and the fact is no longer shown) or `back` (a press undone).
  - The newest row per fact is its state. A fact with none reads as looked up, not checked.
  - `lookup_id` cascades with its fact. `said_by` is set null when its user is deleted. One index, on `(lookup_id, said_at)`.
- **Row security.**
  - Both tables are read by `user_can_research_permits()` (the office and every estimator), the rule inside a scalar subquery as `20261111013000` set the other permit tables.
  - A fact is inserted only by a dev account, under its own name (`looked_up_by = auth.uid()` and `is_dev()`).
  - A press is inserted by anyone who reads permits, as oneself.
  - No update and no delete on either.
- Closed to `anon`; `SELECT` and `INSERT` to `authenticated`. The four fences close the file.

## Apart from the office's own word

A looked-up fact never sets a builder's kind (`builders.kind`), its mark (`builder_marks`) or an answer on the call list (`builder_call_answers`). Nothing here reads or writes those tables.

## The write path

No function, no scheduled job and no dev-seat verb writes a fact. The lookups are run once by an agent session signed in to the app as the dev, which inserts the rows through the app's own connection. The row rule is what holds that to a dev account.

## How it was checked

Applied twice on a throwaway local Postgres 15 with stub tables and the row rules' real shape, then a scenario of twelve checks:

- a dev adds a fact as itself, and not under another name
- an estimator and the office cannot add a fact
- a fact with no page, a page that is not a web address, a kind outside the six and a fact outside the three are refused
- a kind that could not be told needs no page
- no fact is updated or deleted
- an estimator and the office read the facts, and a field tech reads none
- an estimator presses as themself only, on a fact that exists, with one of the three verdicts
- the office presses Wrong and Put back, and a field tech neither presses nor reads
- no press is updated or deleted
- `anon` holds nothing on either table
- a deleted account leaves its facts and its presses standing, with nobody named

The stub is not the app's schema and Postgres 15 is not the app's 17, and the four fence appliers were stand-ins. So this proves the SQL's syntax and logic. The dry run at the push proves the fit.
