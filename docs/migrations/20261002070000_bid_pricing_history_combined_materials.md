# 20261002070000_bid_pricing_history_combined_materials.sql (2026-10-01, v2.4371)

`CREATE OR REPLACE` of **`bid_pricing_history(p_service_type_id uuid)`**, the read behind the Pricing tab's win/loss strip (*This number vs your history*, `usePricingMarginHistory` → `PricingMarginHistory`). Only the `materials` CTE changes. Same return type, so the grants stay. Body otherwise identical to `20260822143810_bid_pricing_history_tab_low.sql`. Still `STABLE` and `SECURITY INVOKER`.

**The bug.** `materials` summed the items of the three By Stage POs (`cost_estimates.purchase_order_id_*`) only. A Combined bid (`materials_model = 'rough'`, every bid since May 2026) prices its materials from `bids_takeoff_rough_part_lines`, so it entered the history with $0 of materials. Its estimated margin read 97–99.9% on every decided Combined bid but one, above the strip's 95% sanity band, so the strip left them out. On 2026-10-01 the Plumbing strip had no usable dot at all.

**The rule now** is `bid_estimate_breakdown`'s (`20260911175700`): the stage POs when they hold anything, else Σ line quantity × unit price × the fixture count.

- **Version scope: the version the Workbench priced.** This is `pickActiveVersion` in SQL: `bids.selected_bid_version_id` when it is one of the bid's versions, else its first by `sort_order` (then `created_at`, `id`). An unsplit bid reads its `bid_version_id IS NULL` base. A split bid keeps no NULL-version count rows or lines on prod, so a base-only read would give every versioned bid $0. The bid's one cost estimate carries labor rows synced from the active version's counts, and the Workbench's own margin (the strip's ▼) reads the same version. That makes the active version the only scope where a dot and the ▼ compare like with like. Summing the base versions (the *Mark sent* value rule) would double-count the decided bids whose base versions are copies (BP18, BP286, BP362) against one labor set. `bid_estimate_breakdown` picks the newest version instead, for the job budget's own reasons.
- **Counts as on Pricing.** A count of 0 reads ×1 (`roughCountMultiplier`), and so does a line whose count row sits outside the version. `bid_estimate_breakdown` reads a 0 count as 0 and multiplies an out-of-version line by the other version's count. No decided bid had either case on 2026-10-01 (BP366, not yet decided, holds the only 14 out-of-version lines).
- **No order rounding.** Pricing adds the Sold in sticks extra (`roughMaterialsTotalWithRounding`). No part line on prod carried an `order_increment` on 2026-10-01, so the history would read up to that extra below Pricing once rules are used. That is a few sticks per part, small against a margin read to the half point. It does not justify a second copy of the rounding rule in SQL.
- **PO first, not model first.** Pricing and the Approval PDF (v2.4368) pick by `materials_model`. This rule matches the job budget instead. The two differ only on a bid that holds both stores. On 2026-10-01 no Combined bid linked a stage PO with items. BP159, the one decided By Stage bid with parked part lines and no PO items, gains $14.96.

**Dry run** on prod in `BEGIN … ROLLBACK` (as `postgres`, every service type, before vs after): 73 rows before and after. 24 Combined rows gained their materials (+$502,577 in total) and one By Stage row gained $14.96. Spot checks against the app's Labor tab: BP190 +$8,368.34 and BP483 +$47,211.84, BP483 being its selected base version, not the $46,282.85 alternate. BP339 +$140,598.95, BP363 +$23,203.62. Plumbing's strip went from 0 to 6 dots (2 won, 4 lost on price; average won margin 86.6%). BP286 ($1,450 bid value against $3,501.60 of parts) now falls outside the band. Plumbing runs in about 21 ms.

**Still different from the Workbench** (left as they were; aligned in v2.4372, [`20261002080000`](20261002080000_bid_pricing_history_kernel_parity.md)): labor hours use the pre-v2.3291 rule (`is_fixed` or × count; no sub, task or per-100-ft kinds), and the estimator time ($10 a count row, or the flat amount) is still added, though v2.3294 took it out of the Workbench's cost.

Apply with `supabase db push` after the PR merges. No client change is needed and the return shape is unchanged, so deploy order does not matter and the types do not change.
