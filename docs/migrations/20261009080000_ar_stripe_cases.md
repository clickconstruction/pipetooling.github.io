# 20261009080000_ar_stripe_cases.sql (2026-10-08, v2.4950)

A card chargeback or a failed bank debit on a Stripe bill is a case beside the checks that came back (punch list #76, piece 1). A bill paid through Stripe read paid whatever happened after. Stripe takes the money back while a customer disputes a card charge, and keeps it if they win. A bank debit (ACH) can fail days after the customer pressed Pay. `stripe-webhook` read none of it.

- **`ar_stripe_cases`** — one case per Stripe object, `stripe_object_id` unique: a dispute (`dp_…`) or a failed payment intent (`pi_…`).
  - Columns: `kind` (dispute · debit_failed), `stripe_mode`, `stripe_charge_id`, `stripe_invoice_id`, and the bill, job and payment it found (`invoice_id`, `job_id`, `payment_id`). None is a foreign key, so a case outlives a payment or a bill that changes.
  - Stripe's words: `amount`, `reason` (the dispute's reason code, or the bank's failure message), `stripe_status`, `due_by` (the dispute's answer deadline), `occurred_at`.
  - The case's state: `opened_at`, `lost_at`, `closed_at` / `closed_reason` (won · paid · voided · taken_off · put_back · settled_other_way · not_coming) / `closed_by` / `closed_note`, `notified_at`, `lost_notified_at`.
  - RLS: the AR roles read. Every write goes through the functions and triggers below. Three indexes: open cases, by payment, by bill. The three house block calls.
- **`record_ar_stripe_case(p_kind, p_object_id, p_mode, p_charge_id, p_stripe_invoice_id, p_amount, p_reason, p_status, p_due_by, p_occurred_at)`** — service role only (`stripe-webhook`). Returns `{case_id, opened, lost_now, closed}` or `{skipped}`.
  - A known object brings its case up to date: the status, the due day and the reason. A dispute `won` or `warning_closed` closes as won. The first `lost` stamps `lost_at` and leaves the case open, and `lost_now` is true only when the case was open.
  - A new object opens a case on the bill whose `stripe_invoice_id` Stripe names. A dispute keeps the bill's payment of that amount, else its newest payment.
  - It skips `unknown_invoice` (not a bill of ours), `mode_mismatch` (the bill's `stripe_mode` against the event's), `already_paid` (a failed debit on a bill a later try paid) and `already_won` (an inquiry closed before we heard of it).
- **Triggers** — `jobs_ledger_payments_close_stripe_case_gone` (`AFTER DELETE` on `jobs_ledger_payments`) closes a dispute whose payment came off its job as taken_off. `jobs_ledger_invoices_close_stripe_debit_case_upd` (`AFTER UPDATE OF status, stripe_invoice_status`) closes a failed debit when its bill is paid (paid) or its Stripe bill is voided (voided). `jobs_ledger_invoices_close_stripe_debit_case_del` (`AFTER DELETE`) closes it as voided when the bill is sent back.
- **`close_ar_stripe_case(p_case_id, p_reason, p_note)`** and **`reopen_ar_stripe_case(p_case_id)`** — the AR roles. They close as settled_other_way or not_coming with a note of at most 500 characters, and reopen only a case the office closed.
- **`put_back_lost_dispute_bill(p_case_id)`** — the AR roles; an open dispute with `lost_at` only, its payment still on the job. In one transaction:
  1. The case closes as put_back. It closes first, so the payment's delete trigger leaves it alone.
  2. The payment is deleted, with a `removed` row in `jobs_ledger_payment_events`. Its reason is `stripe_dispute_lost: dp_… · <reason>` and the actor is the person who pressed.
  3. `payments_made` is recomputed.
  4. The bill drops its Stripe link: `stripe_invoice_id`, `hosted_invoice_url`, `stripe_invoice_status`, `stripe_invoice_memo`, `external_send_channel`, `external_send_note`, `sent_to_customer_at`. It goes back to `ready_to_bill`, or stays `billed` while another payment sits on it.
  5. A `paid` job that now owes money goes back to `billed` through `update_job_status`.

  The client then moves the job to Ready to Bill when no billed line remains (`syncJobToReadyToBillIfNoBilledInvoicesRemain`), as every send-back does. Stripe is not called: its invoice stays paid, and its dispute is the record.
- **`list_ar_return_cases`** — the return type gains `stripe_case jsonb`, so the function is dropped and made again in the same transaction (grants and comment restored). The first two `RETURN QUERY` blocks are 20261008100000's, with `NULL` in the new column. A third adds the Stripe cases:
  - `source` is `stripe_dispute` or `stripe_debit`, and the case's id rides in `mercury_transaction_id`.
  - `kind` is `card` or `bank debit`, `failed_at` is when Stripe raised it, and `bank_reason` is Stripe's reason.
  - `recorded_payment` holds a dispute's payment while it is on the job.
  - `promise` is the newest They said… on the job since the case opened.
  - `stripe_case` holds the kind, the object, the mode, the status, the due day, `lost_at` / `lost_notified_at`, the bill's number and status, the job, and whether the payment is still on the job.

Why no automatic put-back on a lost dispute: neither existing reversal can take back a bill Stripe charged. `reverse-stripe-invoice-out-of-band-payment` needs our own out-of-band mark, and `void-stripe-invoice-for-revert` answers 409 on real money. And nothing comes off a job until a person presses, the rule every case follows. So the lost dispute stays open and red, and is told a second time, until someone presses **Put the bill back**.
