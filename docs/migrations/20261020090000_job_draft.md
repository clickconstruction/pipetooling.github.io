# 20261020090000_job_draft.sql (2026-10-20, v2.5659)

An estimator's job draft (punch list #129 follow-up, the owner's call of 2026-10-20). The live walk as an estimator found the won bid's Job block offering *Open the job* on BP166 although #878 existed, and the New Job form's Save refused by the `jobs_ledger` INSERT policy (42501). The owner's answer: an estimator does not open a job, they send Dispatch a draft of it, and the inbox opens New Job with it. The client works before the push: `list_bid_jobs_opened` is read fail-soft beside the table read (an estimator sees no job chip until the push, as before), and the draft rides a column that already exists. Push after the PR merges.

## What it does

- **`dispatch_requests.pending_payload`** gains a second use, documented in its comment: on an `open_job_from_bid` request, `{"job_draft": {v, jobName, address, gcKey, gcName, carry, serviceTypeId, wantedBy, note}}`, written by `lib/bids/jobDraft.ts` (version 1). The column (v2.1615) was made for a pending action's own context, so no column is added. The request's policies hold it: the sender inserts and reads their own row, Dispatch members and devs read and close it.
- **`list_bid_jobs_opened(p_bid_ids uuid[])`** (STABLE, SECURITY DEFINER): the jobs opened from each bid (`jobs_ledger.bid_id`), newest first, with id, HCP number, C#, name and created day only, for dev, master, assistant, controller and estimator (the job-accounts strip's audience, `list_bid_job_account_strip`). Nothing for any other role; a primary keeps the table read its own policies give.

Nothing on `jobs_ledger`'s policies changes.

## Why the shape

- The draft is the estimator's word, not a job: it goes where Dispatch already looks, and the office still makes the row, so scheduling stays with the assistant and nobody widens the INSERT policy.
- The job chip needs only the job's number and name, which the job-accounts strip already gives estimators; the function answers that one question without the strip's supply-house rows.
