# 20261010043000_gc_trade_sign_sow.sql (2026-10-09, v2.5126)

GC mode, the real build, the trade partner portal's P2c-i (`to-dos/gc-mode/PORTAL_REAL_BUILD.md` → *The PRs, in order*, item 6; plan `to-dos/gc-mode/mockups/portal-p2c.md`, on branch `spike/gc-mode`). It adds the verb with which a trade signs its statement of work from its portal. Nothing calls it yet: P2c-ii's `sign_sow` kind in `submit-gc-trade-portal` will, with the portal's sign block.

It changes no table. It needs B6-a (`20261009140000_gc_award_and_sow`, for `gc_sows` and its signer's fields) and B6-b-i (`20261010009000_gc_papers`, for `company_id` on `person_contract_documents`) applied first.

## What it does

1. **`gc_trade_sign_sow(company, sow, printed name, signature path, ip, user agent)`**, returning `timestamptz`. It is `SECURITY INVOKER`, revoked from `PUBLIC`, `anon` and `authenticated`, and granted to `service_role` only. The submit function has already turned the link into its company, and passes that company first, as P2a's verbs take it.
2. **It signs only a statement of work the office sent**, the company's own, once the company has signed our master agreement. That is a `person_contract_documents` row with its `company_id`, `doc_type` `agreement` and status `signed`, the rule B6-b-i's `gc_company_paper_kept` keeps for the `msa` promise. It locks the row first, so two presses at once sign it once.
3. **It writes**:
   - status `signed`, and `signed_on` the app's day;
   - `signer_printed_name`, trimmed;
   - `signer_signature_storage_path`, the drawn signature the submit function stored, or null for a typed one;
   - `signer_consented_at`, the database's time;
   - `signer_ip` and `signer_user_agent`, blank as null.
4. **It keeps the `sow` promise** of that company, job and trade, through `gc_keep_promises`, as the prototype's `promisesKeptBy` did.
5. **It returns the consent time it wrote.** P2c-ii's submit function gives it to the `esign_consents` row it writes after (`recordEsignConsent`, record type `gc_sow`, which B6-a put in that table's CHECK), so the row and the ledger match, as `accept-contract`'s one timestamp does.
6. **Refusals** are keys with the plain words in `DETAIL`, `RAISE EXCEPTION '<key>' USING ERRCODE = 'P0001'`, in this order:

| Key | When |
|---|---|
| `notFound` | no statement of work with that id |
| `notYours` | another company's, said before its status |
| `alreadySigned` | signed already, or a second press |
| `sowNotSent` | a draft the office has not sent, or one it cancelled |
| `msaFirst` | the company has not signed our master agreement: "Sign our master agreement first." |
| `nameNeeded` | a blank name |
| `tooLong` | a name past 200 characters |

`alreadySigned`, `msaFirst` and `sowNotSent` are new keys for the trade. P2c-ii gives each its status in `TRADE_SQL_ERRORS` and its words in `TRADE_ERROR_WORDS`. Until then `gcTradeSubmit.test.ts` lists them as waiting on P2c-ii.

The scenario's fixture is a building clinic with five trades: one sent to a company that signed our master agreement, one sent to a company whose agreement is only sent, a draft, a signed one and a cancelled one. Its papers are made by name (`gc-company:<id>`), so B6-b-i's trigger fills their `company_id`. It checks:
- only the service role may call it;
- every refusal above, by its key and its words, and nothing signed by any of them;
- a drawn signature: the time returned, and the status, the day, the trimmed name, the path, the time, the IP and the browser written, with the promise kept;
- a second signature refused;
- a typed signature once the company's agreement is signed: no image, and a blank IP and browser kept as null;
- a signed-in dev refused with `42501`.
