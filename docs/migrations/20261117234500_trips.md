# 20261117234500_trips.sql (2026-11-17, v2.7655)

A trip record's tables. Punch list #379, PR 2 of nine. The rules they hold are the kernel of PR 1 (`src/lib/trips/`, v2.7653). No screen reads or writes them yet: the Trips look on the Development page is the PR that follows.

A trip is one going-out to a site away from home and coming back. It is a reading laid over the clock. No table here names a clock row, a bill or a cost, and saving, changing or deleting a trip changes none of them.

## The tables

| Table | One row is | What it holds |
|---|---|---|
| `trips` | a trip | `development_id` (the site), or `job_id` for a trip a job with no development holds, one of the two always (`trips_has_a_home`). `purpose` (what for), `asked_by` (`us`, `them` or null), `asked_who`, `asked_how`, `outcome` (how it went), `billing` (`inside`, `extra`, `ours` or null), `via` (`app`, `phone`, `mcp`) with `agent_label`, who made and last changed it. |
| `trip_people` | a person on a trip | `person_id` (the roster person, how the crew's days name them), `user_id` (the login, how the clock names them and how they read their own trip), `person_name` (always, and alone for someone not in the app), `days`. One name a trip. |
| `trip_jobs` | a job a trip's work goes on | Optional for good. A trip belongs to the site, and no job is made to hold it. |
| `trip_dismissals` | *Not a trip*, once | A person's days at a site that somebody said were not a trip, both ends in, so *Looks like a trip* does not offer them again. Read by the finder of PR 5. |

**A person's days** are one `jsonb` object of day to kind: `{"2026-10-30": "road", "2026-10-31": "site"}`. The kinds are `ready`, `road` and `site`. A day off in the middle is a day that is not there. `trip_days_ok(jsonb)` is the check on the column: every key a real calendar day, every value one of the three.

Where a trip stands (Planned, Out now, Back) is not stored. It is read from the days and today.

## Who

- `trips_office()` names the audience in one place: dev, master_technician, assistant, controller and primary, the same roles as the Development page's office door. They read and change every row.
- A person on a trip reads that trip, who else is on it and its jobs, through `trip_is_mine(trip_id)`. It is a definer with row security off because `trips` and `trip_people` would otherwise ask each other. It answers yes or no about the caller.
- Nobody else reads a row. An estimator reads none. `anon` holds no grant.
- *Not a trip* lines are the office's alone, and a line is kept in the keeper's own name.
- The four fences are called at the end, so a training account, a digital twin and an archived caller are held as on every table.

A crew member starting a trip from the phone is PR 7, which widens the write side with its own migration.

## The one write door

`trip_save(p jsonb, p_dry_run boolean DEFAULT false)` saves a trip whole or not at all: its fields, its people with their days and its jobs, in one transaction. It is `SECURITY INVOKER`, so the policies above decide who.

- An `id` that exists is replaced by what `p` says.
- An `id` that does not exist is made under that id. That is how a delete is undone.
- `p_dry_run` checks and counts and writes nothing. The dev seat's `plan_trip` will read it.
- It refuses in words: a trip with no person and day, a person with no name, one name twice, days that are not days, billing or asked-by outside their words, a trip with no development and no job.

The screen and the dev seat both save through it, so there is one write path.

## What goes with what

- A development deleted takes its trips, their people, their jobs and its *Not a trip* lines.
- A job deleted comes off the trips its work went on, and those trips stand.
- A job deleted takes a trip it holds. Combining two jobs deletes one of them, so a trip held by the job that goes is lost with it. Trips held by a job arrive with the Job window's **+ Trip** in PR 6, and that PR carries the trip across a combine.

## Order
