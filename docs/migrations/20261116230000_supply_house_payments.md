# 20261116230000_supply_house_payments.sql (2026-11-16, v2.7450)

Supply house payments, the ground (the owner, 2026-11-16: record a payment to a supply house the way People pay does, over one bill or several). PR 1 of a seven-PR train. No screen reads the new tables yet.

- **`supply_house_payments`**: one money movement to a house. `paid_on` (a calendar day in the company's time zone), `amount` (what was sent, zero when a credit memo covered the bills), `method` (`card` | `ach` | `check` | `cash` | `other`, NULL until someone says), `method_ref`, `receipt_link`, `memo`, `source` (`recorded` typed in the window, `mark_paid` made by a by-hand Mark paid, `before` made by the backfill), `removed_at` / `removed_by` / `removed_by_name` / `removed_reason` (a removed payment counts for nothing; the screens come in a later PR), `created_by` / `created_by_name`. A house deleted takes its payments.
- **`supply_house_payment_lines`**: where a payment was put, one row per bill (`payment_id`, `invoice_id`, `amount`). Above zero on a bill, below zero on a credit memo. A bill deleted takes its lines.
- **On account** is not stored: it is a payment's `amount` less the sum of its lines.
- **`supply_house_invoices.amount_paid`** (numeric, default 0): what the live payments have put on a bill, below zero on a credit memo a payment has used. An unpaid bill has `amount - amount_paid` open, so a reader that totals unpaid bills takes a part-paid bill's balance off the row. Kept by the sync below, never written by hand.
- **`supply_house_invoice_sync_paid(invoice_id)`**: sets one bill's `is_paid`, `paid_at` and `amount_paid` from the lines of its live payments. A bill above zero is paid when they reach its amount, within half a cent; `paid_at` is noon in the company's time zone on the latest payment's day, as the invoice form writes a typed day. A $0.00 bill is left alone. While it writes the bill it sets `supply_house_payments.syncing` for the transaction, which is how the bill's trigger tells its write from one made by hand. It locks the bill `FOR NO KEY UPDATE`, so a line's foreign key never waits on it.
- **Triggers on the lines** (`supply_house_payment_lines_guard`, `…_sync`): a line stays on its payment, its bill is the payment's house's, its sign is its bill's, and a credit memo marked used by hand takes no line. The guard locks the bill and the payment first, so two lines on one of them land one after the other. No bill carries more than it is for and no payment places more than it sent; each bill touched is read again.
- **Triggers on a payment** (`supply_house_payments_guard`, `…_sync`): its amount never drops under its lines, it stays with its bills' house, and a changed day or a removal reads its bills again. A removed payment is put back only whole: every bill it paid must still be at its house with room for its line, or the update is refused with the bill's number.
- **Trigger on `supply_house_invoices`** (`supply_house_invoices_payment_sync`, after insert or an update of `is_paid`, `amount`, `paid_at` or `supply_house_id`): the by-hand direction.
  - Marked paid, or a paper with no payment that becomes a paid bill: a `mark_paid` payment for what is open.
  - Marked not paid: its press payments go. If typed payments still cover the bill the write is **refused** with the reason: the money was sent, so the payment is removed or the bill taken off it instead. Letting it through and marking paid again would count the money twice.
  - A press payment (live, source `mark_paid` or `before`, no method, one line) follows its bill's amount, paid day and house. A bill carrying a typed payment cannot move house.
  - A bill's amount lowered under its typed lines trims them, newest payment first, and the difference waits on account. A bill set to $0.00, or turned from an invoice into a credit memo, lets go of its lines and keeps the paid state it was written with.
  - A credit memo is marked by hand as before until a payment line has used it. After that the payment owns it: marking it unused, lowering it, turning it into an invoice or deleting it is refused with the reason.
- **`record_supply_house_payment(p_supply_house_id, p_paid_on, p_amount, p_lines, p_method, p_method_ref, p_receipt_link, p_memo, p_keep_on_account, p_from_account)`**: the payment and its lines in one statement, one house at a time (an advisory transaction lock). `p_lines` is `[{ invoice_id, amount }]`. The lines may come to less than was sent only when `p_keep_on_account` is true. `p_from_account` is `[{ payment_id, invoice_id, amount }]`: money an earlier payment left on account, placed as a line of that payment. Returns `{ payment_id, lines, placed, on_account, placed_from_account }`.
- **The backfill**: one `before` payment per paid bill above zero that has no line, for the bill's whole amount, on the day in `paid_at` (the company's time zone), with its `payment_link` as the receipt link. It runs as two set-based inserts before the two new tables have their triggers, then sets `amount_paid` on those bills with the `updated_at` trigger held still, so nothing a reader already reads on a bill changes and no screen sees the bills as just edited. A second run does nothing. A paid credit memo and a paid $0.00 bill get none.

`record_supply_house_payment` is `SECURITY INVOKER`, office staff only by its own check and by the tables' policies. The trigger functions are `SECURITY DEFINER`, revoked from every API role, so the books are kept whoever makes the edit. Both tables are office staff only (`is_office_staff()`), all four commands. Ends with the four fence calls. Additive and idempotent.

**Locks.** `ADD COLUMN` (a constant default, so no rewrite) takes a short lock on `supply_house_invoices` that holds reads too, for as long as the migration's transaction runs. `CREATE TRIGGER` on it and the foreign keys to it, to `supply_houses` and to `users` take a lock that holds writes to those tables. The backfill takes the same lock on the bills by name, so no bill is marked paid half-way through it. Twelve thousand paid bills took about three seconds on the bed, start to finish. `lock_timeout` is 3 s, so a push that cannot get the lock fails fast.

**Tried** on the bed `supabase/tests/supply_house_payments/` (`npm run test:pg:supply-house-payments`, Docker), run here under PGlite: it applies twice and every scenario passes, the row-level security ones as the API's role.

**Reviewed** by a fresh reader before it merged. The review found that a by-hand "not paid" which let go of a typed payment's line, followed by Mark paid, counted the money twice; that a removed payment could be put back over a bill paid since; that one statement carrying both "not paid" and a new amount left a press payment behind; that an amount lowered under its lines stayed over-covered; that the first backfill slowed with the square of the bills; and a lock order that could deadlock a Mark paid against a line landing on the same bill. Each is closed above and has its scenario, except the lock order, which a one-session bed cannot run.

**Left as it is.** A typed paid day on a bill that a typed payment covers goes back to the payment's day: the payment owns the day.

**Order.** Push right after the merge. An older client keeps working either way: before the push nothing has changed, and after it a by-hand Mark paid or Apply Payment makes its payments through the trigger.

**After the push:** `npm run gen-types:linked`, then `node scripts/build-dev-mcp-catalog.mjs`, in one types PR.

**Applied** to prod by 2026-11-18: `npm run check:migration-drift` read 1,366 local files against 1,366 remote versions, fully applied, this version among them (run in the docs pass after #8630).
