# 20261019080000_paid_claim.sql (2026-10-19, v2.5648)

They say they paid (punch list #129 PR 3a, `to-dos/estimator-money-word/README.md`): the customer's word on paying is one record, a day they will pay by or that they already paid. On `20261019060000_job_people.sql`; push after it, after the PR merges. The client's sheet shows the paid side before the push and its Save then says the function is missing, so client first is fine.

## What it does

1. **`job_payment_promises.kind`** (`promise` | `paid_claim`, default `promise`), **`method`** (`check_mailed`, `paid_online`, `bank_transfer`, `card_by_phone`, `not_sure`; null on a promise) and **`amount`** (numeric > 0; null for all of it, and on a promise), held by `job_payment_promises_kind_known`, `_method_known`, `_amount_positive` and `_kind_shape` (a claim names how; a promise names neither how nor an amount). Every row before this is a promise.
2. **`add_job_payment_claim(p_job_id, p_date, p_method, p_amount, p_said_by, p_channel, p_note)`**: a `paid_claim` row with `promised_date` the day they say they paid, `heard_by` the signed-in person, `source` office, filed under the job's payer by the bill-to rule; the same gate as a promise (`can_write_payment_promises()` or `is_job_people`). It never touches `job_promised_pay_dates` and never marks the job paid. Refuses in words a missing day, a method that is not one, an amount of nothing or less, a channel that is not one, a stranger.
3. **The reads**: `list_job_payment_promises()` and `list_payment_promise_records()` answer `kind = 'promise'` only, so the Lien desk, the legal packet, Owner Billing and the pay-speed record never read a claim as a promise. `list_job_payment_promises_for(job)` answers both kinds with `kind`, `method` and `amount`: the sheet's record, the job's trail and the money line read it.
4. `void_job_payment_promise` voids a claim as it voids a promise, by the promise's own job (unchanged).

## Why the shape

- One table, not two: a claim is the customer's word about paying, heard by someone, filed under the payer, voided the same way; a second table would copy every column and every reader.
- The company-wide reads stay promises-only rather than carrying `kind`: five office readers treat each row as a day to keep, and the kernel that scores kept/late/broken must never score a claim. The per-job read is the one the three surfaces that show claims use.
- The board's one-per-job date is untouched: the chip means *they named a day to pay by*; a claim is PR 4's row chip and allocation suggestion.
