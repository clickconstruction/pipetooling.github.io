# 20261118110000_job_finish_calls.sql (2026-11-18, v2.7807)

A person's own word on when a job finishes. Punch list #396, PR 3a. No screen reads or writes it yet: the pace card's **My finish date** is the PR that follows.

## Why

The superintendent's pace card reads a planned finish the office froze at award, and the day this pace lands on, worked out from hours. Neither is what the man on the job thinks. The owner asked for a press where he reports when he thinks the job will finish.

## The table

| Column | What it holds |
|---|---|
| `job_id` | The job, `jobs_ledger`. The row goes with the job. |
| `user_id` | Who said it. Defaults to the caller. |
| `finish_on` | The day he says the job finishes. |
| `plan_was` | The job's planned finish when he said it, so *the plan* can be told from a day of his own. Null when the job had none. |
| `why` | What is in the way, in his words. Optional. With words when given, and at most 300 letters (`job_finish_calls_why_has_words`). |
| `created_at` | When he said it. |

One row is one saying. A new saying is a new row. Nothing is changed and nothing is taken back, so the rows are the history of what he said and when. The newest row a person has on a job is his word today.

**It moves nothing.** No function reads it into the planned finish, the pace line or the score. Those stay the office's, the owner's call of 2026-11-18.

## Who

- A person **writes** his own row on a job he can read. The insert's check asks `jobs_ledger` under the caller's own row-level security, so the table adds no rule of its own about who is on a job.
- A person **reads** his own rows. Anyone who can read the job reads what was said on it, the same way.
- Nobody signed in changes or deletes a row: `authenticated` holds `SELECT` and `INSERT` alone. `anon` holds nothing.
- The four fences are called at the end, so a training account, a digital twin and an archived caller are held as on every table.
