# 20261118100000_trips_deleted.sql (2026-11-18, v2.7800)

Deleted trips are kept: the ground. No screen names any of it yet. The client that follows adds a **Deleted** fold to the Trips look, with **Put back**.

A deleted trip was gone for good after the one minute of Undo its toast gave. `trips` was never among the tables the deleted-records archive covers, and the Undo lived in the browser that pressed it. A trip is the record of work past the price: who went, which days, who asked. Since Put on a bill a customer's line can come from one.

## What it adds

| Piece | What it is |
|---|---|
| `zzz_archive_on_delete` on `trips` | The archive's own generic capture (`archive_deleted_record()`), grouped by the trip's id. |
| `trip_child_archive_on_delete()` on `trip_people` and `trip_jobs` | The same capture under the trip's id, only when the trip itself is gone. |
| `trips_settle_archive()` on `trips`, after insert | A trip made again under its own id marks its archive rows restored. |
| `trip_deleted_list(p_development_id)` | A site's deleted trips for the trips office. SECURITY DEFINER, read only. |

No table is created, so no fence call is owed.

## Why the people and the jobs have a capture of their own

`trip_save` saves a trip whole. It deletes the trip's people and jobs and inserts them again on every save. A deleted job takes its `trip_jobs` rows with it. Neither is a deleted trip, and the generic capture would have kept both.

The test is the trip's own row. A cascade from the trip runs after the trip's row is gone, so the capture finds no trip and keeps the row. A save, and a deleted job, find the trip still there and keep nothing.

## What the list answers

`trip_deleted_list(site)` returns an array, newest delete first, at most 50. Each entry is `{ deleted_at, deleted_by_name, jobs_gone, trip }`.

- `trip` is the trip whole, in the shape the app reads one: its fields, `trip_people` and `trip_jobs`.
- A trip is listed once, by its newest delete that nothing has put back, and only while no trip holds its id.
- A job the trip named that has since been deleted is left out of `trip_jobs` and counted in `jobs_gone`.
- A roster row or a login that is gone since is left out of that person, who stays as a name alone.

Those two rules exist so that putting the trip back does not fail on a row that is no longer there.

## Who reads it

1. Nobody signed in is refused. `anon` has no grant.
2. An archived caller and a digital twin are refused by the function itself. A definer stands behind the tables' fences, so it holds them itself, as `trip_phone` does.
3. The trips office reads it: dev, master_technician, assistant, controller and primary (`trips_office()`).
4. Everyone else is refused in words, a person who was on the trip among them.

The archive table itself stays dev-only. A training account may read the list, as it reads a trip, and can put nothing back, because `trip_save` is closed to it.

## Putting a trip back

There is no new write. The client sends the listed trip to `trip_save` under its own id, the door the Undo already uses. The trips tables' own policies decide who may. `trip_save` gives a deleted trip made again the number it had (`20261118040000_trip_numbers.sql`).

`trips_settle_archive` then marks the trip's archive rows restored, with who put it back. That covers the Undo, Put back, the dev seat and the dev's own restore, so no list offers a living trip.

## What stays as it was

- The archive's rules hold. A capture never blocks a delete, and a row is the row as it was (`to_jsonb(OLD)`), so the dev's `restore_deleted_records` works on a trip's bundle.
- The daily purge takes rows past 90 days. A trip deleted longer ago than that is gone.
- A trip's line on a job keeps its words and its price when the trip is deleted, and loses its mark (`ON DELETE SET NULL`). Putting the trip back does not tie the line again.
- In the dev's Recently deleted a trip's bundle reads by the first eight characters of its id, since that list's labels do not know a trip.

## Locks

Three `CREATE TRIGGER` statements, each a short lock on a small table. The file starts with `SET lock_timeout = '3s'`. It is idempotent: the triggers are dropped and made again, the functions replaced.

## Tests

`supabase/tests/trips/60_deleted.sql`, the fifth scenario of the trips bed (`npm run test:pg:trips`):

- the three tables carry the capture, and `trips` settles on insert;
- a save that rewrites the people and the jobs leaves nothing in the archive, and neither does a deleted job;
- a deleted trip is kept whole, in one moment, by the person who deleted it;
- the office reads it with its number, its words, its people, their days and its job, and another site reads none;
- a master reads it; a person who was on the trip, an estimator, a twin, someone who left and a caller with no login are refused; an assistant reads nothing from the archive table itself;
- the listed trip saves again under its own id with its number, leaves the list, and the archive says who put it back;
- a trip deleted twice is listed once, by its newest delete;
- a job and a login gone since are left out, and the trip still goes back;
- the dev's restore takes a trip's bundle;
- a trip that goes with its site is kept whole.
