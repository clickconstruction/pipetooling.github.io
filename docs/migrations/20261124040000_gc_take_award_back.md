# 20261124040000_gc_take_award_back.sql (2026-11-24, v2.8434)

Taking an award back: one function, `gc_take_award_back(p_package_id uuid)`, the write behind **Take the award back…** in Compare quotes. It is `gc_award`'s mirror (`20261009140000`, restated with its flag by the award guard, `20261010025000`).

- **Why a function**: `gc_trade_packages_award_guard` refuses a signed-in person's plain write of `awarded_invite_id`, `awarded_by` and `awarded_on`, a dev's too, so nothing could clear an award. The walks of punch list #416 awarded two test trades that then could not be undone. The owner's word of 2026-11-24: build the tool to unaward.
- **Door**: the same as `gc_award`. A dev while GC mode is built (*Only a dev takes an award back while GC mode is built.*), so call W moves both first checks together. A training account, a digital twin and an archived caller are refused next in their own words: the read-only fence hides the trade from the function's row lock, so a training dev would otherwise read *No trade with that id.* `SECURITY INVOKER`. `REVOKE … FROM PUBLIC, anon`, `GRANT EXECUTE … TO authenticated`.
- **What it takes back**: the three award columns, cleared under `gc.award_write` (the flag the guard reads, on for the one UPDATE and off after it), and the drafted statement of work (`gc_sows`, its `gc_sow_lines` by cascade, the paper picked for it a column of the row). One transaction.
- **What it refuses**, the first found naming the refusal as *‹trade› stays awarded to ‹company›, since …*, each with where it is:
  - the statement of work was **sent** (*Nothing takes a sent statement of work back yet.*), **signed** (*A signed statement of work changes through Change orders on the job's card.*) or is otherwise no longer a draft;
  - something stands on the statement of work: a pay application (`gc_draws`), a back charge (`gc_back_charges`), a change the trade asked for (`gc_trade_change_requests`), a change order sent to the trade or signed into a line (`gc_change_order_trade_sends`, a `gc_sow_lines` row with `change_order_id`), the trade's report of work in place (`gc_sow_line_reports`), a bill day's answer (`gc_bill_day_nothing`);
  - something stands on the award, which names the company on the trade: a paper sent for the trade (`gc_paper_sends` with this `package_id` and company), a submittal on the trade (`gc_submittals`), an RFI the company asked on it (`gc_rfis`), a day the company gave us for it (`gc_trade_promises`). Left behind, these would read as the next company's once the trade is awarded again.
- **What reads right afterwards with no write**: the board, a sheet's send card, the scorecard and the trade's portal all read the award live from the three columns. The trade goes back to what it carried (`carried_invite_id`, `carry_budget`), which the award never touched. The ask, its quote and its story stay as they were.
- **The trace**: an award is recorded only in the columns this clears, and the company's Activity shows it as a line read from them. So the function writes a line of the company's own on its call log (`gc_company_contacts` with no ask, `how` *note*), which that Activity and the schedule's call list show: *Award taken back on Sitework, ‹job›. Awarded Oct 8 by ‹name› for $66,500. The drafted statement of work went with it.*, with `by_user_id`, `by_name` and `contacted_on` as who and when. A note on the ask itself would be kept and shown nowhere, since the board's kernels leave an ask's notes out of its story.

No table is created, so no fence call is owed. `CREATE OR REPLACE`, idempotent. The guard and `gc_award` are not restated.

**The bed**: `supabase/tests/gc_take_award_back/20_scenario.sql` (`scripts/pgtest-gc-take-award-back.sh`, in `scripts/sql-beds.txt`): every role's gate, each refusal above with its words, records that are not the award's passing, the take-back itself, the award guard still refusing a plain write afterwards, and the trade awarded again.
