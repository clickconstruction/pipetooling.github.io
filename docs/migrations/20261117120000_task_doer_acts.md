# 20261117120000_task_doer_acts.sql (2026-11-16, v2.7542)

What the person doing a task may do with it, without waiting on someone (punch list #369, step C1). The ground alone. No screen calls any of it yet, so the merge holds no deploy. The tools on the card are the next PRs.

**Why.** A person given a task could mark it done, tick its lines, and add a note, a photo and a missing place. They could not move it to another day, say it no longer applies, hand it on, or put a map on it. Each of those is a write to `checklist_items` or to the assignee tables, which only the office or the task's maker may write. So an old task sat on a list until someone in the office dealt with it.

## What it does

**1. Six more kinds of line in a task's thread.** `checklist_instance_events.event_type` gains `moved`, `stuck`, `unstuck`, `dropped`, `handed` and `map`, beside the six of `20261106230000`. The check is dropped by what it holds and added back under the same name.

**2. A stuck line is a person's own words.** The insert policy *Insert own comments on visible instances* lets `stuck` and `unstuck` through beside `comment`, `progress` and `criterion`: written as oneself, on a day row one can read. The other four new kinds are written only by the functions below.

**3. One gate, `task_doer_may(p_instance_id uuid, p_act text)`.** Signed in, and the office or a person the task is given to (`can_work_checklist_instance`). Never a training account (`is_read_only`), a digital twin or an archived account. It raises in words with the act in them, such as *A training account cannot move this task.* `EXECUTE` is revoked from `PUBLIC`, `anon` and `authenticated`: it is called only from inside the four functions.

**4. Four functions**, each `SECURITY DEFINER`, `search_path = public, pg_temp`, returning `jsonb`, in the shape of `add_task_place_from_the_spot` (`20261113010000`):

| Function | The one write | Refuses |
|---|---|---|
| `task_move_day(p_instance_id, p_day date)` | `checklist_items.due_date`, and a `moved` line whose body is the day | no day, a day gone by or before the task's start, a day past a year, the day it is already due, a task that repeats, a task already done |
| `task_no_longer_applies(p_instance_id, p_reason text)` | the day row's `completed_at` and `completed_by_user_id`, then a `dropped` line whose body is the reason | a reason under 3 characters (tidied, cut at 200), a task that repeats, a roadmap task, a vehicle task, a task already done |
| `task_hand_on(p_instance_id, p_to uuid, p_reason text DEFAULT NULL, p_from uuid DEFAULT NULL)` | one person for another on `checklist_instance_assignees`, and on `checklist_item_assignees` for a one-time task; a `handed` line whose body is who to, then why | handing to oneself, a taker who is archived, a twin, a sample, the walk tester or has no login, `p_from` named by anyone but the office, a `p_from` who does not have the task, a roadmap task, a task already done |
| `task_add_map(p_instance_id, p_map_id uuid)` | `painted_map_id` on the task, or on the roadmap task behind the row, where it is null; a `map` line whose body is the map's name | a map the caller did not paint (the office may name any), a map with no line and no place, a task that already has a map |

## Four things it will not do, on purpose

- **The day row does not move.** `task_move_day` writes the due day and nothing else, so the task keeps its place in the list and its age. The change lands in `checklist_item_due_changes` through `record_checklist_due_change`, with `auth.uid()` as who.
- **A close is a completion, so two kinds of task are refused.** Completing a roadmap task's day row marks its roadmap task done (`sync_roadmap_task_from_instance`), and a vehicle task's marks the service done (`sync_vehicle_maintenance_task_completion`). Neither would be true of a task nobody did. The person says why in a note, and the roadmap's lead or the office takes it off.
- **A hand-on keeps the task.** The office's FWD made a new one-time task for the other person and deleted the first day row, with its thread. This swaps the person on the same rows. A one-time task moves whole. A repeating task gives away that day's row alone. A roadmap task's people are the roadmap's, so it is refused.
- **A map is added, never swapped.** `task_painted_map_guard` (`20261115063000`) asks whether the writer can read the map. Inside a definer function it would read as the owner, so `task_add_map` asks its own question: the caller painted the map, and the map has a line or a place on it.

## Order of two lines

`task_no_longer_applies` sets `completed_at`, and the transitions trigger writes the `completed` line at the transaction's time. The `dropped` line is stamped with `clock_timestamp()`, so it is the later of the two and the thread reads *completed*, then why. A client tells a closed task from a done one by a `dropped` line at or after the last `completed`.
