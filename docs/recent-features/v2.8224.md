# v2.8224 — Punch list: a tick can be read by the others who open the board (2026-11-21)

One migration, [`20261122000500_punch_list_claims`](../migrations/20261122000500_punch_list_claims.md). No screen changes.

## Why

The tick went live as v2.8199: a checkbox beside each card that means *I am working on this*, seen by the person who ticked it and nobody else. The same day Taunya asked for more, in her words: *"I think it might be a good idea for us to add to the punch list when a user has claimed a card that they are working on, And then they should release that claim after they've not updated the card in 12 hours. Does that make sense? If so, help me add it."*

A second person on another computer is about to work from the list, and nothing on the page says which cards are taken. So a tick becomes a claim. This is the ground for it. The screen is the next PR but one, after the types.

## What changed

- **Everyone who can open the board reads every tick.** That is a dev or a master. Writing is unchanged: only the person who ticked a card can change or take off their tick.
- **Each tick carries its claimer's name**, stamped by the database from the person's own account on every write.
- **The time on a tick is the last time the person said so**: the tick itself, or a **Still on it** press when the screen has one.

## What this changes for a tick already made

Nothing. The table held no row at the push. From this version a tick is no longer private: v2.8196 and v2.8199 said *nobody else sees it*, and that sentence is retired by this one.

## What is not here

- The 12 quiet hours. The page works them out when it reads, and no job expires a row.
- Any screen. The live page keeps reading a person's own ticks until the claims screen deploys.

## Verified

A stand-in rig on a local Postgres 15: the first migration, this file twice, then 16 checks with made-up accounts. The migration doc lists them. The push and its proof follow the merge.
