## What a merge moves

Prices, the price history, assembly items, takeoff lines, stage splits, the job and bid tallies, purchase order lines, submittal parts, on-site movements, supply paper lines and house numbers: every table that points at a part.

- **A price from the same house on both copies**: the newer dated one stays, on the surviving part's own price row, and the takeoff lines that named the other row are pointed at it.
- **An assembly that held both copies**: it holds the survivor once, the quantities summed.
- **The copy's name** goes into the survivor's notes as *Also known as: …*, which the parts search reads. A maker, a link, a part type or a *Sold in* rule the survivor lacks is taken from the copy.

## Limits

- A takeoff line in a bid version somebody else locked refuses the merge with the lock's own words. Nothing is merged, and the parts stay as they were.
- Parts of two service types, and a robot's part, are not merged.
- A merge cannot be undone.
