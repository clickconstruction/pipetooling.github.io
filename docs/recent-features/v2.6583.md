## Change

- **`plan_set_crew_on_job(job_id, part_id)`**: the one predicate for the crew's writes — the signed-in user is on the job's crew (`jobs_ledger_team_members`) and the job builds from the part. `SECURITY DEFINER` with `row_security = off`, as `helper_on_job` (v2.5526) is, because the inline `EXISTS` on the team table ran under the caller's RLS and that table's SELECT policy leaves helpers out, so a helper on the crew never passed the insert rule.
- **`plan_sets_insert`**: the same rule as v2.6312 (an as-built or a paper, on a job they are on whose part is the set's), through the predicate.
- **`plan_sets_update`**: the author's `WITH CHECK` now asks the predicate on the row they leave behind, so `job_id` and `part_id` can move only between jobs they are on that build from the part, never to a job they are not on, and never to null. `USING` and the office branch are unchanged, as is `plan_sets_delete`.
- **SQL bed `plan_sets_author`** (`supabase/tests/plan_sets_author/20_scenario.sql`, `scripts/pgtest-plan-sets-author.sh`, in `scripts/sql-beds.txt`): two standalone jobs, each with its own part. A subcontractor on job A's crew keeps an as-built on it and changes its memo; moving it to job B (not on its crew), to B with B's part, or to no job answers `42501` and the row stays; a new as-built on B, on A under B's part, or a drawing is refused; the office moves the row and back; a helper on A's crew reads no crew row of his own, yet the predicate answers true on his job and false off it, and he cannot touch the subcontractor's row; put on B's crew, the subcontractor still cannot put the row on B under A's part, moves it onto B with B's part, and removes his own. Both policies are read from `pg_policies` for the predicate's name, and the predicate's definer and `row_security` from `pg_proc`.
