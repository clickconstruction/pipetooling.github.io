# v2.7922 — Permits: another tenant's plumbing permit no longer closes a job (2026-11-19)

On Prospects → Permits a job read as having a plumber whenever any plumbing permit sat at its street address. The address carries no suite, so in a shopping centre or a tower that was often another tenant's job. The same match named the Builders list's *plumber they use*, which is how permit filers came to be shown as plumbers.

## How it was found

A trial that looked up 20 San Antonio builders (2026-11-19) noticed the Builders list naming an assistant project manager as one builder's plumber. Her jobs were in suites 300 and 400 of a tower. His were in suites 180 and 220, a year later.

Reading the newest San Antonio permits then showed the larger fault. An application filed Nov 11 at a strip centre read as taken, by the contractor of a nail salon in another unit. A permit that reads as taken never reaches the skim.

## What each city's feed does

Read on prod on 2026-11-19, read only, over every row of `permits`:

| City | Plumbing permits | On a building permit's number |
|---|---|---|
| San Antonio | 45,744 | 5,144, of which 4,977 under the applicant's own name |
| Austin | 2,714 | 0 |
| New Braunfels | 180 | 0 |
| San Marcos | 73 | 0 |
| Seguin | 174 | 0 |

San Antonio issues a project's plumbing permit on the project's own number and under the applicant's name. A plumbing permit there with a number of its own is a separate job. In the other four cities the plumber pulls a permit of his own, and the address is the only thing that ties it to the job.

The first plan was one strict rule for every city: count a plumbing permit only on the number or from the same applicant. Counting it showed it would have reopened 273 of Austin's 276 taken jobs. So the rule turns on how the city issues its permits.

## The rule now

- A plumbing permit on the building permit's own number counts, as before.
- In San Antonio a plumbing permit at the address counts only when the same applicant filed it. That is what ties an application to the permit issued for it on another number.
- In Austin, New Braunfels, San Marcos and Seguin a plumbing permit at the address counts, as before.
- Everywhere, the plumbing permit must be no older than 30 days before the job was applied for. The skim already held to that. The builder counts did not.
- A name is shown as the plumber only when it is neither the applicant nor the applicant's company.

The rule lives in one database function, `permit_address_plumbing_counts`, which the three reads call ([migration note](../migrations/20261119063000_permit_plumbing_ties.md), with the before and after counts).

## What the office sees

- **San Antonio's skim:** about 68 permits filed since August come back. 52 of them are applications.
- **San Antonio's Builders list:** no builder shows another applicant as its plumber. A builder whose jobs have plumbing permits under its own name reads **not named on the permits** (`plumberUsedWords`), where it would have read *nobody yet*.
- **Needs you:** San Antonio's count of permits waiting on a skim rises by the ones that came back.
- **The other four cities:** nothing changes.

## Not in this change

- A plumbing permit at the address that no longer counts is not shown on the row. It could be shown as a note, *a plumbing permit at this address*, for the skimmer to judge.
- One applicant's two jobs at one address still tie to each other. An application carries no unit.
- The bid board's **This site** block reads plumbing permits at a bid's address in its own code. It was not read here.
