# v2.7206 — The dev seat sets a job's last day of work for the lien clocks: plan_last_work_day and apply_last_work_day (2026-11-13)

The owner's ask of 2026-11-13. Three jobs at one property read *lien gone* on the Lien desk because nobody had told the app when the work ended; the master plumber set the three days in the Lien window. The owner then asked whether the dev seat could set such a day, with its reason, if asked. It could not. No migration. `dev-mcp` is server 0.84.0 with 147 tools (0.83.0 is v2.7186's).

## Why

The seat had two Lien desk writes, the property record's lien facts (`plan_property_record`, v2.6950) and the end of our contract (`plan_contract_ended`, v2.6795). `plan_job_update` covers the Edit Job form's columns, and the last day of work is not one of them. Every lien date counts from the month of that day, so it is the one fact that moves a job on or off the desk's lists.

## What was added

- **`plan_last_work_day` / `apply_last_work_day`** take `day = { job_id, last_work_on, reason, anyway? }` or `{ job_id, clear: true }`.
  - **The write** is the Lien window's own save (`LienLastWorkDayLine.tsx`): one PATCH on `jobs_ledger` under RLS as the dev, with `lienLastWorkDayPatch`'s four columns (the day, the reason, the moment, the person). Clear writes `LIEN_LAST_WORK_CLEAR`. The apply reads the columns back to confirm the row landed.
  - **The dry run** reads the job, its property's kind, its approved clock months (paged, the SQL readers' rule: approved, not rejected, not revoked) and the months its live § 53.056 notices name. It runs them through `lienLastWorkPreview`, the kernel behind the window's confirm.
  - **The plan's reply** carries the day before and after with where each comes from, and the window's own table: the months the notice names, the § 53.056 day of the last month, the § 53.052 affidavit day and the § 53.158 suit day, each before and after. A new month whose notice window already closed is named as one that would read as missed, as in the window.
  - **The window's rules hold.** A day after today, a day earlier than the clock hours and the day the job already reads are refused in the window's words. A reason is required, as the window's *Set the day* stays off without one.
  - **A day far from the clock hours** is the window's amber second look, where its button reads *Set the day anyway*. The plan refuses it and says to take it to the person who knows the day; it stands once the ask carries `anyway: true`. `anyway: true` on a day that is not far is refused, so it cannot become a habit.
  - **What it does not do** is said in every plan: clock hours and pay are not changed, and no notice or affidavit is drafted, approved, sent or recorded.
- **The kernels come from the mirror.** `scripts/edge-kernels.mjs` gained two entries, `lib/jobs/lienLastWorkDay.ts` and `lib/jobs/lienLastWorkDayPreview.ts`. The copy took five files: those two, `lienDeadlines.ts`, `lienSendTracking.ts` and `forecastWorkMonths.ts`. The index exports nothing of them; the seat imports each by its path. A PR that changes one of the five regenerates the copy (`npm run gen:edge-kernels`), as `edgeKernels.test.ts` will say.
- **Kernel** `supabase/functions/_shared/devMcpLastWorkDay.ts`: the shape, the read, the dry run, the patch and the apply's words. It names no RPC.

## One limit, stated in the verb's own description

`lien_last_work_set_by` is the dev whose key the seat runs on, and the reason is typed by the agent. For a date an affidavit swears, the person who knows the day pressing it in the window leaves the better record. The verb is for a day and a reason a person gave in their own words.
