# v2.3192 — Stage-weighted field reports: which stage, how far, and the app does the weighting (2026-09-09)

Migration [`20260909141454_stage_progress.sql`](../migrations/20260909141454_stage_progress.md). Part C of the owner-approved mock-up *Burn, Budget and Stages*; the idea is the owner's (2026-09-09): "if they were 20% complete of a stage worth twenty percent of a job, they've really moved the job forward four percent." Today a tech guesses a whole-job number; with stages the guess shrinks to the piece they are standing in.

## The rule

- **Weights** = a stage line's share of the value of all stage lines (Order + Any rows with a price). "—" rows (permit, dumpster) are outside the percent. Computed server-side so no price reaches the field.
- **Job % = Σ weight × stage %**, rounded to a whole point. A paid draw counts its stage as 100 whatever was reported.
- **Stage mode needs an Order row** (`stageModeAvailable`). The v2.3083 backfill made every legacy line item kind `any`; keying on "any priced row" would have turned a three-line service job into a three-stage report. Once one Order row exists, Any rows join the picker beside it. A single-line job reports exactly as before.
- **The job % still lands in "How complete is the job?"** — `list_latest_report_completion_pct`, the thread stats, `reportSaysJobComplete` / the Ready-to-bill prompt, `propagateReportPctToJob`, Job Summary and Burn all read that field and change nothing. The report also carries a human-readable **Stage progress** field ("Rough-in: 100% (35% of the job) → job 60%") that the report view prints like any other field.

## What shipped

- **Data + doors** — `jobs_ledger_fixtures.progress_pct / progress_at / progress_by / progress_report_id`; `can_report_stage_progress(job)` (office roles, or a field user who is a team member / has a clock session / passes `subcontractor_can_read_jobs_ledger_row`); `list_job_stage_progress(job)` (rows with `weight_pct`, progress, `draw_paid` — no amounts); `record_stage_progress(job, fixture, pct, report)`. Field roles cannot read the fixtures table under RLS, which is why both are SECURITY DEFINER.
- **Kernel** [`stageProgressReport.ts`](../../src/lib/reports/stageProgressReport.ts) (9 tests): `stageProgressRowsFromRpc`, `stageEffectivePct`, `jobPercentFromStages(stages, override?)`, `defaultStageToReport` (first Order not done → first Any not done → last), `stagePickerNumber` (1..N / ◆), `stageReportSummary` ("Rough-in 60% × 35% of the job = 21 pts · job 46% → 46%"), `stageProgressFieldValue`, `stageModeAvailable`.
- **[`ReportStageProgressField`](../../src/components/ReportStageProgressField.tsx)** (render test): the stage list (number or ◆, name, "35% of the job · any time / draw paid", the current %; a paid or 100% stage reads ✓ 100%), the picked stage's slider with 25 / 50 / 75 / **Done ✓** quick picks, and the green derived box: **"46% of the job · was 40% · Rough-in 60% × 35% = 21 pts"** with **Set the whole-job % instead**.
- **[`NewReportModal`](../../src/components/NewReportModal.tsx)**: on a job-ledger job it calls `list_job_stage_progress`; when stage mode is available the template's `percent_0_100` field renders as the stage picker instead of `ReportTemplatePercentField`; the derived % is mirrored into `fieldValues[label]` (so the copy text and the seed hint agree) and is authoritative in the submit payload alongside the **Stage progress** field; after the insert, `record_stage_progress` stamps the stage (best-effort). "Report by stage instead" restores the picker after switching to the whole-job slider. A missing RPC (client ahead of the push) or any error = the plain slider, unchanged.
- **Stage plan** — [`_shared/stagePlan.ts`](../../supabase/functions/_shared/stagePlan.ts): `StagePlanFixture.progress_pct?`; a row's `pct` is the sub sheet's progress when an order exists, else the crew's; an Any row is done at either's 100%. [`stagePlanForm.ts`](../../src/lib/jobs/stagePlanForm.ts) reads the column loosely until types regenerate. The Bill tab's stage line therefore prints *Stage 2 · 60%* from the report.
