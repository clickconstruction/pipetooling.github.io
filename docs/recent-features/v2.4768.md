## What changed

- **`court_areas`** ([migration page](../migrations/20261007110000_court_areas.md)): one row per precinct polygon — county, the county's own precinct label, a GeoJSON Polygon or MultiPolygon, `drawn` or `imported`, a source note, who drew it, active. Every signed-in user reads it; the office cohort writes it.
- **`customer_addresses`** gains `jp_precinct`, `jp_precinct_source` (`map` · `hand`), `jp_precinct_note`, `jp_precinct_at`. A hand-typed precinct wins over the map.
- **[`courtAreas.ts`](../../src/lib/legal/courtAreas.ts)**: `classifyCourtPoint(point, areas)` — the active area holding the point names the county and precinct (turf's `booleanPointInPolygon`, already in the app for the Map page's filter), and every other area of that county whose edge lies within 100 m (`COURT_LINE_METRES`, `pointToLineDistance`) is named beside it, so a point on a line reads *2 or 3 — on the line* for a person to settle rather than one wrong answer. `courtAreaFromRow` refuses a row whose polygon is not one; `courtClassificationWords` and `courtCoverageWords` are the Map page's and the record's words.

## Is this the best we can do?

- **Counties never come from this map.** The geocoder and the parcel roll already give the county; the layer is precincts only, so there is half as much to draw and a county line is never traced by hand.
- **A point on a line names both sides.** A geocoded point can sit a few hundred feet off; the 100 m rule turns that into a question for a person instead of a wrong precinct on the firm's page.
- **Every area carries its source and date**, because precinct lines move at redistricting and the firm's page says where its answer came from.
