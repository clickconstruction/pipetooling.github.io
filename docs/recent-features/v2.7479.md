# v2.7479 — Development page: History draws the mail tied to the site's jobs and bids (2026-11-16)

On 2026-11-16 a Gmail conversation was tagged onto a bid of a development and did not appear on that development's History. The page's read (`fetchDevelopmentPage.ts`) took bid notes, plan filings, stage moves, bills, payments, thread notes, the customer's contact log and the kept entries, and never `messages`. The work-around was a History line typed by hand, a second copy of the same words. No migration, no edge function.

## What draws now

Every email tied to one of the site's bids or jobs in Messages is a line on History: the rows of `messages` on channel `email` whose `bid_id` or `jobs_ledger_id` is one of the page's. It does not matter how the mail was tied: the My email press, the dev seat's `apply_mail_tag`, a company box's reader or a tie made in the thread.

The line (`mailLines` in `src/lib/developments/history.ts`, a new `ChangeKind`, `mail`):

- **When**: the mail's own time (`messages.at`), not the time it was tagged. The time is drawn at the line's end, as on a kept entry.
- **Which way**: the verb is *Email in* or *Email out*.
- **About**: the bid or the job it is tied to, as a door like every other line's.
- **Subject**, then the **words**. The body is kept whole on the line, so the feed folds it behind *more ›* past `HISTORY_FOLD_AT` exactly as it folds a long entry, and the window shows the subject over every word.
- **Who**: the row's own words (`who_text`, which the readers and the tag write as *sender to recipient*), else our person and theirs by name.
- **Open in Messages**: the mail in its thread, open at the mail. The door is Job activity's (`threadHrefByKind`): the Messages page, or GC projects' Messages for a trade partner or an architect.

The line wears the kept email's mark (the envelope, amber) from the one table in `historyLegend.ts`, whose *An email* row now says both. It sits under the **What happened** chip, narrows to its piece like any line, and the search reads its subject. Mail never folds into a same-day run: each mail is its own act.

## One mail, one line

`email_write_line` keeps one row per Gmail id, and a row carries at most one of `bid_id`, `project_id` and `jobs_ledger_id`, so the two reads never bring the same mail twice. `mailLines` still drops a row it has seen, by its id or by Gmail's.

A line someone kept by hand about the same mail (`development_history_entries`, source email) is not merged and not hidden. It carries no Gmail id, so any match would be a guess on words and time, and a kept line never changes. It stays as written and the tied mail draws beside it, once. From here on a tagged mail needs no hand-kept line.

## Who reads it

The read is the reader's own (`readTiedMail`, no definer function), so the policies on `messages` decide what comes, as on the Messages page: `messages_team` gives the GC office team every line, and `messages_candidate_door` keeps a candidate's thread to the people who may read candidates. The page asks only for the roles that open Messages (`canOpenMessages`, the same team), and the same rule gives the line its door. So an estimator's History shows the mail Messages shows them and nothing more. A primary, who reads a narrow slice of `messages` in Job updates and cannot open Messages, gets no mail lines here.

The read fails soft. A failure hands back no mail and its reason, History draws every other line, and one quiet line above the list says the mail did not come and why. The people the mail is with (`contacts`, for a name and the door's kind) are a second read that fails softer: the line reads with the words its row kept.

## Weight

The read brings each mail's whole body, 100 ids a request and at most 300 mails a slice, newest first. On 2026-11-16 the whole company held a handful of emails tied to bids and a few dozen tied to jobs, so this is light today. If a site's tied mail grows heavy, the fix is the one punch list #280 made for kept entries: a heads view over `messages` (the first 400 characters and the length) and a read of the rest on *more ›*. That is a migration and was left out here.
