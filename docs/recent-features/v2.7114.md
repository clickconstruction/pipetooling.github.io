# v2.7114 — Materials: What we buy, every supply house bill's lines added up (2026-11-12)

Punch list #298 PR 4a (`to-dos/supply-bill-lines/README.md`, frame G of its mock-up). This is the page the owner asked for on 2026-11-09: how many of a line item the company buys, how often and at what price. It is built on the house's own code, as the measure said it could be, so it is full before anyone has matched a code.

## What a person sees

Materials → **What we buy**, a new tab in the Purchase group, for the office roles.

- **Four figures**: Spent on parts with the bills it is on, Charges (freight, fuel, fees), Distinct parts with how many have no part yet, and Bills not counted with a link to their reasons on Supply houses.
- **A row per thing bought**: the part's name from the book, or the line as printed with *no part yet · ‹house› ‹code›* under it. Then how many bills carry it, the quantity in the unit the bills print, the spend, a bar for each month, the houses (the most spent first) and the last price paid for one.
- **Filters**: a house, a job, and a span of months that opens on the last six. **Sort**: Spend, Quantity or How often.
- **The foot** states what is counted and the limits the card names: a bill split by percent, a part counted by its house's code until it is matched, big-box card purchases missing.

## How it is built

- **`src/lib/materials/lines/whatWeBuy.ts`** (pure, `whatWeBuy.test.ts`, 16 tests):
  - `lineIdentity` says what a line is. Its own `part_id` first. Else the house's number for a part in `supply_part_codes` (the crosswalk of punch list #308), so one part bought at two houses under their two codes is one row. Else the house and its folded code. Else the house and its folded words, for the lines that print no code. A charge line, and a code the crosswalk marks *not a part*, is a charge.
  - `whatWeBuy` sums the rows, the months and the figures. Held to a job, a bill gives that job its percent of the quantity and the spend, and a bill with no share on the job is left out.
  - `priceEach` is a line's price for one, through its pricing unit, or its extension over its quantity when no price was read.
- **`purchaseLinesIo.ts`**: every count reads the view `supply_purchase_lines` and never the tables, so a quote or a paper whose sums are off cannot be counted. Each read is paged. A ZZ test house's lines are left out. A read that fails throws, and the page draws it on `OfflineRetryPanel`.
- **`WhatWeBuyTab.tsx`**: the page. The month bars are one series in the app's blue, with the month and its dollars on hover and the figures read out to a screen reader.
- **The tab**: `what-we-buy` in `materialsTabs.ts` (office only), in the Purchase group (`materialsGroups.ts`), mounted by `Materials.tsx`, with a door in the search (`doors.ts`).

## Choices made on the card's defaults

- **Built on the house's code first**, the default of the measure's last call. The matching list (2a) comes after.
- **Charges are spend under Charges, never a part** (call 6).
- **An estimator does not see it yet.** Call 7's default names the estimator beside the office. The papers' read policy is the office's, so an estimator would open an empty page. Widening it opens every bill's lines and prices to estimators, and it reaches the bills' jobs too. That is a change to who reads accounts payable, so it is listed for the owner and not made here.
- **No service type filter yet.** A service type belongs to a part in the book, and no bill line is matched to one today. It comes with the matching list.

## Is this the best we can do?

For the question asked, it answers it today on real bills. It is weaker than it will be in three ways, each with its next step.

- **The same part at two houses is two rows** until someone teaches both codes. 70 percent of codes are bought on one bill, so the tail is long. The matching list, sorted by dollars, is the fix, and the top two hundred codes are 61 percent of the spend.
- **Freight printed under the subtotal is not in Charges.** A paper keeps that amount in its own freight field, and the view returns lines. It is small (charges are about 1 percent of spend) and would need the view to carry it.
- **Every line is read into the browser** and summed there: about 4,400 lines today, five paged reads. It is quick now. At ten times the lines the sums belong in a database function.
