# v2.8759 — Bids: a row's split adds up to the row, to the cent (finding 346, punch list #46) (2026-11-27)

Finding 346, from the walk of v2.8754 on a ZZ test bid on 2026-11-27. Client only, no migration, no function. It owes nothing on prod, and it changes no saved number.

## What was seen

On the Pricing Workbench a row's chevron opens its split: Material, Sales tax and Labor. On one row of the walked bid the three Revenues read $11,631.91, $959.63 and $4,208.45, which add to $16,799.99 under a row of $16,800.00. Their unit prices added to $5,600.01 under a row of $5,600.00. On another row the three Profits added to one cent under the row's.

## Why

`rowPriceSplit` works the split out unrounded, with Labor as what is left of the price, so the three always add to the row. The screen then rounded each line to the cent **by itself**. Three rounded numbers need not add to their rounded sum. At $2,584.87 a unit plus 50%, material is $3,877.305, a third of a cent off.

It is older than the move of v2.8754. A made-up row with the same arithmetic was run on the tab from before that move and on the tab after it, and the three lines read the same on both.

## The mend, and which line takes the cent

**Labor takes it.** Labor is already the line that is *what is left*: it is never typed, and its note on the screen says so. Material is the takeoff plus a markup, or a price a person typed. Sales tax is a rate on that material. Neither may move by a cent for the sake of a sum.

So the labor line now shows what is left **on the screen too**: the row's own figure as the screen rounds it, less the other lines as the screen rounds them. That holds for its unit price, its revenue and its profit. The three then add to the row to the cent.

- `laborLeftToTheCent` in `src/lib/bids/rowPriceSplit.ts`: the row's figure and the other lines' in, labor's out, in whole cents, rounded by `formatCurrency` and by nothing else, so it can never differ from what the screen shows.
- `src/components/bids/PricingSplitRows.tsx` reads the labor line's three figures from it.

## What it does not change

- **No saved number.** Nothing is written. The split itself is as it was.
- **Material and Sales tax** read as they did on every row.
- **Labor's margin** is worked out as it was, from the unrounded figures.
- **The three cards** under the grid and the print are untouched.
- **A row with no labor cost** still shows no labor profit, and an Equipment row is still one line.
- A row whose lines already added up reads as it did. On the others the labor line moves by one cent, and by two at the very most.

## Proved, and what is not

- `splitLinesToTheCent.test.ts` (5), **written first and failing on main**: the walked row's two cases, a row that already adds up, a sweep of 4,000 made-up rows that holds rows rounding each way and adds to the row on every one, labor that goes under, and a clean number of cents.
- `PricingSplitRows.render.test.tsx` (5), also failing on main first: the walked row's unit prices and revenues, the walked row whose profits read a cent under, a row that already adds up, and labor that goes under.

- Every bids test and every test that reads the source tree pass. Lint, a focused `tsc`, every CI line check.
