# v2.8350 — Working hours: Utilization reads a person's set hours (2026-11-23)

The second of five PRs for punch list #429, after the database door (v2.8345). The kernel, and the first reader. Client only. No migration and no function.

## What ships

**The kernel.** `src/lib/people/workingHours.ts`, pure:

- **The shape.** `workingDayProblem` and `workingWeekProblem` say in plain words why a day or a week cannot be kept, by the rule `working_week_ok` holds in the database, with the 80-hour cap the door adds. `parseWorkingWeek` reads a stored week whole or not at all.
- **The hours.** A day and a week add up in quarter hours, a break left out. `paidWeekOf` is the seven numbers pay keeps for a salaried person. `workingHoursMovePay` says whether a week changes them: a day's length moves pay, a change of times does not. `workingWeekFromPaidWeek` starts a person on a set week from a start time alone, so pay cannot move.
- **The week in force.** `setRowInForce` and `workingHoursInForce`: the newest set row on or before a day. Unlike an answer, the first set row does not reach back before its Sunday, and a row with no week means the hours were taken off. `workingHoursComing` is the row that starts after today. `workingHoursHistory` is every save, the newest first.
- **The lines.** `workingHoursLines` puts days that share their hours on one line, wherever they fall in the week, so a half day on Wednesday reads as *Mon, Tue, Thu, Fri* and *Wed*. The app's pay words join only days that touch. `workingDaysLabel` reads a run of three days or more as a range.
- **The moves.** `withWorkingDays`, `halfDay` and `usualWorkingDay` are what the row's field will do.
- **The words.** The clock as *6:00 AM*, a line's ranges, a week on one line, the sentences a person's own card reads, and what a change comes to before it is kept. Sentences mark their values with `val`.

**Utilization.** `utilization.ts` takes a fourth source, `set`. A week with working hours in force on its Sunday is held to them, for a salaried person too, and counts the hours a punch fell outside them. An earlier week reads as it did. `answerInForce` skips set rows, so working hours are never read as an answer and hours taken off fall back to the answer. An answer's type carries `kind`.

**The read.** `availabilityIo.ts` reads `kind` with the other columns. A database that does not have the column yet answers 42703, and the read is made again without it, every row an answer.

**The row.** People, Utilization draws a person with working hours as *30 h · working hours · 6:00 AM to 12:00 PM Mon to Fri*, with no **Change** press.

## What does not change

Nobody has a set row yet, so every row on the board reads as before, and the existing tests pass unchanged. Pay, cost and the clock's sessions do not read working hours here. The press that sets them is the next PR.

## Tests

- `workingHours.test.ts`, 24 cases: the shapes the database takes and refuses, each said in plain words, the hours and the seven numbers, the week in force and hours taken off, the lines of a half day on Wednesday, the moves and every sentence.
- `utilization.test.ts`, five new cases: working hours over a person's own answer from their Sunday and not before, the hours outside them, a salaried person, hours taken off, and a week that adds up in quarter hours.

## Order

The read names the new column, so this ships after migration `20261123075925` is pushed. A database without it is still read, by the fallback.
