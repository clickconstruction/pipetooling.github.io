# v2.6944 — The dev seat records sub labor on a job and what was paid against it: plan_sub_labor and plan_sub_labor_payment (2026-11-11)

Asked by the owner on 2026-11-11 ("Do we have the dev verbs to add sub labor?", then "Can you help me add those verbs?"). The seat could read a job's sub labor: `get_job` counts the sheets in the profit band and `get_hours_statement` reads a sub's sheets. It could not write a sheet or a payment on one. The case that prompted it: a subcontractor worked a site on an existing job, was paid from the company bank in several payments, and the days were known only from the sub's own day log. The office wanted the labor on the job without the same dollars also counting as an allocated bank row.

## How the screens do it

Read before anything was designed (punch list #293's rule: the seat goes through the screen's own door).

- **A sheet** is made on Jobs → Subs → Pay → New Sub Labor (`JobsSubLaborFormModal.saveLaborJob`): one insert into `people_labor_jobs`, an upsert of `people_labor_job_assignees` as a backup to the name trigger, then one insert a line into `people_labor_job_items`. No RPC. The person is the name string `assigned_to_name`; the trigger `sync_people_labor_job_assignees` builds the assignee rows from it. The job is `job_ledger_id`.
- **A line** is a flat amount (`direct_labor_amount`, the form's simple mode) or hours at a rate (`count × hrs_per_unit × labor_rate`, its itemized mode). The form holds one mode a sheet, and the Edit window opens a mixed sheet as itemized and blanks the flat amounts on its next save.
- **Edit** updates the sheet row, deletes every line and inserts them again. **Delete** deletes the sheet row; its lines, payments and assignee rows cascade.
- **A payment** is one insert into `people_labor_job_payments` (`useSubLaborLedger.recordLaborJobPayment`): amount, memo, date, order. There is no approval step, no status, no method or reference column and no link to a bank row. A payment's Remove is the RPC `remove_labor_job_payment`, which keeps a snapshot on `people_labor_job_payment_events` for a 30-day Undo.
- **Cost**: a sheet counts on its job for what it owes, paid or not (`laborJobSubCost` into `buildJobProfitSummary`). A bank row allocated to the job (`mercury_transaction_job_allocations`) counts as a card charge unless it is labelled Internal Transfer or tied to a supply-house invoice. Nothing subtracts one from the other, so a sub paid from the bank whose row was split onto the job counts twice. The office's rule is the Payroll label in Banking for contract labor.
- **RLS**: the office writes sheets, lines and payments (`is_office_staff()`, or one of five roles on a sheet they own; a dev on any).

## What was added

Two plan → apply pairs on `dev-mcp` (server 0.70.0, eighty-five write verbs). No wrapper RPC and no migration. Kernel `supabase/functions/_shared/devMcpSubLabor.ts`.

1. **`plan_sub_labor` / `apply_sub_labor`**, three actions.
   - `add` makes a sheet for one subcontractor or primary on one job: `{ job_id, person, date, through?, lines, note?, stated?: { source }, second_sheet_ok? }`. The rows are the form's own: the sheet (yours, the job's address and number, no miles, the stage at Working), the assignee row, the lines. A sheet whose lines are refused is taken off again.
   - `change` moves a sheet's day or replaces its lines whole. The new lines land first and the old ones go after, by id, so a refusal part-way leaves the old lines standing. With `job_id` it moves the sheet to another job, as the Edit window's *change* beside the job does: the sheet takes that job, its number and its address, and its payments go with it. The plan shows both jobs before and after.
   - `remove` deletes a sheet. It is held while the sheet carries a payment, which the screen would delete without a trace.
2. **`plan_sub_labor_payment` / `apply_sub_labor_payment`**, two actions.
   - `record` inserts the payment row Make Payment writes. The method, the reference and the note are joined into the memo. `hidden_from_sub` hides the memo on the sub's portal.
   - `remove` POSTs `remove_labor_job_payment` with the reason.

## Where a note, a span and the stated mark go

A sheet has no column for any of the three. A line's `day` and the `stated` mark ride in the line's own words: `2026-10-14 · Rough-in (stated, their own day log)`. The sub's portal, the printed sheet and a signed hours statement read the line, so the mark travels with it. The note, the span and the source land on the job's activity as a note in the dev's name, when the ask carries one of them.

The app keeps one day a sheet. A signed hours statement counts every line of a sheet on the sheet's day, whatever day a line names in its words. The plan says so, and says to make a sheet a day when the hours must fall on their own days.

## What the plan shows

- The job and the person as they stand. The person is a subcontractor or a primary, the two the form's Subcontractors list offers. A primary's sheet is one the Pay ledger files under Crew pay, outside what is owed to subs, and the plan says so; it still counts on the job. Refused: any other teammate (paid through payroll from their hours), an archived person, a sub on the bench, a twin, a name two people share, a job RLS hides, a job with no address.
- The sheet and each line as it would land, what it owes the sub (`laborItemsSubtotal`) and what it costs the job (`laborJobSubCost`).
- The person's other sheets on the job, found by the assignee row or by the name on the sheet. One on the same days refuses the plan unless `second_sheet_ok`.
- Every sheet on the job, the job's sub labor before and after, and the profit band before and after as `get_job` works it out.
- The job's allocated bank rows that may be this same pay: money out to a payee that reads like the person, or of the same amount.
- What the write sets off and what it does not do.

The hash commits to all of it but the profit band. A sheet added or changed on the job since the plan refuses the apply. A cost elsewhere on the job does not.

## The bank row

A sheet payment cannot hold a bank row, so the payment plan reads the row and writes it nowhere. With `mercury_transaction_id` named, the plan refuses money in and a returned payment, sets the row's payee and amount beside the payment's, and lists in `counts_twice` every job the row is already allocated to as cost. The first warning says in words that the dollars would count twice and names each allocation. The verb never moves an allocation: `plan_cost_batch` does. With no bank row named, `may_count_twice` lists the job's allocated rows that read like the payment.

## What the screens do that the verbs do not

- A sheet for several people, or for a teammate who is neither a sub nor a primary.
- Miles and the invoice link on a sheet.
- The stage, the payable-after date and the pay hold.
- A backcharge, a payment moved to another sheet, a payment edited in place.
- A work order or an agreement for the sub to sign.
- Assigning a sheet to a person on People → Subs.

## Verified

- `src/lib/devMcp/devMcpSubLabor.test.ts`, on made-up people and jobs (`subLaborFixture.ts`): a sub with a stated three-day sheet, a second sheet for the same days, a payment whose bank row is already allocated, and the refusals. The fake reader holds every table, column and embed to the generated catalog. The tests also pin the form's and the ledger's own payloads, so a change to what the screens write fails here.
- Live and read-only, before the deploy: the kernels ran on a dev machine with every read sent through the deployed seat's own `read_rows` and `get_job` (GET only, as the dev). `plan_sub_labor` add answered against the ZZ TEST Riders Walk job and the sample subcontractor account in nine reads; change and remove answered against the test sheet on the ZZ Test Walk Project job; `plan_sub_labor_payment` answered against that sheet with no bank row named, and again with a bank row that is allocated to a job, where `counts_twice` named the allocation and the first warning read *These dollars would count twice*. Nothing was written to prod. The walk found one gap, closed here: a sub with an account and no roster row gets a sheet no assignee row ties to them, and the plan now warns.
- Twelve mutants of the kernel, each breaking one rule (a second sheet let through, the bank warning dropped, the profit band in the hash, a teammate taken as a sub, the stated mark dropped, a sheet with payments removed, money in taken as pay, a payment twice, a benched sub, a mixed sheet, the flat sheet's rate, the memo's reference): every one fails a test.
- Two real cases from another session were then read against the plans, read-only: a primary paid as a sub, and a sheet filed on the wrong job. Both were refused or missing as first built, and both are presses the screen has, so the person rule and the job move were added before the merge.
- No apply was run: the first real sheet is the office's to make.
