# v2.7822 — Stages onto the job: the rule for bringing a won bid's stages across (2026-11-18)

Punch list #397, PR 2 of the train, with the card and its mock-up. One pure file and its tests. No screen, no migration and no edge function.

## Why

The owner asked for one look at every place the app says "stage": the Jobs page, bids, GC mode, the Development page and the superintendent's Dashboard. The review found five things under the word and one in real use. Everything new that reads a job's stages reads its lines, and a job opened from a won bid arrives with one line, *Bid price* (`bidImportFirstLine`). The bid's Rough In, Top Out and Trim Set are dropped at the win. He picked the direction that fills the lines there by an offer the office accepts, and took every default: *build on defaults, build it all*.

## What landed

- **`src/lib/bids/jobStagesFromBid.ts`**, pure:
  - `bidStageSplits` lists the splits the window will offer: the bid's own schedule of values first when it holds two or more priced lines, then the Multiple Segment Generator's two presets.
  - `defaultBidStageSplitKey` picks the chip that starts lit: the bid's schedule, else 40/40/20 for a residential customer, else 30/30/30/10.
  - `bidStageLines` turns a price and a split into job lines that add up to the cent. A preset uses the generator's own sum, so the numbers match what the generator would write. The bid's own lines use the schedule's own scaling.
  - Each stage's hours go to the lines that name it, shared by price when there are several. What crosses is the hours: a line stores them valued at the company's cost rate, as a share of its price, so every reader of a line budget reads the bid's own hours back.
  - A bid that fails the award's own test (`bidBaselineUsable`) gives no hours. Its lines store no budget and read the company's split for their kind, marked assumed, as a typed line does.
  - Every line arrives **Any time**. `stageRecognition.ts` already draws lines with these names as stages, and the billing rule stays behind the In order badge.
  - `bringBidStagesStanding` says whether a job that exists may be offered the same stages in place of its one line, and the reason and the door when it may not: on a bill, a pay application, the crew's percent, a checklist answer, a stage window, a trip, a tuned budget.
  - `BID_CARRY_WORDS` holds the window's words, and each press names what it does: *Bring 4 stages · $48,000*, *Carry $48,000 over*, *Start at $0*.
- **The card**, `to-dos/stages-onto-the-job/`, with the review's findings, the owner's six calls, the guard rails, the seven PRs and a mock-up of six screens on made-up figures.

## Two findings that shaped the rule

- **The bid cannot give the dollars.** The app's own reader of a bid's schedule (`loadBidScheduleForJob`) found one on no won bid on 2026-11-18, because the schedule is built from materials by stage and nearly every won bid has none. So the dollars follow the company's terms and only the hours follow the bid.
- **A line's hours are read at the company's rate.** `calcLineBudget` divides a line's labor dollars by the Settings rate, whatever rate the line stored. The first live walk, on the ZZ bid, showed what that does to hours valued at the bid's own rate: a stage the Labor tab put at 23 h read 15 h on its line. So the rule values the hours at the company's rate, and the preview uses `calcLineBudget` itself, so the window and the Bill tab cannot disagree. The share is a whole percent, so a line reads within a percent of its price of the bid's figure.
