# v2.6896 — Lien window: it says when an affidavit takes a second sheet (2026-11-10)

Punch list #305. The owner, 2026-11-10, on the print test: "if any part of the notary box gets cut off it should push that section to the next page and say so." The push has existed since v2.6810 (`filingKeepTogetherRuns`). Asked where it should say so, on the paper or in the window, he chose the window.

## What changed

Under **Print for notarization** on the Lien window's affidavit tab and its release tab, a line appears when the paper takes more than one sheet:

- *This paper prints on 2 pages. The last statement, the signature and the notary’s block moved to page 2 together, so none of it is cut.* when the closing crossed the foot of a sheet and was moved.
- *This paper prints on 2 pages. The signature and the notary’s block are on page 2.* when the closing was already past the first sheet.
- Nothing on a paper that fits one sheet.

## How

- **`filingDocPrintHtml(…, { sheet: true })`** writes the print sheet's rules for every medium, so the same document lays out on screen as the printer will. `FILING_SHEET_WIDTH_PX` and `FILING_SHEET_ROOM_PX` are a Letter sheet's width and the room between its head and foot bands; the band's height is one constant the CSS now reads too.
- **`src/lib/jobsDocuments/filingSheetBreak.ts`** (pure): `measureFilingSheet` reads the paper's height and each keep-together run's top and bottom; `filingSheetBreak` walks the runs down the sheets, moving a run that crosses a sheet's end to the next sheet and pushing what follows; `filingSheetWords` writes the line.
- **`FilingSheetNote`** (`src/components/jobs/`): a frame nobody sees, 816 px wide, `sandbox="allow-same-origin"` so nothing in it runs, read when it loads and again when its fonts have.
- **`LienFilingTabs`** builds the sheet document from the same blocks and print options the print press uses, so the count is for the paper that prints. The § 53.056 notice is left out: its pay page and invoices print behind it.

## Decisions

- **The window, not the paper.** A line on a sworn instrument is wording counsel has not seen, and the print engine cannot tell the paper where it broke.
- **Silent on one sheet.** The line is a warning before a trip to the notary, not a status.
- **Runs, not lines.** The walk knows where the keep-together runs sit, not where each line of text breaks. It is exact for a paper of one sheet or a little over. Deep into a long paper it can be a line off.

## Proof

- Against Chromium's own print engine (Playwright `page.pdf`, then `pdfinfo` and `pdftotext` per page): 208 sample affidavits, the legal description growing three words at a time across the foot of page 1 and on to a third sheet, on the short form's sheet, the paper's own leading, with and without the homestead notice, and the earlier form. The window's count and the page of the closing matched the printed pages on 206. On the short form's sheet, 102 of 102. The two misses are papers at the paper's own leading that end at the very foot of page 2: the window said two pages and the print took three.
- In the local preview on two real jobs whose approved affidavits are on the earlier form, read only: the line reads two pages with the closing moved to page 2, as the print of the same papers does. It did not flicker.
- Unit tests for the walk and the words, a render test for the note.

Not observed: a real printer, and the release tab on a real job (no recorded affidavit on prod has a release to draw).
