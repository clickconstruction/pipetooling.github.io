## Why the rule is *no record anywhere*, and why the match is loose

A kept address carries no name, so it cannot be told as a test by a ZZ. The rule is the other half of the owner's line: an address a record depends on stays.

The card's row said *no job and no bid*. The cache is keyed into from many more screens than those two: the Map page's jobs, bids and estimates, the bid board's map, a job's map and materials tabs, trips and far sites, the legal map, a painted map's home, On my way, the clocked-in map's office. Each reads its address from a column that holds one. So the rule asks every such column, and a test reads the generated types and fails when a column with *address* in its name is neither listed nor named as left out.

A screen may key the cache with the address and its city joined. So the match is the street line alone, the words before the first comma, each word in order with anything between. A loose match can only keep an address that could have gone. It never clears one that is in use. A street line under six characters, or with no letter or no digit, is never checked and stays.

## What it is not

It does not list every kept address nothing is written under, as the card's row asked. That needs the whole cache read against every source, which is a job for the database, not a browser. The dev finds an address by its words. The one leftover on prod is found by a word of its made-up street.

It does not touch `address_geocode_misses`, the addresses no map service could place. That table has no delete policy, and a miss stops standing by itself after 30 days.
