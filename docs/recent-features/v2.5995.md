# v2.5995 — GC mode: the schedule builder’s kernel, a template of phases and rows put on a job from a start day (2026-10-27)

PR 1 of punch list #204 (`to-dos/schedule-builder/README.md`, the mock-up `mockup.html`'s D2 to D4), the plan the
owner approved on 2026-10-27 with all seven calls taken as drawn. Pure TypeScript under `src/lib/gc/schedule/` with
unit tests: no screen, no migration, no edge function. The old template (`TemplateLine`, G-44, v2.5188) was a timing
overlay on the lines a job already has; a template with rows draws every row, the ones the job lacks too.

## The shape, version 2

- [`types.ts`](../../src/lib/gc/schedule/types.ts): `TemplatePhase {key, name}` in order, and `TemplateRow {phase,
  label, who, days, after, offset, milestone, place?, parts?}` with `who` one of a trade, Us or Office (call 3, never a
  company or a person) and `after` naming rows by name (`rowRef`: the label, or *Who · label* when two rows share one).
  `ScheduleTemplate` gains the optional `phases`, `rows`, `kind`, `workingDays` (call 4) and `versionOf` (call 2).
  `lines` stays, so every template saved before still reads. `ScheduleActivity` and `ScheduleMilestone` gain an
  optional `phase`, the phase's name.
- [`builder.ts`](../../src/lib/gc/schedule/builder.ts) `templateRows` reads any template as rows: its own when it has
  them, else its lines, each a trade's row with its stage as the phase, the two inspections as Office rows, and its
  waits by name. A template with no word on working days counts every day, as every template saved before did.
- `templateRowsShape`, which `templateShape` now returns beside the old lines: every trade's line as a trade row, our own
  package's lines as Us rows, the job's own work as Office rows (Us when it was *Our own crew*'s), the inspections as
  Office rows, the milestones as milestone rows, each waiting on the kept bar that finished last on or before its day
  with an offset of minus one when it is that day. The stage of each is its phase in build order: a line's from its name,
  the job's own work's from what it waits on, else from the day it sits on. The old rules on places and parts stay.

## The draw from rows

- `rowsDraft(project, start, templateRows(t), stageDays?)`: every row placed on the template's calendar in wait order,
  after the last of its waits by its offset and never before a gap set on one of them, or its offset after the start
  day. Where each lands (`rowLandings`, call 7): a trade's row on the job's line of the same trade and name, else as the
  job's own work on that trade's package with the trade named, else with no package, which the job shows as *No company
  yet*; a Us row on our own package, its line of the same name when there is one; an Office row named an inspection as
  the city's inspection (*Rough-in inspection* and *Final inspection* by the first draft's own ids); any other Office
  row as the job's own work with *Office* named; a milestone row as a date to meet on its day, on its trade's package,
  ours or the job's own. A row after a milestone with no gap starts on the milestone's day.
- The job's scope lines in no row take the stage days as today: `scheduleDraft` draws them around the rows, the rowed
  lines pinned to their days through its `like`. The rough-in inspection comes after the job's own rough-ins the
  template has no row for and the final after all the work, and whatever waits on either moves with it. With no
  milestone rows, the draft's three stand. Every rowed bar and milestone carries its phase by name; a line in no row
  carries none.
- `draft.ts`, `network.ts` and `schedule.ts` run on the edge too (`scripts/edge-kernels.mjs`), so they are read and not
  changed; the shared copy under `supabase/functions/_shared/gcKernels/` is untouched.

## The words before the draw

`rowsFit` counts what a template makes of a job and `rowsFitLines` says it in D3's six labelled lines: the rows and
phases with the weeks and the finish, the rows on the job's trades and the trades it has no package for yet, ours and
where they land, the office's, the dates to meet by name, and the scope lines in no row. A list longer than twenty
words goes on in a new sentence (*Then …*), so every sentence keeps to `src/lib/plainWords.ts`.
