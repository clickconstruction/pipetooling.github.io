# v2.6154 — Office hours: who hears a reply, drawn by the hour (punch list #217, office-11) (2026-10-30)

Punch list #217, *Show it, don't say it*: the scan's office-11 finding, which Taunya picked to build on 2026-10-30. With *The office is open around the clock* on, the Office hours card said in one amber line the hours nobody in the office is pushed, *Nobody hears a reply from 11 PM to 6 AM. Rosa, Cora and Max are all off then.*, computed from each person's Push me hours, and drew none of them. The owner could not see whose hours nearly cover the gap, or which one change would close it.

## What changed

- **`lib/messages/coverageGrid.ts`** (new, 7 tests): `coverageGrid` over the same office members the line reads (`CoverageMember`, now with the person's `id`). One panel per set of days with the same hours, labelled by `daysWords` (*Every day*, *Mon to Fri*, *Sun and Sat*), so a week where every day is alike draws once and days that differ each draw. Each panel has one row per person with their bar in minutes from midnight and its words, and the stretches no bar covers as columns with their times (*to 6 AM*, *from 11 PM*, *noon to 1 PM*). A day nobody covers is no panel: the stretches of a day or more, `coverageGaps`' long ones, are chips, *Fri 8 PM to Mon 6 AM*. `coverageDoor` says where a press on a row goes: your own hours on Your account (`#settings-push-hours`), another person's desk (`?person=u:<id>` on the page, the Desk's route-agnostic deep link), since only they can set their hours. Null when there is no gap, as the line is.
- **`whoIsOff.ts`**: `clock` and `dayClock` exported for the chips; nothing else moves.
- **`pushCoverageIo.ts`**: the member carries the user's id.
- **`components/settings/CoverageGrid.tsx`** (new): the drawing. Rows on a midnight-to-midnight axis with the hour marks under the last row, the bars blue with their words when wide enough, a red hatched column per uncovered stretch through every row, its words on the top edge, *Off* on a row with no bar that day, the long gaps as red chips. A row's name is a button where the app has a door.
- **`OfficeHoursSettingsCard.tsx`**: the grid sits above the amber line, which stays as its caption; both draw for the owner or a dev while the switch is on and a gap exists. The card reads the viewer through `useOptionalAuth` for the *You* row.
- **The smoke** (`OfficeHoursSettingsCard.render.test.tsx`): the grid draws with the line, one row a person with the bar's words, two red columns, the weekend chip, the viewer's row as *You* opening Push me, another row opening their desk, and no grid while the switch is off or for a reader.
- **Guide** *turn on the reply push*: the amber line's paragraph now describes the drawing above it.

## Decisions

- One weekday drawn, with the weekend as a chip, as the mock-up's second pass held: the gap the line names is the same every weekday. When the days differ, each set of alike days gets its own rows rather than a day picker, since `coverageGaps`' intervals already say which days are alike.
- A press on another person's row opens their desk, not their hours: the app lets each person set their own Push me hours alone, and the desk has their number.
- No migration, no function change.
