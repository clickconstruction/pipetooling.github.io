# v2.6636 — A sub’s sheet lines make a signed hours statement too (punch list #212, residual D) (2026-11-07)

The fourth residual after the five-PR train on the card (`to-dos/signed-hours-statement/README.md`): the kernel's `sheet` kind (v2.6069, a sub's rows grouped by sheet, *On the sheets, this job*, paid per sheet, the attestation for the company) had no reader, so a sub's paper was made by hand. The card is amended in the close-out PR once the residuals land.

## What changed

- **The kernel** `src/lib/people/hoursStatementSheets.ts` (pure, tested): a sheet is a `people_labor_jobs` row — its day is `job_date`, else the day it was made — numbered in day order among the sub's sheets on the job (*Sheet 2 · May 4, 2026*); a line's hours are the agreed units (`count × hrs_per_unit`, or the hours alone on a fixed line; a flat amount has none), its note the fixture with its count, its rate the line's own over the sheet's, its amount the line's cost as the Sub Labor screens price it (`lineLaborCost`); one pay group a sheet with the sheet's total as the due and `people_labor_job_payments` on their days (a payment with no day on the sheet's); the span's counts for the window's line.
- **The reads** (`hoursStatementRead.ts`): `listHoursStatementPeople` adds every sub (`people.kind = 'sub'`) assigned to a sheet on the job who has a login (`kind: 'sheet'`, the sheets counted in `sessions`; a sub with no login is left off, since a statement is saved on the login); `listHoursStatementJobs` adds the jobs on a sub's sheets for the desk; `loadHoursStatementInputs({ kind: 'sheet' })` reads the sub's sheets on the job, their lines and payments, and hands them to the kernel — always on the paid basis, the first recorded day the earlier of the clock's and the sheets' (a sheet may predate the first punch: 2026-03-11 on the live data), the standing from the login's role (*subcontractor*), no approver.
- **The window** (`HoursStatementModal.tsx`): a sub reads *Dale Whitfield · 2 sheets* in Who (a job on the desk *· 2 sheets*); picking one reads the sheet kind; the charged switch is held with *A sub's sheet is agreed work, so the paper reads the sheet's lines as paid.*; the pay switch stays theirs. The paper draws as the kernel's sheet kind always did (Day · Line · Hours · Amount, the sheet as the heading row, Paid per sheet, *I, …, for <company>, state that we did the work above*).
- **The window's words** (`hoursStatementWindow.ts`): the span line for a sub — *The app has 4 lines on 2 sheets for this sub on this job in this span.* — and the press names the subs — *Hours statement… · 3 people here, 1 on the sheets* (a desk: *4 jobs, 2 on the sheets*); the empty section's tail says *the clock, the sheets and the pay reports*. The section (`HoursStatementsSection.tsx`) counts the subs for the press.
- **Tests**: `hoursStatementSheets.test.ts` (the day, the numbering, the hours, the note, the rows, the pay groups, the counts, and the paper through the kernel), `hoursStatementWindow.test.ts` (the new words), the window's render smoke (the sub in Who, the read's kind, the held switch).
- **Docs**: the guide *make a signed hours statement* gains *A sub's sheet lines*; `GLOSSARY.md`.

## Decisions

- **A sub's hours are the sheet's words, not a clock.** Many live lines are a lump sum written as one hour at the price (*1.00 h at $8,500.00*); the paper prints the sheet as agreed, since that is the company's record of what it bought, and the kernel's Paid lines carry what was paid against it. Nothing is reinterpreted.
- **The company on the attestation stays empty.** The roster row's name is the sub's name as the office keeps it (sometimes the company, sometimes the person); `hours_statements.labor_company` stays null until the office keys one, so the attestation reads *I, <name>, state that we did the work above*.
- **Left for later**: the Legal desk's **Ask <name>…** still lists only who clocked (its workers come from clock sessions); the dev MCP's `get_hours_statement` reads the clock only. Both are named on the card.
