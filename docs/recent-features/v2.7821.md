# v2.7821 — Pay: the dev seat changes the pay setup of a person who exists (2026-11-18)

Two verbs on dev-mcp (server 0.102.0, 125 write verbs): `plan_pay_setup`, then `apply_pay_setup`. No migration, no screen, no new gate.

## Why

The owner asked a session to put a helper on a weekly salary. The seat could not do it: `plan_account` carries a pay row only for a new person, and refused pay on a person who exists. The session signed the browser pane in as the dev and pressed three controls on the person's sheet. That worked, but it was slow, and the sheet's save leaves no line on the pay log. The owner then asked: "Can you help me add the dev verbs so you can do this in the future if needed?"

The same session found two facts about the app that a pay change must say out loud. The app holds a salary as an hourly wage with a Salaried flag: a flat 8 hours on each weekday today, no weekly amount. And it keeps no effective date and no wage history: most readers apply today's pay row to every past day.

## What landed

- **`plan_pay_setup(pay)` then `apply_pay_setup(pay, plan_hash)`**. `pay = { person, reason, hourly_wage?, office_hourly_wage?, is_salary?, record_hours_but_salary?, start_date?, end_date?, workday_start? }`. These are the things People → a person's sheet → *Pay & schedule* changes. A person is named by a users.id, a people.id, an address or a name. A roster row with no users row is found by its people.id or its exact name, and is paid by its roster name, as the sheet pays it.
- **`supabase/functions/_shared/devMcpPaySetup.ts`**, the one new kernel. It is pure. The reads come in through a reader (`readPaySetupWorld`), so they are tested too. One function, `resolvePaySetup`, gives the refusals, the before and after, and the writes. The plan and the apply both call it, so they cannot disagree.
- **The pay row goes through `set_pay_config`**, the app's audited door from v2.3584, with only the keys that move and the reason. `pay_admin_events` keeps the before and the after. The sheet's own upsert never left that line. A pay row with no roster link is linked afterwards, as the sheet's upsert links it.
- **The other writes are the sheet's own.** The dates are its PATCH on `people`. The workday template is the Hire form's row when there is none, and only the start of the block when one stands, so its days and its job focus stay. Turning Salaried on with a users row syncs today's salary sessions. Turning it off calls `pay_staff_clear_salary_schedule_by_person_name`, which clears the template, the day overrides and today's unapproved auto-sessions.
- **One more pay log line** records the dates and the workday start, which `set_pay_config` does not log. It is an insert as the dev under the table's own policy, with the same action name.
- **`plan_account` now names this pair** where it refuses `pay` on a person who exists.

## What the plan says before anything is written

- Before and after for each field, and what a salaried wage comes to a week: wage × the day's hours × 5.
- **Payroll.** The whole weeks since the last pay report with no report. With Salaried on, how many of them payroll catch-up will show as unreported, and about how much. With no start date that is every one of them. The employment dates clamp payroll only.
- **Job labor.** The past crew days that re-price: the flat salaried day × the wage on weekdays, 0 on weekends, logged hours ignored. A start date does not shield them. A wage change alone re-prices them too.
- **Pay reports.** The reports already made keep the hours, rates and amounts stored on them.
- **The clock.** Whether a workday template stands, and what the person's clock does after the change.
- **The fixed day.** A salaried day is 8 hours today. A person who works a shorter day is still paid and shown 8.

## What is refused

- A workday named with a length that is not the person's salaried day: `workday_hours`, `workday_minutes` or `workday_end`. Nothing is stored. The words give the hours the sessions would run.
- A wage over 500, as a weekly or yearly figure in the wrong box, with the sum to do.
- Salaried with no wage. *Also record hours* on an hourly person. A workday start on an hourly person, on a person with no users row, or over a split day set on the sheet.
- A sample, a twin, the walk tester, an archived person, a primary.
- A pay row that carries the person's id under another name. The names are made to agree first.
- A change that changes nothing.

## A salaried day's length, in one place

The owner approved a per-person salaried day on 2026-11-18, the same day. It lands in other PRs: a column `people_pay_config.salaried_day_hours`, then the readers, then the workday window. This PR is built on `main` as it stands, where every salaried day is 8 hours. So the kernel never writes a bare 8 or 480. `salariedDayHoursOf(row)` answers 8 until the pay row carries its own day, and the weekly figure, the payroll credit, the job labor words, the template's length and the length check all read it. The follow-up is small: add the column to `PAY_ROW_SELECT`, and take `salaried_day_hours` in the ask. A test runs a 6-hour row through the kernel today to hold that.

## How it holds

- The plan's hash covers the pay row, the roster row's dates and the template as read. A save by anyone since refuses the apply.
- Apply stops at the first step that fails and names it. What landed stays. There is no transaction across the steps, as on the Hire form.
- The reply reads the rows back, with the newest lines of the pay log.
- The calendar rules are restated in the kernel and pinned by test to `salariedPayrollDays.ts` and `unreportedPayrollWeeks.ts`, day for day and week for week.

## Not in this change

- A person's own salaried day. It waits for the column; see above.
- An effective date or a wage history. The plan names the gap; it does not close it.
- Time off, the vehicle arrangement, *show in hours*, a pay report. Each stays on its own screen.
