# v2.6323 — A clock-out with no signal keeps the session's job (punch list #228) (2026-11-02)

Punch list #228 (`to-dos/offline-clock-out-keeps-job.md`, deleted in this PR), found by v2.6198's live check on clicktooling.com on 2026-10-31: a Clock Out pressed with no signal was kept on the phone and landed at its pressed moment, but the session it closed lost its job, and its hours read *No Job or Bid*. Client only, no screen change.

## Why the job went

The clock-out window (`ClockInOutButton.tsx`) opens with an empty pick and then reads the session's job (`jobs_ledger`) or bid (`bids` with its customer) to draw it. With no signal that read fails and the pick stays empty. `handleCompleteClockOutReview` sent `jobLedgerId` and `bidId` from the pick, `landFieldPress.ts` always maps them to `job_ledger_id` and `bid_id`, and `land_field_press`'s clock-out writes a key that is in the payload, null included; it keeps the session's job only when the key is absent. So the kept press carried `jobLedgerId: null` and the server wrote it over the job. The window also has **Clear** on the job chip, which removes the job on purpose, so an empty pick alone cannot be read as "keep the job". Online the same loss could happen when the window's read failed at the moment of clock-out, rarely, since the read lands first. Job Mode's direct apply (`applyUpdateFocusDirectImpl`) was never affected: it sends the open session's own job and bid.

## What changed

- **The kernel** [`src/lib/clock/clockOutJob.ts`](../../src/lib/clock/clockOutJob.ts) (`clockOutJob`, `clockOutPickOf`; 6 tests): which job a clock-out sends, from the pick, whether the person touched it, and the open session. Touched, meaning the person picked a job or a bid or pressed Clear, the pick stands, empty or not. Untouched, the press carries the session's own `job_ledger_id` and `bid_id`, which the clock knows with no signal from the open session it already drew.
- **The clock** (`ClockInOutButton.tsx`): a ref, `clockOutPickTouchedRef`, cleared when the clock-out window opens and set by `pickByHand`, the one door for the person's own hand on the pick: a dispatch pick, a working-board bid, **Use last**, a search result, and the three **Clear** presses. The window's read-back of the session's job and the reset on open go on setting the pick without touching it. `handleCompleteClockOutReview` sends what the kernel answers; its `about` words and the *last job* it remembers still read the pick, so a kept clock-out on an unloaded job reads no job on the bar, as before.
- **The server** is unchanged. It already keeps a job when the key is absent; the client now names the job it means instead of omitting keys, so a late press and an online one read the same.

## Decisions

- **Typing in the search box is not a touch.** The box clears the pick as you type (so a result can be picked), and a search abandoned without a pick used to send the clock-out with no job, online too. Now the session's job stays unless a job is picked or Clear is pressed. The card's rule named those two and no other.
- **No screen change.** The card offered drawing the chip from the session's own ids when the read fails, so the person sees the job will stay; the mock-up field said not required, and the fix is to what the press sends. A person who opens the window with no signal sees no job picked and keeps it anyway, which is the safe side.
- **The guide's sentence.** *Clock in and out with Job Mode* → *What the clock-out review asks, and why* says *The job list confirms where the time goes. The job you are clocked into is first with a You are here tag. So the usual answer is just to leave it.* That holds with no signal too: the list may be empty, and the time still goes to the job you are clocked into. Punch list #225's session owns that guide tonight and carries the sentence; this PR does not edit it.
