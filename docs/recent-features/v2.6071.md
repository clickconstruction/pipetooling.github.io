## What changed

- **The kind** ([`fillInPurchases.ts`](../../src/lib/needsYou/fillInPurchases.ts), [its test](../../src/lib/needsYou/fillInPurchases.test.ts)): the rows are the Tally page's own read (`list_my_linked_mercury_transactions_for_tally`, paged) narrowed the way the line's count is (`count_unlinked_mercury_transactions_for_tally_stale`): no job split, no invoice link, not marked payroll, past the org's date floor, posted more than `TALLY_STALE_MIN_AGE_DAYS` ago. Oldest posting day first, the biggest charge of a day leading, titled *$184.20 at Ferguson Plumbing · Oct 27* by the swipe day (a refund reads *$40.00 back from …*). What the app knows: the swipe day, the posting and its age and whether it came after the swipe, Mercury's category in words, the holder's memo, Mercury's memo, the account. The decision is the Sort screen's: one job takes the whole charge, two or more split it evenly in whole cents, an amount typed balances on the last other line (`sortModeSplit.ts`), and Save waits until the split adds up. `purchaseWrites` is the RPC's rows, signed as the charge is; `purchaseTrailWords` says where it went.
- **The fields** ([`PurchaseFields.tsx`](../../src/components/needsYou/PurchaseFields.tsx)): the jobs on the viewer's clock and schedule the day the card was swiped and the day each side, as chips, read per row through the Sort screen's own read (`fetchSortModeDayJobs`, the swipe day's clocked jobs first); the split block with its amounts once two are picked; **Another job**, the Assign window's search for the holder's own card (`search_jobs_for_tally_mercury_assign`), whose pick joins the chips. With nothing on the clock those days the card says to search.
- **The io** ([`fillInPurchasesIo.ts`](../../src/lib/needsYou/fillInPurchasesIo.ts)): the rows, the floor (`job_tally_min_posted_ymd`) and the payroll marks read together, the marks failing soft to none as on the page, where only a payroll approver may read them; Save is `replace_mercury_job_splits_for_my_linked_card`, the Sort screen's RPC, and Undo the same RPC with no rows, since a row is on the deck only while it has none.
- **The shell**: `FILL_IN_KINDS_BUILT` gains `purchase` on the `tally-self` line, so the line's button reads **Fill them in** and its rows lead the deck (the line ranks 30, above every office kind); `useFillInRows` reads the kind under the line's gate (`tally`, any signed-in role), failing soft with the others; a save or an Undo re-reads the line through `useTallyUnlinkedCounts`' `refetch`, now on `useNeedsYouInputs`' handles, so the figure falls by one; the deck's end counts *N purchases are on jobs*.
- **Whose rows they are**: the purchases line is the viewer's own whoever they are (`needsYouActor.ts`: `anyone`), so `fillInKindIsViewers('purchase', role)` is true for every role, and the owner's and the controller's station now opens on the deck too when their own purchases are waiting, their purchases first and the office's rows after; with none waiting they open on the card as before, after the read.

## Is this the best we can do?

- The picker is the Sort screen's, not the Banking sorting station's modal the card named: the station's modal sorts the office's rows with a person on each, and for the holder's own charge the day's jobs with a search is the shape the phone already walks. Both write the same RPC.
- The deck's rows and the line's count are built from the same three rules, but the count's RPC also drops a charge marked a duplicate (`duplicate_of_transaction_id`), which the Tally page's read does not carry; the page's own unlinked list has the same gap. A duplicate on a holder's card would be one extra row on the deck.
- The day's jobs are read per row, as the Sort screen reads them, so a deck of a hundred purchases costs one small read a card, not a hundred at once.
- Not pressed live: the rows on prod are a real holder's charges. The save ran through the injected io; the deck was walked live on the real rows as the dev, read-only.
