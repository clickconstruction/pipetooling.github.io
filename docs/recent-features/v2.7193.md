# v2.7193 — A last day of work set by hand is the day every lien date counts from (2026-11-13)

Punch list #335, row 1, on v2.7187. The owner's answer of 2026-11-13 to *which last day of work counts for the lien clock?*: **"The day set by hand"**. A job can carry a last day of work typed by the office (`jobs_ledger.lien_last_work_on`, v2.4676) and approved clock hours after it. The billed row's clock counted from the hand day (v2.4830); the Lien window, the affidavit's work period and four SQL readers took the later of the two. One live job read *Lien gone* on its row and *File the affidavit, 94 days* in its window. Client and one migration.

## The rule, in one kernel

`lienClockMonths(sessionMonths, handDay)` in `lienLastWorkDay.ts`: a day set by hand is the last day of work. Its month is the last month on the clock, months of approved hours before it stay, and months of approved hours after it are off the clock (`hoursAfter` names them). A job with no hand day reads as before. `lienHoursAfterHandDay(job)` is the last approved clock day when it follows the hand day, so a screen can say the hours are there.

## Later hours do not vanish silently

- The timeline's LAST WORK stop reads *last day set by hand · later hours do not move it* (`LAST_DAY_BY_HAND_LATER_HOURS_WORDS`) with its **change ›** door, in the Lien window, the Lien desk's timeline and the Job window's History.
- `LienLastWorkDayLine` (the Lien window's editor, Edit Job) prints *Hours were approved after it, through Oct 20. They do not move it.* beside the day.

## The sworn paper

On a job with a hand day and later hours, the affidavit's work period now ends on the hand day, and the months it names stop at the hand day's month. The paper's wording is not changed: only the dates and months filled in. One live job fits today, and it has no affidavit drafted. No drafted, approved or filed paper was redrawn or re-approved. **Worth counsel's eye**: whether a return visit after the last day set by hand is new work under the contract is a question of law, and the rule now always takes the earlier, cautious day.
