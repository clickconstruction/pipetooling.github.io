# v2.6142 — Tally’s Team queue day card: the holder’s day as a strip, behind a Day strip switch (punch list #217, jobs-money-3, a prototype) (2026-10-30)

Punch list #217, *Show it, don’t say it*, found 131 places where the app tells instead of shows, and drew each as a before/after pair on a mock-up page (`to-dos/show-not-tell/mockup.html`, PR #6521). Of jobs-money-3, Tally’s Team queue day card, the owner wrote on 2026-10-30: *"This has great potential, but I’m uncertain of what it’s going to disrupt. I would like to see this as a prototype."* So this is a prototype behind a switch: off, nothing on the page differs from before. Draft PR, no auto-merge, no migration, no edge function.

## The finding

`/tally?tab=transactions` → Team → a holder’s day card said the holder’s day in a sentence (*Clocked on JP101 Hill Street from 7:02 AM to 3:40 PM. Nothing else that day.*) and marked the day’s chip *likely · only job clocked that day*. The evidence is times, but the eye had to place a 9:14 AM swipe inside *7:02 to 3:40* by reading, and a 6:52 PM swipe three hours after the clock-out went unmarked. The kernel’s own table puts *clock-one-job · likely* at 53% agreement with the sorter, and Mercury posts a charge a median 8 hours after the swipe.

## What changed

- **`lib/tally/tallyDayStrip.ts`** (new, tested): from a card’s clocked spans (`TallyDaySuggestion.clockedJobs[].spans`), the holder’s schedule blocks that day and the day’s charges, the strip: 6 AM to 8 PM, widened to the hour around anything outside and never past the day’s midnight; each session a bar with a colour index (`--timeline-job-0…7`, one per job in clock-in order), each schedule block a hollow bar, each charge a tick with its verdict — `inside` a session, in `scheduled` hours never clocked, `before` the first clock-in, `between` two sessions, `after` the last clock-out, after an `open` clock-in on a past day, or `none` on a day off the clock — and the hour marks (*6 AM, 9, noon, 3, 6, 8 PM*). A session with no clock-out runs to now on today and to the strip’s end, drawn open, on a past day. The words (`tallyTickPlaceWords`) pass `plainWords`; the lead (`tallyStripLeadJobId`) is the session’s or block’s job, none for a gap; the switch’s key is per person (`tally_team_day_strip_<userId>`), `'1'` on, anything else off.
- **`components/tally/TallyDayStrip.tsx`** (new): the picture, theme tokens only. Amounts over the ticks (two rows when two swipes sit close), the hollow schedule lane, the bar lane with the job’s number on each bar and a dotted right edge on an open one, a now line on today, each tick a 22-px-wide press whose name reads *9:15 AM, $88.20, in JP101’s hours*, the hours under.
- **`TallyTeamDayCard`**: takes `strip` (null draws the card as before). With a strip: it sits under the header and above the sentence; each line gets a place chip, and each line carries the id a tick’s press scrolls to and lights for a moment; the picker leads as *What this touches* says.
- **`TallyTeamQueue`**: the *Day strip* button at the right of the *To sort / Sorted* row, `aria-pressed`, read from and written to `localStorage` under the person’s key; a map of strips per holder-day, built only while the switch is on. `TallyQueueScheduleRow` carries `time_start` / `time_end`, and the schedule read selects `time_end` beside the `time_start` it already ordered by.
- **Tests**: `TallyTeamQueue.sideBySide.render.test.tsx` (a dev with `?sbs=1` sees both drawings and one set of picks; without the parameter, or as an assistant, the page as it is); `tallyDayStrip.test.ts` (the axis, the widening, every verdict, the open session on today and on a past day, the label rows, the card adapter, the words against `plainWords`, the lead, the gap rounding, the switch’s key); `TallyTeamQueue.dayStrip.render.test.tsx` (off by default with the old picker order; on, the strip’s bars and ticks, the inside line’s chip and lead, the gap line’s words and untouched picker, the day chips and their *likely* mark unchanged, the chip’s press picking for one line, a tick’s press scrolling to and focusing its line, the switch remembered and turned off again). The old `TallyTeamQueue.render.test.tsx` passes unchanged, which is the point.
- **Guide** *sort the team’s card purchases from the office*: a section *See the day as a strip* names the switch.

## What this touches

When the switch is on, and only then:

- **It adds** a strip at the top of each day card and one place chip on each charge’s line. The chip’s words are the strip’s verdict for that swipe: *in JP101’s hours* · *in JP101’s scheduled hours, not clocked* · *30 min before clock-in* · *1.5 h between JP101 and JP202* · *3 h after clock-out* · *after clock-in on JP101, with no clock-out* · *off the clock all day*.
- **It replaces no chip words.** The day’s chips keep their job, their *likely* mark and their reason (*only job clocked that day*); a line’s own store and category chips stay; the sentence under the header stays, now under the strip. The mock-up’s *shape* drops the sentence and reads *in JP101’s hours* **instead of** *likely*; this PR draws both so they can be read against each other, and which words go is the owner’s next call, not this PR’s.
- **It changes the picker’s lead** on a line whose swipe sits inside a session’s bar or a schedule block’s hours: that job becomes the first option under *Pick a job*, and the place chip is a press that picks it for that line alone. On a line in a gap, before clock-in, after clock-out, after an open clock-in, or on a day off the clock, the picker’s order is as today and the chip is words only. Nothing arrives selected, as before.
- **It leaves alone** the sorter (`tallySortSuggestion.ts`: the rules, the chip order, each line’s `best`), the *likely* marks, what a day chip’s press selects (still every line on the card, the gap line included, which is exactly what the strip now shows the sorter), the writes (`replace_mercury_job_splits_for_linked_card_as_staff`, one call per charge, as before), Undo, the pay bar, *My card*, Sort mode and the holder’s phone.
- **Off**, which is the default for everyone until they press it, the only new thing on the page is the button.
- **It reads** one more column, `job_schedule_blocks.time_end`, on the read the queue already made.

## Side by side, for a dev

The owner asked to see every #217 prototype live beside today's look. A dev who opens `/tally?tab=transactions&sbs=1` sees each day card twice in one dashed frame: today's card under *Today*, the card with the strip under *With the strip*, on the same live day and the same picks (a chip pressed on one shows on both; each drawing has its own line ids, so a tick's press finds its own line). The *Day strip* switch stays what it is, per person and off by default; the comparison is the URL's. Anyone else, and any URL without `sbs`, sees the page as it is.

The frame and the hook are **`src/components/prototypes/SideBySide.tsx`**: `useShapeView(key)` (true for a dev when `?sbs=1`, `?sbs=all` or `?sbs=<key>`; `shapeViewAsked` in `lib/prototypes/shapeView.ts` is the pure part, tested) and `<SideBySide shapeLabel today shape>`. A sibling session on the rhythm-strips branch was writing the same helper under the same names; its branch was not pushed when this PR was cut, so the helper was written here with those names, and whichever PR lands second takes the first's file.

## What the mock-up asked for that this does not do, and why

- **Reusing `MyTimeDayClusterVisual`.** The My time day editor’s strip is a `role="slider"` with pointer handlers over a split-editor state, merge modals and a vertical layout; laying it sideways would have meant threading a split state through a card that has none. The strip here takes its shape (sessions on an hours axis, percent-of-span placement, the hour marks) and redraws it as a plain picture in 200 lines.
- **Dropping the sentence and the *likely* reason.** Kept, so the two can be compared on real days before anything goes.
- **The second pass’s schedule block** is drawn, and a swipe in scheduled hours the holder never clocked gets its own verdict and leads the picker, since *schedule-one-job* is the second most common sort.
