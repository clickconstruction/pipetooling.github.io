# v2.7039 — Pay calendar: a contract on their form names it, and opens it (2026-11-12)

Punch list #323, PR 7 of seven, stacked on v2.7037 and built before PR 6 because it stands alone. The card asked how a calendar gets entered without typing it thirty times, and one answer was the stored paper: the clause is read off the contract once, where the contract is recorded.

## What changed

- **A key term.** `THEIR_PAPER_TERM_KEYS` gains `pay_calendar` after `payment_contingency` (label *Pay calendar*, hint *in by the 25th, paid the 30th of the next month*). `key_terms` is a jsonb list read defensively, so no migration: an older record has no such term, and a reader that does not know the key drops it. The sheet, the record's read and the Legal desk's terms list all walk the key list, so the term shows wherever the others do.
- **The press.** `TheirPaperSheet` takes the job's payer (`theirPaperPayer` in `src/lib/jobs/theirPaperPayer.ts`: the rule every bill reads, `paySpeedPayer`, asked of the job with no bill in hand). With one, *Set <payer>'s pay calendar…* sits under the key terms and opens the Pay calendar window on that job, with the term's clause and words carried in (`initial`) and drawn above the sheet (`zIndex`: the sheet sits at 1300 or 1400, the window's usual layer is 1200). Both callers pass the payer: the contract strip and the Contract window.
- **The window** (`PayCalendarModal`): `initial` fills the clause and its words when nothing is stored for what the window opens on; a stored calendar wins. `zIndex` is 1200 unless the opener says otherwise.
- **Guide**: *From a contract on their form* in *set a payer's pay calendar*.

## Not changed

- The paper does not set a calendar by itself, and saving the paper writes nothing to `payer_pay_calendars`. A person picks the days.
- Reading the clause out of the file's text. The card's second pass dropped it: a pattern match on legal prose that is wrong once costs more trust than typing three numbers costs time.

## Is this the best we can do?

- **Two saves.** The calendar is saved in its window, then the paper in its sheet. A person who sets the days and closes the sheet without saving keeps the calendar and loses the clause on the paper. The two records are separate on purpose, and the sheet does not warn.
- **The jobs the paper covers.** A paper can cover several jobs through `covers_group_id`. The window offers a development's other jobs, not the paper's. For a paper that covers jobs outside one development, each is set from its own row.
- **The term is words, the calendar is numbers, and nothing checks one against the other.**
