# v2.4242 — Hours typed by hand are recorded as typed (2026-09-30)

The owner's ask, from a worker's text: *on Friday it didn't let me clock in — my hours were 10 AM to 9 PM.* The assistant can already type that day for him (any assistant can insert, edit and approve anyone's clock sessions), but nothing recorded that she had: a typed session looked the same as his punch, and she could approve it herself. The owner's calls, 2026-09-30: whoever typed the hours cannot approve them; nobody approves their own hours; the mark shows on every list hours are approved from. This PR is the database half, first of a train (punch list #70).

## What changed

- **A ledger of typed hours** — `clock_typed_entries`, written by two triggers on `clock_sessions` that compare a person's day before and after each transaction. `added`: time that the clock had not recorded. `trimmed`: punched time that stopped existing. Each row says who, when, the stretch, and the day's total before and after. Day-level on purpose — the split and replace RPCs delete and re-insert a day's rows, so a job split records nothing; a real clock-in or clock-out records nothing (an open session is given ten minutes of slack either way).
- **The hold** — `clock_session_approval_hold` and trigger `clock_sessions_guard_approval`: the typist cannot approve the session, and nobody approves their own. It is on the table, so it holds from any screen. `approve_clock_sessions` (rewritten from its live body) skips a held session instead of failing the batch; `approve_clock_sessions_v2` adds `held_own` / `held_typed`.
- **The second look** — another approver's approval confirms the typed entries under the session. Hours typed onto a session that is already approved stay approved (an in-place time change never dropped the approval, and un-approving inside a trigger would fight the six paths that keep `people_hours` in step), so those wait for `confirm_clock_typed_entry` — "Looks right" — from someone other than the typist.
- **Readers for the screens to come** — `clock_typed_stamps(ids)` and `list_typed_hours_waiting()`.
- **The switch** — `app_settings.typed_hours_second_look_v1`, seeded `test`: the hold applies to sample accounts and ZZ-named people only. The ledger runs in every mode.
- **A test bed** — `npm run test:pg:typed-hours` (`scripts/pgtest-typed-hours.sh`, `supabase/tests/typed_hours/`): 18 scenarios on a throwaway Postgres, by Docker or a local install (`PGTEST_PGBIN`).

## Not in this PR

Nothing a screen shows changes. The stamp on the nine approval views, the Needs You card, the worker's *it did not clock me in* door, the Hours grid stopping before it writes over clocked hours, and the dev switch in Settings follow — `to-dos/typed-hours-second-look/`.

## Known limits

- Hours typed onto an already-approved session count in pay before the second look (as they did before, unseen); the look is recorded, not enforced.
- A device clock more than ten minutes off makes a real punch read as typed by the person himself.
- `people_hours` can still be written directly from the Hours grid (a typed 0, or a roster name with no login); the grid stop is a later PR.
