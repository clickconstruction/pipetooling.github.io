# v2.5404 — Messages: the Lien desk's letters in a customer's thread, behind the scenes (2026-10-12)

Residential build, R8 · 7 (`to-dos/gc-mode/messages/RESIDENTIAL_BUILD_PLAN.md` on spike/gc-mode; the after state is card 7 of `customer-messages-integrations-mockups.html`). A kernel, `src/lib/messages/legalLines.ts`, pure, with no loader, no screen and no migration. It reads the Lien desk's rows and touches none of the desk's files.

- **Where the desk keeps its sends.** `job_lien_filings` holds a notice, a retainage notice, an affidavit and a release. Each filing's `sends` says who it went to (`owner` or `original_contractor`), how (`certified_mail`, `traceable_courier`, `email`, `hand`) and the day. A sent `job_lien_desk_items` row marked `letterTwo` names the filing that was the second letter, through `sent_filing_id`. `job_demand_letters` holds the demand letters, with their `sent_at` day and `sent_method`.
- **The mappers.** `legalSendsFromFilings(filings, { items, recipient })` and `legalSendsFromDemandLetters(letters)` turn those rows into `LegalSend`s. `recipient` picks the person's copy: the owner's for a residential customer, the original contractor's for a GC. A paper with no copy to them has no line. Two papers are the exceptions: an affidavit shows once it is served, and a release always shows. Voided rows are dropped. The time is the send's day. It is the recorded time when that falls on the same day, else noon of the day in the app's zone.
- **The lines.** `legalLines(sends, contactId)` gives `LegalLine = ThreadMessage & { legal: true; paper }`, oldest first. Each is channel `event`, direction `out`, who *the office*, id `legal:<filing|demand>:<id>`. The subject is the paper's name as the customer's timeline says it: *Notice of unpaid balance*, *Retainage notice*, *Lien affidavit*, *Lien release*, *Demand letter*, *Second letter*. The body is one sentence, *Sent by certified mail and email from the Lien desk.* An event already draws as its own grey row, and the stamps and the move line skip events, so a certified letter never reads as an email we sent. `thread.ts` is unchanged. The mockup's `·` between the parts became a subject and a sentence, since `plainWordsFailures` reads a `·` inside a sentence as glue.
- **The box.** `standingLegalLine(lines)` is the newest legal line on any job whose newest legal line is not a Lien release, so a release on one job leaves another job's notice standing. `boxStateAfter(lines, role)` keeps the box open for the office (`isLienOffice`: dev, master, assistant, controller). For everyone else under a standing line, the box is off with *Replies on a legal line go to the office. Open the Lien desk to answer.*
- **The reply.** `routeReply(inbound, lastLegal, officePick)` handles a reply from them that comes after a standing line. It returns R5's shape (`hasRule.ts`, #5561): `{ has: 'office', userId, name, reason: 'legal', why, write }`. With the office's pick, `write` is `has_user_id` with `has_how: 'rule'`. With no pick, `write` is all nulls, for whoever in the office answers first. A stored `legal` would widen `contacts_has_how_known`, so it is left out until a screen needs to tell the two apart. Null hands it back to the usual rule.
- **Tests** (`legalLines.test.ts`, 13): Dana Whitfield after a Notice of unpaid balance on job 1312. They cover the line, the GC's copy, the paper names and the second letter, a served affidavit and a release, a demand letter, and the plain words. The box is off for five field and bid roles and on for the four office roles, and it comes back after a release while another job's notice keeps it off. The reply goes to the office with and without a pick, and is left alone before the notice. Two pins were broken on purpose and each failed its test. Dropping the office check failed *is on for the office, the master included*. Routing a reply from before the notice failed *leaves the usual rule to decide before the notice*.
