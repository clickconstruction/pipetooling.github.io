# v2.8721 — Settings says what the labor burden factor is and where it reaches (2026-11-27)

No card. Client only. No migration, no edge function. Nothing owed after the merge. The number itself is not changed.

**Taunya's word, 2026-11-27.** Asked whether the 1.20 labor burden should stay, she said: "for the 1.2, leave it and verify the setting in dev settings is well define about where it reaches and that it is meant to represent depreciation on the tools and truck the persons use". So 1.20 stays, and from that day the factor stands for depreciation on the tools and the truck a person uses.

**What was found.** No screen set the factor and none showed it. `app_settings.labor_burden_factor_v1` is seeded at 1.20 by a migration, and screens that use it say only *burden factor* or *at ×1.2*. The only words on what it means were a code comment in `src/lib/appSettingsKeys.ts`, which read *taxes, insurance, the truck*. So there were no words to make plain. The managing session picked, as this thread proposed, a new read-only block. The pick is theirs and hers to flip.

**What is new.** Settings → Data & recovery → **Labor burden factor (dev)**, a folded block a developer alone sees. Opened, it reads the number from the database and says:

- the factor, as *×1.20*;
- what it is: the app multiplies a field wage by it wherever it prices a crew hour;
- what it stands for, in her words;
- one wage worked out from the number itself: *A wage of $30.00 an hour counts as $36.00 an hour.*;
- that it is not the office's overhead, which the overhead pool counts by itself;
- where it reaches, six lines, and where it does not, five lines;
- that no press there changes it.

**It has no editor.** The block holds one press, its own fold, and no field. Changing 1.20 stays a write in the database on her own yes: one press would move every bid's crew rate and every score at once.

**Each line was checked against the code before it was written.**

| The block says it reaches | Confirmed in |
|---|---|
| A bid's crew rate | `src/lib/bids/crewRate.ts`: the company rate is the 90-day field wage times the factor |
| A bid's price | `src/pages/Bids.tsx`: Pricing's labor rate is the bid's own, else that company rate |
| A job's labor budget | `JobBudgetCard.tsx`: typed hours are priced at the company rate |
| The superintendents' scoreboard | the database's `superintendent_scores`, which reads the factor |
| Team scores, the field score | `src/lib/scorecard/fieldScore.ts`: the hours' wages times the factor |
| Team scores, the estimator's score | `src/lib/scorecard/scorecardReads.ts`: a finished job's team labor at the factor |

It does not reach a job's burn, Job Summary, Crew P&L, the pay stubs or the overhead pool: none of the kernels named `jobBurn`, `jobSummary`, `crewPnl`, `payStub`, `generatePayStub`, `overheadPool`, `overheadLens`, `jobCostsVerdict` or `jobDayLedger` reads the factor or the company rate.

**One line is narrower than the list this thread was handed.** That list said the factor does not reach a job's Costs tab. The Job Budget card on that tab prices its hours at the company rate, so the factor does reach the budget there. What it does not reach is what the job has cost so far, and the block says that.

**The code comment is mended** to her meaning, dated, and it says what it read before.
