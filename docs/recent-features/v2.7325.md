# v2.7325 — People → Review → Scores: the field and the estimators, two numbers against one hurdle (2026-11-15)

Punch list #360, PR 1. A prompt from outside the company proposed four team scores for a plumbing shop; the owner asked for the parts worth keeping, then for a design spike. Kept: one frozen formula per number, gross profit on every paid field hour against a hurdle, the estimator's number corrected to the actual when the job closes, no vanity cards, and a flag on hours with no bill. Left out: a dispatch score, a burden split into tax, comp and benefits, last year's overhead as a constant, and a second backlog number. The spike ran four passes on live data in one day and ships as this PR. Client only: no migration, no edge function.

## The tab

People → Review → **Scores** (`/people?tab=scores`), the scoreboard's gate (`canSeeScoreboardTab`: a dev, the owner, anyone with payroll access), since the read carries wages. A window of 4 weeks, 8 weeks or 90 days; the hurdle is built from the same window.

- **Field** (the crew): gross profit ÷ paid field hours, against the hurdle. Gross profit is what the window's hours earned of their jobs' contracts (`earnedRevenueInWindow`, Job Summary's and the Bridge's rule) less those hours' wages at the burden factor (`labor_burden_factor_v1`), the materials and the subs dated in the window. Every paid field hour on a job counts: an overrun hurts in the labor and in the hours. The drill-down is one row per job with the same arithmetic; a job with hours and no bill is flagged with *Open the job to bill it*, and the job name opens the job window. Above the jobs, the score cut by who runs them (the scoreboard's own mapping: the project's superintendents, else the GC project's one; a shared job splits evenly; jobs no superintendent runs fold into one line).
- **Hurdle**: the window's overhead pool ÷ its paid field hours (break-even) plus the target profit per hour (`app_settings.scorecard_hurdle_v1`, `{ targetProfitPerHourUsd }`, default $25). A dev sets the target on the tab; the write rides the devs' app_settings policy, and the owner's own policy on the key is PR 3.
- **Estimating & sales** (one shared number): a bid counts its priced gross profit (`bids.priced_revenue_usd − priced_cost_usd`, frozen at send) at award when that profit over the award's labor hours (`job_budgets.awarded_labor_hours`, else the bid's own `bid_estimate_breakdown` total) clears the hurdle. Awarded under the hurdle it counts zero and stays there, whatever the crew does later. Once the job is billed or paid the actual gross profit (revenue − team labor at the burden − subs − parts, through `loadJobChargesTimelineInputs`) replaces the estimate, on the day of the job's first Billed move in `job_status_events`. The period's number is a flow: what award added in the period plus how far the jobs that closed in the period moved their bids. The estimator and the account manager each carry every bid they share. Drill-down: awarded above the hurdle, closed in the period, awarded counting zero with the reason, and priced proposals out with their days out; proposals out past 120 days are a count, the Bid Board's to close.
- **A bid that cannot be judged** is held with the words an estimator can act on: no priced stamp; labor not estimated (hours on fewer than nine rows in ten, the breakdown's own bar, the rate aside since the company crew rate stands in); rows uncosted at send. *Finish these before they can score* lists them with a door to each bid's Labor tab.
- **The ramp**: until the first judged bid is awarded, the estimating card's figure is *N of M*, the priced bids in play that can be judged, and the words say what starts the score. It retires itself the day a judged bid is awarded, over the hurdle or under it.

## What the live data said

Field, 56 days: $47/h on 1,817 paid hours against a $37/h hurdle ($12/h break-even + $25/h target); 81 jobs, 9 with hours and no bill, all real unbilled work (a lift-station site visit at 139 hours, a concrete cut and pour, a line repair, a walkthrough, five small ones). Estimating: *0 of 20*. No bid ever awarded carries a priced stamp, every stamped bid's labor estimate reads unusable, 24 of 25 have uncosted rows, and the 12 finished bid-linked jobs have neither a stamp nor award hours, so history cannot be backfilled. The score's first effect is on the estimating desk: fill the Labor tab before a bid goes out. None of the window's 60 clocked jobs has a project on record, so the superintendent cut reads one line until projects get superintendents.
