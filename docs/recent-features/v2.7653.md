# v2.7653 — Trips: the rules for a trip to a site away from home (2026-11-17)

Punch list #379, PR 1 of nine: the rules alone. A pure kernel and its tests. No screen, no migration and no edge function. Nothing here reads or writes a row.

## Why

On a site away from home the hours on its jobs do not say what the owner asks. Some hours are a crew at the site, some are a truck on the road, some are a person at home loading what the trip needs. Until now the one way to set a trip apart was to make a second job for it. The trip record makes a trip its own thing on the development, and reads the clock through it. This PR is the reading.

## What landed

`src/lib/trips/`, four kernels:

- `tripDays.ts`. A trip is people, and for each person their days. A day is `ready`, `road` or `site` (*Getting ready*, *On the road*, *At the site*), and a day off in the middle is a day that is not there. `nextDayKind` is the one tap on a painted day: the site, the road, getting ready, off, and round. `tripDayKind` reads a day over everyone on the trip (the site when anyone was, else the road, else getting ready), which is the day a strip draws and *days at the site* counts. `tripStanding` reads *Planned*, *Out now* or *Back* from the days and today. `tripDayOfPerson` finds the trip a person was on that day, and a day two trips claim goes to the one that went out first, with `overlappingTripDays` naming the clash.
- `tripPlace.ts`. `placeOfPunch` reads one punch against the site's point: `site` when a place read from the phone is within two miles, `elsewhere` when the phone gave a place and none is the site, `none` when the punch carries no place. A place guessed from the network counts as the site within twenty-five miles and proves nothing past that, so it reads `none`. With no point for the site nothing can be checked. `tripsLookShows` is the rule for the look: a site with a trip, a site 150 miles or more from the shop (`TRIP_FAR_MILES_DEFAULT`, the owner's call 1), or a site a person turned trips on for.
- `tripHours.ts`. `splitTripHours` gives each hour one of five figures: *At the site*, *On the road*, *Getting ready*, *No place on the punch*, *At home for the site*. A row on a trip day takes the day's kind. On a day painted *At the site* a punch somewhere else counts *On the road*. A row with no place is *No place on the punch* whatever the day was painted, and is never added to *At the site*. A row outside every trip the person was on, a day off in the middle included, is *At home for the site*. The figures add up to the hours handed in, by site, by trip, by person and by job.
- `tripFinder.ts`. `findTripsNotMade` is the finder behind *Looks like a trip*: runs of punches at the site that no trip covers, a weekend allowed inside a run, people whose days run together as one offer. It passes over days somebody said were *Not a trip*. `tripPeopleFromSuggestion` fills *Make it a trip* from the punches, and `dismissalsForSuggestion` is what *Not a trip* remembers.

## Two rules the card left open, and how they were settled

- **A punch somewhere else on an *At the site* day.** The card says such a punch is not at the site and does not say what it is. It counts *On the road*: the person is out on the trip and not at the site. The five figures stay five.
- **A place guessed from the network.** The clock falls back to a network guess when the phone will not give a place. It is good to a town at best, so it cannot tell the site from the motel. It counts for the site within twenty-five miles, on a day somebody painted *At the site*, and reads as no place past that.

## What a trip never does

A trip is a reading laid over the clock. Nothing in this kernel changes a clock row, a bill or a cost, and nothing later in the train will.
