## What was wrong

`groupLienNextUp` ordered Needs you now by the day and the row's key, so rows sharing a day fell in no order that meant anything: on prod data, 2026-11-15, nineteen of twenty shared Nov 16. The Board's first tile said *8 will not make tomorrow as they stand* and nothing in the list said which eight. The approvals the office waits on closed the list, under Coming up, with one press each, *Open*.

## What landed

- `src/lib/jobs/lienBoard.ts`: the at-risk rule the tile and the chips already used is shared with the list. `lienBoardRiskPapers(board)` is the open at-risk chips by `kind:jobId` with their `stepsLeft`; `lienBoardRowRisk(papers, row)` reads a To do row's own paper (a run row takes the longest road among its notices; a reminder, a served copy, a returned envelope and retainage are never at risk); `lienBoardRiskPhrase` is the one wording (*will not make tomorrow as they stand*); `lienBoardRiskPick` is the red line as a pick (`papers`, `words`, no bin lit), and `lienBoardRowInPick` / `lienBoardPickWords` honour it. The day tile carries `lineDestination: { kind: 'risk' }` while a paper is at risk.
- `src/lib/jobs/lienNextUp.ts`: `groupLienNextUp(rows, { waitingOn, risk, riskLabel, amount })` returns `risk` (the now rows at risk, most stages left first), `waiting`, then `now` as *On track*, each marked `part` when there is something to put above the rest; `lienNextUpNowHead` is the head over the parts (*Needs you now · 20*, *and 3 waiting on Malachi*). Inside a day the kinds stay together and the largest comes first. With nothing at risk and nothing waiting the one group stands as before. The waiting group now always sits above On track, Reminders and Coming up.
- `src/lib/jobs/lienPhoneRows.ts`: a phone's days come in two pools. The rows at risk and on track are one run of days with the rows at risk first in each; the waiting rows follow; the days coming up stand where Coming up stood.
- `src/lib/jobs/lienLeaderReminder.ts`: `lienReminderPlan` (a notice is a message each, the affidavits one for the lot, a retainage notice and a paper with no item held with the reason), `lienReminderWords` (the press, the confirm window, dead once sent this session) and `lienReminderOutcome`.
- `LienDeskNextUp.tsx` draws the head and the parts (`data-lien-next-up-now-head`, `data-part`, an `h4` a part) and the press on the waiting head (`data-lien-next-up-remind`; on a phone a row under the head, a thumb tall). `LienBoard.tsx` draws the day tile's red line as a press when the desk hands it `onTile`. `lienDoNowBar.ts` adds the count at risk to the pinned bar with the same press.
- `LienDesk.tsx`: `riskPapers`, `rowRisk`, `riskLabel`; `phoneAmountFor` is built on a computer too, so the list has the amounts to order by; `boardRisk` beside `boardBins` for the pick; `remindLeader`, which asks, sends each notice's message through `tellLeaderOfLienApproval` and the affidavits' through `tellLeaderOfLienAffidavits`, keeps going past one that fails and names it, and remembers the send for the session. The office only; the leader's own rows are his to approve.
- The Board guide gains *Needs you now, in three parts*.

## What "is this the best we can do" changed

The card said *ready before held*. That put the papers one press from done on top and the ones that need a fact last, where they run out of time. The order is turned around, and it is the Board's own rule rather than a second one: the stages a paper has left against the working days left. So the tile's count, the marked chips and the list's first rows can never disagree.

The reminder press could have sent on one press. It sends a message to a person, so it asks first with what goes and to whom, and it goes dead with the time once sent, so the same reminder is not sent twice by a second look at the list.
