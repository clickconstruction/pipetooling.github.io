# v2.4098 — Contracts & terms: read any contract as the customer sees it, with sample information (2026-09-28)

Client, plus one edge function (`get-job-contract`, deployed 2026-09-28 before the merge; the change is backward compatible). No migration. The owner, 2026-09-28: *I would like for a user to be able to see a contract template in a modal and be able to read it with sample information.* Plan, mock-up, critique and the better plan on the Design canvas artifact `EQ5hcH6ahKiXwWtzUwPpBT`.

## Why

Settings → Contracts & terms (v2.3977) shows each contract's wording on its card, cut at 200 px with *Show all*, and the templates as documents lived elsewhere: the Contract Book's preview opens a new tab, the sweep's pane is per job. Nothing in Settings opened a contract in a modal, and nothing read it with sample data.

## The two plans

**Plan A** (drawn first): a *Read it* button on the card opens the wording in a generic document frame — letterhead, *Prepared for [CUSTOMER NAME]*, the paragraphs, signature lines. **Why not:** the frame is invented. A checkbox sentence, a bid's exclusions and a notice to an owner do not live on a service agreement, so the reader would show a document no customer gets, with sample data written a second time.

**Plan B** (built): the app already renders every customer page with one sample fixture — Settings → What customers see iframes `/contract/sign?t=sample`, `/estimate/accept?t=sample`, `/bid-room?t=sample`, the sample emails and the paper builders (`paperSamples.ts`). The reader shows *that*: the customer's own page in sample mode, in a modal, with the card's wording found and lit on it.

## Decided here

- **One sample, not two.** The reader shows the page the customer gets, from the fixture the edge functions already lay over today's Settings; no second sample story to keep in step.
- **Cards seen nowhere have no button** (the bid letter's closing and fixed block, the bid's lien-release form): there is no rendered surface to show yet. The card still prints its wording.
- **The wording is found by its sentences, not marked by the page.** No public page changes. The reader searches for the first three real sentences (unnumbered, a short label before a colon dropped) and each long sentence's tail — a page may swap the noun near its start (*this estimate* for *this agreement*) — plus the sentences the catalog pins to page source. When none is on the page, the reader says so and, with more than one page, points at the chips.
- **The page's DOM is never changed.** The first cut wrapped the phrase in a `<mark>`; React owns the text nodes on a live page, and the estimate terms page threw on its next render and went blank. A short block is lit by inline style; inside a long block the phrase is painted with the CSS Highlight API (a `Highlight` on a `Range`, `::highlight(contract-reader)`), which draws without touching the tree; a browser without it lights the block.
