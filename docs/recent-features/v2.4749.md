# v2.4749 — Legal portal: the Lien grid reads better (2026-10-07)

Client only; no migration, no edge function. The owner (2026-10-06), on a screenshot of the firm's Lien grid: the address under the job name and number; *Com* and *Res* spelled out, with *Homestead* or *No homestead* on the line beneath; the unpaid total on the first line and the months under it; the missed months left out of the § 53.056 column; and the GC search *much more attractive*, showing each GC's job count and total, extending to the bottom of the page if needed. Mockup: `to-dos/legal-portal-firm-ready/mockup-lien-grid-reads.html` (the owner's screenshot beside it).

## What changed

- **[`legalLienGridView.ts`](../../src/lib/legal/legalLienGridView.ts)**, a new kernel over `lienGridRows`: `legalLienGridCells` folds each book row into the screen's cells (job, address, owner, kind words, homestead words, last on site, the total, the unpaid months, the live § 53.056 notices, the four hand-filled facts); `legalLienGridGcs` builds the rail; `filterLegalLienGrid` adds the no-GC choice; `findLegalLienGcs` filters the rail by name. The memo's twelve-column print (`lienGridHtml`) is untouched.
- **The address under the job.** The Job cell is the name in bold with the address in muted text beneath; the Address column is gone. Eleven columns on screen, twelve on paper.
- **Property in words.** *Commercial*; *Residential* with *Homestead* or *No homestead* under it. A kind the office has not entered reads *Commercial* followed by the red `?`, with the reason on hover, since commercial dates are what the grid shows for it. The column is headed *Property*.
- **The total first.** *$38,625* in bold, the months *Jun, Jul, Aug, Sep* in muted text under it, wrapping as they need. Headed *Unpaid · months*.
- **§ 53.056 without the closed windows.** One line per live month, *Jul: Oct 15*, *Aug: Nov 16*; a sent month reads *Jun: Aug 27 · sent* in green. A month whose window closed, or that was skipped on purpose, is left off. A job whose every window has closed shows a muted dash with *Every month's § 53.056 window has closed* on hover, never the red `?` (the timeline folds such a job's months into one step with no month, so before this the old grid drew a `?` there, which reads as a fact not entered).
- **The rail.** `All GCs · 19 jobs · $123,716` leads, then every GC with rows in view, largest open balance first, each with its count and dollars, then *No GC · with the owner* for jobs contracted with the owner, which the select never offered. The counts and dollars follow the *Something due / All* switch, so they foot with the grid beside them; the grid's own line names the chosen GC. A chosen GC whose rows leave the view stays listed at *0 jobs* so the choice can be undone. The rail stretches to the grid's height and sticks while the grid scrolls; past ten entries a *Find a GC* box filters by name, keeping *All* and the chosen one.
- **Something due / All** is a two-button switch in place of the select.
- **A phone** (≤ 720 px, `src/index.css`) draws the rail as a row of chips, name and dollars, above the table; the table scrolls sideways inside the card as before (#85 item 8).

## Is this the best we can do?

- **The address under the job, not a column.** The job cell is the row's name; the address is where it is. One cell, two lines, and the table loses its widest column. The print keeps the memo's columns, which counsel asked for by name.
- **Missed months left off, not struck through.** A closed window is nothing counsel can act on. The row still lists the month as unpaid, so nothing about the money is hidden; only the dead deadline goes.
- **A rail, not a select.** Every GC is in view with its dollars before the firm clicks, and a GC's count and total are the first two things counsel asks. Largest first follows item 11's rule for the matters.
- **Not done: sorting the grid by the rail's order, or a per-GC print.** The grid keeps the book's order (next date, then balance); *Print the grid* prints what is in view, so a per-GC print is one click on the rail then the button.
