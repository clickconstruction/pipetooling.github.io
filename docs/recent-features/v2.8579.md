# v2.8579 — GC card bills: the card page is due on the day the contract names, to the end of that day (2026-11-25)

Punch list #343, the finding beside its three faults: `gc-card-bill`, the only other maker of a Stripe bill, still sent a count of days. The owner's answer of 2026-11-25 was *Fix it now*. One edge function. No migration, no client change.

**After the merge:** `bash scripts/deploy-functions.sh gc-card-bill` from `main`, then `npm run check:edge-drift`. That is the one function owed. Until the deploy the function makes a card bill as it did, and nothing reaches it: the switch `gc_card_bill_on_v1` read `false` on prod on 2026-11-25 and `gc_owner_card_bills` held no row.

## What was wrong

The portal door worked out the due day right: the certificate's day plus the contract's days to pay when that is ahead, else the company's today (`gcCardBillDue`). It then sent Stripe `days_until_due`, the whole days from today to that day, and Stripe adds a count to the minute the bill is made.

- **A card bill due today was sent as due in 0 days.** Its due instant was the second it was made, so it stood past due from then. Whether Stripe takes a count of 0 was never seen.
- **An evening press landed on the next day read in UTC.** After about 6 PM Central the minute of the press is on the next UTC day, and so is that minute moved on by whole days.
- A press within the hour either side of a clock change could land a day off in the company's zone too.

It is a smaller cousin of the fault v2.8551 mended in `create-stripe-invoice`: there the count was never under 1 and rounded from noon UTC, so a bill due today came out due tomorrow at any hour. Here the count could be 0 and had no rounding.

## What changed

`supabase/functions/gc-card-bill/index.ts` sends **`due_date`**, one instant on the due day, from the kernel v2.8551 added, `supabase/functions/_shared/stripeBillDueDate.ts`:

1. `stripeBillDue(dueYmd, nowMs)` gives 23:59:59 UTC of the due day when that is at least ten minutes ahead. Else the last second of the day in the company's zone. Else, in the last ten minutes of a day, a count of one day.
2. One read of the clock (`nowMs`) gives the company's today, the bill's number and the due instant, so the three cannot disagree across midnight.
3. **A refusal of the due day never stops the card page.** If Stripe refuses `due_date` (`isStripeDueDateRefusal`), the bill is made on `stripeBillDueFallbackDays`, a count of civil days in the company's zone and never under 1, and the function logs a line that starts `gc-card-bill: Stripe refused due_date`. The refused call made nothing, so the number is still free. Any other failure goes on as before: the half-made invoice is voided and the pending row cleared.

**The shared kernel `_shared/gcCardBill.ts` is not edited.** Five functions import it (`gc-card-bill`, `create-stripe-invoice`, `customer-portal`, `gc-customer-email`, `gc-office-notices`, the last also through `customerSampleFixtures.ts`), and an edit would have put each behind. `gcCardBillDue` still answers `daysUntilDue`, which no function sends now. `stripeBillDueDate.ts` is not edited either.

## Tests

`src/lib/gc/gcCardBill.test.ts`, *the due day Stripe is given*:

- the old count on a bill due today: due the second it was made, and the next day in UTC for an evening press;
- a card bill due today, pressed in the morning and in the evening: the end of today in the company's zone, at least ten minutes ahead;
- a contract day that is ahead: that day in the company's zone and in UTC, at 3 AM, 9 AM and 7:24 PM Central;
- five minutes from midnight: one day on, never 0 days;
- the function's source: one read of the clock, the kernel's answer sent, the fallback on a refusal, and no count from `gcCardBillDue`.
