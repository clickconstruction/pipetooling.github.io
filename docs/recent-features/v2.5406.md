# v2.5406 — Messages: the rules behind reading a voicemail, behind the scenes (2026-10-12)

Residential Messages R8 · 4 (`to-dos/gc-mode/messages/RESIDENTIAL_BUILD_PLAN.md` on `spike/gc-mode`, card 4 of `customer-messages-integrations-mockups.html` v0.2): the kernel that reads a voicemail on the office line. It is a new file, `src/lib/messages/voicemailRead.ts`, with its test. No screen and no schema. `owed.ts`, `quo/events.ts` and `followUp/queue.ts` are untouched. `docs/GLOSSARY.md` → *Messages (GC mode, the build)* gains the clause.

- **The line** (`voicemailLine(message)`): Quo's transcript in quotes is the line's words, with Quo's summary on the hover and the length from `callLength` ("0:41 · Quo’s transcript"). While Quo has sent no transcript, the line is "Voicemail · 0:41" alone, and the meta says *No transcript yet. Hear it in Quo.* The message is the thread's `ThreadMessage` fields with cut 5's `transcript` and `summary` from `QuoLine`, since `ThreadMessage` does not carry those two.
- **The day heard** (`heardDay(message, now)`): cut 2's `readDay` reads a day box's words whole, so over a sentence it finds nothing. This file tries it on every run of one to five words, one sentence at a time, and the first day said wins, the longer run first at one place. It writes no day rule of its own. It reads from the voicemail's own day, so "tomorrow" on a Tuesday voicemail is Wednesday whenever the office plays it, and `now` only marks a day that has gone by. Two guards:
  - A bare three-letter weekday ("I sat") is a word, not a day.
  - The voicemail's own day ("calling today") stands only when no other day is said.
- **The time** (`heardTime`): a time after *after*, *before*, *by*, *around*, *about* or *at* rides on the chip. An hour with no AM or PM reads as a working day's: one to six is PM, seven to eleven is AM, and twelve is noon. So "Thursday after two" is **Thu Oct 8, after 2 PM**. A count is not a time ("at one point", "about two weeks"), and neither is a year.
- **The presses**: **Keep the day** and **Another day**. Keep the day returns cut 2's offered `message_promises` row for the line (`heardDayWrite`: `message_id`, `state 'offered'`, `kind` null), with the day in `dayKept`'s shape. The office then says what the day is a promise of on Follow up, as for any day heard (`promises.ts`). The row holds a date or words, never both (`message_promises_promise`), so the time stays on the chip and is not saved. Another day writes the same row with the day picked from `dayChips`. No RPC writes an offered row yet: the migration leaves that to the readers, so the insert is the caller's.
- **Follow up** (`voicemailNeedsReply`): a voicemail from them opens the conversation as a text does. That is `stateMoveOf` on main, the same rule `messages_move_state` runs in SQL, and `needsReply` adds *Call back in Quo*. This is a pin, not a second rule.
- **Tests** run over Dana's voicemail at Tue Oct 6, 2:14 PM Chicago (made up): the line, the hover, the bare line with no transcript, the chip **Thu Oct 8, after 2 PM**, the write, and "tomorrow" read from the voicemail's day three days later. They also pin "at two" and "after two" as PM and "seven" as AM, the guards, and Dana on Needs a reply with *Call back in Quo*. Reading from the day it is played fails 1 of the 15 tests, and reading one to six as AM fails 4.
