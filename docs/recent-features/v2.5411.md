# v2.5411 — Messages: a quick reply for customers knows their two flows, behind the scenes (2026-10-12)

Residential R3, the `stepsByFlow` kernel (`to-dos/gc-mode/messages/RESIDENTIAL_BUILD_PLAN.md` → R3 on `spike/gc-mode`), by Customer communication helper 10, directed by the lead (Docs review and customer handoff). It rebuilds the v2.5411 branch first written on another Mac and never pushed. It stacks on R4's facts and seed (#5568), which stacks on R0 (#5565). The rules are the residential prototype's (`customer-messages-prototype.html` v0.8: `FLOWS`, `stepName`, `stepOptions`, the strip's `groups` and the editor's `grouped`). No migration, no edge change and no screen.

- **Steps per flow** (`src/lib/quickReplies/kernel.ts`).
  - `stepsByFlow(flows)` keeps each kind's flows apart, each with its name and steps. A customer has two: the residential flow, then the GC customer's. A flow with no steps is left out.
  - `FLOW_NAMES` holds the prototype's names: Residential customer, GC customer, Trade partner, Architect and Sub.
  - Each `StepRef` now carries its `flow`.
  - `stepsByKind` is `stepsByFlow` flattened, one list per kind. Its signature is unchanged, so GC Review's cut 3 screens (#5500, #5502, #5515) build as they are. A line for customers now finds the residential steps too.
- **A step named across flows**: `qrStepName` is the old private `stepName`, exported. It looks on every flow of the line's kind, so `qrTagWords`, `editorGroups` and `versionWords` name a residential step.
- **One flow per step id**: a quick reply keeps only its step's id (`quick_replies.step_key`, with no flow column). The new pin test holds that no two flows of one kind share an id.
- **The editor's groups** (`editorGroups`): a kind with two flows runs flow by flow and names the flow in each step's title, as in "Customers · Residential customer · Booked". A kind with one flow, or steps passed in with no flow, reads as before.
- **The strip** (`qrGroups`): a new optional `flow` input. With it, a customer's strip leaves out the lines on the other flow's steps, and Find a reply keeps them. Without it, the strip is unchanged.
- **The Spanish and the phone in the history**: `QuickReplyChange` gains `es` and `phone`, and `QuickReplyVersion` gains `fromPhoneOrder` and `toPhoneOrder`. These match R3's SQL (#5588, `quick_reply_versions`). `versionWords` reads them:
  - `es`: wrote the Spanish, changed the Spanish, or cleared the Spanish, with the words struck and added.
  - `phone`: put it on the phone, moved it on the phone, or took it off the phone, with "place 2" struck or added. A change to the name alone reads "renamed it on the phone".
- **Not here**: `editQuickReply` writing the `es` and `phone` versions waits for #5558's `PhoneLine`. It lands in this PR if #5558 is on main first, otherwise in R3's screens PR. The residential guesses in `qrGuess` (on our way, what we found, the bill) are left for the screens.
- **Tests** (`kernel.test.ts`), over the prototype's lines for Dana, a residential customer on Working:
  - the two customer flows and their names;
  - the one-flow-per-id pin;
  - `stepsByKind` as the flattening;
  - a step named on either flow;
  - the strip with and without `flow`, and Find a reply;
  - the editor's groups flow by flow, and with one flow;
  - each `es` and `phone` history row.
  - The customer step list and the editor's GC customer titles were updated for the second flow.
