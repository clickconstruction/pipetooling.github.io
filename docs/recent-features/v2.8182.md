## Taunya's four answers, 2026-11-21

Step 4 waited on the look. Asked which calls it truly stood on, she answered four:

1. **The job's page is the whole walked flow**, with *What puts a person in breach* and *What the site must have* on every topic. The Contract Book holds those lines first, so that is built before the page.
2. **The flow first, the list after.** The flow sets a job's settings the first time. One page of sliders makes a later change.
3. **The disclaimer's box is ticked once for a paper on a job**, before it is first required.
4. **After the first signature, any change**, tighter or looser, as a new version everyone signs again.

Answers 3 and 4 change what the database holds, and answer 1 needs the Book to take new lines. That is this PR.

## What changed

- **Any change after a signature.** `job_paper_set_settings` no longer refuses a looser change, or one that tightens one setting and loosens another. It is a new row that everyone signs again, as a stricter one was. Step 3 (v2.8145) had held it to stricter only.
- **The basis and the tick on the job's row.** `basis` is the level the office picked before walking the topics. It marks the page and is never part of the paper, so `job_paper_set_basis` moves it in place and nobody signs again. `notice_read_by` and `notice_read_at` keep who ticked the notice's box and when.
- **A notice must be ticked to require.** A paper whose settings list opens on a notice is required only with its box ticked. A list with no notice asks for nothing.
- **The Book's lines.** A choice of a setting may carry `breach` and `site` lines, and the list a `notice` and a `siteNote`. The database stops a shape that is not plain lines.
- **The kernel** (`jobPaperSettings.ts`): `settingsChangePlan` answers *revised* for a looser or a mixed change after a signature, with which way it went.

## What it does not do yet

- The Contract Book's screens do not take the new lines. That is the next part.
- No page sets a job's settings. That is the part after it.
