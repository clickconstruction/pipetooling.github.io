# v2.8262 — Sub papers: the papers past twelve get their own email, and a failed read draws no sheet as unpaid (2026-11-21)

Client only: no migration, no edge function. Punch list #350. The three lines the audit of 2026-11-20 left open under *Still open* and *The two rough edges*, built on the owner's word of 2026-11-21 (*Please do*).

## 1. Papers past 12

**Before.** One email carries 12 papers, the function's own limit. A statement of account sent the first 11 open work statements with it and a yellow line named the rest. Nothing sent the rest: a second statement attached the same first 11 again, so the office opened each held sheet's work statement and sent it alone.

**Now.**

- `subPaperSendParts(lead, attached, max)` in `subPaperSend.ts` cuts a pick into the emails it takes: the paper the window is on leads the first, each carries the limit at most, in the order given. The first email is what `subPaperSendSet` says rides.
- The send step holds which email it is on (`email`, from 0) and sends one email at a time. `subPaperSendPlan` takes `part: { n, of }` and answers `next`, the press that opens the email the held papers go in, with the count that email carries: *Open the next email, 3 papers*.
- Before the email went, that press sits in the yellow line as a link and says *This email is not sent by that press.* So a window opened again later reaches the held papers without sending the statement twice. Once the email went, the press leads the foot of the step and **Done** sits beside it.
- A later email reads *More papers from <company>, email 2 of 3* and *Here are 3 more papers from us. They did not fit in our last email.* `subPaperSendSubject` takes the part, and the connected window hands it `plan.part` when it builds the subject again from the numbered lead.
- *Back to the paper* starts the send over at its first email. The last press's line belongs to its email too (`subPaperPlace` takes the email), so *Sent 12 papers* does not stand over the next three.

Each email registers its own papers as a set, as one email always did. A held sheet that has no number yet is still named by its job on the statement of account in the first email: it takes its number when its own email is made.

## 2. Held papers of one job read alike

A held work statement with no number was named *the work statement for #3012 Cedar Hollow Clinic*, so two sheets of one job read the same. `paperName` adds the sheet's day: *… of Oct 12, 2026*. A file's name is not changed: a file is named for its number once the paper is made.

## 3. The ledger and a read that fails

**Before.** `useSubLaborLedger` read the sheets, then each sheet's lines and payments, and ignored a refusal of either. A failed payments read drew every sheet with no payments, so every sheet read unpaid. The papers stand on the same rows, so a statement of account made then would have said so under a number. Seen in the code, not on prod.

**Now.** `subLedgerReadFailure({ lines, payments })` in `src/lib/subWorkOrders/subLedgerRead.ts` words a read that must not be drawn. The hook stops there: it keeps what it last read whole, and hands the page the words. *The sub sheets were not read again: their payments did not load, and without them every sheet would read unpaid. <reason>. Reload the page to try again.* On a first read nothing is drawn. The Pay view and People → Subs both read through this hook, so the papers window on either stands on whole rows or on none.

The payments read keeps its chain of older column lists: only a refusal of the last one is a failure.
