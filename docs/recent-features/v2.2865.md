# v2.2865 — Work Orders: "Needs a work order" derived from the sheets (2026-09-05)

PR 1 of the [`to-dos/work-orders-one-row-spine.md`](../../to-dos/work-orders-one-row-spine.md) train — the two derivation bugs the owner found comparing Jobs → Work Orders with Jobs → Sub Labor, plus the crew-pay exclusion the one-row spine needs.

## The bugs (verified live 2026-09-05, before)

1. **The list read `people_labor_jobs.paid_at`, which nothing sets.** Every sheet counted as unpaid, so job 892's three fully paid Miguel Rodriguez sheets sat in "Needs a work order · 20 jobs".
2. **It only saw jobs in the Pipeline cache.** `jobsNeedingWorkOrder(jobs, …)` walked the Jobs page's `jobs` list, so sheets whose `job_number` has no `jobs_ledger` row (977 Springtown, $40,000 open) never appeared.

## The rule (after) — one kernel

[`src/lib/subWorkOrders/sheetsNeedingWorkOrder.ts`](../../src/lib/subWorkOrders/sheetsNeedingWorkOrder.ts) — `sheetsNeedingWorkOrder({ sheets, assigneesBySheetId, roster, commitments, jobs, todayYmd })`, 17 tests. A sheet needs a work order when all three hold:

- **It is a roster-sub sheet.** Every assignee (junction `people_labor_job_assignees` first; the `' | '`-delimited `assigned_to_name` for legacy sheets) resolves to a `people` row of kind `sub` that has **no login, or a login whose role is `subcontractor`** (`isRosterSub`). Live finding that shaped this: teammates carry `kind = 'sub'` too (Abraham is `sub` + a superintendent login; Michael A is `sub` + a subcontractor login), so `kind` alone cannot tell crew pay from a sub — the account role can. A sheet with any teammate on it is crew pay and never counts (1004 Kane "Abraham | Misses Taunya TESTING", 273 "Malachi | Abraham | Michael A | Behar Kraja", 931, 523). Unresolved names do not count.
- **Money is open on it**, `subLaborJobBalance` (items minus payments minus back-charges) `> 0`, **or it is unpriced** with nothing paid — a sheet that exists with no agreement behind it (shown as "unpriced").
- **Nothing live or signed covers it**: `buildJobWorkOrderCoverage` over the orders anchored to the sheet (`labor_job_id`) plus those anchored to its Pipeline job (`job_id`); `none` or `declined` means it needs one.

Rows carry `agreed / paid / open`, `unpriced`, `subNames`, and a label: `#hcp · customer` + address when the sheet's number has a Pipeline row, else `#<sheet job number>` + the sheet's address with `jobId: null`. Sorted by open money, then job number. `jobsNeedingWorkOrder` is removed from `workOrderCoverage.ts` (the board was its only caller).

## The tab

[`JobsWorkOrdersTab.tsx`](../../src/components/jobs/JobsWorkOrdersTab.tsx) loads sheets with their items, payments and assignees in one embedded select, the roster (`people` + `users.role` behind `account_user_id`), and hands everything to the kernel. Copy: **"Needs a work order · N sub sheets with money open and nothing signed"**; each row shows the label, a "not in Pipeline" tag when there is no ledger job, the sub names, `$X open` (or "unpriced"), and **Draft a work order…** now opens the assembler on the sheet (`laborJobId` + the sheet total as the price) instead of the bare job. The overflow line points at the Sub Labor tab.

`SubLaborBalanceInput` (`subLaborOutstanding.ts`) accepts item/payment shapes without display fields so the kernel can cost a sheet from the lean select.

## Live result

Jobs → Work Orders on prod data, dev server: **20 jobs → 2 sub sheets** — 977 Springtown (Texas R & A, $40,000 open, not in Pipeline) and 880 Reliant Health (Airfordable, $4,200 open). 892's paid sheets and every crew pay sheet are gone. 273 Dudley's Edgar sheet, which the to-do expected to see, nets to zero ($8,500 agreed, $4,500 paid, $4,000 back-charge) and correctly stays out.
