# v2.6820 — What a person signed is one press and one door away on their HR file (2026-11-09)

Diane, 2026-11-09, reading a former teammate's HR file: she could not see at first glance the contracts he had signed in the app, and asked that a person reading People → HR and an agent reading the dev seat both see them easily. Client and the `dev-mcp` function (server 0.53.0). No migration.

## What was there

The HR Timeline already drew `person_contract_documents` as its `contract` source: a signed paper stood on the day it was signed in the Paper lane, with *signed*, the paper's name and who signed it, and the strip counted *N papers signed*. Three things hid it. A long file (a few hundred lines) put an old signing many months down. The strip's chips were words, not presses. And every contract line's door read *Contracts ›* and opened the whole Contracts tab, where the reader then had to find the person again; the tab already honored `/people?tab=contracts&doc=<id>`, the signing page's thank-you deep link into the signed-record window, and nothing on the HR file used it.

## What changed

- **A signed line's door opens its own record** (`contractRecordDoor` in `supabase/functions/_shared/personTimeline.ts`): *Open the signed record ›* → `/people?tab=contracts&doc=<id>`, the signed-record window with the agreement's words and the signing's facts. The office-section line carries the same door. A sent, owed or expiring line keeps the tab's door, since it has no signed record to open.
- **The notes say how it was signed** (`contractSignedHow`): the e-sign consent's clock, the drawn mark's file or a sent link read *Signed electronically.*; a scan keyed in from paper reads *Signed on paper; the scan is the record.*; a row marked signed with nothing from a signing reads *Recorded by the office as signed; no copy from the signing.* When the office made the row after the day it was signed, the notes add *Recorded <day>.* — a paper signed in spring and keyed in in late summer reads both days. An hours statement signed through the app reads *hours statement · signed*. The two contract reads (the tab's `personTimelineIo.ts`, the seat's `CONTRACT_SELECT`) add the four columns that tell the three apart; the reader already had them under RLS, so nothing is widened.
- **Every fact chip is a press** (`PersonFileFactChip.filter`, drawn by `PersonFilePanel`): *1 paper signed*, *3 papers owed* and *sent, waiting* narrow the Timeline to the Paper lane's Contracts; *licenses expired* to the Missed lane's licenses; *no-shows* to the Missed lane's attendance; the rest to their source. The pressed chip reads pressed, and a second press clears the filter. The theme filter clears with the press so the chip's count and the lines agree.
- **The dev seat lists the same papers** (`signedPapers`; `get_person_file` → `signed_papers`): one entry per signed paper, newest first — `paper`, `doc_type`, `signed_on`, `signed_as`, `how`, `sent_on`, `recorded_on`, `office_completed_on`, `expires_on`, `door` — with a note on what is and is not a signature. The lines in `months` carry the new door too.

## What this does not do

Hours statements that were only printed are not signatures and are not listed; one signed through the app lands as a `person_contract_documents` copy and is listed. Job contracts, lien papers, lien releases, checklist sign-offs and commitments a person signs in other roles are not on the HR file; nothing on prod named the person who prompted this in any of them.
