# v2.7316 — Lien desk: the affidavit and retainage lists stack their pile titles like Monthly notices (2026-11-15)

The owner, 2026-11-15, on By paper: the Monthly notices list stacks its pile titles — a 30 px bar per pile with the count at the right, the pile in view lit, passed titles stuck to the top and coming ones to the bottom, a press scrolling to the pile — while the Lien affidavits and Retainage notices lists drew each pile as a flat muted caption with no count, no sticking and no press. Make the two lists' pile titles look and stack like the Monthly notices list's. Client only: no migration, no edge function.

## What changed

- **[`src/components/jobs/LienPileHeads.tsx`](../../src/components/jobs/LienPileHeads.tsx)** (new): the notice list's title wiring, extracted so the three lists share one code path. `useLienPileHeads(shown, phone)` holds the scroller's ref and the lit pile and gives `onScroll` (lights the last pile whose rows the scroll reached, `lienPileReached`) and `scrollToPile(p, i)` (scrolls the pile's `data-lien-pile-rows` under the titles over it, `lienPileScrollTop`, and lights it); `lienPileHeadCount(rows, total, counted, finding)` is the count's words (a find's *n of total* through `lienFindPileCount`, else the desk's count for the pile, else its rows); `LienPileHead` is the `.lienPileHead` bar itself — `data-lien-pile-head`, `data-on`, `data-empty`, placed by `lienPileHeadPlace(i, n, phone)`, the `lienPileHeadBtn` press with the `data-lien-pile-count` span, the pile's own presses as children, and the *show every pile* press (`data-lien-pile-all`) when a pile is picked. The rule of where a title sits stays in [`lienPhoneLists.ts`](../../src/lib/jobs/lienPhoneLists.ts); the CSS is the same `.lienPileHead` block of `index.css`.
- **`LienDesk.tsx`**: the Notices list calls the hook and the component in place of its own `listRef`, `litPile`, `scrollToPile` and `relightPiles` (its run and deliveries presses ride as children). The Affidavits list (`data-lien-affidavit-list`, `position: relative`) and the Retainage list (`data-lien-retainage-list`) each build their `pilesShown` from their pile order — every pile with rows, or with rows before the find while one is typed — call the hook, and draw one `LienPileHead` and one `data-lien-pile-rows` block per pile where the flat captions were. The chips above each list still narrow it to one pile, whose title then offers *show every pile* (not while the held-only list of v2.7209 is up, which has its own way back). `affVisibleAll` and `retVisibleAll` are the rows before the find, so a find's title counts the pile's total as the notice list's does.
- **`LienDesk.render.test.tsx`**: for each of the two lists, two piles draw two `.lienPileHead` titles with their counts at `top: i·30` and `bottom: (1−i)·30`, the first lit; a press on the second scrolls the list and lights it; a chip narrows the list to one title with *show every pile*, which widens it; a find for one job keeps both titles, *0 of 1* greyed and *1 of 1*; on a phone each title is 40 px at `top: 0`. Three older smokes that found a pile's chip by a regex now name it (`To draft · 1`), since the title's press answers the same words.
- Guide *send lien notices from the Lien desk*: the piles section says the two lists stack their titles the same way.

## Checked live (2026-11-15, the dev server over prod data, read only)

Lien affidavits at 1,400 px: three titles (Needs the property facts 7, To draft 3, Ready to file 12) stacked at the top and the bottom; scrolling to the To draft rows stuck the first title at the top and lit To draft; pressing Ready to file put its first row right under the three titles and lit it; the To draft chip left one title with *show every pile*, which brought the three back. Retainage notices: one title, To draft 1, lit. At 375 wide: each title 40 px at the top, the pile being read pinned and lit, the rest scrolling with their rows.

## Not changed

- No row's words, the chips, the find box, the print bar over the affidavit list or the held-only strip. The notice list's titles behave as before; only their code moved.
