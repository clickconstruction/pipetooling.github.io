# v2.6160 — The Workbench's margin as two bars, each caveat a mark on the part it touches (punch list #217, bids-2, a prototype) (2026-10-30)

Punch list #217, *Show it, don't say it*: eight scans found 131 places where the app tells instead of shows, and a mock-up page draws each as a before/after pair for the owner to pick from. Of bids-2, the Pricing tab's money strip and the caveat sentences stacked under it, the owner wrote on 2026-10-30: "I would like to see the before and after on this." This is that prototype, a draft PR with no auto-merge, behind a per-person switch that is off by default, with a dev-only side by side on the live bid. No migration, no edge function.

## The finding

The strip was four tiles (Revenue, Profit, Margin, Multiple) and up to five sentences under them, each a caveat on a figure above: the margin the bid went out at with its inputs, labor at a default crew rate (said only in Revenue's hover), revenue on rows with no cost, labor from another version's counts, the sent figure against today's book, and what today's book adds to the materials. The reader did the arithmetic to see which figure each one touched and by how much. The mock-up's whole-tab page is the spec: the strip keeps its chips and presses; on the left a revenue bar with the sent figure and today's book as a hatched sliver, and a cost bar in its parts with a legend under it carrying each caveat as a mark with its door; on the right the Profit · Margin · Multiple tiles and Margin · Markup · Tax · Solver as today; the stamped *went out at 31%* a ghost mark.

## What changed

- **`workbenchBars.ts`** (`src/lib/bids/`, new): the pure kernel from the strip's own inputs to the two bars, the way the Legal desk's `moneyBars.ts` draws a claim. `workbenchBars(input)` takes the strip's revenue, the bid's sent day and figure, the cost breakdown (labor, materials, tax, the whole), the labor rate and its source, the revenue on rows with no cost and how many, today's book's drift, whether Labor's hours follow another version's counts, and the margin stamp, and returns both bars as fractions of one scale (the largest of revenue, the sent figure and the cost with the drift): the **revenue bar** as the sent figure solid with today's book as a hatched sliver past it, or today's figure solid with the shortfall dashed to the sent mark, or one solid figure on an unsent bid; the **cost bar** as labor, materials with their tax, the drift hatched, the other direct costs, the revenue on rows with no cost as a dashed stretch, and the margin as the rest, with the stamped margin a ghost mark at the cost that margin would mean on today's revenue; the **legend**, one line per caveat on the part it touches with its door (*labor · at the crew rate $35.76/h · a default ↗ set Labor's rate*, *materials · $15,100 · +$624 at today's book ↗ Takeoffs*, *no cost · $2,100 on 4 rows ↗ the rows*, *margin · went out at 31% · $33,600 cost on $48,700*); the amber chip *labor on last version's counts ↗ Labor*; and one sentence for the hover and the screen reader. Null with nothing to draw. Seven tests in `workbenchBars.test.ts` over the mock-up's bid and its edges (unsent, a cheaper book, no labor rate, cost over revenue, another year's send).
- **`WorkbenchBarsView.tsx`** (new): the two bars, their marks, the legend with a swatch per part and a press per door, the chip. Reads the kernel's fractions only. Smoke `WorkbenchBarsView.render.test.tsx`: the bars, marks, legend and doors; the switch off and on through `ShapeSwitch`; the side by side; an unsent bid with no caveat.
- **`BidsPricingTab.tsx`**: the strip's row is now one block (`stripRow`), and the four caveat lines (`PricedMarginLine`, the labor warning, the sent-against-today line, the materials note) are built once as constants and drawn where they were. With this person's switch on (**Show the bars** on the strip, `useShapeView('workbench-bars')`, remembered in localStorage per person, off by default), the row drops its Revenue tile (the bar's caption carries revenue to the dollar) and keeps everything else, the bars take the left two thirds and the row the right third on a wide screen (one column under 1000 px, or while the solver is open), and the four caveat lines give way to the legend. Each door goes where the sentence's link went: Labor's rate and the labor warning open the Labor tab, the drift opens Takeoffs, the rows with no cost turn **Show no-cost rows** on. With `?sbs=1` and a dev signed in, the strip draws as today, and under it **Today** (the four lines) above **With the bars** (the bars beside Profit · Margin · Multiple), with no switch. Nothing changes for anyone else.
- **`src/components/prototypes/SideBySide.tsx`** (new here): the shared #217 helper, copied as is from PR #6528 (v2.6138); whichever #217 PR lands first carries it.
- **Guide** *price a bid with the Workbench*: a bullet under **Know where you stand** names the switch and the two bars.

## What the real bids make of the bars (2026-10-30)

Read on a dev server from this branch at `/bids?tab=pricing&bidId=<id>&sbs=1` as the dev, on the two newest sent bids that carry a sent figure:

- **The newest** (sent Oct 27, the book still prices it there): the cost bar is materials alone at 19% of revenue, then a dashed *no cost* stretch over 64% of the bar (18 rows), then an 11% margin segment labelled *margin 81%*. No labor segment at all, since the bid has no labor rows, and the legend says labor is at the default crew rate. The four sentences under today's strip said the same in words — *no labor rate · $128,115 on rows with no cost* — and the bar makes the 81% read as what it is: a sliver of real margin beside a wide dashed stretch of revenue nobody costed.
- **The one before it** (sent Oct 19 at one figure, the book $10,900 lower today): the revenue bar is today's figure solid with a dashed stretch to the sent mark; the cost bar is materials at 22%, *no cost* over 9 rows, and *margin 78%*; the stamp's ghost mark sits apart from both, since the margin was kept on a third revenue figure.
- So on today's bids the bars **differ from bid to bid** and **say more than the tiles**: the honest margin on both is the small green segment, not the tile's number, and the dashed stretch is where the estimate is not done. The mock-up's bid, with labor costed and a small drift, is the shape the bars take on a bid whose Labor tab has been filled in.

## What the mock-up asked for that this does not do

- **Marks under the revenue bar for both figures.** The caption carries both (*$156,514 sent Oct 19 · $145,601 at today's book*) and a mark sits at the sent figure only when the two differ; the mock-up's second mark for the book's figure is the bar's own end.
- **The labor chip's fixture count.** *labor on last version's counts · 3 fixtures* needs the sync plan's rows; the chip says the fact and opens Labor, as today's warning does.
- **The side by side on one strip.** The strip's row (its chips, the coverage chip, the solver and the three tools) is drawn once, as today; the side by side shows the part that changes, today's four lines above the bars beside the three tiles.
- **The margin as the gap between the bars.** The bars share a scale, so the gap reads at a glance; the margin is also drawn as the cost bar's last segment, since a gap with no fill has no place for its number.
