# v2.7220 — Paint a map: walk or drive the line (2026-11-13)

Punch list #345, PR 3. The owner's idea as he said it: a person walks with location on and the phone draws their line. This is the first thing in the app that follows a phone, so most of the PR is what it will not do.

## What changed

- **Walk or drive it** on the start sheet, and **Walk or drive a line** on a saved map. The sheet says, before the phone is asked anything, that the screen must stay on, that the line stays on the phone until Save, and that painting stops by itself.
- **The red bar**: *Painting · 2:14* for every second the phone is read. It turns grey and says *The phone is not being read* on any pause.
- **Painting** (`usePaintedWalk` over `paintedWalk.ts`): `watchPosition` with high accuracy and no cached reading, and the screen held on with the Screen Wake Lock API where the browser has one. Where it has none the window says to touch the screen now and then. The map follows the walker. The window shows the length so far beside how sure the phone is.
- **A stretch the phone was not sure of** (a margin past about 65 ft) is drawn as faint dots and left out of the length. The window says so while it is happening.
- **A rough location** (a margin past about half a mile) is told from a reading: the window says how to turn on Precise Location on an iPhone and on Android, and offers drawing by hand.
- **A locked screen**: a browser hands a page its position only while the page is on the screen. So a lock, a call or another app pauses the painting. The window says the line has a gap and for how long, and offers **Keep painting from here** (a new stroke) or **Join the gap with a straight line**.
- **Stopping by itself**: five minutes with no real step pauses it, and an hour of painting ends it. A pause does not count against the hour.
- **Look it over**: Done stops the reading and shows every stroke, framed whole, with its length, its give or take and walked or driven (told from the middle speed). **Fix this line by hand** opens the stroke's corners in the drawing tool, and a line set right by hand reads *walked, a part drawn by hand*. **Leave this line out**, **Keep painting**, **Throw it away**.
- **Save** writes each stroke as a shape. `pointsToJson` is the one door a line leaves the phone through, and a test holds that no reading's time or margin goes with it.
- **A place during a walk**: **＋ Place** puts the dot where the phone last was, with how sure it was, and the painting goes on behind the sheet.
- **Kept on the phone**: the walk is written to the phone's own storage as it goes, so a reload or a trip to the camera does not lose it. It is cleared when the lines are saved or thrown away, and after a day. A walk closed unsaved is offered back by the host on the next screen: *A walk from 2:14 PM is still on this phone. It was not saved.* with **Look it over** and **Throw it away**.

## What it will not do

- **It does not follow a locked phone.** No browser allows it, on any phone, and nothing here tries. The limit that rules out a phone in a pocket is the same limit that protects the person carrying it.
- **It does not read the phone outside painting.** The reading starts on **Walk or drive it** or **Keep painting** and stops on Pause, Done, a hidden page, a refusal, or leaving the window.
- **It sends nothing until Save.** The office sees no line being painted. Thrown away, a walk was never anywhere but the phone.
- **It keeps no clock.** The readings carry their time on the phone, to tell a walk from a drive and to notice standing still. What is saved is pairs of numbers.
- **It is not a record of a person.** A painted line is not on the clocked-in map and is not read against hours or pay.

## Is this the best we can do

- **One fixing tool, not three.** The card drew *trim an end*, *redraw a part* and *move a place* as three tools. A walked stroke is already straightened to a few corners, so the drawing tool does all three: remove the corners of the knot at an end, drag or add corners across a faint part. One tool a person already knows from drawing by hand.
- **The standing-still rule was tightened** in PR 1 for this: a step counts only when it is longer than the new reading's own margin.
- **Look it over frames the whole walk.** The first build left the map where it had followed the walker to, on the last few feet. Found on the browser walk's picture.
- **Kept on the phone and sent on a press, not by itself.** The card said a walk saved with no signal would wait in the app's outbox and send itself. The outbox lands a press through one database function with a fixed list of kinds, so that is a migration and a server change of its own. For now the walk stays on the phone, the window says so, and Save is pressed again with signal. Sending itself is a follow-up.
- **The one-day try was not a separate page.** The owner said build it all and that he would test it live. The card's numbers for a walked length stay marked as reckoned until he has walked a taped line.

## Checked

- 27 new kernel tests (`paintedWalk`, `paintedWalkWords`), 19 render tests of walking in the window, 9 of the host, and the start sheet's.
- **A headless walk on the worktree's dev server at phone width**, with the browser's own location moved along a made-up path by Playwright: the bar and its clock; the length and *Sure within 20 ft*; three readings with a 150 ft margin, drawn faint with the amber line; the page hidden and shown again, the walk paused as hidden and found in the phone's storage with its map; **Keep painting from here** and a second stroke; **Look it over** with both strokes framed, the faint part named; **Save the map** writing two lines whose points are pairs of numbers; nothing left in the phone's storage after; the line under the job's address.
- **Not seen, and it matters:** a real phone. Headless Chromium has no screen to lock and no wake lock, and its location is whatever the test says. How an iPhone and an Android phone behave on a real walk, and how close a walked length comes to a taped one, is the owner's test.
