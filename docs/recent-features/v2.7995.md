# v2.7995 — Papers: send a paper to a customer, a supply house or someone on no list (2026-11-19)

Punch list #403, step 3, the first of its two PRs: **Who is it with?** No migration: the columns, the door and the view's kinds are PR 1's (v2.7977). Four edge functions change, and eight more bundle a shared file that did.

## What is new

- **Who is it with?**, the second window of **Send a paper** on Documents → Agreements (`PaperPartyPicker.tsx`). It stops being a roster search. It finds a teammate, a sub, a customer and the people on file there, and a supply house and its reps. Chips narrow it: **All**, **Team**, **Subs**, **Customers**, **Supply houses**.
- **One pick names the party and the signer.** A person on file at a company signs for that company. A company picked alone has its signer named in the next window. A person on the roster signs for themselves, as before.
- **Someone not in any list** is the last row, always (the owner's call 6). The office types a name, an email and a company.
- **The Send for signature window says more for a party.** Under the title: *Mutual secrecy agreement · Tallgrass Supply Co.* Above the preview: *The paper will say: Lena Park, Branch manager, signing for Tallgrass Supply Co.*
- **Keep a new signer with their company.** One tick, on by default: *Keep Wren Castillo with Tallgrass Homes*. It saves them as a person on file there, so the next paper offers them by name.
- **An address the app already keeps is named before the send**, so nobody is added twice. An address that is someone on the roster is refused, with the way out: pick them from the list.
- **The list draws these papers.** A new chip, **Outside companies**. Each line is tagged *Customer*, *Supply house* or *Not on a list*, and says who signs after the party: *Lena Park signs, Branch manager*, then *signed by Lena Park, Branch manager*. A waiting line carries **Send again** and **Copy the signing link**. A signed one opens its signed record.
- **The signing page and the signed record say who signs and for whom**, when the paper keeps a title or a business.

## How a party's paper is written

Nothing is written until **Send email**, as for a quick-send pick. At the press, in order (`preparePartyPaper` in `AgreementsSendPaper.tsx`):

1. A typed signer the office ticked to keep is saved with the company (`customer_contact_persons` or `supply_house_contacts`).
2. The party's contact is found, and made when the record has none, through PR 1's door `paper_party_contact`. Someone on no list is found by their address, or made from it and named (`contact_make_for_channel`).
3. The plan is People's own (`quickSendPickPlan`), on the party's stored name `party:<contact id>`. A party that already has the paper out gets the same copy again, and no second row is written.
4. The row is written with `party_contact_id` and `party_name`. No packet assignment is made, so a later change to the Contract Book never mints a copy for an outside party.
5. The send function writes the signer, their title, their address and `signing_for` in the update that activates the link.

A send that fails after step 4 tries again on the same row.

## The reads

- **On the press**, with the Contract Book: the roster's kinds ride the roster read that was there (`sendRoster`).
- **When the second window opens**, three reads together: the people on file at customers, the supply houses, and their reps (`readPartyShortLists`). They are short lists, held whole and capped at 500 rows each.
- **As the office types**, one read for customers by name, once the typing rests and from two letters (`searchPartyCustomers`). Customers are too many to hold.
- **In the email window**, for someone on no list only: one look-up of the typed address, once it is an address and the typing rests.

## The edge functions

- `send-contract-for-signature`: the email's *For* line and the kept copy name the person who signs, never the stored name. `signing_for` rides the signer's write. A person's own paper is byte for byte as before (the pin test).
- `get-contract-for-signer`: answers `signer_name`, `signer_title`, `signing_for` and `audience`, and never the stored name.
- `open-contract-form-pdf`: a party's file downloads under the party's name. Its gate is unchanged.
- `_shared/esignConsent.ts`: the audience `outside`, for a signer who is neither a customer nor a sub. A customer's contact signs as *customer* and a trade company's as *sub*. `accept-contract` and seven other functions bundle the file and are redeployed with it.

## Choices worth knowing

- **People → Contracts keeps its own picker** (the owner's call 5). Nothing there changed but one filter: a party's paper is no person's, so it is not listed on People, as a trade company's is not.
- **Someone who signed a paper is not offered a second copy**, as People's picker has it. The roster rows are the people who have not had it and the ones it is out with.
- **Trade companies are not in this window.** Sending a company's paper is a dev's today, and the card's fifth and sixth steps open that door.
- **The party's name on a line is the record's name today.** The name frozen on the paper is what stands when the record is gone. For someone on no list it is the company typed, else the signer.
- **The address opens on the roster's for a person**, as before. For a party it opens on the person on file's address, or on the address the paper last went to.
- **Under All, with nothing typed, the list is the roster.** That is who the office sends to most. A supply house or a customer is one typed word away, or one chip.
