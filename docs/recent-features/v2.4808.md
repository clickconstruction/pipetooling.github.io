# v2.4808 — Legal portal: easy to read on a phone (2026-10-07)

Client only; no migration, no edge function. The owner asked how the firm's portal looks on a phone; the investigation (the live portal at 375 × 812, every tab and panel) found the page fine and its tables not: *"Build all three, commit, push and merge."*

## What was wrong

[v2.4639](v2.4639.md) made the page one column on a phone and let each table scroll sideways inside its card. In practice every table was wider than its card. The long text column wrapped a word per line, so one ledger row ran seven lines tall while its Balance sat off the right edge. The Jobs table hid On file and Balance, the money. The property record showed two of seven columns. The Lien grid kept its 960 px table and showed a column of job names with counsel's eleven other columns behind a scroll the firm had to discover. Table headings were 10.5 px and section labels 11 px.

## What changed

- **Every matter table folds into cards** when its own box is narrow. `PortalTable` draws each cell through [`CardCell`](../../src/components/jobs/legal/LegalCardCell.tsx): the cell carries its column's name (`data-label`), and `.legalCardTable` in `src/index.css` lays a row out as a card, each cell its column's name beside the value. The first cell leads the card in bold, figures are bold, a cell with nothing in it drops out (an absent PDF, an Undo the firm cannot press; a `—` stays), and a column with no name spans the card (the envelope's letter, the PDF link, Undo). The answers band under a notice stays with its card. Account, Paper, Record of contact, Evidence, Fees & steps and a pulled-back matter's conversation all fold; the Exhibits list already fit.
- **A container query, not the window.** `.legalCardWrap` is a size container and the cards apply under 560 px of its own width (`@container legal-card`). A phone folds, and so does an iPad held upright, whose main column is 390 px beside the side cards. A computer keeps the tables, and so does the office's preview of the firm's view (up to 980 px).
- **The Lien grid folds the same way.** Each job is a card led by the job and its address, every fact beside its column's name, the court's chip and the amount in bold. The facts the office has not entered drop out of the card and read as one red line at its foot, *Not entered yet: payment bond, paid out to the GC, 10 % reserved, contract completion.* (`lienCardMissingLine`), a cell only a card shows (`.legalCardOnly`). On a computer the grid is the twelve-column table it was, with its `?`s.
- **Small type reads 12 px on a phone.** The portal's sizes under 12 px (section labels, table headings, the Lien grid's cells and chips, helper lines, the Reach the office label) read `portalSmall(px)`, which is `var(--legal-small, <px>px)`; the phone query sets `--legal-small: 12px` on `.legalPortalPage`. The desk's preview is not inside the page, so it keeps the desktop sizes.
- **Colors stay in portalTheme.** The card CSS reads the hairline and the label color from `--legal-card-hair` and `--legal-card-label`, set inline from `HAIR` and `FAINT` (`LEGAL_CARD_VARS`).
- **Table roles are explicit** (`table`, `rowgroup`, `row`, `columnheader`, `cell`), so a screen reader still reads a table once the cards change its display; the column headings stay in the tree, hidden from sight on a card.

Kernel: [`legalPortalCards.ts`](../../src/lib/legal/legalPortalCards.ts) — `cardCellEmpty`, `portalSmall`, `LEGAL_CARD_VARS`, `lienCardMissingLine`.

## Is this the best we can do?

- **Cards per table, written by hand?** No. One cell component and one block of CSS fold all fifteen matter tables and the Lien grid, and a table added later folds by itself. A hand-made card per table would read a little better for the ledger and drift the first time a column is added.
- **The window's width or the table's?** The table's. The window says phone or not; the table's box says whether its columns fit, which is the real question. The iPad case is what decided it.
- **Hide the Lien grid's `?`s on a phone?** Not hide: move. Four question marks a card is noise; one red line naming them keeps every fact the office owes in view.
- **Match inline font sizes from the stylesheet** (`[style*="font-size: 11px"]`)? No. It would have touched nothing in the components and depends on how the browser writes the style attribute. `portalSmall` names each small size where it is set.
- **Left out.** The shared Lien timeline strip on Paper keeps its own 9 and 10 px marks: it is the desk's component and its owner's call. The Fees & steps forms are cramped at an iPad's upright width (the page's phone query stacks them only under 720 px); a follow-up.
