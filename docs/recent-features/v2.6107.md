# v2.6107 — Legal desk Timeline: a strip of days on top, the counts as chips, and the overdue steps in red (2026-10-30)

Punch list #214 (`to-dos/legal-desk-show-not-tell/`), PR 4 of 4. The Timeline already showed — one line split around today, lanes, diamonds for the clock. Two things on it still told: the header's counts were a sentence (*7 planned · 2 overdue · 10 done · 3 deadlines from the lien clock*), and every sent paper printed its whole *Sent Oct 27 by certified mail to …* record under its title, so one send day read the same three sentences on the Evidence tab and again here. And an overdue step looked like any other row until the eye reached its words.

## What changed

- **The strip** ([`timelineStrip.ts`](../../src/lib/legal/timelineStrip.ts), pure, 5 tests; [`LegalTimelineStrip.tsx`](../../src/components/jobs/legal/LegalTimelineStrip.tsx)): one column a day from the earlier of the first item and two weeks before today to the later of the last item and two weeks after, never more than `STRIP_MAX_DAYS` (120) with today kept in view, a deadline a year or more out (the § 53.158 suit) never stretching it. Each item is a dot in its own shape — `late` a red ring, `deadline` a diamond, `done` filled, `planned` a ring, and small lane dots for `did`, `paper` and `said` — at most `STRIP_DOTS_PER_DAY` (4) per day, the ones that matter first, with *+N* for the rest. Mondays and the first of a month are labelled. A press on a day unfolds the past (`Show N earlier`) and scrolls the line to that day (`data-legal-timeline-day` on every row and burst). The strip narrows with the lane chips, since it reads the lane-filtered timeline.
- **The counts as chips** (`timelineCountChips`): *planned 7 · overdue 2 · due today 1 · done 10 · ◆ deadlines 3*, nothing for a zero, the old sentence on the hover; `data-legal-timeline-count` per chip.
- **A paper's chip** ([`legalTimeline.ts`](../../src/lib/legal/legalTimeline.ts)): `LegalTimelineDocument.chip` and `LegalTimelineItem.documentChip` — *sent · certified mail* · *sent · email* · *received* · *draft*, '' on a final paper (`documentChipWords`), filled by both document readers (the desk's rows, the portal's payload). The row draws *document · sent · certified mail* as the kind chip with the full state sentence on its hover and no sentence under the title; a final paper keeps its *Added to Evidence* line, which is the day fact.
- **The overdue row** is hot: a red rule down its left edge (`data-legal-timeline-hot`) and **Mark done** first, as the primary press (`data-legal-timeline-done-first`), on the office's side.
- **Guide** *review a collections account before it goes to your attorney*: the Timeline section says what the strip draws and what the chips mean.
- **Tests**: `timelineStrip.test.ts` (new), `LegalTimelinePanel.render.test.tsx` (+2: the strip, the hot row and the day press; Mark done first and the sent paper's chip; the two count assertions read the chips).

## What the mock-up asked for that this does not do

The mock-up collapsed the three papers sent on one day under one dated row with a sub-label. The line already folds four or more papers *added* on one day (v2.5850); papers *sent* on one day stay on their own rows, each with its chip, because each is its own paper with its own record.

## Deploy

The client deploys with the merge. The firm's portal draws the same panel, so the strip and the chips reach it with the client; no function change.
