# v2.8168 — Intro to HVAC: the system under conditions, lessons 19 to 25 (2026-11-21)

PR 3 of four of the Intro to HVAC course (Taunya, 2026-11-20; PR 1 is v2.8158, PR 2 is v2.8163). Content and drawings only: no migration, no edge function, no screen.

- **The lessons** (`src/content/training/intro-hvac/`, Part 3, *Under conditions*), each a strip of four frames whose last frame is what the crew does: 19 a clogged condensate drain (the thermostat to off, the clog out through the capped tee or the far end, never a cut, and a pour of water as the proof); 20 a frozen coil (too little air or too little refrigerant; off, fan on, let it melt; a second freeze with good air is a carded technician's); 21 a water heater that backdrafts (the four causes, the signs on top of the heater, off until the cause is fixed); 22 a gas smell at the furnace; 23 a cut line set; 24 a unit that starts by itself (the power cut, locked and tested dead; the float switch that lets a unit start again as the pan drains); 25 an attic in August. `course.md` reads `lessons: 25`, `minutes: 140` and gains the part; the bank grows to forty.
- **The sources read on 2026-11-21.** The gas safety pages of CenterPoint Energy, Atmos Energy and Texas Gas Service (leave on foot, no switch, no phone, no flame, no car, call 911 and the utility from a safe distance, never assume someone else has called, never look for the leak; a nose gets used to the smell). The makers' safety data sheets for R-454B (Chemours, revised 2025) and R-410A (Honeywell, revised 2018): gas under pressure, frostbite from the liquid, a boiling point of -59.6°F for R-454B, vapor heavier than air that displaces oxygen, hydrogen fluoride in a fire, and the R-454B sheet's flammable gas warning. OSHA's heat illness first aid page (the signs of heat exhaustion and of heat stroke, and 911 and cooling for heat stroke). International Mechanical Code 2024, 306.3 and 306.3.1 (the passageway's 24 in of solid floor and 20 ft, the 30 in by 30 in level work space, the 20 in by 30 in opening, the light switched at the opening and the outlet at the unit) and 307.2.5 (a clog cleared without a cut). Lesson 22 writes no rule of the company's own about shutting gas at a meter: the utilities' steps are the lesson, and the company's rule for its plumbers is the master plumber's to add.
- **Not yet checked against any source** and said so on the lesson: how a drain is cleared (the wet vacuum), all of lesson 20, the causes and signs of a backdraft and the smoke test, and lesson 24's order of work beyond the lock and the test that Intro to electrical already teaches.
- **Seven strips**: `clogged-drain-strip`, `frozen-coil-strip`, `backdraft-strip`, `gas-smell-strip`, `cut-line-set-strip`, `starts-alone-strip`, `attic-strip`. The house red is on the thing that is wrong and on nothing else: the spilling gas, the loose refrigerant, the switch not to touch, the drywall not to step on. Shot at 420 px in light and 340 px in dark; thirteen titles and lines that ran past a frame's edge were shortened from the shots.
