# v2.3013 — Test-gap audit, pass 25: the Wheels report loader gets its first tests (2026-09-07)

Follow-up to v2.3010. Tests only; no kernel, client, migration or edge-function change.

## Why this one

`people/wheelsData.ts` (194 lines) gathers everything the Wheels report (People → Vehicles, People → Review) shows: the fuel-tag charges on company cards by attributed person, approved field hours by login, the company trucks with their current holders, insurance, registration and service, and hands them to the pure kernel in `wheels.ts`. The kernel and the fleet helpers were tested; the loader — eight reads, four sub-loaders and the fuel-family split — was not.

## What the tests found

Nothing wrong. Contracts now pinned that were only implied:

- The window is the trailing 90 days ending today; transactions are bounded with an exclusive upper day, sessions and service events with inclusive dates, and possessions and insurance periods come newest-first.
- The fuel tag is the one flagged `fuel_vehicle`, else the first whose name says "fuel", else none. A charge counts as fuel when its accounting label's tag, else its bank category's tag, is that one, so a labelled charge that belongs to no tag stays out.
- Only card purchases can be someone's fuel: a supplier ACH under the fuel family is reported off-card and never counted; a company-card purchase is reported as company spend; the rest are attributed by the Banking attributions, and what nobody is attributed to is listed by card nickname.
- A truck's holder is the current possession unless it is the motor pool; running cost is fuel + insurance (only while on a plan) + registration + service over the 90-day window, and rate per field hour follows the holder's approved field hours.
- Pay-config people link to logins by trimmed name; an unknown arrangement reads as None; a manual override wins over the computed rate.
- A failed card-directory read degrades to "no company cards"; a failed core read throws.
