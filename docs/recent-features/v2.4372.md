# v2.4372 — Pricing: your history strip adds up past costs the way Pricing does (2026-10-01)

The follow-up v2.4371 left open. The Pricing tab's win/loss strip, *This number vs your history* (`PricingMarginHistory` over `usePricingMarginHistory`), sets each past bid's margin from the RPC `bid_pricing_history`. The RPC's `est_cost` still used two rules the Workbench had dropped on 2026-09-11, plus a third of its own. The dots therefore sat a little below the margins their bids were priced at, and the ▼ of the bid on screen was measured differently from them.

## What changed
- **Migration `20261002080000_bid_pricing_history_kernel_parity`** (`CREATE OR REPLACE`, same return type, on top of v2.4371's body). `est_cost` now equals `computeBidCostBreakdown`'s `totalCost`, materials aside.
- **Labor hours** follow `laborRowMultiplier` (v2.3291): a sub line 0, a fixed or task row ×1, a per-100-ft row count ÷ 100, else × count.
- **Estimator time is out** (v2.3294). The old body added $10 a count row, or the flat amount, and counted every version's rows. The `count_rows` CTE goes with it.
- **Distance** is the number the text starts with, as the kernel's `parseFloat` reads it (`96.4 mi` → 96.4). The old read gave 0 unless the whole text was a number. `bid_estimate_breakdown` keeps its own reading. Why, and the four readers side by side: `docs/migrations/20261002080000_bid_pricing_history_kernel_parity.md`.
- **`bidPricingHistorySql.test.ts`** pins the newest body to the kernel (no estimator time, the four labor branches, the distance regex against 14 strings), so the next kernel change fails here until the RPC follows.
- The kernel's comments named the estimator change v2.3293. It shipped as v2.3294 (#3022), and the comments now say so.

## Verified
`BEGIN … ROLLBACK` dry run on prod over every service type, comparing the live body, v2.4371's and this one. 73 rows under all three. Each bid's inputs were run through `computeBidCostBreakdown`, and all 73 match this body's `est_cost`. Only estimator time moved anything on today's data: no labor row on prod is a sub or a per-100-ft row, and every distance is a plain number or blank. It came off 69 rows, $26,640 in all.

How the dots move (Plumbing; HVAC never draws): v2.4371 draws 6 dots, all past 65%. This body draws 5. BP298 (won) goes from 94.70% to 95.01% and leaves the 95% band, and the other five gain 0.03–1.0 points at the right end. BP483's ▽ tab mark moves from 56.3% to 57.8%. With one win left, a Workbench margin between about 80% and 95% reads *Above every recorded win* instead of *Mixed territory*.
