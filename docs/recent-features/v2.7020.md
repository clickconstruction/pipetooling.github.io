# v2.7020 — Messages: a conversation a developer tagged for you says so on the thread (2026-11-12)

A tagged email conversation shows a grey line on the thread: *<Name> tagged this conversation from their mail. Later mail in it lands here by itself.* The name was the person whose mailbox the conversation is in. It read the same whether that person pressed the tag in Messages → My email or the dev seat ran `plan_mail_tag` → `apply_mail_tag` as that login. The owner's helper met a thread on 2026-11-12 whose line named a teammate twice in one minute, and had to ask whether he did it. The record said the seat did: 17 conversations in that box were tagged in two minutes on 2026-11-09, each beside an `apply_mail_tag` row in `dev_mcp_calls` under a dev's key. Nothing on the screen said so. These lines can become evidence on the Legal desk, so who did what matters.

## The record: no new column

`email_tag_threads.tagged_by` already means *who made the tag*, and the seat's tag onto an HR file already stamps the dev there (v2.6715). A job tag through the seat now does the same. `user_id` stays the box the conversation is in. A row whose `tagged_by` is not its `user_id` is a tag someone made for that person.

A column that says *through the seat* was weighed and left out. It would need a migration against prod, a push ordered ahead of the function's deploy, and a second fact to keep in step with the first. The one case it would settle that `tagged_by` cannot is a dev tagging **their own** box through the seat: the row then reads as their own press. The line's words still say *through the dev seat*, and `dev_mcp_calls` holds the call.

## What changed

- **`myMailTagEventWords(who, seat?)`** (`src/lib/myMail/mineFunction.ts`, mirrored on the edge). With a seat: *Nadia Reyes tagged this conversation from Marisol Vega's mail, through the dev seat. Later mail in it lands here by itself.* The dev's name, else their address. The dev's own box reads *from their mail, through the dev seat*. With no seat the words are the old ones, letter for letter.
- **`myMailTaggedBy(actor, jobKey, before)`**: who the row names. Whoever made the tag: the person on their own press, the dev through the seat. A second tag onto the job the conversation is already on keeps the first tagger, as the grey line keeps its words (it is written once and only rewritten on a move). A tag onto another job, or over a row the app placed by itself, is the new tagger's, and the moved line is rewritten in their words.
- **`isTagTie`** takes a recorded tie stamped by the row's tagger **or** by the box's person. Before this change a seat tag left `tagged_by` as the person while `tie_message`, run as the caller, stamped the dev on each line it tied. So a line the seat tied was never the tag's own: the person's untag left it on the job, and a tag onto another job left it behind. That is fixed for tags made from now on.
- **`gmail-mine`**: the seat's door hands the dev to `tag()` (`ctx.seat`). The row's `tagged_by`, the grey line and the `gmail_mine_tag` log line carry the dev. The follow already stamps `tagged_by` on what it ties later, so it stamps the dev too.
- **`plan_mail_tag`**'s `lands` line now ends *A new tag's grey line on the thread names you, through the dev seat.* so the dev reads it before applying.
- The guide *tag an email onto a job from your own mail* says what the line means.

The thread draws event lines from their stored words, so the client did not change.

## Past tags (proposed, not done)

Tags the seat made before this deploy keep the person's name on the row and on the line. They can be told apart after the fact: `dev_mcp_calls` holds every ok `apply_mail_tag` with the dev (`user_id`), the time, and the arguments whole (`args->'tag'`: the account, the Gmail thread id and the job). An account and a thread id name exactly one `email_tag_threads` row.

The proposal is a one-time correction in a migration file, so it is reviewed, names nobody and is applied with `supabase db push`:

1. For each such call whose row still has `tagged_by = user_id`, is not app-placed and is on the job the call named: set `tagged_by` to the call's dev.
2. For that row's grey line (`messages.quo_id = 'mytag:' || id`, `channel = 'event'`), only where the body is still exactly the old sentence for that person: rewrite it to the new sentence with the dev's name.

Setting `tagged_by` also repairs the untag for those rows, since the lines the seat tied are already stamped the dev. A conversation the person has since moved to another job by hand is left alone: that row and line are truly theirs. Nothing is written until the owner's helper says so; the first step is the same join as a `SELECT`, to count the rows.

## Is this the best we can do? The seat's other lines

Every write verb of the seat was read for who the row names (`supabase/functions/_shared/devMcpWrites.ts`, `supabase/functions/dev-mcp/index.ts`). Only the tag line is fixed here.

**Written in another person's name**

- **`apply_cost_batch`**: a `thread_note` op lands on a job's activity under `author_user_id` from the payload. `cost_batch_apply` checks that it is a user and never that it is the dev. The note shows that teammate as its author.
- **`apply_person_report`**: the HR entry is labelled *Field report — <reporter>*, and its words may be the seat's own rewrite (`p_content` is used as given).
- **`apply_clock_sessions`**: a changed start, end, description or job stays on the worker's own session with no stamp of who changed it. The approval is stamped the dev. Whether the office's own edit leaves a stamp was not determined.

**Written as the dev, with nothing that says the seat** (the screen reads as the dev's own press): `apply_bill` (the text and the email go to the customer as from the dev), `apply_hours_statement` (the signing email), `apply_license` and `apply_paper` (the ask comes from the dev), `apply_activity_note` (which can be back-dated), the mail tag onto an HR file, `apply_hr_entry`, the person evidence and document verbs, the matter narrative, document, gap answer and open verbs, `apply_theme_review`, `apply_materials_move`, the job, payment, combine, collections and contract verbs, sub labor, every bid verb, the submittal verbs, `apply_plan_set`, `apply_bid` and `apply_mail_file`. `apply_property_record` writes a row with no author column at all.

**Already marked**: `apply_history_entry` (*via MCP*), the four Materials on site verbs (*· MCP as <dev>*), `apply_theme_marks` (*dev seat*), the matter plan item, event and statement (*· dev MCP*), and `apply_file_plans` with its default memo.
