# v2.7707 — Trips: each trip has a number, and a link that opens it (2026-11-17)

Punch list #379. The client for the trip's number (the migration is v2.7703) and a direct link to one trip. Client only: no migration, no edge function.

**This must not reach the live app before `20261118040000_trip_numbers.sql` is pushed.** The Trips read now names the column `trips.trip_number`, and PostgREST refuses a select that names a column it does not have. Merged ahead of the push, the whole Trips look, the Job window's trips line and the Scoreboard's cut by trip would fail to read on every site. There is no fallback read, on purpose: push the migration, then let this deploy.

Until now a trip was told by its days alone, *Oct 23 to Nov 4 · Lift stations before the pour*. That is right on a screen and thin everywhere else. There was nothing short to say in a text or to cite on a bill, two crews out on the same days were told apart only by who went, and nothing linked to one trip. The owner asked for both before the bill work starts.

A trip stays a reading laid over the clock. Nothing here changes a clock row, a bill or a cost.

## The number

- **Read and kept.** `TRIP_SELECT` asks for `trip_number`, `TripRow` carries it and `TripRecord` gains `tripNumber`, a positive whole number or null (`tripNumberOf` in `lib/trips/tripRecord.ts`). A new trip starts with none. `tripSavePayload` sends `trip_number` when the record has one and never otherwise, so the delete's **Undo**, which saves the same record again under its own id, asks for the number the trip had, and the database gives it back. On a kept trip the database holds the number whatever is sent.
- **Known at once.** `trip_save`'s reply names the number. `saveTrip` hands it back, and `useSiteTrips` and `useJobTrips` put it on the trip they hold, so a new trip wears its number before the read that follows the save.
- **One place for the words.** `tripName` in `lib/trips/tripsLook.ts` names a trip in each form a screen needs, from its number and its first and last day: *Trip 5 · Oct 23 to Nov 4* for a heading, *Trip 5* for a tight place, *Trip 5, Oct 23 to Nov 4* inside a sentence, and *Open Trip 5* for a door. A trip with no number is told by its days alone, as before: *Oct 23 to Nov 4*, *the trip of Oct 23 to Nov 4*, *Open this trip*. The year is said as `tripSpanWords` says it, so a trip across a year end reads *Trip 7 · Dec 29, 2026 to Jan 3, 2027*. No surface builds the string itself.
- **Where it shows.**
  - The Trips look's row: **Trip 5** as a small cap over the days, at desk and phone width.
  - The open trip's heading: *Trip 5 · Oct 23 to Nov 4 · Lift stations before the pour*.
  - The year strip's bar: its hover line, *Trip 5 · Oct 23 to Nov 4: open this trip*, and its screen reader label, *Trip 5, Oct 23 to Nov 4. Open it.*
  - The delete: *Delete Trip 5, Oct 23 to Nov 4?*, the toast *Deleted Trip 5, Oct 23 to Nov 4. No clock row changed.*, and the Undo's *Trip 5 is back.*
  - History: the verb is *Trip 5 out* and *Trip 5 back*, so two trips that leave on one day are told apart at the start of the line.
  - The Job window's line: *3 trips · last Trip 5, Oct 23 to Nov 4, Luis, Priya · next Trip 6, Dec 1 to 5*.
  - Plans by date: the bar's tip and the card's title lead with *Trip 5 · Oct 23 to Nov 4*, and the dated list says *Trip 5 until Nov 4* and *Planned Trip 6 until Dec 5*.
  - The Scoreboard's cut by trip: the row's name is *Trip 5*, and its days lead the small words beside it, *Oct 29 to Nov 4 · Lift stations before the pour*. The name column is tight, so the short form goes there.
  - The printed trip log: each block is headed *Trip 5 · Oct 29 to Nov 4, 2026 · Back*.
- **Where it does not.** An offer under **Looks like a trip** names no number: it is not a trip yet. The trip sheet has no field for it, and nothing anywhere lets a person type or change it.

## The link

- **The address.** `/developments/<development id>?trip=<trip id>` (`tripHref` beside `tripsLookHref` in `lib/trips/jobTrips.ts`; `tripIdAsked` reads it). `?trip=` implies the Trips look: `asksForTripsLook` is true for either query.
- **The landing.** The page unfolds the Trips look, and once the trips are read the card opens that trip's row, shows every trip if a chip had narrowed them, and turns the strip to the trip's year (`tripStripYearOf`). The page then lands on the trip's own row, its top under the pinned strip, and not on the top of the look: the row wears an id (`tripRowAnchor`) and the cards' `scroll-margin-top`, and `useLandOnLook` gained `targetId`, so the one scroll a landing makes goes to the row when it is on the page. It happens one time for each link: a reader who closes the row or scrolls away is left alone, and a later read of the trips neither opens the row nor scrolls again. `useLandOnLook` also gained `askKey`, so a second link to another trip on the same mounted page lands again.
- **The query stays in the address**, as `?look=trips` stays today. Reloading the page lands on the trip again.
- **A link to a trip that is not here** (deleted, or another site's) lands on the top of the look with no row open, and a quiet toast says *That trip is no longer here.* once. No error, and nothing retries. On a site that shows no Trips look the toast is said the same way.
- **Copy link**, a quiet press beside **Change** on an open trip, for everyone who sees the look. It copies the app's address with the path and the query (`appUrl`, the helper the customer timeline's link uses) and says *Link copied.* When the clipboard is closed it shows the address to copy by hand, in the app's usual words.
- **A trip's name is a door** wherever the reader can open the page:
  - A trip's two History lines end with *Open Trip 5 ›*, which opens the trip on the same page. The feed's mail line carries its door the same way.
  - The Job window's *last …* and *next …* each close the window and go to their own trip. **Open the site's trips** stays as the look's door.
  - A Plans by date card's press is **Open Trip 5** and opens that trip. The Trips lane's own card keeps **Open the Trips look**.
  - A Scoreboard trip row's name opens its trip, at desk and phone width. The last row, **At home for the site**, is no trip and stays plain words.
  A press on the same page moves no address: it opens the look if it is folded, opens the row and lands on the row, once a press.

## Not built

- **The header's search does not find trips.** A trip is reached from its site's page, its link, or a name that is a door.
- **A reader outside the office door** who is sent a link lands on the Development page as it is for them. They read no trips, so no look opens and nothing is said.
- **The Development page's row in `docs/twins/APP_DIRECTORY.md`** lists none of the page's query keys and not the Trips look, so `?trip=` was not added there.
