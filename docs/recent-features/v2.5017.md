# v2.5017 — The open asks count money put on the job: the map's owed line follows the rule (2026-10-09)

Client only. No migration, no edge function. This is piece 4 of `to-dos/where-the-checks-went-residuals.md`, filed under v2.5006 and taken under the owner's call of **2026-10-09**: *a payment put on a job with no bill picked is applied oldest bill first everywhere*.

## What was wrong

`jobOpenBillingRemainderDollars` (`src/lib/jobsStagesBoard.ts`) is what a job's ready-to-bill drafts and billed bills still ask for. It netted each against its **linked** payments only. After v2.5006 the Billed board counts money put on the job with no bill picked, oldest bill first, but this reader did not. So a bill that money had paid still asked for it in full.

Two readers use it:

- **The Pipeline map** (`jobsMap.ts`): each billed or ready-to-bill pin's *$X owed* line, and the *to collect* totals by distance band (`jobsMapRail`). Job 273's pin said $17,585 owed while its bills owe $16,685.
- **Capable to bill** (`jobCapableToBillAmounts`): value created, less the payments received, less these open asks. Money the rule put on a bill came off twice: once as a payment, and again as an ask still open.

**The capable-to-bill half was overstated when it was filed.** Unlinked money only reaches a bill after it has paid all the work on no sent bill. A job in that state has nothing left to bill: its figure is at most value created − revenue − drafts, never positive. The double count only made an already-negative per-job figure more negative. The Working header, the Capable list (`toBill > 0`) and the Ready-to-ask tile all clamp at zero, so they never read low. On prod no Working job carries unlinked money and an open ask; the three that do (251, 273, 473) are billed.

## What changed

- `jobOpenBillingRemainderDollars` nets a sent bill by `billAppliedOnJob`, the rule with the job's revenue. A draft takes no unlinked money and keeps its linked money. The job type now carries `revenue`.
- The card's piece 4 is marked shipped and says what was overstated.
