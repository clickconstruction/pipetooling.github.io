# v2.8510 — Lien desk: one check recorded over every bill it pays (2026-11-24)

Piece 3 of punch list #435 (`to-dos/lien-payoff/README.md`), built the same day as pieces 1 and 2 on the owner's *Yes, build 3*. A payer hands over one check for several bills on several jobs at one place. Until now the office typed it bill by bill, the check number once for each, and nothing added the rows up to the check.

## What it does

In the Lien desk's Payoffs window, a place where a job with a lien on record still has an open bill gets a press beside its headline: **One check for this place ›**. It opens the check pane in the window, in place of the list ([`LienPayoffCheck.tsx`](../../src/components/jobs/LienPayoffCheck.tsx)).

- **The check, typed once**: *Check amount*, *Check number*, *Received*. The amount starts on what pays the place's liens off, so the office checks a figure and does not work one out.
- **What this check pays**: the place's open bills, the liened jobs' bills first, each job's oldest bill first, then the bills on jobs with no lien. The app places the check in that order, to the cent. Each amount is a value changed where it sits, and the rest of the check is spread again around what a person keeps. **Spread it again** drops what was kept by hand.
- **Three totals**: *The check*, *Placed*, *Left to place*.
- **One sentence**: *Pays all 3 liens' bills in full.*, or *Pays 2 of 3 liens' bills in full.*, with *$3,545.00 of the check is not placed.* when it is larger than the bills.
- **One press**, in the batch rule's words: **Record 4 payments**, or **Record 3 payments · 1 pays part of a Stripe bill ▸**. It is dead only with a reason: no amount, no check number, or lines that add up to more than the check.

## What it holds, and why

A line that pays **part of a Stripe bill** is held, with *Record it on the bill ›*. A part payment there is a credit note Stripe never takes back, so it goes through the bill's own window, where the office reads what it will do. A whole Stripe bill paid by check is a ledger row only, and Stripe closes it once the check has had its days, exactly as the bill's own window does (`heldStripeMark.ts`).

## The write

No migration and no new function. Each ready line is one `mark_invoice_paid`, the function the bill's own *Record payment* calls: type Check, the check's number, the day received, and a note that names the whole check, *Payoff check 1184, $16,455.50 over 4 bills*, so the rows of one check can be told from any other ([`lienPayoffCheckIo.ts`](../../src/lib/jobs/lienPayoffCheckIo.ts)). The function refuses an amount over what the bill still owes, so a line pressed twice cannot pay a bill twice. The lines are written one after another and the run keeps going past a line that fails. A write is tried once: a lost reply is not answered with a second try.

When every line took and none was held, the pane closes on the list, where each paid lien now reads *Make the release ›*. Otherwise it stays and lists each line as *Recorded*, *Not recorded* with the server's reason, or *Held*, each with its door.

The card proposed a check-mark column and one function writing every row in one transaction. With a payoff in hand that day, the existing function a line at a time shipped with no database push, and the batch rule wants a run that keeps going past a failure in any case. The shared number, day and note are the mark. A column can follow if a returned check needs to find its rows by more than those.

## Money open with no lien

Payoffs now also reads the other billed jobs of the same customers. One at a liened place shows under the place's headline: *$640.00 more is open here on 2071. It is not liened.* It is never part of what pays the liens off, and in the check pane its bills come last and start at nothing.

## The rule

[`lienPayoffCheck.ts`](../../src/lib/jobs/lienPayoffCheck.ts), pure, 14 tests: `checkBills`, `liensPayoffAmount`, `spreadCheck` (in cents), `checkPlan`, `parseCheckAmount`, `payoffCheckNote`. `lienPayoff.ts` gains `lienPayoffBills` (a job's sent bills that still owe, under the one payment rule) and the place's `other` jobs, with three more tests.

## What it does not do yet

- A check is the only kind of money the pane records. Cash, a wire or a card goes on each bill as before.
- It does not open the returned-check case's eyes: a check that comes back is taken off its jobs in Accounts Receivable as today, one case, and nothing there names a release made on it. That is piece 6.
- When the bank later shows the deposit, Accounts Receivable offers *Link that payment instead* a row at a time.
- No payment was recorded through it against live data. The first real check is its first write.
