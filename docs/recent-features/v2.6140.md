# v2.6140 — A write-up's attendance card shows ninety days of dots, each late sized by its minutes (2026-10-30)

Punch list #217, "Show it, don't say it", finding people-docs-3, picked by the owner on 2026-10-30 ("I really like this view. Let's build it."). The Write-ups attendance card (`/people?tab=writeups` → one person in Subject → *Show attendance (derived)*) read *Late 6× (1 excused) · median 18m · NCNS 1 · On time 52 of 59 scheduled days*, and its write-up door prefilled a sentence of dates. The reader could not see which days, against which block, or how the 15-minute grace fell, before a disciplinary paper started from them. No migration, no edge function.

## What changed

- **Kernel** `src/lib/people/attendanceStrip.ts` (`attendanceStrip`, tested): the person's ninety days to today, one cell a day — kind (`onTime` ring, `late` amber dot, `excused` grey slash, `ncns` red diamond, `noClockIn` faint ring, `none`), the late dot's size by minutes past grace (`attendanceDotSize`: ≤15 small, ≤45 mid, else big), the last thirty days flagged `recent`, each marked day's words and receipt (the block's start and job, the first clock-in, the minutes past the grace, the excuse with who and when, the NCNS with who filed it and its note). It also returns the axis labels (the first day, each month's first, the recent edge, today; a month's first within six columns of another label is dropped), the counts as chips with hover words (`attendanceCountChips`), the warning flag (three unexcused lates in the recent run, as before), and the write-up's context lines. Salaried is read from the sessions: any `origin = 'salary_schedule'` row in the window, and then no day is marked late or on time, the strip says why, and a filed NCNS still draws.
- **`scheduleLateness.ts`** (additive): `PersonDayLateness` carries `scheduledStartMinutes`, `clockInMinutes` and `jobLabel` for the receipt; `earliestStartByCell` and `firstClockInByCell` are exported (the by-cell kernel now reads them); `minutesToClock` is exported; `LatenessSessionRow.origin` and `SALARY_SESSION_ORIGIN`; `fetchClockInsForUsersInRange` selects `origin`.
- **Card** `src/components/writeups/AttendanceStripCard.tsx` (new, pure, render-smoked): the chips at the head, the strip as a grid of day buttons (today outlined blue, the recent run shaded amber, weekends washed), the labels under it, a press on a marked day opening its receipt under the strip with a Close, the legend, and the amber warning with *Start a tardiness write-up →*. Theme tokens throughout.
- **`WriteupsContractsSubTab`** draws the card from the kernel over the rows it already loads; the fetch window is the strip's ninety days (`ymdAddDays(today, -89)`); the excuses carry the author and the day; the NCNS rows carry who filed them, when and the note. The prefilled `contextNote` is the kernel's lines joined, and **`WriteupEditorModal`** renders it `pre-line`, so the write-up's context reads one late a line: *Tue, Oct 27: block 7:00 AM on J996, clocked in 7:35 AM, 20 minutes past grace.* `computeAttendanceSummaryForUser` stays exported and tested; the tab no longer reads it.
- **Guide** *see a person's lates and start a write-up* (new, Office) walks the card: the chips, the strip's marks, the receipt, the salaried line and the write-up door. *schedule people onto jobs* → Who came in late and *find my way around the People page* → Paperwork link to it.

## Not done, and why

- The receipt opens under the strip rather than as a pop over the dot (the mock-up's second pass had the pop run off the frame's edge); the facts are the same and nothing can run off a phone's edge.
- The on-time rings stay, as the mock-up's second pass kept them: the gaps, days with no block, are what make a late day readable against the roster.
