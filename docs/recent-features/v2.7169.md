# v2.7169 — People → Subs: a sheet with a sub and teammates counts for the sub, once (2026-11-13)

Client only. No migration, no edge function, and no stored row changed.

The owner asked what makes up what we owe each sub, and passed on the office's report that the app encourages double counting when two people are on a job. The read of prod (88 sub sheets, 2026-11-13) found no dollar owed twice. It found two pages that disagreed about one sheet, and a panel that told the office to fix sheets that were right.

## What was wrong

- **Jobs → Subs → Pay** names a sheet by its roster sub (`sheetParties`): a person of kind `sub` with no login or a `subcontractor` login (`isRosterSub`). Anyone else on the sheet is a teammate who rode along, shown as *with …*, and paid through payroll. A sheet with teammates and no sub is **Crew pay**.
- **People → Subs** counted every active person of kind `sub` on the sheet's junction rows as an owner (`buildSubsHqRows`). A teammate carries kind `sub` too, behind a team login. So a sheet with one sub and a teammate read as *Multiple subs*, and its money was left off every Balance due.
- On prod that was 8 sheets, $4,350 agreed, 7 of them paid in full and one with $2,150 open. The Pay view showed that $2,150 as owed to one sub. People → Subs showed that sub as never worked, with no balance. The page's Balance due column summed to $11,250 against the Pay view's $13,400.
- The amber panel listed all 8, plus 2 crew sheets as *No roster match*, under the words *Their open balances are missing from every sub's Balance due column until each sheet is linked*. Its **Assign…** on a *Multiple subs* row replaces every name on the sheet with one. The other way to give each person a balance is a second sheet with the same lines, which would owe the money twice. No such pair of sheets exists on prod.

## What changed

- **`buildSubsHqRows`** (`src/lib/people/subsHqRows.ts`) takes the whole roster with each login's role (`roster`, the list `useRosterSubKinds` builds for the Pay view) and sets the teammates aside before it counts a sheet's owners. One sub left: the sheet is theirs, whole, once. Two or more subs left: `shared`, on nobody's row. Nobody left and every name resolved to a teammate: the new reason `crew`. A name the roster does not know beside a teammate keeps the sheet `unmatched`, so a sub's money cannot go quiet. One person named twice on a sheet is one person. Without `roster` the kernel behaves as before.
- **`PeopleSubsTab`** reads `people` (every kind) and `users` (every role) once each, in place of the two filtered reads, and passes the roster. The rows of the page are still the kind `sub` people. Crew sheets leave the amber panel and get one grey line with a link to Jobs → Subs → Pay.
- **Tests**: ten kernel cases under *a sheet with several names counts once* (a sub and two teammates, two subs, a payment and a backcharge on a mixed sheet, a paid mixed sheet, crew pay, one person written twice, an archived sub with a teammate, an unknown name, and a mixed book where every open dollar is in exactly one place), and one render case.
- **Guide** `review-your-subs`: what *Multiple subs* means now, the teammate rule, the crew line, and a warning against a second sheet with the same lines.

## What it does on prod

Read with the kernels in a local page against prod, before and after: the amber panel goes from 10 sheets to none, one sub's Balance due goes from nothing to $2,150, another sub's track record gains 7 settled sheets, and the column sums to $13,400, the Pay view's *Owed to subs*. An assistant reads the same roles a dev does for the people on these sheets, so the page reads the same for the office.
