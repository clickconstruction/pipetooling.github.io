# v2.8677 — Recently deleted: a deleted test person can be put back (2026-11-26)

Punch list #443, row 12: one of the take-backs that need a migration, on the owner's answer of 2026-11-25 (*All eight*), each push on its own yes.

## What was left

**Delete…** on a person made by mistake (`delete_fresh_person`, v2.6789) removes four rows: the pay row and the workday template the Hire form wrote, the People record and the users row. Deleted records kept a copy of the People record and of the pay row, since those two tables carry the archive trigger. It kept none of the users row and none of the template. A People record may not stand without its users row (`people_needs_users_row`), so the copy that was kept could never go back: **Preview restore** on the person's line answered with that trigger's refusal. The delete's confirm said *for good*, and it was.

## What changed

- **Migration `20261127022551_deleted_test_person_put_back.sql`**, two functions and nothing else: no table, column, policy, grant on a table, trigger or row, and no table locked. Page: `docs/migrations/20261127022551_deleted_test_person_put_back.md`.
  - `delete_fresh_person(p_user_id, p_dry_run)` is replaced in place with the same gates, scan and deletes. For a test person, one whose `users.name` or `users.card_name` starts with ZZ, it first writes a copy of the users row and of the workday template into `deleted_records_archive` under the People record's id. The answer gains `copy_kept`, on a dry run too. Anyone else is deleted as before, with no copy of the users row.
  - `restore_deleted_test_person(p_group_key, p_dry_run)` is new, a developer alone and a test person alone. It puts the users row, the People record, the pay row and the workday template back in one transaction, the users row first, and answers in the envelope `restore_deleted_records` answers. It refuses, each as an answer with its sentence: nobody signed in, anyone but a dev, a blank key, a bundle with no users copy, a name that does not start with ZZ, a person on the roster already, an email another person holds, a People record that stands and a pay row that stands under the name.
- `src/lib/deletedRecordsRestoreDoor.ts`, new and pure: `restoreFunctionFor(bundle)` answers `restore_deleted_test_person` for a bundle of kind `person` whose tables hold `users`, and `restore_deleted_records` for every other bundle.
- `src/lib/deletedRecordsArchive.ts`: `restoreDeletedRecords(groupKey, dryRun, fn)` takes the function's name. The old call is unchanged. The new name is called as an untyped RPC until the types PR after the push, and its real run is sent once.
- `src/hooks/useDeletedRecordsArchive.ts`: `runPreview` and `runRestore` take the bundle, not the key alone, and pick the function with `restoreFunctionFor`. `DeletedRecordsSection.tsx` passes the bundle. Nothing else in the section changed: it already draws the answer's error, blockers, warnings and counts.
- `src/lib/deletedRecordContents.ts`: three table names gain their words, *account row*, *workday template* and *pay row*, so a person's line reads its chips in words.
- `src/lib/people/freshPersonDelete.ts`: `readFreshDeleteAnswer` reads `copy_kept` into `copyKept`, and an answer without it is no copy. `freshDeleteConfirmWords(name, removed, copyKept)` says, when a copy is kept, *This is a test person, so a copy is kept for 90 days. A developer can put them back from Recently deleted.* and leaves out *for good*. With no copy the words are as they were, to the letter. The 90 days are the archive's own purge (`20260716120000`).
- The guides *hire someone*, *archive user accounts* and *recover a deleted job or bid* each say it. The glossary and `docs/ACCESS_CONTROL.md` each gain their line.

## Who sees it

The put-back is a dev's: Recently deleted is drawn for a dev and the function gates on `is_dev()` itself. The confirm's new words are read by whoever may delete a person made by mistake, which is a dev, the controller or a pay-approved leader, and only on a ZZ-named person. Nothing changes on a real person.

## Order

The client calls a name only this migration creates, so `check:calls-ahead` labels the PR *push right after the merge* and the deploy holds until the push. Before the push the client is safe: an answer with no `copy_kept` reads as no copy, so the confirm says *for good*, which is true of the old function, and no bundle holds a users copy, so none is sent to the new name.

## Known and not covered

- A real person deleted by mistake still cannot be put back.
- A person deleted before the push has no users copy, so their line keeps the old function and its refusal.
- The archive's 90-day purge takes the copies.
- A put-back whose pay row carries pay leaves one line on the pay log, by `screen`, through the pay row's own trigger (`20261126033215`). A preview leaves none.
- A column added to one of the four tables after the copy was taken is left out of the put-back's insert, so it takes its default. A `NOT NULL` column added with no default still refuses the put-back, in the database's own message. `restore_deleted_records` names every column and has the older limit.
- The pay row keeps a line of its own in Recently deleted, under the person's name. The put-back takes the pay row from there and that line then leaves the list. The person's line is the one to press.
