# v2.6213 — The business-records affidavit as a preset on the statement window (2026-10-31)

Punch list #220 (`to-dos/legal-desk-affidavits/`), PR 6 of six, the owner's fifth answer (2026-10-30: build it if it adds value). Its value: Tex. R. Evid. 902(10) makes a business record self-authenticating when this affidavit rides with it and both are served fourteen days before trial, so the firm puts our invoices, the clock and the job thread into evidence without calling anyone to testify; it asks for one on every account that reaches suit.

- **`lib/legal/businessRecords.ts`** (new, pure): Rule 902(10)(B)'s preface and six paragraphs word for word (`BUSINESS_RECORDS_FORM`, from the Supreme Court's order of August 19, 2014, Misc. Docket No. 14-9174, read from the order itself), `businessRecordsParagraphs` (the plain reading of each with the company and the standing filled in, the page count or the blank, and after paragraph 2 one paragraph naming the records attached — the form is not exclusive, and the firm should not guess), `businessRecordsTitle`, `businessRecordsRole`, `BUSINESS_RECORDS_SERVICE_WORDS` (Rule 902(10)(A)'s fourteen days).
- **The window**: on a new statement, **Start from** — *What someone saw or heard* (as before) or *Business records, Rule 902(10)*. The preset asks the affiant's standing (the custodian of records, an employee, an owner), the page count and **The records attached** as pressed chips over the matter's Evidence rows (the desk hands the window `documents`); each pick rewrites the title, the role line and the paragraphs until the office edits the paragraphs by hand, when the chips say so and stop. The opener, the form, the county, the print faces and the filing are the statement's own.
