# v2.8157 — Write someone up: the ground for a write-up from a phone (2026-11-21)

Punch list #410, PR 1 of the train in [`to-dos/write-someone-up/`](../../to-dos/write-someone-up/README.md). One migration, [`20261121131500_person_report_write_up`](../migrations/20261121131500_person_report_write_up.md), and its bed. No screen changes.

## Why

Taunya said on 2026-11-20 that writing someone up was not made easy enough for a leader or a superintendent from their Dashboard. The assessment found a leader's only door 8.7 phone screens down the full Dashboard, and no door at all for a superintendent: the row rule on `person_reports` names leaders and devs. The record also kept only who, the day and the words. On 2026-11-21 she said to build the whole train.

The phone sheet needs three things from the database before it can be drawn. This PR is those three, alone, so the screens that follow call names that already exist on prod.

## What changed

- **`person_reports` gains twelve columns**, all nullable or defaulted: the kind, the job and its name as the writer saw it, the time of day, the writer's role, whether the writer saw it themselves, whether they were on the clock at that job, who else saw it, whether the person was told, the report this one adds to, the phone's per-press id and when Send was pressed.
- **`write_up_people()`** answers the list the sheet opens on. The roster without the caller, without sample accounts and twins. Each person carries the newest job-day they shared with the caller in the last seven days, with the job and their first clock-in. A superintendent's shared days are the job-days of their assigned projects. It also answers the caller's own jobs of those days.
- **`land_person_report(p)`** is the one door a send lands through, for a leader, a superintendent or a dev. It refuses a write-up about oneself, a kind off the list, no words, and a day after today, each in words a person can read. The writer's name, role and on-the-clock fact are worked out on the server. A press that arrives twice lands once.
- **`withdraw_person_report(id, reason)`** lets the writer take back a report that still waits. The row and its words stay. It reads as dismissed with *Taken back by the writer:* and the reason.

## Decisions

- **Built on the HR report queue, not on Writeups.** A report's subject is a roster row, so a person with no login can be written about. An author reads only their own. Nothing reaches a file until the office files it.
- **The function is the superintendent's door.** The table's insert rule is left as it is. A second way to insert would be a second place for the rules to drift.
- **A take-back is a dismissal with its own words.** The Timeline, the queue and the dev seat know three states. A fourth would have needed every one of them taught, and a deploy of the seat.
- **An addition is a second report.** It points at the first with `adds_to`, waits in the same queue and is filed the same way. No new table.
- **One argument, a JSON object.** The phone keeps a send in its outbox as the object it will send, and a later key needs no new signature.

## Verified

- `npm run test:pg:person-report-write-up` is the bed, 42 checks: the list for a leader, a superintendent and the office; a send with its facts; a press twice; every refusal; an addition; a take-back. Docker is not running on the Mac, so before the PR it ran on a stand-in rig, `postgresql@15` with a stub of the tables it touches, the two real migrations and this one applied twice. CI's bed job is its first run on the whole schema.
