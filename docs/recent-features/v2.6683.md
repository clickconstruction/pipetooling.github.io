Punch list #125 (`to-dos/pipeline-customer-conversation/README.md`), PR 1 of four, on the owner's pick of 2026-11-08: option (a), the customer's lines in the row's activity box. This PR is the read alone; PR 2 draws it. One migration (`20261108030000_pipeline_customer_lines.sql`, `docs/migrations/20261108030000_pipeline_customer_lines.md`); no edge function; no guide change, since nothing on screen changes yet.

## What changed

- **The read** `pipeline_customer_lines(p_job_ids uuid[], p_per_job int default 3)`, `SECURITY INVOKER`, `STABLE`: per job the newest `p_per_job` (1 to 10) calls, texts, emails and portal lines between us and the people on it, under Job activity's rule (`loadJobMessages` → `jobActivityMessagesFromRows`) and the customer sheet's (`lastLinesOnJob`): every line tied to the job; while the job is not paid, the untied lines of the customers on it (`contact_jobs`) since the job began; never a note, an event or a team line. Each row carries who the line is with (kind, name, goes by, the customer's record naming a nameless contact), who of ours wrote it, a call's length, summary and words, Quo's delivery mark, the app's own-line mark and `tied`. Words come back cut at 400 characters. The messages policy (`gc_office_team()`) decides who reads, so a tech's call returns no rows. A partial index `messages_job_lines (jobs_ledger_id, at desc)` serves the tied side.
- **The kernel** `src/lib/jobs/pipelineCustomerLines.ts` (4 tests): `pipelineCustomerLineRow` reads a wire row, `pipelineCustomerLinesByJob` folds the rows to one `JobActivityMessage[]` per job, oldest first, through `jobActivityMessagesFromRows` itself, so a line's who, words and tone are the ones the expanded Job activity draws, and `lastLinesOnJob` over them gives the sheet's three.
- **The IO** `src/lib/messages/pipelineCustomerLinesIo.ts`: `loadPipelineCustomerLines(jobIds, perJob)` in slices of 200 (the stats' slice) under `withSupabaseRetry`; PostgREST's *could not find the function* (the client deployed before the push) reads as no lines, never a failure.
- **The hook** `src/hooks/useBoardCustomerLines.ts` (3 render tests), the dot's shape (`useBoardDots`): `refresh(ids)` is called in `JobsStagesTab`'s stats effect, on the same debounced ids at the same moment as `refreshJobThreadStatsForJobIds`; `reload()` after a send from the customer's sheet, beside the dot's; the page's one Messages channel (`useMessagesLive`) reads again when a line lands. A read that fails keeps the last lines. Off for anyone but the office (`canOpenMessages`).
- **The row context** `StagesRowRenderContext.customerLinesByJobId` (`jobsStagesRowShared.tsx`), threaded through `JobsStagesTable`, `JobsStagesUnifiedTable` and `JobsStagesCardList` as `boardDotByJobId` is. Nothing reads it yet.
- **The SQL bed** `supabase/tests/pipeline_customer_lines/20_scenario.sql` (`npm run test:pg:pipeline-customer-lines`, in `scripts/sql-beds.txt`): the same fixture as the kernel's test, the same ids; the newest three on an open job, ten brings every line the rule allows with a trade's tied text among them, the count held to 1..10, a paid job drops the untied lines, a nameless contact reads its customer's name, two jobs in one call keep apart, a tech reads nothing, no jobs no rows.

## Read closer than the card

- **The card's "between us and the job's customer"** is read as Job activity reads it: a line tied to the job counts whoever it is with (a trade's text tied to the job shows under All there, and in the sheet's last lines), while an untied line counts only for the customers on the job. The box's orange is the customer's; PR 2 draws anyone else's tied line plain, as Job activity does.
- **The card's SQL bed "proving the function and `loadJobMessages` pick the same lines"** cannot run TypeScript in Postgres. The bed and `pipelineCustomerLines.test.ts` share one fixture by hand (the same ids, times and expected order, each file naming the other), and the kernel folds through `jobActivityMessagesFromRows`, the same mapper `loadJobMessages` ends in, so the two reads differ only in which rows the database hands over, which the bed pins.
