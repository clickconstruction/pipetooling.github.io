# v2.7951 — The sample of a sub's page shows the customer paid, and how long this customer usually takes (2026-11-19)

One of the two gaps punch list #388 left when it closed on 2026-11-19. The `sub-portal` function's sample answer, a guide and the docs. No migration, no client code. The function is deployed only on the owner's go that names it, and until then the sample reads as it did.

## Why

The office learns what a sub reads from **Settings → What customers see → Sub portal**. The sample had two sheets, and its one sheet at Waiting on customer carried a payable-after date, so it read *Queued* and showed neither of #388's sentences. On 2026-11-19 every real sheet on prod stood at Work and none was tied to a bill, so the sample was the only place the office could see either sentence without staging test rows.

## What landed

- **Two more sheets**, added by `sampleSubPortalPage` (`supabase/functions/_shared/customerSampleSub.ts`) after the two `sampleSubPortalResponse` already gave. `sub-portal` now answers the sample token with it.
  - `sample-sheet-3` waits on the customer with no pay day. It carries `usualPayWeeks`, and the page draws *This customer usually pays 4 to 6 weeks after we ask.* under the waiting sentence.
  - `sample-sheet-4` waits on the customer and its bill is paid in full. It carries `customerPaidOn`, yesterday, and the page draws the green *Customer paid* chip and *The customer paid on … Your payment is next. We will give it a pay run day.*
- **Through the page's own rules, never typed.** The weeks are `usualPayWeeks(SAMPLE_SUB_PAYER_GAP_DAYS)`, six made-up gaps, one over the floor of five. The day is `customerPaidOn` on a made-up bill marked paid with one payment. If either rule changes, the sample changes with it, and below the floor the sample would say nothing.
- **The queued sheet stays.** `sample-sheet-2` still reads *Queued*, so the sample shows the three things a sheet at Waiting on customer can say.
- **The totals** hold the two sheets: *Owed to you* is the four sheets' open money added up.
- **Its own file.** `customerSampleFixtures.ts` is imported by seven other public functions, and `check:edge-drift` puts every one of them behind when it changes. The first cut of this change edited it and the check named all seven. The two sheets live in `customerSampleSub.ts`, which `sub-portal` alone reads, so one deploy covers the change.
- **The guide** `see-what-a-sub-sees-on-their-portal` has a short section, *See these sentences on the sample*.

## What it never does

- It changes nothing on a real sub's page. Only the answer for the sample token differs.
- It puts no days, no count of payments and no payer on the wire. A test reads the whole sample answer for those words.
- It adds no Spanish. Both sentences are the strings the owner read on 2026-11-18.

## Checked

- `src/lib/subPortal/sampleSubPortal.test.ts`: the sample parses, each sheet says what it should through `subPortalUsualPay` and `subPortalCustomerPaidDay`, the weeks are the rule's own answer, and the open total is the sheets added up.
- `src/pages/SubPortal.render.test.tsx`: the page drawn from the sample shows one usual pay line, one *Customer paid* sentence and one *Draw requested* chip.
- Drawn in the dev server at 375 px and 1280 px, in English and Spanish, with the page's fetch answered by this branch's sample built on the live function's letterhead and pay run. No sideways scroll, no console error.
