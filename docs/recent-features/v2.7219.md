# v2.7219 — Lien desk: By job is a tab of its own, and the papers say what they are (2026-11-13)

The owner, of the second row under By paper (*Notices · 18, Affidavits · 13, Retainage · 1, Timeline · 24*): "I don't love how [they] roll out, they are not intuitive either." He picked Set A of three: name each paper for what it is, and move Timeline up as a fourth view. It follows v2.7207, which named the first three views.

## What changed

| Was | Is |
| --- | --- |
| To do · By date · By paper | To do · By date · By paper · **By job** |
| Notices · 18 | Monthly notices · 18 |
| Affidavits · 13 | Lien affidavits · 13 |
| Retainage · 1 | Retainage notices · 1 |
| Timeline · 24 (a fourth tab under By paper) | By job (a view of its own, no count) |

- **Why Timeline moved.** Three of the four tabs were kinds of paper and the fourth was every job's path. Now the top row is one idea: the work, then the same jobs by date, by paper and by job. Only To do carries a count, as before.
- **`LienDesk.tsx`.** The view list is `next`, `calendar`, `paper`, `timeline`. `paperShown` and `lastPaperKind` no longer hold `timeline`, so By paper reopens on the paper last looked at and never on the book. By job has no second row; on a computer its second line is the tool row, as on By date. Each paper's tab has a hover line that names its section of the law.
- **Nothing in the code is renamed, and no address moved.** `kind` keeps its six values. `?tab=timeline` opens By job. `?tab=notices`, `affidavits` and `retainage` open their papers under By paper. `data-lien-desk-view="timeline"` is the new tab's hook.
- **The one-line title bar starts at 1,540 px, not 1,500.** v2.7213 puts the tools on the title line in a window wide enough for the longest line the desk can draw. The fourth tab widens the views from 259 to 290 px (measured with a three-figure count), so that line is 1,528.6 px and `LIEN_DESK_ONE_LINE_MIN_WIDTH` is 1,540 (`lienDeskTitleBar.ts`, its test, the wide-window smoke and the map). A 1,512 px laptop, which had one line since v2.7213, keeps the two lines it had before it. Agreed with the header's session, which dropped its own change to that number.
- **Doors that name a tab**: *Open it on Monthly notices ›* and *Open it on Lien affidavits ›* on a stop's window, the By date draft button's hover line, the retainage pane's line about the Lien affidavits tab, and the Dashboard's `lien-suit-year` card (*Open By job*, *The Lien desk's By job tab lists each job with its date.*).
- **The card behind ?** has eight rows: By paper names its three papers, and By job is new.
- **Nine guides**, `GLOSSARY.md`, `PROJECT_DOCUMENTATION.md`, `twins/APP_DIRECTORY.md` and the map. The desk guide's section is *The four views*, and *The Timeline tab* is *The By job tab*. No link pointed at either heading.

## Words left as they were, and why

- **The list of notices is still "the Notices list".** *The Notices list*, *the Affidavits pane* and *← Notices* on a phone say what is in the list. Only the tab's own name changed: the word before "tab", the word in bold in a lien guide, and *on Notices* in a door.
- **The rails on To do** (Notices, Reminders, Affidavits, Retainage) and **the lien month's zones** (Reminders, Notices, Liens, Catch-up). They were not asked about, and the rail's label column is narrow.
- **The Legal desk's Timeline tab**, a customer's Timeline and a job's Cost Timeline are other things.
- **The printed help sheet** says *It opens the Affidavits list*. That is the list, not the tab.
- **No edge function's words changed.** The status message's sections already read *Retainage notices*.

## The script

`scripts/codemods/lien-desk-view-names.mjs` gains a second step. All four old names are everyday words, so its rules run only in the fourteen files it lists (`PAPER_FILES`), each read first. The sentences that needed rewriting are in its `SENTENCES` list, and it now runs rules and sentences again until nothing moves, so one sentence's new words can be the next one's old ones. The code change in `LienDesk.tsx` and the tests' tab names are by hand.

## On a phone
