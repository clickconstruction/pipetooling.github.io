# v2.8731 — A job's burn prices its labor the way the bid did (2026-11-27)

Punch list #448 (`to-dos/burn-labor-at-the-burden.md`). Client only. No migration, no edge function, no kernel the edge copies. Nothing owed after the merge.

**The ask.** Taunya, 2026-11-27, asked *Should burn price labor at the burden too?* with an example of 100 hours at a $30 wage, budgeted as $3,600 and burning as $3,000: *Yes, burn at the burden*. A mock-up of the Costs tab drew two homes for the one new line, and the person at the keyboard that day picked A, *Picks stand*: the line on the **Spent so far** tile and in the Labor row of the by-section table.

**What was wrong.** A job's budget prices its labor hours at the crew rate, the average field wage times the burden factor (`app_settings.labor_burden_factor_v1`, 1.20). The burn added up team labor at raw wages. One comparison used two rulers, and the burn read about a sixth kinder than the bid on labor.

**What changed.**

- **One multiply, on the way in.** `src/lib/jobs/burnAtBurden.ts` is a new pure kernel. `chargeEventsAtBurden` hands back a job's charge events with each `team_labor` event at its wage times the factor, and every other event as it is. The burn is built from that list.
- **`jobBurn.ts` is not touched.** It is copied to the edge functions, and a change inside it would leave every function that bundles it reading as behind. The card said to try the loader first for that reason. `npm run gen:edge-kernels -- --check` reads the copies as matching.
- **The factor is read once a visit** by `useLaborBurdenFactor`, the same key and the same parse as the crew rate and the scorecard. A missing setting, a failed read or a value outside 1 to 3 is 1.20.
- **Where the burn is priced at it:**
  - a job's window, the Costs tab: the four tiles, the by-section table, the chart's cost line and the daily bars in the folded detail;
  - the printed costs packet, which reads the same verdict and burn;
  - Job Summary: the Burn column and the projected margins that read it;
  - the Pipeline's burn card;
  - Bids → Bid Costs → Bid vs actual, which builds each linked job's verdict with the Costs tab's own calls.
- **The one new line.** On the **Spent so far** tile: *labor $3,000 wages × 1.20 = $3,600*, its three values drawn apart from its words (`ValueSentence` over `burnLaborLineWords`). The by-section table's Labor row reads *100 h · $3,000 wages × 1.20* in place of *team hours × wages*, and the hourly rate beside it is the wage at the burden. The packet prints the same line as plain words.
- **Settings → Data & recovery → Labor burden factor (dev)** said a job's burn reads wages as paid. It now lists a job's burn under *Where it reaches*, seven lines, and **Where the money went** under *Where it does not*.

**What did not change.** Where the money went and its Team labor line. The Cost Timeline's lines. Job Summary's Labor, Gross and True profit columns. Crew P&L. Pay stubs. The overhead pool. Subs and parts inside the burn. The line budgets under the verdict. The baseline strip's *average wage*, which stays the wages paid, since it is what a job gives the labor book.

**GC mode's own-work cost** (`src/lib/gc/ownWorkCost.ts`) was read before the build, as the card asked. It adds up a Pipeline job's charge events with `spendByComponent` and does not read the burn. It is a job's spend at wages paid, and it is left so.

**A job that read on budget may now read over.** That is the change working. A finished job's Burn on Job Summary is no longer its Gross profit under another name: the Burn column is at the burden and Gross is at wages.

**Checked.** `burnAtBurden.test.ts` holds the card's example: 100 hours at 30 burn 3,600 of labor where they burned 3,000, subs and parts unchanged, a factor of 1 gives the old numbers, and a factor that is missing or under 1 reads as 1. `jobCostsVerdict.test.ts`, `bidVsActualBurn.test.ts`, `jobSummaryLedgerView.test.ts` and `JobCostsVerdict.render.test.tsx` hold the row, the tile's line and the Burn column. `laborBurdenWords.test.ts` holds the Settings lines. A focused typecheck and lint ran on the changed files. **Not run here:** the whole suite and the whole typecheck, which CI runs. **Not walked live.**

## Is this the best we can do?

- **The line budgets under the verdict still read labor at wages.** `JobCostsLineBudgets` takes the spend by component as it is. Each line's budget is hours at the crew rate, so the same two rulers may meet there. It was not on the card and is not changed. It wants a read and its own call.
- **The first draw can use 1.20 before the read lands.** The factor's default stands until the setting is read, once a visit. The two differ only if the setting is ever moved off 1.20, and then for a moment.
- **One factor for everyone**, as on a bid. A crew with a higher real burden reads kinder than it is.
