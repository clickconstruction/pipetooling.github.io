The press is older than the card. Client only. No migration, no edge function, and no kernel an edge function copies. Nothing owed after the merge.

**Read.** `BridgeRule.tsx` kept one `open` flag with four writers: the line's `onMouseEnter` and `onMouseLeave`, the button's `onFocus` and `onBlur`, and the button's `onClick`, which toggled. So one press did two things in order. The pointer arrived or the button took focus, which opened the reading, and then the click toggled it shut. A second press opened it. A tap on a phone is an arrival and then a click, so the first tap showed nothing.

**The mend.** A press is kept apart from a look.

- `src/lib/bridge/ruleReading.ts`, new and pure: the state is `{ open, held }` and `ruleReadingAfter` takes four events. A **look** (hover, focus) opens the reading, and the look ending shuts it, as before. A **press** on a reading no press has taken keeps it open, whether or not a look had opened it, and the next press shuts it, though the pointer still rests there. **Dismiss** shuts it.
- `BridgeRule.tsx` feeds it through `useReducer`. While the reading is open the page listens for Escape and for a press off the line and its reading, and each dismisses. A press on the reading's own words is not a press outside; where the browser takes the focus off the **?** on such a press, the lost focus shuts the reading as it did before.
- Hover and focus are as they were for a mouse and a keyboard, the brief's ask.

**What a person sees that is new.** One press opens the reading. Escape shuts it, which no key did before. A press off the line shuts it, where before only the lost focus did, in the browsers that give a pressed button the focus. All three are the brief's. **Differs from the brief: nowhere.** The pointer leaving and the focus leaving shut the reading as they always did, pressed open or not.

**The guide.** *The Bridge* said nothing of the press. Three short sentences under the doors' list now name it.
