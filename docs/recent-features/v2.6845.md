# v2.6845 — A customer who pays in kind carries a chip beside the name (punch list #301, mock-up 2) (2026-11-10)

The owner, once the first arrangement was live: *where in the app should a user be able to see the in kind balance due or credit for a customer, such as next to a customer's name?* The mock-up (`to-dos/in-kind-arrangements-chip-mockup.html`) drew one chip and four places. This is the chip and the first two places. The job window and Bill Customer follow.

## What changed

- **The chip's kernel**, `src/lib/inKind/inKindChip.ts`: a customer's standing over the open deals (`inKindStandings`, from the In kind page's own rows), the chip's words (`inKindChipWords`: a month due first, then what is to come, then the work we owe; a short form), what it reads out, the door to the ledger, and an open balance split in two (`moneyApart`: what a deal pays, and the money).
- **One shared read**, `src/lib/inKind/inKindStandingIo.ts` over `useInKindStandings`: the In kind page's read with the bank, held for a minute, dropped when a deal changes. Office only, since the deals are the office's to read. A failed read shows no chip and holds no screen.
- **`InKindChip`**: quiet blue on schedule, amber when a month is due, a link to the deal's ledger on the customer's page. A plain form with no press for a spot that is already a press.
- **The customer's page**: the chip beside the name.
- **The Customers list**: the chip beside the name (the short form under the name on a phone); *Total open balance* and *Owes money* count the money part only, with *$X more is scheduled in kind* under the total; the row's *owes* chip is the money part; an **In kind (N)** filter (`?inkind=1`), shown when anyone pays this way; the rail's first cell reads *paid in kind* when all of it was.

## Verified

Kernel and render tests. Walked on prod on the first arrangement: the list's total fell by the deal's open amount with the line under it, *Owes money* lost the customer, *In kind (1)* listed it, the chip opened the ledger, and the customer's page carries the chip.
