# v2.8324 — Customers: a merge carries everything linked to the other customer (2026-11-23)

The last of punch list #307's leftovers: *merging customer rows that are one GC*. The owner's word on 2026-11-23, asked whether a merge should carry every reference or the GC's ties only: *every reference*. Migration `20261123040000_merge_customers_every_reference.sql`, no edge function.

## What was there

**Edit customer → Merge with another customer** (`merge_customers`) moved ten references by name, then deleted the other row. Read on prod, read only: 46 columns point at a customer by a foreign key. The other 36 went with the row. Its properties, portal links and portal address, the names on permits tied to it, its GC statement marks, pay calendar and difficulty ratings were deleted by cascade. A job's GC link (349 jobs name a GC today), the file index, sent papers, payment promises, legal matters and owner record requests were left blank. A legal matter also names its payer by text, `c:` and the customer's id, and the app finds a customer's matter by that key, so even a moved matter would have been lost to the customer it moved to.

## What changed

- **Every reference moves.** The merge reads the foreign keys off the catalog, so a table added later is carried from its first day. Five references with no foreign key move too: a portal page view, a legal matter's payer key, a word ask's list of GCs, and an open dispatch or estimator request's payload. A closed request, the dev seat's call log and an RFI's sent-to list record what already happened, and stay as written.
- **Where two rows may not both hold a thing, a rule says which stands.** The kept customer keeps its own portal address, follow-up setting and pay calendar. A second live portal link, open proposal room or sent paper is turned off, closed or taken off, and kept. For a week's statement mark or a word ask's answer, the newer word stands. A duplicate listing or due date on one bid is dropped. A property both rows hold folds into one when the other's copy holds nothing more. Any other property moves whole, with its legal record.
- **Nothing is dropped out of sight.** Every row a rule drops is kept in the deleted records, in the same bundle as the customer row that went, where a dev reads it or brings it back.
- **The window says it all first.** Under the six counts it used to show, it now lists everything the other customer holds, largest first, folded after six kinds behind a press. Then it lists what each rule will do, the portal address that stops working, and the jobs that will name the GC only.
- **Two legal matters stop a merge.** A payer holds one matter. The window says so and the merge press stays dead, and the database refuses it too. The words say to ask a dev to join the two matters.
- **A last check before the delete.** If anything still names the row that goes, the merge is refused and nothing is changed. A table that lets a customer hold a single row, with no rule yet, stops the merge with its name.
- After a merge, the note says how many linked records moved.

## How it is built

- The migration: `customer_reference_columns` (the catalog's list), `merge_customers_keyless` (the five with no key, counted or moved), `merge_customers_exceptions` (the rules, one definition for the preview and the merge), `merge_customers_drop` (drops into the archive), `merge_customers_plan` (what the preview answers), and `merge_customers` itself. `docs/migrations/20261123040000_merge_customers_every_reference.md` has each.
- Three guards refused the merge's move and now let it through, and nothing else: a bid version locked by someone else, a document send, a difficulty rating. Each asks `customer_merge_moves_only`, true while the merge's transaction flag is up and the row differs in its customer column alone. The flag is the one accepted estimates already use, and the merge lowers it before it returns.
- `src/lib/customers/customerMergePlan.ts`: the plan in words, a line for every reference the database counts and one for every rule. A table it has no words for shows by its own name. `src/components/customers/CustomerMergePlan.tsx` draws it in `EditCustomerForm.tsx`, which shows the six counts as before when the database has no plan yet.

## How the references were found

- The foreign keys: `pg_constraint`, on prod.
- The rest: every list-of-ids column (37), every text column named like a key, an id or a ref (332), and every JSON column of a table under 60,000 rows (198), each read for a value that holds a customer's id, by count only.

## Tests

- The bed `merge-customers`, on the whole schema in CI: part A as it was, and part B, where an assistant merges a GC typed twice. The other row holds something in every kind of place a customer is named, a locked bid version, a rating and a live send among them. Afterwards nothing names it. What moved, what each rule did, what the archive holds and what the preview said beforehand read as laid down. The three guards still refuse an edit with the flag down and up. A table the merge never heard of is carried. One with a single-row rule stops the merge by name. Two rows that each hold a legal matter are refused, with nothing changed.
- The same scenario passed all 56 checks on a throwaway local Postgres holding every table the merge touches, shaped off prod's catalog.
- `customerMergePlan.test.ts` and `CustomerMergePlan.render.test.tsx`: the words, the order, the fold, the legal block, and the old answer read as no plan.

## Not done here

- A bid that names one row as its own GC and lists the other as another GC keeps both after the merge, as the same customer twice. Neither is lost.
- A legal matter on each row needs a dev to join them. No screen does it.
