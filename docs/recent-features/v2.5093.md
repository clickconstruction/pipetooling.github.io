# v2.5093 — Lien desk: the claim counts a payment with no bill picked (2026-10-09)

Client, plus migration `20261010024000_lien_claim_follows_the_rule.sql` (**`supabase db push` after merge**). No edge function.

## Why

The owner's call of 2026-10-09: *a payment put on a job with no bill picked is applied oldest bill first everywhere*. v2.5006, v2.5010 and v2.5017 brought the board, GC Review, the statement, the Dashboard and the customers onto that rule. The lien claim was left out, and v2.5006's list of readers left alone on purpose did not name it.

`lien_billed_open()` (v2.4969) and its client twin `lienBilledOpen` (v2.4970) netted each sent bill only of the payments tied to it. So a notice could claim money the board already counted as paid. Job 273 on prod is the case:
- a $56,365 job with three billed bills of $13,420, $665 and $3,500;
- $39,680 paid on the job with no bill picked;
- the board says the bills owe $16,685, and the notice claimed $17,585.

A § 53.056 notice is a sworn number, so it should never claim more than the books say is owed.

## What changed

- **The SQL** (`lien_billed_open()`, the same signature): the claim is what the sent bills still owe under `attributeJobPayments` (`_shared/paymentAttribution.ts`).
  - Linked money is its bill's, in full.
  - The job's unlinked money (each payment above zero, so a refund is never applied) pays the part of the job on no sent bill first: revenue less every sent bill, billed or paid. There is none when revenue is NULL.
  - What is left walks the sent bills, billed or paid, oldest first by `sequence_order`, then `billed_at`, then `id`.
  - The claim sums what the billed ones still need. Paid bills owe nothing; the shell arm is unchanged.
  - The desk's three readers (`list_lien_notice_months`, `list_lien_affidavit_windows`, `list_lien_retainage_windows`) call it, so they read the new claim as they are.
- **The client twin** [`lienBilledOpen`](../../src/lib/jobs/lienBilledOpen.ts) runs `attributeJobPayments` over the sent bills with the job's `revenue`. That covers the Pipeline's Deadlines runway, the Dashboard's lien reminder (`useLienWatchNudge`, which now reads each bill's `sequence_order` and `billed_at`) and Put a GC on notice's rows (`gcNoticeRowsWithBilledOpen`).
- **The bills under the claim** ([`lienClaimBills`](../../src/lib/jobs/lienClaimBills.ts), the Months card's Billed · Paid · Owed) take *Paid* from the same rule, so the owed column still adds up to the claim. Job 273's first bill reads *$900 paid · $12,520 owed*.
- **Guide** *send lien notices from the Lien desk*, *The claim is what is billed*: a payment with no bill picked counts too, paying the work not billed first, then the oldest bill.
- **Docs**: the Lien desk map's hazard and kernel row, the Glossary's Lien desk and payment-rule entries, BILLING_FLOWS (the lien claim leaves the linked-only list) and PROJECT_DOCUMENTATION's `revenue` note.

## Tests

- **`lienBilledOpen.test.ts` (7, +4):**
  - job 273's shape claims $16,685, and $38,780 (exactly the work on no bill) leaves $17,585;
  - a paid oldest bill takes its turn, by `sequence_order` and on a tie by the earlier day;
  - no revenue means oldest bill first alone;
  - a refund is never applied, and an overpay stays its bill's;
  - the GC run's row reads 273 at $16,685.
- **`lienClaimBills.test.ts` (6, +1):** job 273's bills read $900 / $0 / $0 paid and the owed column sums to $16,685.
- **SQL bed** `scripts/pgtest-lien-claim.sh` (new; `npm run test:pg:lien-claim`; wired into the SQL beds workflow). It runs the two tables as the baseline types them, the migration twice, then 16 cases with the client tests' numbers. It passed on PGlite (Postgres 18) before the PR. The v2.4969 function fails it on job 273.
- **Mutants**, each made alone and each failing named cases:
  - the claim without the job's total;
  - the Months card on tied money only;
  - the old SQL function (the bed fails at job 273).
