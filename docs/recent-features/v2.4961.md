# v2.4961 — GC mode: the schedule’s presses can save (2026-10-08)

PR 6b of the schedule's real build (`to-dos/gc-mode/SCHEDULE_REAL_BUILD.md`; its plan `to-dos/gc-mode/mockups/schedule-pr6.md`, both on branch `spike/gc-mode`): the writes. No migration and no screen.

- `src/lib/gc/schedule/writes.ts`, pure: what each press sends, built from the kernels' records. `barsForRpc(before, after)` gives every bar whose plan changed, its days always and its limits, waits or parts' days only when they changed (null takes a limit off). `moveForRpc(project, move)` gives a `ScheduleMove` as `gc_schedule_move` reads it, with each kind's own keys (a change order, a late notice, a pull, days got back, a part's move) and the bar's name that day. `draftForRpc` gives a first draft or a file's schedule. An inspection's and the job's own work's kernel ids get row keys, and every wait that names them is pointed at the new key. A line keeps its scope line's id. `addedForRpc` gives the job's own work with its row key, the bars that wait on it and what that pushed. `partsForRpc`, `failureForRpc`, `placesForRpc`, `theirDatesForRpc` and `waitForRpc` give the rest. The plain writes' rows (`walkRowOf`, `crewMarkRowOf`, `verifiedMarkOf`, `milestoneRowOf`, `waitStepOf`, `pushBackOf`, `templateRowOf`, `roughRowOf`, `whatIfRowOf`, `actualDatesOf`) are in the shape `rows.ts` reads back.
- `src/lib/gc/scheduleIo.ts` gains one function per press, each returning the job read back through `loadSchedule`. The plan writes send the version the press read and the press's words (`SchedulePress`). A stale press throws the `DatabaseError` that `scheduleChangedRefusal` reads. The records (places, an inspection passed, their dates, a wait) call PR 5's functions with no version. The plain writes cover the walk, a bar's real days, the job's own work done, a date to meet set or taken off, the superintendent's check, our crew's mark, a wait's steps and its removal, the push back on a late notice, a template saved, renamed or set aside, the rough, and the person's own what-if copy made, tried in or thrown away. A write keyed to a row that is gone says so in plain words.
- A template's name: `templateSaveProblem` and `templateNameProblem` first. A second person saving the same name at the same moment meets the table's unique index (`23505` on `gc_schedule_templates_name_once`) and reads the kernel's own words, now one constant, `TEMPLATE_NAME_TAKEN`.
- `writes.direct.test.ts`: Fair Oaks Shops, Building D moved every way the kernels move a bar (a drag, the editor's waits and limits, a limit taken off, a change order, a late notice, a pull, days got back side by side and by a second crew, a part inside its line and one past it). Each move and its bars agree the way `gc_schedule_move` checks them. A first draft gets row keys for its inspections with its waits pointed at them. A draft from a template carries its place, parts and template. The job's own work names its key everywhere. The plain writes' rows read back through the mapper as the kernel's records. Every key PR 5's functions read (`->>`, `->`, `?`, `#>>`) is a key some press sends to that function; Keep's `fromWhatIfOn` is the server's own.
- Known gap, for the next schedule migration: `gc_schedule_walks_looked` counts `kept` and `move_ids` only, so a walk whose only looks were early finishes answered with Keep the dates (`keptEarly`, G-37) is refused by the table, though the kernel keeps it.
