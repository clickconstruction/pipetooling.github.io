# v2.7037 — Pay calendar: the forecast opens with the cutoffs coming (2026-11-12)

Punch list #323, PR 5 of seven, stacked on v2.7036. The card asked where the cutoff should show before a bill goes out, and answered: no separate calendar page, a strip in the forecast. This is the strip, and the line on a job still being worked.

## What changed

- **The kernel**, `src/lib/jobs/cutoffsComing.ts`: `buildCutoffsComing({ todayYmd, calendars, ready, working })` groups work not yet billed by payer, cutoff and due day, nearest cutoff first. Each line carries its title (*Sable Ridge Builders · in by Nov 25, due Dec 30*), its detail (*1 bill ready, $8,900. 2 jobs working, $21,500 still to bill.*), whether it is inside the cutoff's last week, and the due day a bill one day late would get. A job on its own calendar makes a line of its own; a job that switches the payer's off is left out; so is a payer with nothing ready or working, and a dollar figure of nothing.
- **The Payment forecast** (`BilledPaymentForecastModal`): a **Cutoffs coming** section above the weeks, drawn only when there is a line. Amber, with *in 5d*, inside the last week.
- **Where the dollars come from** (`JobsStagesTab.tsx`): a Ready to Bill row's figure is the header's own (`readyToBillRowsExposureTotal` of that one row); a Working job's is what is not yet on any bill (`jobBillingUnallocatedDollars`). Both read the unfiltered board, so a search or a filter does not change the strip.
- **A Working row**: `JobsStagesTable` takes `jobCutoffLine`, drawn under the job's progress. The Working section passes one that says *Bill by Nov 30 to be due Dec 10. After that, Jan 10.* inside `PAY_CUTOFF_WORKING_DAYS` (10), and nothing before that: the line would otherwise sit on every such row all month. `payCutoffLine` and `cutoffWordsFor` take the verb, *Send* or *Bill*.
- **Guides**: *Cutoffs coming* in *read the payment forecast*, and two more places in *set a payer's pay calendar*.

## Not in this PR, and moved to PR 6

- **The Needs you line.** A new item is a key in the list, a rank, an actor, a door and its own inputs, each pinned by a matrix test. It is its own piece of work, and it belongs with the other readers.
- **The pay application's line** under *Period to*.
- **The phone's two-line rows.**

## Is this the best we can do?

- **The strip grows with the number of calendar payers.** On the walk every commercial payer was given a made-up calendar and the strip ran to eleven lines. Real use is a handful. If it grows, lines past the next two cutoffs fold.
- **Still to bill is the job's unallocated dollars**, not what the work to date has earned. A job 20 % done shows its whole remaining price. The honest figure for a progress bill is the pay application's, which the app holds only once one is drafted.
- **A ready bill with no price says its count alone.** The first walk read *1 bill ready, $0.*; it now reads *1 bill ready.*
