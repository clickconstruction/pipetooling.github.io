## What shipped

- **`src/components/my-time-day-editor/useMyTimeDaySessions.ts`** (new) — a verbatim move of the region-1 engine out of `DashboardMyTimeDayEditorModal.tsx`:
  - the seven state atoms: `authUserId`, `authReady`, `fetchedSessions`, `sessionsLoading`, `sessionsFetchError`, `resolvedSubjectLabel` and `sessionsFetchNonce`
  - who it is: the sign-in lookup, `effectiveSubjectUserId`, `editingSelf`, the title's name loader and `modalTitlePerson`
  - the day's `clock_sessions` read and its skips, `fetchDaySessionsForEditor`, `resolvedSessions`, `pendingAuthForFetch` and `bumpSessionsFetchNonce`
  - the shaping: `sortedSessions`, `sessionsKey`, the 15 s clock (`nowTick`) and `sessionClusters`

  Inputs: `dateStr`, `sessionsProp`, `subjectUserIdProp`, `subjectDisplayName` and `inSaveableRange`. No line inside changed.
- **`DashboardMyTimeDayEditorModal.tsx`** — keeps the engine in the shell, as the map asks. It calls the hook where the state stood and destructures the 17 names the rest reads, so no downstream line changed. The salary prefetch, NCNS and label hooks that sat between the engine's pieces stay put and read its bundle. One thing moved in order: the 15 s clock's effect now runs before those three hooks' effects. It only starts an interval and shares no state with them. Eight callbacks now list `setSessionsFetchNonce` or `setFetchedSessions` in their dependencies, because lint cannot tell a custom hook's setters are stable. 1,856 → 1,695 lines; the three imports it no longer uses are gone.

## Tests (+10)

- `useMyTimeDaySessions.render.test.tsx` — the engine's effects had no tests until now. It runs on the real kernels, with a supabase stub that records each read and can hold one in flight:
  - the subject's day is read once, with the editor's select and the four filters. The rows come back normalized, sorted, keyed and clustered.
  - a parent's sessions are used as given, and nothing is read.
  - outside the saveable range the day is empty, and nothing is read.
  - with no subject it waits for the sign-in, then reads the signed-in user's own day (`editingSelf`).
  - a failed read shows its error and an empty day.
  - the refetch signal reads the day again, through the bump and through the setter. Taking the counter out of the read's dependencies fails it.
  - a read that a newer one replaced never lands. Dropping the `cancelled` guard fails it.
  - the title's name is the given name first, else theirs from `users` once signed in.
  - `fetchDaySessionsForEditor` reads on demand, and returns nothing with no one to read for.
  - the clock ticks every 15 s while a session is open, and stops when none is.
