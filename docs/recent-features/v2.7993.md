## What the office sees

Market now has its five parts, in the card's order, under four tiles.

- **Call this week.** Five builders, each with its reasons as tags, the count of its permits, the jobs it has applied for with no plumber yet, and our history with it. Three presses: **Pursue**, **Not for us**, **Open builder**.
- **Chips** over the list, each with its count: *This week*, *Has a job open now*, *Picking up*, *New and busy*, *Never bid*, *Jobs over $1M*. A chip a city's records cannot fill is left out.
- **Pursue** opens the sheet a kept permit's Pursue opens: what for, who follows up, what they are. It makes the lead the skim makes (`approachFromPermit`), from the builder's newest open job, and the permit joins Pursuing with its lead.
- **Not for us** asks why in one press, or takes no reason.
- **An answer takes the name off the list** for everyone, and the next takes its place. **Answered** under the list shows the answered names with whose word it was, and **Put back** undoes one.
- **Ask for** stands empty, *Nobody looked this name up yet*, with **Google the builder** beside it.
- **Who holds the work**, beside the clock, only where the city's permits name plumbers: the plumbers by jobs, and how many builders use one, lean to one or shop around. The ones that shop around are named, each a press that opens the builder.
- **How much is being built**: commercial permits by whole month, by kind, and how big.

## How each number is counted

The card's *How each number is counted* is the definition. As built (`permitCallList.ts`, `permitMarketShape.ts`):

- **On the list:** a builder with three or more commercial permits in twelve months. Never a name marked Avoid, a name said to file for others, or a name with an answer that still stands.
- **Picking up:** two or more permits more in the last 90 days than in the 90 before. **New and busy:** three or more in the last 90 days and none before. **Slowing:** three or more before and two or more fewer now. A name that is new and busy reads that and not picking up, so the two chips never count one name twice.
- **No trend where the records are short.** The three need two 90-day halves of records. Only San Antonio's reach that far. The other cities draw no trend tag and no trend chip, and the line under the list says why.
- **Open now:** a row the tab holds that is an application with no plumbing permit on file (`plumbingOnFile`) and no answer that closes it (`placeOf`).
- **Never bid, We bid N, won M:** the Builders count for the name, with the bids a tie adds.
- **Jobs over $1M and the typical job:** from the declared values, only where they can be trusted. Where one figure is on a sixth or more of a city's valued permits, the city fills a default in, and no value is read (`valuesReliable`).
- **A named plumber:** a row whose plumbing permit names someone other than the applicant. A builder is judged on three or more named jobs: one plumber on three in four uses one, on half leans, on fewer shops around.
- **A month:** only whole months inside the year and inside the city's records. This month is still filling and is left out.

## The order

The card proposes: a job open now, then picking up or new and busy, then never bid, then the most permits in 90 days. It leaves the order to the builder to refine before the showing.

One key was put ahead of those: **a name that reads as a builder comes first** (`nameReads`). San Antonio's applicant is often a permit service, an engineer, an architect or one person, and nobody has said what kind any name is yet. On the first read of the proposed order alone, most of the first five were not builders. So a name with a design or permit word and no building word, and a name with no company word in it, wait behind the names that read as builders. They carry the tag *May file for others* or *Could be a person*. Nothing is removed. The office's own word, the builder's kind, overrules the reading either way.

## The answers

`builder_call_answers` (v2.7982), append-only. The newest row per name stands, and **Put back** writes `back`. A pursued name may be offered again after 90 days and a passed name after 365. Those two numbers are the builder's choice and are the owner's to change. A name offered again carries a tag that says it was answered before.

## The reads

Still no function. The view now reads a city's commercial permits of twelve months off `permits` (the rows read back as history count, which the skim's read leaves out). The first page asks for the count and the pages after it are asked together: San Antonio's year is four pages, and one after another they took over two seconds. That read replaces step one's read of the applications, so the clock, the call list and the charts count the same rows. The answers are one more small read.

## Where it differs from the card, and why

- **The order has one more key**, above.
- **Two more reasons can show as tags**: *Slowing*, which the card defines, and the two readings of a name.
- **Pursue from a builder with no job on the tab** reads that builder's newest permit and makes the lead from it. No verdict is written on it: a history row was never on the skim.
- **The plumbers and the open jobs come off the tab's rows**, which hold no history. In the cities that name plumbers there is no history yet, so nothing is lost today.
