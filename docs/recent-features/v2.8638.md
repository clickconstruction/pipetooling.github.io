# v2.8638 — A screen reader now says the app's notes: Saved, a refusal, a failure (2026-11-26)

Punch list #445, the second half of finding 239 of the walking thread's sitting 23 (2026-11-26), briefed by the fixing thread as `445-toasts-carry-no-status-role.md`. The fault is older than every open card. It is cited against #445, the newest card whose walk met it; sitting 22 met the same on card #389. Client only. No migration, no edge function. Nothing owed after the merge.

**Read.** Every refusal and every *Saved* the app says by toast was drawn and never announced. In `src/components/Toast.tsx` the usual toast's card was a plain `div` with its words in a `span`, with no role and no `aria-live`. The one role in the file was `alertdialog`, on the toast that stands in the middle of the screen. `ToastContext.tsx`, which draws every toast, had no live region either. A person on a screen reader heard nothing when a locked price was refused, when a save failed or when a send went, and the walkers could find a toast only by its words.

**The mend.** The toast's words carry a role: `alert` for an *error* or a *warning*, `status` for a *success* or an *info*. The rule is one pure function, `toastLiveRole` in `src/lib/toastPress.ts`, and `Toast.tsx` puts its answer on the `span` that holds the words, in the corner toast, the phone's foot toast and the anchored toast alike.

- **On the words alone, not on the card.** The card also holds the action press (**Undo**, **Tell the dev**) and the ×. A role on the card would have the reader say those with the words.
- **The centre toast is left as it was.** It is an `alertdialog` that already names its words through `aria-describedby`. A second role inside it would have the words said twice.

**The shape, and why not the other one.** The brief named two shapes and left the pick to the builder: a role on each toast, or one region that stays mounted in `ToastContext` and takes each toast's words. The second is the surer one for a `status`, since some readers say a live region's words only when the region was on the page before them. It was not built, for one reason: a region with a role that is always on the page is found by every render test that asks for *the* alert or *the* status on its page. 266 test files ask that, and 144 of them draw under the real `ToastProvider` through `renderWithProviders`, so each would find two. A role on each toast changes only a test that shows a toast and asks for the one alert or status at the same moment. An `alert` is the one role the common screen readers announce when it arrives on the page with its words already in it, so a refusal and a failure, the notes that matter most, are heard either way. If a walk on a real phone with VoiceOver finds a *Saved* is not said, the mounted region is the next step and this kernel still decides which role.

No guide changes: nothing a person sees or presses is different.

## What did not change

- How a toast looks, where it sits, how long it stays, its **Undo** and its ×.
- The centre toast.
- Which lines offer **Tell the dev**.

## Tests

- `toastPress.test.ts`: `toastLiveRole` answers `alert` for an error and a warning, `status` for a success and an info, and nothing for the centre toast.
- `Toast.render.test.tsx`, four cases: an error toast and a warning toast are found by `role="alert"` and an info and a success by `role="status"`, each holding the toast's words and nothing else; the action press and the × are outside the role; a foot toast and an anchored toast carry it; a centre toast is one `alertdialog` with no alert or status inside.
- `FirstLookRead.render.test.tsx`, one line changed. After **Send my answer** the page draws its own `role="status"` line and a toast with the same words, so the test's ask for *the* status found two. It now asks for the page's own line by its tag. It was the one test of the 144 that ask for a status or an alert under the real `ToastProvider` to change.

## Seen on the way, not changed

On the tech's page for one look, **Send my answer** says *The office has your answer.* twice: in the page's own line under the press and in a toast. Both are statuses now, so a reader may say it twice. Which one stays is a call on that page and no part of this change.
