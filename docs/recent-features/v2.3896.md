# v2.3896 — Controller access, batch 1: Materials and supply houses (2026-09-27)

One migration, no client change. Punch list **#48**, batch 1 of 5 (`to-dos/controller-access.md`).

## What was wrong

The phone look of 2026-09-27 found the sample controller's Supply houses page empty: *0 supply houses*, $0.00 owed, where the assistant sees the full directory. The controller role (v2.662) was made assistant-like by widening `is_assistant()`; policies that spell their roles out by hand never got the role. On `supply_houses` the write policies were corrected in v2.3167 and the select policy was not, so a controller could edit a house and read none.

The audit, read-only against production: 253 policies on 80 tables and 59 functions name the assistant and not the controller. Read as the sample logins, 43 tables return rows to the assistant and none to the controller. The only controller in production is the sample account.

## What shipped

- **`supabase/migrations/20260927200000_controller_reads_materials.sql`** — `ALTER POLICY` on 56 policies across the 17 Materials and supply-house tables (`supply_houses`, `supply_house_invoices` and their allocations, `material_parts`, prices and price history, templates, `purchase_orders` and items, `part_types`, `assembly_types`, `fixture_types`, `fixture_labor_defaults`, `material_po_generator_entries`). Each policy keeps its name, command and expression and gains the controller beside the assistant.
- **`to-dos/controller-access.md`** (#48) — the audit: how it was run, every table by batch with its read counts, the 59 functions in two batches, and three calls for the owner before the last.
- **`docs/ACCESS_CONTROL.md`** — the controller's line says the hand-written lists are being brought into step.
