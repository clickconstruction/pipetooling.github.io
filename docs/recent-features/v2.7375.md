## Before

On 2026-11-15 the crew's job window, opened as the sample superintendent on a job he is on the crew of, read *Could not read the job. Try again* under Plans. The card took it for a fault of the Plans section. It is wider: `jobs_ledger` opened to a field role only through a clock session of their own on the job, or, for a subcontractor, through the team or the schedule. A helper, a superintendent or an estimator put on a crew read no row of the job until their first clock-in there. So on the first visit, the visit the window is for:

- Plans failed, since the section's first read is the job's row;
- the window fell back to the Dashboard's row, which has no customer, no number to call, no status and no development;
- anything that asks `on_site_can_touch_job` (the job's own plan part, materials on site) answered no.

Counted on prod that day: on working jobs, 20 of the helpers' 58 crew rows and 13 of the superintendents' 23 had no clock session behind them.

## What changed

One permissive SELECT policy on `jobs_ledger`, `"Crew can read jobs ledger for team or schedule"`, `USING (helper_on_job(id))`: the team table, or a schedule block on the job this week or last. It is the predicate the thread notes (v2.5526), the plan parts (v2.6604) and the checklists already ask, a definer that reads the team table and the blocks and never the ledger. No write policy moves.

## What a person can now read that they could not

The job's whole row, on jobs they are on the crew of. That is what a clock-in already opened to them, and what a subcontractor's rule already opens; the crew's screens select the columns they draw. The row holds the job's revenue. Said here so the choice is on the page: the narrower way was a definer read of a few columns for the window alone, and then Plans, the checklists and materials on site would each have needed their own.

## Is this the best we can do?

v2.6583 and v2.6604 both wrote that the ledger shows a crew member their job; it did only after a clock-in. This makes the sentence true with the predicate those two already use, so "on the crew" means one thing in the database.
