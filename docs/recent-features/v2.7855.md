# v2.7855 — Pricing: a row can be a percent of other rows, and it follows them (2026-11-18)

The Percent row on Bids → Pricing. An estimator asked for *a way for 40% of the cost of pipe to be calc. for the connections auto*; the owner asked for an easy way to add that line, saw the mock-up, asked for the new line to be drawn live in the window, and said *build it all* (2026-11-18). This is the first screen piece. The calculator's two presses and dragging a line onto it follow.

## Why

Her bids carry one row, Connections, priced at 40% of what the pipe rows sell for, in place of counted fittings. The number was worked out on the calculator and typed. It went stale when a pipe row changed, nothing on the screen said so, and nothing recorded the rule. A read of the bids that carry the row found most at exactly 40% and two that were not.

## What ships

- **＋ Percent row**, on the line above the grid beside the price book. It opens one window.
- **The window draws the row live.** Its top is the row as the grid will show it: the name, the kind, the rule's chip, the price per unit, the revenue and *no cost*, with the bid's revenue before and after. Under it: the name, the percent, **Revenue** or **Cost** each with its number for the rows picked, the rows (each kind on the bid, then each tag), and one tick box, *Keep following these rows until the bid is sent*. The press names the act and its number, *Add Connections at $2,480.00*, and is dead with its reason beside it until a name and a percent are in.
- **It opens filled with the last rule made on this device.** The first time the percent is empty: the app suggests none of its own.
- **A name already on the bid sets that row.** Her older bids carry a typed Connections row, so typing that name gives the row its rule, and the window says so.
- **The row follows.** Its price is an ordinary saved price. The Workbench re-writes it whenever it is not what the rule comes to, while the bid is unsent and the open price is loaded. Each price option follows its own prices when it is open.
- **The row's chip**, in the Book entry column: *40% of Pipe revenue · $6,200.00*. A press opens the window on the row. On a sent bid it reads *kept as sent* and the price holds.
- **A typed price drops the rule**, with a line saying the row no longer follows. The solver holds a rule row as it holds a pinned one.
- **History** captions these writes: *Added a percent row*, *A percent row followed its rows*.

## How

- `src/lib/bids/percentRow.ts`: the rule, what it comes to, the rows it may read, the drift the Workbench writes, the window's live reading, and what the window opens on. A rule reads rows by kind or by tag, never itself and never a row with a rule, so rules cannot chain.
- `src/lib/bids/percentRowIo.ts`: the count row a new Percent row is (count 1, last on its version) and the rule's save, fail-soft where the column is not pushed.
- `src/hooks/usePercentRows.ts`: reads each rule off the engine's count rows, writes through the Workbench's one price write tagged `percent-row`, once for each answer, and holds the window.
- `src/components/bids/PercentRowWindow.tsx`, and the wiring in `BidsPricingTab.tsx` and `PricingGridPieces.tsx`.

The rule's column and its copies are v2.7848 (`20261118220000_bid_count_row_percent_rule`).

## Tests

`percentRow.test.ts` (46), `PercentRowWindow.render.test.tsx` (11), `usePercentRows.render.test.tsx` (13), and five cases added to `BidsPricingTab.render.test.tsx`: the chip and the followed price, a sent bid writes nothing, a typed price drops the rule first, the window's press adds the count row, a sent bid refuses the press.

## Not in this change

- The calculator's **Put on a row…** and **Make a new row…**, and dragging a row or a breakdown line onto it.
- A cost for the row. With no takeoff behind it, its price counts as all profit, as her typed row does today.
- The Cover Letter does not re-write a rule row. It reads the saved price, which Pricing keeps right while it is open.
- A rule row still gets a Labor row with no hours, which counts toward the share of rows the estimating score needs.
