# v2.7592 — Tasks: an assistant can put a photo of the spot on a task (2026-11-17)

The third of the dev seat's three task PRs, on `dev-mcp` (server 0.98.0, 119 write verbs): **`plan_task_photo`** → **`apply_task_photo`**. With it the owner's ask is whole: assign a task, set its location, attach its photo.

## Which photo

The app has two kinds of photo on a task, and keeps them apart on purpose.

- **The giver's photo of the spot**: one picture on the task as written, in the `task-spot-photos` bucket, shown beside the place. This is what the pair writes.
- **A photo on a day's card** (`task_photos`): the doer's proof of work. It counts toward the finishing photo a task can ask for. A giver's picture among them would let a task close on a photo taken before the work began. No verb writes one.

## How the file travels

The bytes never ride in a tool call.

- The plan commits to the file's size and sha256, and hands back a signed address in the dev's own folder of the private `dev-seat-staging` bucket, good for two hours. The address is made after the hash and is no part of it.
- The caller PUTs the file there.
- The apply reads the staged copy back and checks its size, its JPEG header and its sha256 before anything is written.

The picture is a JPEG of 2 MB at most. The app shrinks every spot photo to a JPEG before saving it, and 2 MB is the bucket's cap. A refusal says how to make one.

## The writes

The two writes a giver's press makes, as the dev under RLS:

- The picture into `task-spot-photos` as `item/<task>/<a new id>.jpg`. A file is never written over.
- The task's `spot_photo_path`, in an update that asks for the row it changed.

A path that does not save takes the new picture back out and leaves the staged copy, so the same plan can be applied again. `replace: true` puts a picture over the one a task has and takes the old file out of the bucket. `remove: true` takes the photo off. A task from a roadmap keeps its photo on the roadmap task, as its place is kept. No RPC and no migration. It tells nobody.

## What an independent review found

- **A real second act was turned away.** A photo put on, taken off and put on again hashed like the first time, and the call log refused it. The verb is refused by the task's own state now.
- **A removal was reported that was never checked.** The reply says what the bucket answered: taken out, or still there.
- **A dropped connection after the picture was stored** left the picture in the bucket with nothing pointing at it. It is taken back out.
- **A roadmap task whose own row points at a photo**: the card reads that row first, so that is the row the verb writes.
