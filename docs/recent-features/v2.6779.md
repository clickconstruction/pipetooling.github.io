## What changed

- **The roster's loaded rate prices the labor kept** (the fourth call: *the roster's loaded rate ($29.01) and the line budgets' $45 disagree — which one prices the labor kept?*). `superintendent_scores` computes the roster's rate inside the database with the arithmetic of `crewRateFromLedgerDays` — recorded field sessions over the 90 days ending the period, each at the person's field wage, Σ$ ÷ Σh × the burden factor — and prices labor earned as the award's hours × the signed-off percent × that rate; labor kept is that minus the team at the burden factor and the subs on their sheet hours, as before. The award's labor dollars (the bid's price of the labor, at the line budgets' rate) still leave as `awarded_usd` for the Costs tab and never score. With no field hours in the window each row falls back to its award's own rate and the read says so (`rate_source: 'award'`). The rate leaves only to payroll access, as a wage.
- **The $2,500 floor** (the second call: *a $350 service job sat on Projects with no baseline*): one constant, `BASELINE_PRICE_FLOOR_USD` in `src/lib/jobs/baselinePriceFloor.ts`, read by the deck's kernel (`projectsWithNoBaseline`, so the Needs you count and the deck's cards agree) and by the Scoreboard's fold (`superintendentScores`, so the board, the superintendent's own card and his *Scored this month* line agree). A project priced under it never appears: not listed, not held, not counted. A project with no price yet is not under the floor. The read now carries each row's `price_usd` so the client's one constant is the rule.
- **The rule card** (`SCORE_RULE_LINES`, `rateRuleWords`, `PeopleScoreboardTab.tsx`): the Baseline line says the line budgets' sum is never scored (v2.6776's call), the Kept line names the roster's loaded rate, a seventh line names the floor, and under them one line names the rate the month was priced at — *Labor kept is priced at the roster's loaded rate, $29.01 an hour: the last 90 days of field wages at the 1.2 burden factor. The line budgets' rate stays the bid's price.* — or says the roster had no field hours and each job read its award's own rate.
- **Words**: *read the superintendents' scoreboard and set the bonus share* (the Baseline and Kept lines, the floor, the rate line), *give a project its hours baseline at award* (the floor on the deck).
- Tests: `baselinePriceFloor.test.ts`, `fillInBaselines.test.ts` (a $350 job never on the deck, an unpriced one still there), `superintendentScores.test.ts` (the parse of the new keys, the floor in the fold, the rate words), `PeopleScoreboardTab.render.test.tsx` (the rate line).

## Read closer than the card

- **Why the rate is computed in the database, not passed in.** A superintendent's own card reads the same function for his bonus, and his client may not read wages, so it cannot compute the roster's rate; the function reads the roster the way the Labor tab does and lets the figure out only to payroll access. The same arithmetic, over the same tables (`clock_sessions`, `people_pay_config`, `people.account_user_id`, the office job in `app_settings`), ends at the period's end rather than today, so a past month is priced at that month's roster.
- **Why the floor stays in the client.** The owner's floor is one number read in two places; the function hands back the price and the kernel applies it, so changing the floor is one edit with no migration. The function drops nothing.
- **What the floor does not touch.** The award block on New Job and the Costs strip still write a baseline for a small project when asked; the floor keeps it off the deck and the board, as the owner said, and nothing more.
