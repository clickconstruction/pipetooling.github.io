# v2.7963 — Trips: join a trip with the next (2026-11-19)

## Why

The last press on the trip record's mock-up (card #379), and the way back from **Split in two**, which had none. Two trips can be one going out: a crew presses **End the trip** by mistake and starts again, or one trip is typed twice. Until now the fix was by hand: delete the second trip, then paint its days onto the first with **Change**. Prod held no kept trip when this was built. The owner chose to close the gap before the first real split, on eight rules he said yes to.

## What changed

- **Join with the next** on an open trip, beside **Split in two**, for a reader who can change trips. The last trip on a site does not show it.
- **The window** (`TripJoinWindow`): nothing to pick. It draws both trips as they stand and the one trip they become. It names each day both trips give one person, what the joined trip takes from the next one, where a line ends up and where the next trip goes. The press is **Join into one trip**. Two different billings, or a line on each trip, stop the press and say why.
- **One write**, `trip_join`, which v2.7960 made (`docs/migrations/20261119180000_trip_join.md`): the joined trip through `trip_save`, the line's tie, then the delete of the next trip, all in one call. It takes both ids and refuses a second that is not the next trip on the same home. This PR adds no migration.
- **Deleted trips**: a trip that was joined away reads *Joined into Trip 7* with no **Put back**.
- **The kernel**, `src/lib/trips/tripJoin.ts`: which trip is next, the joined trip as the database will make it, what stops a join, and every word.
- **The split's words**: the window and the guide now point at the join where they said a split has no undo.

## The eight rules

1. The next trip is the one that went out next on the same home, in the look's own order: first day, then last day, then id. Same home is the same development, or the same job for a trip with no development.
2. The open trip keeps its id, number, words and via. Where what it was for, who asked or how it went is empty, it takes the next trip's. The next trip's number is not given again.
3. People merge by name, as `trip_save` tells them apart. Each keeps every day from both. On a day both give one person, the open trip's kind stays, and the window names the day.
4. The jobs are both trips' jobs.
5. An empty billing takes the other's. Two different ones refuse until **Change** makes them match.
6. A line only the next trip holds is tied to the joined trip. A line on each refuses.
7. `ended_at` is the next trip's, so a trip still growing on the phone keeps growing.
8. The next trip is deleted and kept 90 days, marked with the trip it was joined into, and offers no **Put back**. A join is taken back with **Split in two**.

## Decisions

- **Rule 8, the mark.** Put back as things stood would return the trip's days beside the joined trip, every one twice. The capture keeps `joined_into` beside the row, as it keeps `held_line_id`, and the fold reads it. The other two choices were to hide the trip, which leaves no trace of a join, or to leave **Put back**, which doubles the days.
- **The line's step is a definer.** The lines table's own policies are narrower than the trips office. An invoker that could not read a line would lose its tie without a word when the next trip went. `trip_join_line` reads and moves the tie for the trips office, and only for a trip and its next trip.
- **The pair is fixed at the press.** The window holds the two trips it was opened on, and the database refuses a pair that is no longer next to each other, in words.
- **The save does not refuse a joined trip made again.** Only the fold hides the press. A client loaded before this ships could still offer **Put back** for a joined trip until it reloads; the days would then stand twice, and a delete fixes it.
