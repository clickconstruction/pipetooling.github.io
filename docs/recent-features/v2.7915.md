# v2.7915 — Painted maps: the press opens on your last job, and the picker never runs short (2026-11-19)

Punch list #392, on the owner's word of 2026-11-19. He opened **Which job is it for?** from the Dashboard as himself, with nothing on his schedule and no clock in 45 days, and met a grey sentence over a gap. He asked for his last jobs there and said he did not love the window. Three ways were drawn (`to-dos/paint-a-map-press/picker-mockup.html`). His pick: *I like C and then if they click to change the job it should show B.*

## What the rows said first

Read through the dev seat for seventeen real people, reads only. A superintendent and a lead never met the empty window. Most of the office did, and so did the field techs who are scheduled and do not clock. One helper has 26 scheduled jobs and no clock, ever. Older clocks, past days on the schedule and the team fill the list for nearly everyone, and each already has its index. The counts are on the card.

## What changed

- **Your last jobs** replaces Lately (`src/lib/paintedMap/paintedMapJobPicker.ts`). It holds up to six jobs the person was on, each known one of three ways: they clocked onto it at any age, it was on their schedule on a past day, or they are on its team. `pickerLastOrder` keeps each job once at its strongest sign. The jobs with a day lead, newest first, and the team fills what is left. A row that is not a clock says so: *scheduled* over its day, or *on the team*. A job on a schedule does not prove the person went. The 45-day wall is gone, as Tally's own Recent list never had one (#216). A day in another year says its year.
- **The read** (`paintedMapJobPickerIo.ts`, `loadPickerLists`). Three reads at once beside the day's: the newest 300 clocks, the newest 200 past schedule blocks and the 60 teams joined last. Each is by an index on the person. The first 24 jobs in the list's own order are then read whole, in the two batched selects that were already there. Seven reads to open, where there were five. Nothing is read while the window sits open.
- **The press opens on the last job** (C). `PaintAMapStartSheet` opened with no job reads those lists once. When the newest job with a day exists and no job is on today's schedule, the sheet opens on it (`pickerGuess`). A line under *For* says how the app knows: *The last job you clocked onto, Friday.* or *The last job on your schedule, Nov 10.* The team alone is no guess, since it says nothing of when. A read that fails falls to the picker, which reads again and keeps **Paint now and pick the job later**. This covers every door that starts a map with no job: the Dashboard's press, the header's search and the Documents tab.
- **Change the job shows the refitted window** (B). `PaintAMapJobPicker` puts the search on top with **Near me** beside it. While a search is typed, what it found takes the lists' place under the box, over a phone's keyboard. The picker opens on the lists the sheet has just read, so **Change the job** reads nothing.
- **A desk gets a window** (`paintedSheetFrame.ts`). On a phone the start sheet and the picker are still sheets on the bottom edge. At a desk, past the app's own line at 768, each hangs from near the top with four round corners, so it stays put while typing shortens the list. The cursor starts in the search at a desk and not on a phone, where it would raise the keyboard over the lists.
- **Words.** The promise under the search is *Near me asks where you are once, for this list.* The reason a device gave no place names the browser at a desk, not a phone. A person nobody has put on a job reads *You have not been on a job yet. Search for the job, or press Near me.* The wide press's second line in Job Mode is *On your last job, or one you pick* when the day names no job.
