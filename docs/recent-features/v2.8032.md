# v2.8032 — Task Mode: Where my tasks are draws on Google Maps, with one press to the other map (2026-11-20)

The owner walked the app on an iPhone on 2026-11-20 and asked for this in Task Mode: *the map that is showing should be a google maps map with the option to switch between it and the open streets map map with an icon in the bottom right and the "show where I am" button should just be icon and in the bottom left*. It is also step 5 of punch list [#387](../../to-dos/painted-maps-on-google.md), the person's task map. Client only: no migration and no function.

## What a person sees

- **Checklist → Today, Where my tasks are** draws on Google Maps. **Satellite** is Google's photograph with street names and **Streets** is its road map, as on the Review tab's map.
- **A press at the bottom right** switches to the OpenStreetMap map and back. It is an icon, named *Switch to the OpenStreetMap map* or *Switch to Google Maps*. The device keeps the pick, for this map alone.
- **Show where I am** is an icon at the bottom left. Its name and its title are the words it used to wear: *Show where I am*, *Finding you…* and *Find me again*. It turns while the phone is asked and is blue once the person is on the map.
- **The zoom buttons** are at the top left on both maps, so nothing moves when the map under them changes.

## Why the card was on Leaflet, and what was checked before it left

v2.7809 moved this card into `PinsMapFrame` on its Leaflet half and said why it stayed there: dropping `leafletOnly` was #387's one line, *after its billing look*. Nothing on the card needed Leaflet. Each piece was read on the Google half before the hold was dropped.

| Piece | On Google |
|---|---|
| The numbered dots, one for tasks that share a place | The pins canvas's own labelled markers, as the Review map draws its counts |
| The person's dot | A pin like the others, dark with a white ring |
| The distance on each task's line | Worked out from the reading and the task's place. No map is asked |
| **Satellite** and **Streets** | The frame hands Google a map type |
| **Time** | The frame takes Google's map down and draws the archive on the Leaflet canvas, then gives Google's map back. It still looks at a task's place and never at the person's own dot (`timeSpot`) |
| The Precise Location line (#402) | Drawn under the map from the reading. No map is asked |

So the switch has nothing to warn about: neither map lacks a press the other has.

## How it is built

- `src/lib/map/mapProviderPick.ts`, new: the pick a device keeps, the rule that fits it inside what the page can draw (`resolvePickedMapProvider`), the switch as it stands (`mapProviderSwitch`), and where a corner press sits.
- `PinsMapFrame` takes `providerSwitchKey`, the name the device keeps the pick under. A card that hands one wears the switch. A card that hands none is as it was, and no other card hands one.
- The page's own rule still stands first. With no browser key, or once Google fails on the page (`useGoogleCanvasDoors`), the map is the Leaflet canvas and there is no switch, since there is one map to draw. A kept pick of Google never draws a map the page cannot.
- The switch waits while **Time** is on. The archive draws on one canvas whichever map was up before it.
- `PinsMapGoogleCanvas` takes `zoomAtTopLeft`. The frame sets it for a map with the switch, so the bottom right is free and the two maps read alike. Every other Google map keeps its zoom buttons where Google puts them.
- A corner press sits 40 px above the map's bottom edge on both maps. That clears Google's logo and terms, and the Leaflet map's credit line where a phone wraps it to two lines.
- `taskPlaceWhereIAmPress` in `taskPlace.ts` names the icon's three states.

## Is this the best we can do

- **One place for the presses on both maps.** Google puts its zoom buttons at the bottom right, where the owner asked for the switch. Moving the switch above them would have put it in a different place on each map, and a person pressing it twice to compare would chase it. The zoom buttons moved instead.
- **The switch is hidden, not dead, when there is one map.** A dead press would need words for a thing the person cannot change.
- **Not built: keeping the view through a switch.** The map frames the dots again when the map under it changes. The zoom the person had is not carried over.
- **Not built: one Google map kept for the visit** (`reuseMaps`, as the painted map's canvas asks). Each switch back to Google, and each return from Time, makes a new Google map. The pins canvas is shared by five cards, and that change belongs to all of them.
