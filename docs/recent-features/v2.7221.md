# v2.7221 — The Bridge: set each job’s percent with its lines, its last day worked and its price in front of you (2026-11-13)

Client only. No migration, no edge function, and no kernel that is copied to the edge functions changed: the Monday email does not draw this list's rows, and `callDive.ts` and the new files are not in the generated copy.

The owner, of the list *Set a percent on the jobs without one* on the Bridge's card **What to do now**: "to be able to quickly mark these jobs' % on the Build view, I need to be able to see the line items, last worked date and by who (between the clock sessions or the schedule), and the amount we're charging."

His screenshot was older than v2.7113, which had already put the hours and the people on each line. What was still missing: the price, the lines, the last day worked, and the schedule as a second reading of it.

## What changed

- **A kernel for what a percent is judged from** (`src/lib/bridge/pctFacts.ts`, new, pure). For each listed job: what we are charging (the job's total, or that it has no price, never $0), its named lines in order with their amounts and whether each is on a bill that went out, what has been billed, the last day worked, the next scheduled day, the hours line, and the newest percent anyone wrote down.
- **Last worked is the newest of two readings, and says which.** A day someone clocked time on the job, or a day gone by that the schedule had someone on it. A day both name is a clocked day. Today and later on the schedule is never *last worked*: it is the next scheduled day.
- **The read** (`pctFactsIo.ts`, new; `useBridgePctFacts.ts`, new): `jobs_ledger_fixtures`, `job_schedule_blocks`, `jobs_ledger_invoices` and `job_pct_events` for the listed jobs only, side by side, each paged past 1,000 rows, then one read for the names on the schedule. It runs only while the move is on the card or the percent window is open, and again after a refresh. The hours are the ones v2.7113 already reads.
- **The line** (`callDive.ts`): the Contract cell says the total, or **No price** in amber where it was blank. The last column reads **Last worked**: the day, *clocked* or *scheduled*, and who. Until the read lands it is **Worked by**, as before. `BridgeMoveTable.tsx` did not change.
- **The window** (`BridgePctRun.tsx`, new): the app's own percent window (`FieldPctUpdateModal`, which gained one optional slot above its control), so a save files the same status report and mirrors the same percent as everywhere else, with the same prompt at 100%. Above the control: the facts, then the job's lines, four and then the rest on a press. A job with no price says it is also on the list *Price the jobs with no price*. A job with no lines offers the job.
- **Going down the list.** A save moves the window to the next job not yet set, then back to any skipped, then closes. **Skip ›** and **‹ Back** move without saving. **Not now**, Escape and × close. The list is the one that was on the card when the window opened, so *Job 2 of 5* holds still.
- **A set job leaves the list** (`Bridge.tsx`): a job set above 0% on this visit is off the move's list and off its count, so the heading drops from five to four, and the move goes when the last one is set. A job set to 0% still has no percent by the page's rule, so it stays and says what was set. The Build reads are patched in place as before.

## How many presses

Two a job, a percent and then Save, with one more to open the window on the first. Before: open the job to see what was sold and when it was worked, come back, then three presses.

## Why the window and not the row

Setting the percent in the row would also be two presses a job. It would need the row to hold a control, a Save and a list of lines, on a phone as well, and a second copy of the save path. The window is the app's own save, already draws a multi-line job line by line, and has room for the lines with no press to open them.

## Not in this PR

- The Build window's own lists of jobs. They open the percent window bare, as before.
- The **Jobs with no %** strip behind the soft numbers door. Its rows open the percent window bare.
- A job's notes and its costs so far, which would also help a percent on a job with no lines.
