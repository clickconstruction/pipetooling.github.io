# v2.8310 — Legal portal: a number names a job only where it stands as one (punch list #85, a review residual) (2026-11-22)

Punch list #85 (`to-dos/legal-portal-firm-ready/README.md`), one of the residuals the October reviews left: *the contact-scope rule (item 24) reads job numbers out of prose, so a year or a phone fragment that equals a job number matches; `customer_contacts` has no job column.* The owner's direction, 2026-11-22: tighten the matcher, and leave a job column on `customer_contacts` for later. No migration. One function to redeploy: `legal-portal`, the only one that imports the shared file.

## What was found

- The rule ([`_shared/legalContactScope.ts`](../../supabase/functions/_shared/legalContactScope.ts)) already refused the digits inside `$1042`, `1042.50`, `512-555-1042`, `11/22/2026` and a longer number. What it read as a job was any run of the digits standing between spaces: `in 2026`, `ext 1042`, `(512) 555 0142`, `1042 Main St`, `120 days past due`, `100% complete`.
- **Most jobs carry a three-digit number.** On prod on 2026-11-22, 776 of 934 jobs have a three-digit number, 120 a four-digit one, and none reads as a year. So the card's year is the rarer fault. An area code, a street number, a percent or a count of days is the likelier one.
- The old header said a false match *sends an entry to the firm, never holds one back*. That was half the story. A false match on **another** job's number holds an account entry back. A false match on a **matter** job's number sends an entry that names only other work.

## The rule now

A number names a job only where it stands as a job's number. `nonJobSpans(text)` blanks what is plainly something else, then `textNamesJob` matches as before on what is left.

| Not a job | Examples |
| --- | --- |
| A phone number | `(512) 555-0142`, `512 555 0142`, `555-0142`, `ext 1042`, `ending in 1042`, `last four 1042` |
| A date or a year | `11/22/2026`; a number from 1950 to 2049 needs *job*, *J*, *#*, *invoice* or *bill* before it, so `in 2026` and `Nov 2026` name no job |
| Money | `$1042`, `$ 1,042`, `1042.50`, `500 dollars`, `500 USD` |
| A street address | `1042 Main St`, `512 N Lamar Blvd`: the number, a direction, one or two name words, a street word |
| A count of time or measure | `120 days`, `100%`, `300 ft`, `121 hours`, `745 pm` |
| A number labelled as another thing | `check #1042`, `ck 1042`, `PO 512`, `Suite 300`, `Unit 512`, `Lot 214`, `permit 2031`, `FM 1431` |
| A longer run of digits | `10425`, `1042-2026`, `512-1042-5555` |

What still names the job: `1042`, `#1042`, `J1042`, `job 1042`, `job#1042`, `Invoice 1042-1`, *the 1042 balance*, *the 1042 fixtures*, `C-219`, and 2077 (the window for a year is fixed, so the rule reads the same every year).

Choices inside it:

- **A span is blanked whatever stands before it.** *On the job 300 days now* names no job. Only a `#` holds a count or an address off: `#512 days late` is job 512.
- ***Check*, *account* and *building* label a number only with `#` or *no.* between.** *Please check 1042 and call back* is the office's own verb, so it still names the job. `check #1042` is a cheque.
- ***Case*, *claim* and *order* are not labels.** A collections note says *in case 512 pays* and *change order*.
- **Street words leave out everyday words**: no *court*, *way*, *place* or *run*, so *1042 goes to small claims court* names the job.
- **A thing the job has is not a count**: *the 1042 fixtures*, *the 512 units*.

## Read on prod before the merge (2026-11-22, read only, counts only)

- **What the firm sees does not change.** The seven matters have five payers, and one contact note is read for them. It names no job before and after, and it goes before and after. No note moves to held back, and none moves to sent.
- **The whole contact log**, 38 notes for 33 customers, read against each customer's own job numbers: none names a job under either rule.
- **A larger body of the office's own prose**, the 1,693 job thread notes, read against every job number in the company as a test of judgment and not of the portal: the old rule found 228 matches and the new one 51. All 177 it drops were read by eye: 168 are a percent, and the other nine are a pressure, a count of hours, two lengths in feet, two clock times and three pieces of two phone numbers. None was a job. It gains none.
- **Still read as a job** among the 51, because nothing around the digits says otherwise: a clock time with no *am* or *pm* (`Arrive 645`), a count of things with no unit (`284 rows`) and a price with no sign (`920 a fixture`). Those are crew and bookkeeping notes, which the contact log does not hold today.

## Is this the best we can do?

- **A job column on `customer_contacts`** is the structural fix, and it stays on the card. The writers that know the job (call mode, a bid follow-up) could record it, and the rule would read prose only for entries with no job.
- **Why not require a marker, `#` or *job*, for every number?** The office writes *the 1042 balance* and *Walkthrough on 1057*. A marker-only rule would send the firm every such note about another job.
- **A wrong blank costs little.** A matter job's number blanked by mistake leaves the entry as account history, which goes anyway. Another job's number blanked by mistake sends one entry about that job. The office still sees every entry on the desk, and Preview shows what goes.
