# v2.7671 — Supply houses: the bill form's own rules now have their tests (2026-11-17)

The fifth cut of `src/components/SupplyHousesTab.tsx`, step 5 of the *SupplyHousesTab extraction order* in `docs/MATERIALS_TABS_ARCHITECTURE.md`, and the last of its Stage A. A pure move: no behaviour changes.

**What moved.**

- `InvoiceFormValues`, `blankInvoiceForm` and `invoiceFormFromRow` (`src/lib/materials/supplyHouseInvoiceForm.ts`): the form's fourteen fields as one object, how a new bill starts (dated today, due on the house's next pay day when it has one) and how a stored row opens (the amount unsigned, the kind from the row, the paid day only on a paid row). `openAddInvoice` and `openEditInvoice` each set fourteen states by hand; both hand an object to one setter now.
- `typedAmountCents` (the same file): the amount box as cents, for the check of a bill's lines against it.
- `supplyInvoiceDraftFromForm` (`src/lib/materials/supplyInvoiceWrite.ts`): the draft Save hands the tested `saveSupplyInvoice`. A file dropped on the form names its own row, and a file from the house's thread lets the database pick one.
- `planIntoForm` and `noPdfPatch` (`src/lib/materials/bills/billFill.ts`): what a fill, and *No PDF, I will type it*, may write. A field the person typed is never written. The paper's date is written only when the paper has one. The link is left alone once the person asked for a Drive link. A credit's jobs come from the invoice it credits.

**The tab keeps the state.** The fourteen fields are still fourteen `useState` in the tab, written through two small setters (`setInvoiceFormValues`, `applyBillFormPatch`). Step 7 moves them into the form's own hook.

**Size.** `SupplyHousesTab.tsx` went from 3,560 lines to 3,532.

**Proof.** 18 new unit tests: 5 in `supplyHouseInvoiceForm.test.ts` (64 in all), 4 in `supplyInvoiceWrite.test.ts` (20 in all) and 9 in `billFill.test.ts` (18 in all), the last over the same read fixtures the fill's own tests use. They cover a house with no pay day, a row read before a column existed, an amount typed with a dollar sign and a comma, the three shapes of the draft's read, a field the person typed, the Drive link, a paper with no date, a credit whose invoice is on two jobs and one whose invoice is on none.
