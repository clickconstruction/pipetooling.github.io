# v2.7200 — Lien desk on a phone: one header row, and More (2026-11-13)

Punch list #352, PR 1 of eight. The owner picked direction A, the one list. This PR is the header, which A and B share.

## What was wrong

At 390 wide the desk's header took six rows on Do now (181 px) and seven on All filings (207 px on Affidavits, 235 px on Notices), pinned above the work. Of its presses, the view tabs and the paper tabs were 20 px tall, the pile chips 22, the run and § Rules 25, the help key 26. Share sat alone at the top right at 40 px.

## The change

- **One row.** On `isMobile` the title bar draws the way back (44 px), the title and a 44 px **⋯** button. The three views are under it at the full width, 44 px tall. The kinds of paper, on All filings, are 44 px too.
- **More.** `LienDeskMoreSheet.tsx` (new) over `PhoneSheet` lists the doors the header held. `lienDeskPhoneMore.ts` (new kernel) says which rows and in what order: the run first, with its count, while a notice waits; *Put a GC on notice…* on Notices; then § Rules, *An owner is calling*, the help key under its own name, Share and *An owner asked for records*. Each keeps the name it has on a computer. `lienDeskMoreButton` gives the ⋯ button the run's count as a badge and in its name, so a waiting run is never out of sight.
- **A row opens what its door opens.** `pickMore` closes the sheet and sets the state each door already had. `LienRulesDoor` gained `open` / `onOpenChange` / `button`, since its window lived in the door's own state and would have closed with the sheet. The GC list, which dropped from its button, is a centred window on a phone.
- **The pile chips scroll.** With rows a thumb tall, two pile chips wrapping in a pinned header would have made the Affidavits header taller than before (259 px, measured mid-build). The chips are now `affPileChips` / `retPileChips`: in the header on a computer as before, and on a phone one sideways strip that is the first thing inside the list's own scroller.

No migration, no edge function, no change to what any press writes.
