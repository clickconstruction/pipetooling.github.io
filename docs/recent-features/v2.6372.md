# v2.6372 — The SQL beds run eight at a time, so one run stops filling the runner line (2026-11-02)

One run of the SQL beds was one job per bed: 76 jobs and about 213 job-minutes, with the org limited to 20 jobs at once. From 04:00 to 10:15Z on 2026-11-02, 64% of all runner time went to bed jobs and 7% to builds that landed. At 18:00Z, 170 jobs were waiting behind the 20 running.

- **The change**: `changes` deals `scripts/sql-beds.txt` into legs of at most 16 beds, so today's 76 are five legs of 16, 15, 15, 15 and 15. A longer list adds a leg by itself, so adding a bed is still one line. Each leg is one job running the new `scripts/sql-beds-run.sh`. It pulls each image once, gives each bed its own `PGTEST_PORT` and runs eight at a time. A failed bed does not stop the others, and the leg exits 1 when any bed failed. The `sql-beds` gate still reads `needs.bed.result`.
- **The proof, every bed side by side**: a dispatch on a `ci-test/` branch ran all 76 in five legs and all passed, with 3,666 `ok:` lines. Memory peaked at 2.6 GB of 16 GB with no swap, and at least 80 GB of disk stayed free, so nothing shared a file or ran short of memory.
- **The proof, a red bed**: a second dispatch put a real SQL failure and a bed hanging past its limit in one leg. That leg's third bed still ran and passed, the other leg stayed green, and the hung bed's container was removed. Both beds were named in the log and in the run's annotations with their reasons, the leg failed, and the `sql-beds` gate failed on `bed: failure`.
- **What a run costs now**: five runners, not 20, and 72 job-minutes, not 213. A leg took 12 to 16 minutes on the runner, not the 8 the earlier test of 16 beds suggested: a full bed averaged about 400 s beside seven others, with load near 20 on 4 cores, against about 105 s of work alone. The queue model still drains 30 PRs in 4.6 hours against 8.2 at this leg time, because slots are the limit and not leg length.
- **The limits**: the wrapper stops a bed at 20 minutes, the limit each bed had as its own job. The slowest bed in the proof took 512 s. A hung bed is named and the leg carries on. `timeout-minutes` went from 20 to 45, which is two rounds of that limit plus the pull.
- **A flake that should go away**: "failed to set up container networking", exit 125, was `failed to bind host port 0.0.0.0:55448: address already in use`. The default ports sit inside Linux's ephemeral range, 32768 to 60999, so an outbound connection on the runner could hold one. It hit `supply-house-move-window` on 10-27 and `contact-for-customer` on 10-31. The wrapper's ports start at 25000, below that range.
- **Reading a bed**: GitHub's list of jobs now shows legs, and each leg's summary lists its beds with result and seconds, failures first. In a leg's log each bed is a folded group titled with its name, result and seconds, with its `ok:` lines inside. A failed bed's last 40 lines follow in the open.
- **A new rule for a new bed**: side by side, two beds with one container `NAME` would remove each other's container, so the wrapper fails every leg when two scripts share one. Today none do.
- **What it does not change**: the beds, which beds run and when, the gate, and the work a bed does. Each bed still makes 1,068 `docker exec` calls, one per migration, through one Docker daemon. That is the next cost to look at, and it touches every bed script.
