# v2.8738 — Add a part on a small phone: the chips fit above the keys, and the sheet runs down to them (2026-11-27)

Punch list #398. Findings 301 and 310 of the fixing thread's ledger, from the simulator thread's sittings 1 and 2 of 2026-11-27, briefed as `398-quantity-sheet-foot-stands-off-the-keys.md`. It answers the question v2.8656 left open. Client only. No migration, no edge function. Nothing owed after the merge.

**As seen,** in the iOS Simulator, on a box of the ZZ Test Walk Project: **Add a part**, a part the job already has, the quantity step, the number pad up, no scroll.

- At 375 by 667: the number with − and + and the blue **Add 1 to ZZ4** showed. **+10**, **+25** and **+100** did not. They were a scroll away.
- At both sizes about 55 to 58 points of the page behind showed between the sheet's foot and the keyboard's bar: a part's row and Safari's address pill.

**Read.** Two separate things.

- **The chips.** `Sheet` (`OnSiteSheet.tsx`) kept to 90% of the room above the keys. The quantity step is 339 px from its head through its chips to the blue press, so it wanted 377 px of room. A 375 by 667 phone with the number pad up shows about 320.
- **The strip under the foot.** The sheet's foot does stand on the foot of the room the phone reports. That room ends over Safari's own address pill, which floats above the keys. The scrim is that room and no more, so the page behind showed through around the pill. v2.8656's words *the foot sits on the keys' top* were wrong by the pill's height.

**The mend,** in `src/lib/materials/onSiteSheetScrim.ts` and read by `Sheet`.

- **A short room is tight.** `onSiteSheetTight`: under 480 px of room on a phone, which is the keys being up. It is the thread window's own measure.
- **A tight sheet gives up what is not the work.** `onSiteSheetFit`: it takes the whole room where it took 90%, draws no drag handle, and trims its body's and its foot's padding. In `AddPartSheet.tsx` the number's field stands 56 px, as tall as its − and +, where its own padding made that row 69.
- **The surface runs on under the foot.** `onSiteSheetTail`: where the sheet follows the room, its surface fills from the room's foot down. A press on it does nothing.

Every On site sheet draws through `Sheet`, so each takes the same fit with the keys up. With the keys away, and at a desk, nothing changes.

**Measured on a desk, with a made-up keyboard room.** No test page has a keyboard. A scratch page drew the real sheet over made-up rows in a headless Chrome, and the script set what the page's visual viewport answers. No host was asked beyond the page.

So the step now fits any room of 300 px or more. The 320 is worked back from what the simulator saw, not read off a phone.

**What it costs.** With the keys up the sheet reaches the top of the room, so the strip of scrim over it is gone. **×** closes it, as before.

**Not known from a desk.** How tall the room is on a real phone, and what Safari draws over the strip under the foot. A phone in Safari, an app on a home screen and Chrome on Android each report the room in their own way.
