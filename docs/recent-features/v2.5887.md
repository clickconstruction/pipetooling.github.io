PR 4 of punch list #180 (`to-dos/quickfill-read-state/`). The station already read the thing itself, each truck's last reading against its cadence with the days since, and its count sat at 9 and 10 for eight weeks, the size of the fleet, because readings did not land. What the person held in their head was the office's own side: which drivers were asked, when, and who answered.

- **The read** (`lib/quickfill/vehicleAsks.ts`): for each due row the holder's Messages thread, found through `people.account_user_id` → `contacts.person_id` (a team contact), and its lines since `ASK_LOOKBACK_DAYS` (60). An outbound line after the last reading is the ask, an inbound line after the ask the answer. `askRows` sorts **not asked** (never, or asked `ASK_STALE_DAYS_DEFAULT` = 5 days ago with no answer, most stale first), **answered** (the miles not typed yet), **asked** (waiting on the driver) and **walk** (the motor pool, nobody to ask). `askWords` is the standard ask: *Hi Sam, quick check-in on the 2022 Ford Transit: the last reading was 19 days ago. Text back the miles on the dash and anything the truck needs. Thanks!*
- **The station** (`QuickfillVehicleOdometersSection`, now taking `lastMarkedAt`): a count line *N still to do · N late · N not asked · N asked and waiting · N answered*; the rows still to do first, each with its state pill and words (*Sam Pruitt · never asked*, *Ana Marsh answered 1d ago · type the miles from the thread*), **Copy the ask** and **Open the thread →** (or **Read the answer →**) beside the ☎ call, the miles input and the questions as before; *Asked, waiting on the driver* as a fold under them; *Since the last look* (`asksSince`: crossed late, asked, answered); the stance line. The metric is `askCounts.todo`, the Next line `asksNextLine`, the Mark's note `asksMarkNote`. `vehicleCheckinNextLine` in `nextLine.ts` is no longer read by the station.
- Not built, by design: sending the text from the station. The ask is copied and sent from the holder's thread, where the answer lands; the Texts station's standard-words send (#157) can take it over when it ships.
