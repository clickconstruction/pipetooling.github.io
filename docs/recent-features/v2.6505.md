# v2.6505 — The top of a thread is three rows on every screen (2026-11-04)

Grace, 2026-11-04, with a screenshot of a tall desktop thread window for a customer's contact, the empty right half of the header circled: *I don't like all of this whitespace, I would like the header to be more compact so there is more room to look at messages.* v2.6474 had folded the header on a short window alone; a tall desk kept six rows down the left (the name and company, the email, the chips, the sentence, the jobs under their label) with the right half of the header empty.

## What changed

- `threadHeaderPlan` (`src/lib/messages/threadHeader.ts`, beside `threadFolds`): the header's rows and what waits behind the arrow, from the column's fold and the person's facts. At a desk, at any height: *who* (the name; the company line with their first address, then their first number, riding it), *status* (whose move it is, the slots' chips, the stamps sentence on one line cut with an ellipsis, the whole in its title, wrapping under the chips when they leave it less than 14rem), *jobs* (the rail as it was, each job whole under *Their jobs*). The rest of their numbers and addresses wait behind the arrow the company line already had, which draws only when something waits; the *reach* row opens on its press, closed until then. A narrow column (cut 10's fold, a phone or 640 px) stacks the same facts: the company line cut to one, every number and address behind the arrow, the chips in a strip, the sentence on a row of its own, the jobs in a strip.
- `ThreadWindow`: one header at every height. The short window's second header (v2.6474: *Who is this?* inline, the chips, the sentence and the jobs strip on one row) is gone; *Who is this?* rides the line it answers everywhere now, and the tall desk's blue button row is gone with it. `data-thread-short` is gone; `data-thread-header-rows` names the rows drawn. `isThreadShort` still gives the window its whole height and folds the reply box.
- `reachOpen` left `threadFold.ts`: the arrow is closed by default at every width now that the line carries the first of each.
- Guides: *Read a thread*, *Read a thread on a short screen* and *Read a thread on a phone* in `find-every-message-with-a-person-in-one-place`.

## Two more from Grace on the same window, the column beside the thread

That column is their flow folded to its rail (`FlowTab`'s `side`, `FlowRail`), not the sort card: the lane's name, a mark per step, the count of steps done.

- **The chevron under × pointed the wrong way.** It hides and shows the side, and read ‹ while the side was beside, as if something waited to open from the right. Now it points the way the side moves: › hides a side that is beside, ‹ brings a folded one back (`data-thread-side-fold`, the names *Hide their flow* / *Show their flow* as before, since the column is their flow, not the card). The rail's own ‹ does a different thing, opening the rail into its panel, which grows leftward, and the panel's *fold ›* shrinks it back; both already point the way they move, so both stay.
- **The skipped step's dash sat high and left in its round.** The rail's 22 px mark buttons kept the browser's own padding and line box; now each is a flex box with no padding, the glyph centered both ways, and a skipped step's dash (`panel.ts`'s `–` symbol) is a drawn 8×2 rule in the mark's own colour (`data-flow-mark-dash`), since the dash character sits above the middle of the face in light and dark alike.

## Decisions

- **The compact header is the only header.** Grace's ask is the tall window; one shape at every height means one set of smokes and no second header to keep in step. The short window gives back 26 px against v2.6474's shared row, which cut the job to 45% of the row and faded it; the job card reads whole now. A shared second row when the chips, the sentence and the jobs all fit is the next cut if a laptop asks for it.
- **The company line's arrow keeps its job**: it opens the rest of their numbers and addresses under the line, as before, and goes away when there is nothing behind it.
- **The sentence stays visible, never behind the press**: one line at a desk with the whole in its title, its own row on a phone.
