# v2.7882 — Pay: the dev seat's plan reads a salaried person's own week (2026-11-18)

`plan_pay_setup` and `apply_pay_setup` (v2.7821) read `people_pay_config.salaried_week_hours`. No new verb, no new server number, no migration. The owner said yes to it on 2026-11-18, after the per-person week landed while the pair was in the queue.

## Why

The pair was built when every salaried day was 8 hours on five weekdays. It read a day's length from one helper, `salariedDayHoursOf`, shaped for a column named `salaried_day_hours`. What landed instead is a week: seven numbers, Sunday first, 0 for a day off (v2.7826). The helper found no such field and answered 8 for everyone.

So for a person whose week is not the default the plan would have been wrong in four places: the weekly figure, the weeks and dollars payroll catch-up will show, the job labor words, and a note that said a person's own day was not on the pay row yet. No screen sets a week yet, so no real plan was wrong. The seat had to be right before one does.

## What changed

- **The read.** `PAY_ROW_SELECT` names `salaried_week_hours`. The kernel reads it with the app's own rule, `salariedWeekOf`, imported from the edge copy of `salariedEffectiveHours.ts`. A week that is not seven numbers from 0 to 16 reads as the default whole, as it does in the app.
- **`salaried_week`** in the plan: the seven numbers, the week's hours, the one length every paid day shares or null, whether it is the default, the week in words (*6 h Mon to Thu, 4 h Fri, 28 hours a week*) and a note that the seat reads the week and does not set it. It replaces `salaried_day_hours` and `fixed_day`.
- **`weekly`** is wage × the week's hours, by `salariedWeeklyPay`: *$10.00/h × 28 h (6 h Mon to Thu, 4 h Fri) = $280.00 a week*. It carries `week_hours` where it carried `day_hours`.
- **Payroll.** `salariedCreditHours` credits a day its hours in the week. A Saturday pays when the week says so, and a weekday the week leaves out pays nothing.
- **Job labor.** `reaches_back.job_labor` gains `paid_days`, `unpaid_days` and `flat_hours`. The words say *a flat 6 h* when every paid day is that long and *its day's hours in their week* when the days differ.
- **The hash.** `pay_row` in the plan carries the week as read, so a week set by anyone between the plan and the apply refuses the apply.
- **The workday template** is one 480-minute block for everyone, held by the table's own check. The kernel holds that in `WORKDAY_TEMPLATE_MINUTES`. A workday named with another length is still refused, now in words that say why: the template draws the clock strip and pays nobody. For a person whose week is not the default, the plan's `clock` says the sessions run 8 hours while pay follows the week.
- **A sentence that never filled in.** With Salaried on and no start date, the payroll words printed `${dayH}` as written: the sentence was in plain quotes. It now names the week.

## Not in this change

Setting the week. No screen sets it yet, and the press that does is to ship after every reader reads the column, so a person is never paid on one week and costed on another. `set_pay_config` does not take the key. When the sheet's press lands, the seat's ask can take a week and write it the way the sheet does.

A template that follows the week. That waits on the template's own checks.
