# v2.7103 — More supply house bills add up by themselves: the check learned what the papers print (2026-11-12)

Punch list #298 PR 1c (`to-dos/supply-bill-lines/README.md`), the checks the measure after PR 2 ([v2.6882](v2.6882.md)) asked for. The backfill left bills `off` whose paper was right: the check did not know four things a paper does. They were rebuilt as made-up paper in the tests, and the rules were then run, read only, over every line on file.

## The rules

All in the check kernel, `supabase/functions/_shared/paperChecks.ts`, re-exported by `src/lib/materials/lines/paperChecks.ts`.

- **A price rounded on the paper.** A house that sells pipe by the stick prints a price a foot to three places, so the quantity times the printed price misses the extension by a few cents. A line holds when some price that rounds to the printed one gives the extension: the quantity times half a unit of the last place (`roundingAllowCents`; 200 feet at three places may miss by 10 cents). The paper's price width is its widest price (`pricePlaces`), since 1.240 reads back as 1.24. A paper that prints prices to the cent keeps the cent: there a miss is a misread, and a cheap part's quantity would slip by.
- **A pack priced by the piece.** The quantity is boxes and the price is for one piece. A line whose unit is a pack (a box, a case, a pack, a carton, a bag, a roll, a bundle, a sleeve) holds when its extension is the arithmetic times a whole number (`lineArithmetic`). `packFactorOf` keeps the number for the crosswalk's unit factor. A line whose unit is each never passes this way, so a quantity read ten times too small still fails.
- **A charge outside the subtotal.** A shipping row printed under the subtotal and read as a line makes the lines pass the subtotal by exactly that charge. `outsideChargeCents` takes it off the lines' side and puts it on the total's, only when both sums say so. One charge line is tried, then every charge together.
- **A line that takes money off.** A rebate or a credit among an invoice's parts is negative. The reader keeps the sign now (`signedMoneyCents` in `_shared/billRead.ts`; the schema and the system words ask for it on the line and nowhere else). A line read before this is turned by `settlePaper` when the lines pass the subtotal by exactly twice that line and its words say rebate, credit, discount or return. Two lines that fit are left for a person.
- **Found by the measure again**, on the bills the suffix match ([v2.6897](v2.6897.md)) brought in, which are partial shipments: a line ordered and not shipped prints no quantity shipped and no extension. It is no longer held to the quantity ordered, and `settlePaper` writes its quantity shipped as 0, because the purchase view falls back to the quantity ordered and would have counted it as bought. A price of zero beside a printed extension is a price the reader did not find: not checked, and written empty, so it is never a $0.00 purchase.

`paperFromBillRead` settles the paper before it is checked, so the rows written are the rows checked. `checkPaper` answers what it allowed (`packs`, `outsideCents`, `notes`), and the Look window's green strip says it under the sum.

## Is this the best we can do?

For the sums, close to it: every rule asks two witnesses on the paper before it allows anything, and none loosens a paper priced to the cent. Three things are weaker than they could be.

- The price width is read back from the numbers, because the column keeps a price and not the digits printed. A paper whose every three-place price ends in a zero would be held to the cent. It stays off and a person looks, which is the safe side.
- A pack is told by its unit. A house that sells a box under the unit *EA* stays off.
- The 21 that stay off need a person, and no screen lists them yet. That is PR 3, the held list, next.
