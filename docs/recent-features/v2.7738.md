## What it is

A row on the Workbench can have three prices at once: the saved one, one being typed, and one the solver proposes (which a click can veto). The bar's revenue, profit and margin use the typed price, else an unvetoed proposal, else the saved price. The row's own cells show the typed price, else the saved one; the ghost carries the proposal. That rule was 25 lines inline in the render, with the bar's totals and the priced counts beside it, and nothing tested it.

## What shipped

- **`src/lib/bids/workbenchEffectiveRows.ts`** (pure, 25 tests): `parsePriceDraft` (a cell's text as a price), `workbenchEffectiveRow` / `workbenchEffectiveRows` (the rule above, generic over the row so every other field rides along), and `workbenchEffectiveTotals` (revenue, profit, margin, the proposals Apply would write, the vetoes, the costed rows, how many are priced, the cost sitting unpriced).
- **`BidsPricingTab.tsx`**: two calls where the arithmetic stood. 28 lines out.

It takes the solver's preview, its vetoes and the typed drafts as values. It holds none of them and moves none of them: the solver's state stays where it is, in the tab, under the owner's hold.
