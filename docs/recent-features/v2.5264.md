# v2.5264 — Messages: reading contacts and their messages from the new tables, behind the scenes (2026-10-12)

The Messages build's cut 1 loaders, the pure mappers the first screens need, after the kernels (v2.5249) and beside the
SQL (v2.5258, migration `20261012010000_messages_spine.sql`). No client in them: the hooks that fetch the rows come with
the screens (wave 2). The row types are written out until the generated types carry the new tables.

- **`src/lib/contacts/load.ts`**: `contactsFromRows(rows, channels, jobs, records)` turns `contacts`, `contact_channels`
  and `contact_jobs` rows, with the records they point at, into `Contact`. The name is the contact's own when it has
  one (a person a company's row has no room for, or a number that kept a deleted person's name), else the record's
  (a customer, the person at a customer, a trade partner's `contact_name`, a person it named, a roster person), else
  its first number or address. The company and a trade partner's trades come from the record. Numbers read before
  addresses, a STOP stays on its number, and the office's ties read as job keys with the star apart.
  `contactRecordOfRow` and `offFlowFromRow` (cut 4's `OffFlow`, who by name) sit beside it.
- **`src/lib/messages/load.ts`**: `threadMessagesFromRows(rows, { userNames, contacts })` turns `messages` rows into
  `ThreadMessage`, oldest first. Who is the row's own words, else the user's name, else what we call the person, else
  an unknown number's number. A job is one key, `jobKey` / `jobRefOf` / `jobKeyOfRow` ("project:<uuid>"), which reads
  back to the `p_job_kind` and `p_job_id` the SQL functions take. A channel, a kind or an opt-out value this build does
  not know is left out or read as none, so a newer row never draws wrong. Cut 5's `duration_seconds` reads when present.
- **`src/lib/messages/spineRows.ts`**: rows shaped like the seed and the first lines, for the tests and the screens'
  smokes. Made-up ids, names, numbers and addresses only.

Tests: `contacts/load.test.ts` (11) and `messages/load.test.ts` (8), including the loaded rows under the kernels (the
thread's rows, whose move, the stamp sentences, Who is this? by name on a company's row). Five pins were broken on
purpose and caught: the who fallback, numbers before addresses, an unknown job kind, the company of a person on a
customer's row, and off_by read as an id.
