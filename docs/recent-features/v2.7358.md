## What changed

- **The master's sheet** ([`DifficultyRateSheet.tsx`](../../src/components/bids/DifficultyRateSheet.tsx) over `PhoneSheet`, kernel [`difficultyRateSheet.ts`](../../src/lib/bids/difficultyRateSheet.ts), reads `difficultyRateSheetIo.ts`; the canvas's screen 2): *Rate <GC>* with *Bid BP605 · project* over it, a sheet from the bottom on a phone and a centred box at a desk, 44 px rungs. The same scales (`DifficultyScalesPicker`, `scope="gc"`), the same rules and the same save as the GC's card (`saveRating`), so the newest rating stands and every chip reads again. Paying is locked: *1 point from their bills · Paid in 52 days, the median of 7 bills. Not yours to pick.*, or the point the office set by hand on the card. A GC already rated opens on its picks with *Rated by … on …* and the press wakes only on a change. **Open their record** is the door to the card.
- **The consequence before Save**: `difficultyLines` (the kernel that writes the lines) over the bid's own numbers, read in this order: the bid's pin (`bid_difficulty`: the field hours, rate, own cost and carried price its lines were made from), else the margin kept on the bid less its difficulty part with the estimate's field hours and rate, else the estimate alone. *4 points · about +$5,150 in cost*, then *48 field hours, 12 office hours and the wait on their pay. About +$7,357 on the price at 30%.*, the cap's line and any scale that adds nothing as the Workbench says them. A bid with neither hours nor a cost reads *This bid is not costed yet, so the points have no dollars to show.* Under it, **Bids like this** for this GC: the decided bids to them of the same kind of work from `bid_pricing_history`, and the estimated margin the won ones went at (*won 2 of 3 to them, at 28 to 32%*); left out with none.
- **The door on the Bid Board**: the chip *Rated N points* on the list row, the phone card and the opened row, and **Rate them** on the opened row, open the sheet for that bid (`GcDifficultyChip`'s `onRate`) for whoever the price list lets rate; for everyone else the chip still opens the card, as it does on the Workbench.
- **The ask at the end of a job** (`gc_difficulty_asks`, `gc_difficulty_open_asks()`, [`gcDifficultyAsk.ts`](../../src/lib/bids/gcDifficultyAsk.ts), `gcDifficultyAskIo.ts`, `useGcDifficultyAsks`, [`GcDifficultyAsk.tsx`](../../src/components/bids/GcDifficultyAsk.tsx)): a job has no closed status, so the moment is its first move to Ready to Bill, which `update_job_status` already logs. The open asks are derived from those events by a `SECURITY INVOKER` function: the job's master, a GC on the job (the card's rule), that GC not rated since the job closed, nothing on record for that job. Two doors: a line at the top of the job's window (*This job is done. How was <GC> to work with?* · **Rate them** · **Not now**) and **Rate GCs after a job · N** on the Bid Board's tools row, which draws only with something open and lists the GCs, folded so one answer settles every job with that GC. The sheet names the job in its lead and in *What happened*. A save writes the rating and one row per job with it; *Not now* writes the row with the day and says the job will not ask again.
- Guide *rate how a GC is to work with* gains *Rate from a bid* and *The ask at the end of a job*; ACCESS_CONTROL, APP_DIRECTORY and the glossary entry amended.

## Not changed

- The Workbench: its chip still opens the card, and its strip still offers *Read the new rating in* when the GC is rated after the bid was priced. That is how a rating from the sheet reaches a priced bid.
- The by-hand Paying point and the earlier ratings stay on the card.
- The site scale and *What the rating did* (PR 6).

## Is this the best we can do?

- **Doubted: a second press beside the chip.** The brief said to add an `onRate` press beside the chip. PR 3 took *Rate them* off every row because twenty links on a screen were loud; a second control beside every chip is the same noise. Changed: the chip is the press. On the Bid Board it opens the sheet for that bid, and the sheet carries the door to the record the chip used to be.
- **Doubted: writing a row when a job closes.** The plan had a row per ask. A trigger on the status events would have put a write of mine inside every crew's Ready to Bill press. Changed: the open asks are a read over the events already logged, and a row is written only when a person answers or puts the ask away. The cost is one constant, the day the ask began, inside the function.
- **Doubted: one ask per job.** On prod about 22 GC jobs a month reach Ready to Bill and every job's master is the owner. Twenty-two sheets a month for one person is how an ask gets ignored. Changed: the asks fold by GC, a GC anyone has rated since the job closed is not asked about, and one answer settles every job with that GC. The job's own window still shows that job's ask.
- **Doubted: the same picks as before.** From a bid the press wakes only on a change, as on the card. From the ask, *the same as last time, on this job* is the answer most jobs will give. Changed: from the ask, the same picks with *What happened* filled is a rating.
- **Doubted: the Bridge's queue.** The plan said the Bridge's queue if one fits. The Bridge's Needs you deck is a fill-in system with its own row kinds, fields and undo, and a rating is none of those. Left out: the ask stands in the job's window and on the Bid Board.
- **Doubted: the numbers under the consequence.** The sheet reads the bid's kept margin off the Bid Board's row, which is as old as the board's last load. Kept: the box says *about*, and the Workbench writes the real lines.
- **Left for Micah, with my pick:** who is asked. Every job's `master_user_id` is the owner today, so every ask is his. My pick is to keep it to the job's master until projects carry superintendents, then ask the superintendent as well.
- **Left for Micah, with my pick:** whether the owner wants the asks on the Bridge too. My pick is the Bid Board's press alone until he says he does not see it.
- **For the walk:** a dev is asked about customers named `ZZ …`, in the function, so the ask can be walked live on a test job. No real job is touched.
