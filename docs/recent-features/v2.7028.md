# v2.7028 — Pay calendar: a billed row says the day the contract makes it due (2026-11-12)

Punch list #323, PR 2 of seven, stacked on v2.7024. A payer on a Pay calendar now reads **Due** on its billed rows, with when its money usually lands under it. The forecast, the phone rows and the Lien desk read the same model.

The rule the card settled on a second pass: the contract says when a bill is due, the record says when it lands, and the app never passes one off as the other. So nothing that counts money moved off the expected day. What moved is how the expected day is worked out for a payer on a calendar, and what the row leads with.

## What changed

- **The model**, `src/lib/jobs/billedDuePay.ts`: `billedPayModel(input, data, todayYmd, promise, pace, calendar)` wraps `billedExpectedPayModel`. With no calendar it is the pace guess, unchanged, plus four empty fields. With one: `dueYmd` is `dueDayFor` of the bill's reference day; `expectedYmd` is the due day plus the payer's usual lateness against its own due days (`latenessAgainst` over the pay-speeds receipts), and the due day alone with no payment on record; `source` is `terms`; `daysPastDue` counts from the due day while `state` and `daysLate` still count from the expected day. A promise still wins the expected day and carries the due day beside it. With a calendar and no readable pay speeds it still answers.
  - **`billedExpectedPayModel` is untouched**, so its edge copies under `supabase/functions/_shared/gcKernels/` are byte for byte what they were and no function is behind. The emails and the Bridge move in PR 6.
  - The clock is `billedReferenceYmd` (the billed day, else the estimated bill date), the same day the record is measured from, so the due day and the lateness read one clock. No accepted day exists yet (PR 4).
- **The dates block**, `billedDatesLedger.ts`: with a calendar the money row is *Due Nov 10 · 2d past* (green ahead, amber past, red in Collections), its sub-line *Usually lands about Nov 16* (`usuallyLandsShort`: the day leads, since the block is about 210 px wide and cuts a tail; the hover has the math). Every deadline row and the verdict still weigh `moneyYmd`, the expected day. A promise keeps the row and reads *due Nov 10 by their pay calendar* under it.
- **The line under the dates** (`buildReliabilityLine`, `BilledReliabilityLine`): for a payer on a calendar the calendar's own words (*In by month end, paid the 10th*) stand where *Pays in 12–68d* stood, since that spread is the calendar's doing. They are a press that opens the Pay calendar window; the press's name says so (`opens`).
- **The Payment forecast** (`billedPaymentForecast.ts`, `BilledPaymentForecastModal.tsx`): takes the calendars, buckets by the model's expected day, and each such row shows *· due Nov 10 · 2d past*, amber once past. The date on the right is still the expected day.
- **The phone rows** (`jobNextLine.ts`): *2 d past due* in amber for a bill past its due day whose money is not expected yet. *N d past expected* still wins when both are true.
- **The Lien desk and the runway** (`lienCalendarRows.ts`, `useLienCalendarRows.ts`): `lienRunwayForJob` takes the calendars and reads `billedPayModel`, so *Expected to pay* and every *Can run late* is the due day plus lateness.
- **The read**: `useBilledMoneyData` loads the calendars once beside the pay speeds (`payCalendars`, `loadPayCalendars`), fail-soft.
- **Two more doors**: the calendar's words on a billed row, and `PayCalendarLine` on the payer's card in Pay speeds.
- **Guides**: a section in *see when a customer will pay*, *What it changes* in *set a payer's pay calendar*, a paragraph in *read the payment forecast*.

## Not changed

- A payer with no calendar: every date, every word, every bucket.
- The forecast email, the Money waiting email, the Monday Bridge email, Call mode, the job window's bills list, Dashboard AR and the Bridge's Collect pile. They read the pace guess until PR 6.
- How pay speed is measured, and the Pay speeds medians.

## Is this the best we can do?

- **Two dates on a row is one more thing to read.** The row is narrow, so the sub-line was cut on the first live look (*Usually lands about 56d after. Abo…*) and now leads with the day. The honest date is in small grey words under an amber one; that is the right order for a row whose job is to say whether to call, and the forecast puts the honest date on the right.
- **Past due is not past expected, and the board now says both.** For a payer who runs two months past its contract, every bill reads amber for most of its life. That is true, and it is also noise. The Lien desk's verdict line under it is what says whether today needs anything.
- **Readers disagree until PR 6.** A row reads Due on the board while the forecast email still guesses by pace for the same bill.
- **Lateness rests on at most 12 payments**, and a payer with one payment gets that one payment's lateness as its usual.
