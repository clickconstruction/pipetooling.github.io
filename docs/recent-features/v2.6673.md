# v2.6673 — Materials on site: the missing money reads the parts book's price, with the day it was checked (2026-11-07)

Punch list #209, the owner's call 5 and the one leftover the card named as worth building before the week of use. Since v2.6043 the office page priced a shortfall off a unit price the office had typed from an invoice, kept in this browser alone (`priceOf` in the store), so the desk's money was only as good as what one device remembered and read *demo price* beside every figure. The parts book (`material_part_prices`, one row per part and supply house, `effective_date` written by Check prices) now prices the line, with its checked day beside the money.

## What changed

- **`src/lib/materials/bookPrice.ts`**, a pure kernel with its tests: `bookPriceRow` reads a book row (the checked day is `effective_date`, else the row's updated day, else never); `pickBookPrice` takes the supply house's latest price — the row checked most recently wins, a same-day tie goes to the lower price, a row never checked comes after every dated one — and flags it stale past `BOOK_PRICE_FRESH_DAYS` (90, the parts book's own rule); `priceLookup` is the one rule by part key: a fresh book price first, the invoice price where the book's is stale or missing, a stale book price flagged when that is all there is, null when nothing is known; a `text:` key (a name typed on site) only ever carries the invoice price. `priceWords` and `priceTitle` are the chip and its title: *parts book · checked Oct 3*, *parts book · checked Jul 1, 129 days ago*, *parts book · never checked*, *invoice price*.
- **The store** (`onSiteStore.ts`) reads the book's rows for the parts a job's ledger names beside each job's load, once per part, through `loadPartPriceRows` (the paged reader every cross-part price read uses), and again for a part a new press names. `priceFor(key)` is the pick; `priceOf(key)` is its number alone, so `officeFlow`, `manifestComparison` and `jobMaterialsSummary` read the book without a change — the desk's *Missing this week* column, the flow row's red badge, the Job window's chip and the ⋯ menu's count all moved to the book's price with this PR. `rememberPrice` stays: the invoice's unit price is the fallback. A failed book read keeps its reason (`bookPricesState`) and is asked again (`retryBookPrices`).
- **Jobs → Materials → Missing**: the *demo price* chip is gone. Beside each priced figure in *Bought · reported · left · walked away* and *Reported this week* a chip names the source and the checked day, amber when the book's check is older than 90 days, with the supply house in its title; the table's note says which price the money uses. When the book could not be read, a line under the heading says why, says the money reads the invoice prices alone, and offers **Read the parts book again**.
- **Docs**: the guide *see what is on every job site and what walked away* (Missing), `docs/MATERIALS_ON_SITE_ARCHITECTURE.md` (Money, the gaps), the card's status, As built, Where it stands and Left list, the release note and this fragment. No migration, no function.

## Decisions (the card's recommendation, named)

- **Which price when a part has several.** The call said *the supply house's latest price*; a ledger row names no supply house, so the latest is the row checked most recently across the part's houses, the lower price on a same-day tie. The lowest price would understate a loss at the house the job buys from; the newest check is the number someone most recently looked at.
- **What the PO price is.** The call's fallback past 90 days is *the PO price*; the price the office read off the invoice that landed the part is that number on this surface, so it stays as the fallback, and keeps the name *invoice price*. It still lives on the device that read it: putting it on the Delivered row is a column the ledger does not have, left for the week of use to ask for.
- **When the book has none**, the invoice price if one was read, else *no price* as before. A typed name is never matched to a book part by its words: the card's rule is that the office ties it later, and a guessed match would price a loss off the wrong part.
