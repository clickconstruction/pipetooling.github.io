# v2.3525 — Card refunds net on the database side: the six SQL readers (2026-09-16)

PR 2 of the card-refunds train. One migration, no client change, no edge function redeploy.

## Why

v2.3519 made every client reader treat a Mercury job allocation as signed cost (`cardChargeCostUsd = −amount`), so a card refund nets against the job's parts. Six database functions still took `ABS(a.amount)`, so until this landed the billed-aging cost column, the paid-profit chart, the partner ledgers, a kept baseline and the paid-job email all counted a refund as more spend — and disagreed with Job Summary on every job that had one.

## What

[`20260916190000_card_refunds_net_sql_readers.sql`](../migrations/20260916190000_card_refunds_net_sql_readers.md) replaces `get_paid_job_email_payload`, `get_billed_aging_costs`, `get_paid_profit_stats`, `partner_job_cost_buckets`, `partner_job_costing_payload` and `keep_job_baseline` with their newest bodies and only the sign expression changed (`SUM(ABS(a.amount))` → `SUM(-a.amount)` and the three equivalent forms). Generated from the source migrations by a script that asserted each source was the function's latest definition and that exactly the expected number of expressions changed — 939 lines of function body, 7 changed expressions, no `abs(` left.

The paid-job email needs no code change: since v2.3500 its charge timeline splits rows on `kind`, not sign, and draws a charge with a positive delta as money the house gave back. A card refund now arrives from the RPC with a negative amount and takes that path.

## Verified

`scripts/check-migrations.sh` (594 files, lock_timeout preamble present), the release-note drift test. Applied with `bash scripts/db-push.sh` after merge; the paid-profit RPC read before and after for J857 (TJ Brace, a paid job with a Lowe's $84.18 refund) — see the PR for the two readings.

## Left

Two transaction-level readers stay on magnitude by design: Wheels (fuel spend per person, where a fuel refund is its own question) and the week-close's unallocated remainder (a to-do amount, not a cost).
