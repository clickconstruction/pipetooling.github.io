Punch list #217, *Show it, don't say it*: eight scans found 131 places where the app tells instead of shows, and a mock-up page draws each as a before/after pair for the owner to pick from. Of people-docs-8, the houses' own notice dates on Held for suppliers, the owner asked on 2026-10-30 to see it "side by side in the form of a prototype". ## What changed

- **`houseNoticeStrip.ts`** (`src/lib/materials/`, new): the pure kernel from the Lien desk's `LienSupplierJob` (the houses, invoices and words the tab already holds) to rows on one calendar. Per owed house: its unpaid invoices as ticks on their days (*unpaid invoice Aug 12 · $3,120*, this job's slice); its window as a bar from the first day the oldest unpaid month's notice can go out (`lienNoticeOpensOn`, the desk's own reading) to the day our estimate names (`lienSupplierNotice`: the soonest open deadline, `soon` inside the desk's lead days, or the last one to close as a `closed` bar), the days already gone hatched to today; the day the house gave as its own `told` mark with who said it and when it was written down; and a `source` mark — *Bluebonnet Supply · by Nov 16 · guessed from the invoice months*, *Lone Oak Plumbing · goes out Nov 20 · they said · Marisol, Oct 21*, *… · window closed Aug 17 · guessed from the invoice months*. `ahead` is the house's standing as `heldNoticeRisk` reads it. The strip's `axis` is `windowsAxis` over every bar, tick and told day, so an old invoice month is on the calendar; `chips` are the job's state (*paid in full*, *2 houses can still notice*); `legend` lists only what the rows use; `words` is the strip in one sentence. Five tests in `houseNoticeStrip.test.ts`: an estimate from two invoice months, a told day, both on one calendar, a closed window with a told day gone by, a paid-up house left out. (The statute's 15th rolls off a weekend, so the mock-up's *Nov 15* is *Nov 16* in 2026.)
- **`HeldHouseNoticeStrip.tsx`** (`src/components/materials/`, new): the picture in the Lien timeline's mini Windows shape — a name column (*Bluebonnet Supply · $6,104*), a month axis with today's label, today a `--text-link` line down every row, each bar in the lien chart's amber (`--bg-amber-tint` on `--text-amber-800`) or its closed grey struck with the red ✗, the hatch of days gone, a `--text-strong` tick per unpaid invoice, a `--text-violet-700` diamond on the day the house gave, a caption under the bar (*notice window · by Nov 16 · unpaid invoices Aug 12 · Sep 3*); under the rows the source marks, a dashed amber pill for *guessed* and a solid one for *they said*, each guessed pill carrying ↗ as the **They told us…** door for that house; then the legend. ## What the mock-up asked for that this does not do

- **The bar from the invoice itself.** The mock-up's bars started near the newest invoice. The kernel starts a bar where the desk starts a window, the first of the month after the oldest unpaid month (`lienNoticeOpensOn`), so the two calendars agree; the invoices are the ticks under it.
- **The job's own title line with chips.** The mock-up put *paid in full* and *2 houses can still notice* on the job's heading. The tab's heading is the row the list sorts and filters; the chips sit on the red line inside the open statement, where today's lead sentence was.
- **One bar per unpaid month.** A house with invoices in two months draws one bar to the soonest open deadline, as the words did; the earlier month's own closed window is not drawn apart. The Lien desk's Windows calendar still draws every month.
