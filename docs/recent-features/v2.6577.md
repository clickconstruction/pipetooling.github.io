# v2.6577 — Every strip on the Lien desk's Do now opens a step brief, not only Reminders (2026-11-06)

PR 2 of the step-brief plan (v2.6574 was PR 1, Grace's ask of 2026-11-06: the desk should guide while the user keeps final say). v2.6574 gave the REMINDERS rail a brief and left the NOTICES, AFFIDAVITS and RETAINAGE rails with the head, the count and the sort. This PR writes their words.

## What changed

- **`lienNextUpSteps.ts`**: `lienStepBrief(on)` — for the REMINDERS rail it hands to `lienReminderBrief`; for the other three it returns the same shape per rung: `what`, `why`, the `usual` press as the row names it (`Find the owner`, `Draft notice`, `Sign and approve ▸`, `Send the run`; `Fix the property`, `Draft affidavit`, `Approve — file it ▸`, `File the affidavit`), `who` (the leader on Approve, the office elsewhere), `instead` as the presses the notice's, affidavit's or retainage pane already offers (`Not yet`, `Pull back to draft`, `Ask counsel to sign off…`, `Hold — they promised…`, `Hold — I’ll call first`, `The leader said to send it ▸` / `file it ▸`, `Property record ›`, `Fill in the record ›`, `Send the notice first ›`, `Refer to the Legal desk ›`, `Record service ›`, `Open the affidavit tab ›`, `Set the day our contract ended ›`, `The monthly notices on this job ›`, `Back to the office`, `Record the mailing`), and `closes`. Three tests cover the twelve rungs and the hand-off.
- **`LienDeskNextUp.tsx`**: `brief={lienStepBrief(on)}` for every rail; the schedule strip stays the REMINDERS rail's.
- **Smokes** in `LienDeskNextUp.render.test.tsx`: a NOTICES rung at 1,400 reads its what, usual and instead; an AFFIDAVITS rung at 375 reads its usual and instead.
- **Guides**: `send-lien-notices-from-the-lien-desk` describes the brief and the sort where it described the rails; `read-the-reminders-ladder-on-the-lien-desk` says the other strips open the same kind of brief. `docs/twins/APP_DIRECTORY.md` amended.

## What it never does

Change a press or a rule. The alternatives are the panes' own buttons, named; a reader who presses one finds it where the brief said.
