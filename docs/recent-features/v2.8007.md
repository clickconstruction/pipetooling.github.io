# v2.8007 — Document builder: an insurance sheet for the subs on a job you run as the GC (2026-11-19)

Punch list #404, step 3 of ten: the standing sheet and a job's sheet. No migration and no function here. The tables came in v2.7988 and are pushed.

Every word and every amount in the first draft is a placeholder, and no insurance agent has set any of them. The made-up sheet holds no dollar figure: each amount reads *An amount* until a person types one. The app suggests no amount anywhere.

## What changed

- A second builder in the registry, **Insurance requirements for subs**, a *filled* builder: a paper with fields a person types on, where the Sub NDA is a *walked* one. Its row counts the jobs that have a sheet.
- **The standing sheet** is the company's usual: its name and address, the opening lines, and the coverage lines with their amounts. It is the `document_builders` row's draft, and the published copy is what a job goes out on.
- **This job** is one row of `document_builder_sheets` for a job the company runs as the GC (`gc_projects`). It holds only what is the job's own: the names the policy must list, the place, a field typed over the usual, a coverage line not asked for here, and a line added for this job.
- Typing is on the sheet itself (`BuilderField`, a plain-text field). A draft saves by itself a moment after the last key, and the bar reads *Saving the draft…* then *Draft saved*.
- The left card lists what differs on a job, one row each, with **Back to the usual**, **Make this the usual**, **Put it back** and **Take it off**.
- **Make this the usual** writes to the usual's draft and leaves the job's own words alone until that usual is published. A job never loses words it shows a sub.
- **Publish this job's sheet** is dead until the job's blanks are typed, and the page lists what is missing. A publish writes the job's own part and the whole sheet as a sub reads it, on the usual revision it was laid over.
- Publishing the usual goes to each job that is ready and says so on the button with its count. A job with no sheet yet, or with changes of its own waiting, is held and named with its reason. A job that fails is named afterwards and the rest still go.
- The read mark is the insurance agent's here: *Not checked by your insurance agent* until a person types the agent's name against the published revision. A job that differs from a checked usual says how many things differ.
- **Marks are on** shows where each word comes from. Off, the sheet reads as a sub sees it.
- **Print the sheet** prints the published revision in a light page and keeps the copy as a sent copy of kind `insurance_sheet`.

## The rules, each a kernel with its tests

| `src/lib/documentBuilder/sheet.ts` | What the database holds, as a `SheetDef` and a `JobSheet`, or every gap in words. A job's own part laid over the usual (`layOver`). What differs (`sheetDiffers`). What a job still has to type (`jobSheetMissing`). Which jobs a change to the usual goes to (`usualPublishPlan`). |
| `src/lib/documentBuilder/sheetScreen.ts` | The Publish press and why it is dead, the line under the switch, the chips, the stamp at the foot of the sheet, the read mark's press. |
| `src/lib/documentBuilder/sheetPrint.ts` | The printed page. |
| `src/lib/documentBuilder/sheetsIo.ts` | The reads and the writes. Every write names the revision it started from, so two people cannot publish over each other. |
| `src/lib/documentBuilder/insuranceSheet.fixture.ts` | The first draft: a made-up company, placeholder lines, no amount. |

## What it does not do yet

- Nothing leaves the building. The sheet's link is step 4 and the send to a job's trades is step 5.
- A certificate is not held against a job's sheet. That is step 6 and waits on the owner's call.
- The types file does not know the tables yet, so `sheetsIo.ts` casts the client once.
