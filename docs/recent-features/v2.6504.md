# v2.6504 — A press anywhere else in a thread folds the reply box (2026-11-04)

Grace, on a desktop-width thread (Rusty Cotterby at Ardent United, the reply box open with "Rusty," typed and Email picked), liked the folded box of v2.6477 and asked that a click on anything that is not the box fold it. Until now only a short window or a phone folded the box, and only a send folded it back.

## What changed

- `replyBoxFolded` (`src/lib/messages/replyFold.ts`) takes `foldedByPress`: a press outside the box since it last woke. The box folds while nothing holds it open and either the thread asks it to (a short window or a phone, with the box blank, as before) or a press outside folded it, on any window height, words and all. Files waiting on the reply, a text Send texts holds, words the box opened with on its own (a nudge, a won bid's words, a settle under way), a job's box and a STOP block keep it whole either way.
- `ReplyBox` listens for the click through `useCloseOnOutsideClick` with the box's own element as the root, only at the thread window's foot (a job's box and the Answer all desk's never fold) and only while a press would fold it. A press on the box's one-line field, or on a kind, wakes it whole again; typing into a folded field that kept the focus wakes it too. `after` (every send and note) clears the press, so the box goes back to how the window opened it: whole on a tall window, folded on a short one or a phone.
- `useCloseOnOutsideClick` reads inside from the click's path (`composedPath`), not from `contains` on the target: a control that removes itself under its press (a file chip's ×, a picker's row that closes the picker) has left the tree by the time the click reaches the document, and a detached node is inside nothing. It listens on the capture phase when told to (`capture: true`, the box's call), since a window's panel (`ThreadWindow`, `PhoneSheet`) stops its clicks on the way up and the document's bubble listener never heard a press on the thread (the first live walk: the field blurred, the box stayed whole). It ignores a click stamped before it was attached: the row's click that opens the thread reaches the document after the box's effect has attached, and folded the box as it opened (the first live walk at 1400×900). It takes `{ escape: false }` for a caller whose window owns Escape. Its five other callers keep the bubble phase and their behaviour: the first CI run put the whole hook on capture, and the Jobs tools menu closed under a press on an Edit rail, whose row stops the click (`JobsStagesTab.render.test.tsx`, Edit mode).
- Guides: the paragraph under *Open the reply box* in `reply-to-a-person-from-their-thread`, and the reply box line under *Read a thread on a phone* and *Read a thread on a short screen* in `find-every-message-with-a-person-in-one-place`.

## What folds it, what never does

Folds it: a click on the person's lines, the header, the sort card, the owed strip, the window's own chrome, anything in the thread that is not the box. Never: the kinds, the Quick replies strip and the finder and editor it opens, the slash list, the field, the from line, Tie to and its picker, Attach and its sheet and the file chips, Send and its window. Every one of those draws inside the box's element (none portals), so v2.6477's reason stands: a fold never pulls a control out from under the press that caused it. Escape does not fold it; the thread window owns Escape.

## Decisions

- **No mark on a folded row with words.** The one-line field is the same textarea, so the first words show in the row ("Rusty," stays) and the caret comes back on the next press. A mark would say what the words already say.
- **A send puts the box back to the window's opening state, not to folded.** On a tall window the box has the room, and before this PR a send left it whole there; the fold under a press is the person's, and lasts until they press in or send.
- **A click, not a pointerdown.** The hook's reason (v2.3772) holds here too: a scroll gesture on the lines leaves the box as it is, and only a real press elsewhere folds it.
