# v2.6286 — The On site tab no longer shrinks on a phone as cages are added (2026-11-01)

Client only. Malachi, a master technician, on the Tally's On site tab, job 972, sixty cages added in a morning: "as he adds more and more materials, it gets smaller and smaller and smaller." Walked on an iPhone 17 simulator against his job, read-only (the write-up and five screenshots sit in the side-session notes folder, `materials-on-site-shrink.md`).

- **What shrank**: the whole page, not a row. The container list's Places row (*Places ▾ · All 60 · Arena 54 · Gun dog 5 · No place yet 1*) is a flex wrapper inside the tab's grid, and a grid item's default `min-width: auto` let it take the chips' full content width (468 px against a 327 px column) instead of letting the strip scroll. Every element in the column was then laid out wider than the phone and cut off at the right edge. iOS Safari allows a page wider than the screen to be zoomed out, so one pinch while scrolling the long list rendered the entire app at about 0.8 and left it there; every added cage grew the counts and crept the minimum zoom down. The cage screen itself fit; nothing in the Add a part sheet, the stepper or the row cards changed size with the count, and the Materials Estimate tab's *Parts to save* list was stable at six rows.
- **The fix**: `minWidth: 0` on that wrapper in [`TallyOnSiteTab.tsx`](../../src/components/tally/TallyOnSiteTab.tsx), so the chips strip (already `overflowX: auto`) scrolls inside the screen like the day row above it. On the simulator the page fits with a right margin, the Map press and the chips sit inside the screen, and a pinch no longer zooms the page at all, since Safari has nothing wider than the screen to fit.
- **Not done, and why**: a `minimum-scale=1` beside the iOS-only `maximum-scale=1` in `index.html` was weighed and dropped: since iOS 10 Safari ignores both caps for a user's pinch, so the cap would not have held. The layout is the guard.
- **Seen on the way, not chased**: Malachi presses **Count this box** right after every add, as if it were Save, sixty times that morning; the big blue press under a fresh part invites it. The home-screen (standalone) app shifted its content up under the status bar twice with no keyboard involved.
