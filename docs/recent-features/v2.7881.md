# v2.7881 — The sub's page says how long this customer usually takes (2026-11-18)

Punch list #388, step 3 of 3 (`to-dos/sub-page-customer-paid/README.md`). The client, the `sub-portal` function's source and the docs. No migration. The function is deployed only on a go that names it.

## Why

A sub's sheet waits at **Waiting on customer** until the customer pays, and the page said nothing about how long that wait usually is. The owner asked on 2026-11-17 and took the card's picks the same day: yes, on a page with no sign-in, as a range in weeks and never the days figure or the payer's name (call 3), and only from five payments in the last 12 months (call 4).

## What landed

- **The rule**, pure, beside the paid day in `supabase/functions/_shared/subPortalCustomerPaid.ts`.
  - `usualPayDays` is the payer's 25th to 75th percentile of days from a bill to its money. Below five measurable payments it is null.
  - `usualPayWeeks` turns that into whole weeks that hold the days: the fast end rounds down and the slow end rounds up. A slow end of a week or less reads *within a week*. A fast end never reads zero.
  - `usualPayBySheet` picks the sheets and the payer. The line shows on a sheet at `customer_pay` with no paid day and no payable-after date. The payer is whoever the sheet's bill goes to (`effectiveInvoiceParty`), the builder on a builder-billed bill, and the job's own rule on a sheet with no tie. A bill typed to someone else has no payer of record and says nothing.
- **The same receipts as the office.** The figures come from `get_billed_customer_pay_speeds`, the answer behind *Pays in 9–41d*. `payerGapDays` reads its receipts as the office's parser does. A parity test holds `usualPayDays` to `paySpeedSpread` for every payer at the floor.
- **The function** asks for the pay speeds only when a sheet is waiting with no payment and no pay day. It reads those sheets' jobs and tied bills for the payer, and sends `usualPayWeeks: { lo, hi }` on the sheet. No days, no name, no count. Every read is fail-soft.
- **The page.** One line under the waiting sentence, in the three forms the owner read: *This customer usually pays 4 to 6 weeks after we ask.*, *about 4 weeks* and *within a week*. English and Spanish, in `subPortalI18n.ts`. The line goes away once the customer pays, and once the sheet is queued.
- **The office's row** on Jobs → Subs → Pay says what the sub reads: *Sub sees: usually 4 to 6 weeks*, in grey. It reads the same rule on the office's own pay speeds (`loadUsualPayWeeks`). A role the pay speeds are not shown to sees no line.

## What the page never does

- It never shows days, an amount, a count of payments or who pays.
- It never shows a pay time from fewer than five payments, and never falls back to the company's pace.
- It never gives a date. The word is *usually*.
- It never promises a shorter wait than the office's own figure shows.

## The Spanish

The three forms were on the page the owner read on 2026-11-18, side by side with the English: *Reads right*. No word differs from those three.
