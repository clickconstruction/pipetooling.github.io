# v2.6381 — Put many jobs on a part of the site in one press, each job named so a person can pick (2026-11-02)

The Gun Dog design spike (`to-dos/developments/SPIKE_GUNDOG.md`), PR 4 of four, on v2.6379: candidate G of the notes and its second cut. Gun Dog had 24 jobs on no part and four parts; before this each job's part was set one at a time on the job's Documents tab. The first cut listed the jobs as a grid of bare numbers; the owner: *I need a good way to see the job names so a human can quickly make decisions*. No migration: the write is the one `pickPart` the Documents tab makes, per job.

## Change

- **The picker.** `DevelopmentPartsCard.tsx`: under the *N jobs on no part* chip a writer with parts sees one row per job (`developmentPickRow`, `site-pick-<id>`): the tick, the number as the door, the job's name beside it (a long name cut at the column's edge, the whole on its title; *no name* when the job has none), a ticked row tinted; three columns on a desk, one on a phone. **Tick all N**, **Clear**, the part select (`site-part-for-jobs`, *on which part…*) and the primary press `countedLabel('Put', n, 'job')` + *on Lodge* (dead until a job is ticked and a part picked). A reader keeps the plain list of doors.
- **The order.** `src/lib/plans/sitePlans.ts`: `SiteCarrier` carries `name` (the page passes the job's name, else its address), and `orderForPicking` sorts the jobs on no part by name, the nameless last, ties by number, so the *ATV building* jobs sit together and the *Bunker* ones after them.
- **The write.** `SitePlansWrites.putJobsOnPart(partId, jobIds)` in `DevelopmentPage.tsx` runs `siteIo.pickPart` per job under `Promise.allSettled`, toasts `batchOutcomeWords({ verb: 'put on the part', … })` with each failed job's label and `formatErrorMessage` reason, bumps the site read and reloads the page.
