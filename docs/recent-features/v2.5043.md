# v2.5043 — Bids and jobs: the margin a bid was priced at, beside the job's burn (2026-10-09)

Client, plus migration `20261010010000_bid_priced_margin.sql`, which Punchlist pushes after merge. No edge function. This is Burn against the bid, piece 1. It is the first deferred piece of the v2.3297–v2.3306 train, approved at the owner's sitting of **2026-10-09** and sent by Punchlist. The four choices Punchlist confirmed:

1. **What is stored:** the Pricing strip's own Margin, (revenue − our cost) ÷ revenue. It is stored with its inputs, so a reader can see why a stamp is partial: the revenue, the cost, the revenue on rows with no cost, whether a labor rate was set, the version, who and when.
2. **When:** stamped by the workbench's own price writes, never on a tab that is only opened, and frozen at send. It reads **the margin the bid went out at**. Repricing a sent bid does not move it, and the server refuses too.
3. **Where:** beside the job's burn on the job's Costs verdict, and on Bids → Bid Costs → Bid vs actual. Each linked job's dollar spend on the lens is piece 2.
4. **Not here:** the fourth budget footing (price × (1 − priced margin) for a bid with no usable estimate). It is its own PR once stamps exist, named on the new card `to-dos/burn-against-the-bid.md` (#102).

Data when built (read-only, 2026-10-09): 18 jobs are linked to bids, 9 of them open. Every one carries a ◆ snapshot and none is usable. No linked bid has a labor rate, and 9 bids in all do. So stamps begin with the next priced bid, and every surface says when a bid has none.

## What changed

- **The migration** (see `docs/migrations/20261010010000_bid_priced_margin.md`):
  - eight nullable `bids.priced_*` columns, metadata-only;
  - `stamp_bid_priced_margin`, SECURITY INVOKER. It writes only an unsent bid, and otherwise returns why: `sent`, `no_revenue` or `refused`. The `priced_*` columns are not watched by the bid-history trigger.
- **The kernel** (`src/lib/bids/pricedMargin.ts`):
  - `pricedMarginStampInput` reads saved prices only. A pending solve or a price mid-typing is not a price.
  - `mayStampPricedMargin` is the gate: unsent, on the ★ price, of the bid's own GC, and not an add-on alternate. `pricedMarginStampOnScreen` puts the gate and the numbers together for the tab.
  - `pricedMarginStampDiffers` stops a write that would change nothing.
  - `parseBidPricedMargin` reads the stamp back.
  - The words: `31%`, `$33,400 cost on $48,700`, `no labor rate`, `$1,200 on rows with no cost`, and `16 pts under the price`.
- **The IO** (`pricedMarginIo.ts`): `stampBidPricedMargin` and `loadBidPricedMargins`. Both go through the untyped client until the types regenerate, and both are fail-soft.
- **Pricing** (`usePricedMarginStamp`, `PricedMarginLine`, `BidsPricingTab`):
  - The hook lives at the tab's top level, so a reload that unmounts the strip cannot drop a write.
  - It is armed in `freezeSharedPricingAfterWrite`, where every price write ends, the brush included. It is also armed in `attachAndActivateNewBidPricing`, `handlePricingVersionChange` and `makeScenarioCustomerFacing`.
  - An arm carries its bid id, so opening another bid never writes.
  - After a 600 ms settle the hook stamps the numbers on screen.
  - The line under the strip reads *Kept on the bid as its priced margin: 31% · …*, or *Went out at 31% · … · kept at send, so repricing does not move it*.
- **The job's Costs tab** (`useBidPricedMargin`, `buildCostsVerdict`'s `priced`, `JobCostsVerdict`): under the margin at completion, *The bid was priced at 31% direct · this job runs 16 pts under the price*. It adds *it reads high: no labor rate, …* when the stamp is partial. The priced margin is a direct margin, so it is held against the direct margin at completion.
- **Bid vs actual** (`useBidVsActual`, `bidVsActual.ts`, `BidsBidCostsTab`): a **Priced** column beside the read, or *not priced on the Workbench*. The second tile counts *N priced on the Workbench*.
