# v2.8270 — Lien desk: four small mends to the last day of work, the calendar and the tabs (2026-11-21)

The four things v2.8222 and v2.8258 saw in passing and left (punch list #399, the first screen). The owner asked for all of them. No migration, no edge function, no change to a date the desk works out.

## 1. The way back from a hand-set day names where it goes

The open field of a day set by hand offers a press that takes the day off. It read *Back to clock hours* on a job with hours and *Back to the job's creation day* on every other job. Since v2.7224 a job with no hours is dated from its latest sent, unpaid bill before its creation day, so that second wording could be wrong, and so could the message after it.

`lienLastWorkBackTo(job)` in `src/lib/jobs/lienLastWorkDoor.ts` reads the job as it would stand with the hand day off:

| The job | The press | The message after it |
|---|---|---|
| has clock hours | *Back to clock hours* | *Back to the clock hours.* |
| no hours, a sent and unpaid bill | *Back to the bill's day* | *Back to the bill's day.* |
| no hours, no such bill | *Back to the job's creation day* | *Back to the job's creation day.* |
| no hours, and the caller holds no bills | *Back to the day the app works out* | *Back to the day the app works out.* |

The last row is Edit Job's lien row, which reads the job without its bills. It cannot tell the bill's day from the creation day, so it names neither. The desk and the Lien window hold the bills and name the real one. The write did not change: the four hand-set columns are cleared.

The window's own line about undoing named *Back to clock hours* for every job. On a job with no hours it now reads *To undo it, press the day. Its box has the press that takes this day off.*

## 2. The line under the open date box takes the row

The value piece keeps the line under a field as wide as the field. A date box with Keep and Cancel is narrow, so the last day's line ran to three short lines beside empty room. The line's wrapper is now `.lienLastWorkDay`, and `src/index.css` gives it the row while its field is open (`:has(> .vipField)`). At desk width the line is one line. On a phone it has the whole values column, as before. No other screen's field changed.

## 3. A caption and today's label stay inside a narrow calendar

On the timeline's calendar a caption began where its bar began and was capped at the column's width from there, so on a narrow strip it ran past the right edge. The label *today · Sep 24* did the same on the axis. Each now sits in a row that spans the column, after a lead as wide as the bar's start. The lead gives way first, so the words slide left only as far as they must. A caption is cut with an ellipsis only when it is wider than the whole column. Where the words fit they sit where they sat. A bar that starts past 55 % of the column still ends its caption at the column's end, and today's label past 75 % still sits left of its line.

## 4. The desk's tabs keep their flex across the phone's width

A computer's tab set `flexShrink: 0`, and a phone's tab (`LIEN_PHONE_TAB`) set the shorthand `flex`. React keeps one button for both, so a window dragged across the phone's width swapped a shorthand for a longhand on the same element. React warned, and taking the shorthand off also took `flexShrink` off: the tab could then shrink. Both now set the three longhands (`TAB_FLEX` in `LienDeskViewTabs.tsx`). Nothing changes on a screen that stays one width.

## Left where it is

React logs the same kind of warning for `font` with `fontWeight` on a button in `LienDesk.tsx`'s title bar, where a phone's More press and a computer's press take turns in one slot. Another session is working in that file's title bar today, so this change stays out of it.
