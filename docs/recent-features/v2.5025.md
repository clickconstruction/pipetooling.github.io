- **The finding behind the migration:** O3's `gc_answer_change_order` refused every call with no signed-in user, so a press from the portal, which runs as the service role, could not land; and a signed-in user could say `how = 'portal'`. It is restated with O7a's acceptance gate (`portal` only as the service role, `office` signed in), and a decline may keep the customer's reason, one line, in `gc_change_orders.declined_note` (the lead's call, 2026-10-09: optional, shown to the office).
- **The Pipeline's two functions** (the lead's call 5; nobody else on the crew touches the customer portal):
  - `customer-portal` returns `gcJobs` for a customer's link (`_shared/gcPortal.ts`): each GC project of theirs we won, its change orders waiting on them, `canAccept` once every line is billed, and the acceptance with how;
  - `submit-portal-request` takes `gc_change_order_answer` and `gc_accept_work`, checks the project is the link's customer's, and calls the database's own function as the service role with `how = 'portal'`; a refusal comes back in the customer's words. No inbox row and no email (the lead's call).
- **The portal** (`PortalGcJobs.tsx`, screen only): each GC job with **Sign it** (asked once more) and **Decline** (an optional one-line reason) on each change order, and **Accept the work** (their name and a note) when every line is billed; a sample token only says thank you.
- **A sample owner** (`sample-owner`, `SAMPLE_OWNER`): What customers see shows the portal with a sample GC job, the two new journey steps (`gc-portal-change-order`, `gc-portal-accept-work`) render on it, and the GC customer emails' *Sample Owner LLC* has a portal of its own.
- **The office side:** Change orders' chips say *in their portal*, with *Their reason:* beside a decline (`answeredInPortal`, `declinedNote`); Closeout's acceptance says *Accepted Oct 7 in their portal.* (`acceptedInPortal`), and its waiting words read *They walk the job and accept it, here or in their portal.*
