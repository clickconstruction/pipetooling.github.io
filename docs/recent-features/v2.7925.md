# v2.7925 — Schedule emails: the two reads ask who is calling, and hold a primary to his own jobs (2026-11-19)

One migration, `20261119070000_schedule_reads_ask_who_calls.sql` (its page is `docs/migrations/20261119070000_schedule_reads_ask_who_calls.md`), and its bed. No client change and no edge function change.

## What changed

- **`list_schedule_blocks_for_share`** answers the service role for anyone and a signed-in caller for himself.
- **`list_job_schedule_blocks_for_schedule_email`** answers the service role for anyone, the office (`is_office_staff()`) for anyone and a signed-in caller for himself.
- **`schedule_read_gate`** is the new function both ask first. It raises `42501` for any other caller. No signed-in role holds it.
- **A primary named as the viewer or the recipient** reads the blocks assigned to him and the blocks of the jobs he is the account manager of. He read every job's blocks before. This closes family 7 of punch list #400.
- **The office leg asks about the named person.** The preview of a person's day email now lists what that person will be sent. An office person's own email lists every job's blocks, as the table gives them.
- **The grants are restated by name**: `authenticated` and the service role on the two reads, never the public key.

## Why this rule

It is read off the callers. `schedule-share-dispatch` and `schedule-day-email-dispatch` read with the service key. `ScheduleShareModal` previews the sender's own board and passes the signed-in user. `ScheduleDayEmailModal` previews the day of the person picked under *Send to*, and only the office picks someone else, the roles `schedule_day_email_requests_insert_office_any_recipient` lets send that email to another user. So the shared schedule has no office leg.

## Choices worth knowing

- **The functions stay `LANGUAGE sql` with their live bodies.** The gate is one statement in front of the read, so a refusal comes before any row.
- **A primary's clause is the table's rule written inline.** `job_schedule_blocks` holds him to those blocks through `primary_scope_jobs_ledger`, which asks `auth.uid()`. The reads ask about a named person, so the clause names `account_manager_user_id`. It wraps every job leg: a primary also passed the project leg and the crew leg, and the office leg when the office previewed his email.
- **Bid blocks are untouched.** A primary still reads every bid visit.
- **A read answers the same whoever asks it.** Both bodies called `assistants_share_master(<the named person>, the job's master)`, which has ignored its arguments since `20260907160000` and answers `is_office_staff()` for the caller. The office previewing a helper's day email read every job's blocks, and the email held the helper's own. The other way round, a master's email read with the service key left out another master's job that his own preview listed. The leg now asks the four office roles of the named person. `assistants_share_master` itself is untouched.
- **No name, argument or returned column changed.** The migration is safe on either side of a client deploy.
- **A caller with no login is the service role or is refused.** A session with no token at all is refused too.
