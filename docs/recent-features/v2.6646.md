## Read closer than the card

- **The job's lines are not the bid's lines.** The card's *the workbench writes ◆ on the lines it priced* assumed the bid's rows reach the job at award. They do not: a bid import carries one line, *Bid price*, and the bid's rows are per fixture while a job's are per stage. So the ◆ lands on the carried line as the whole bid's labor and materials shares, and a Stage Plan split later divides that line's price, with the company split, until the owner tunes it (PR 1b).
- **The dated sibling is columns, not rows.** `job_budgets` is one row per job with `job_id` as its key, and every reader joins on it. The award snapshot is eight columns on that row, written once; Refresh from bid moves the live columns beside them. The score reads `awarded_*`.
- **The book's reading is the Labor tab's.** `baselineReading` over `job_baseline_rates()`, the median hours per $1k of jobs within a quarter to four times the price when three or more exist, else all of them; the rates are not yet filtered to projects, which the labor book's own PR takes up.
- **The Pricing tab draws no quiet line.** Its workbench rows already read labor, materials, cost and margin per line for the estimator, and the cover letter reads `coverLetterPricingRows`, which carry none of it; a second line would say the same thing twice. The card's lens check stands as the builder-level rule it is today.
- **A typed baseline's name** is "by you" when the signed-in user typed it; a name lookup for another typist waits on the users read the Costs tab does not make.
