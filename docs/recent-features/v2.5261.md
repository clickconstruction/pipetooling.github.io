# v2.5261 — Messages: calls and texts from the company number, read into the thread, behind the scenes (2026-10-12)

The Messages build's cut 5 kernels (`to-dos/gc-mode/messages/BUILD_PLAN.md` → Cut 5, the design taken 2026-10-05 in `to-dos/gc-mode/HANDOFF.md` section 5, on branch `spike/gc-mode`), by helper 3, directed by the GC Review lead. Two pure kernels with their tests over made-up deliveries in Quo's API version 2026-03-30 (never a real event). No screen, no table, no edge function, nothing to deploy. Stacked on v2.5250, whose `textToSend` it sends.

- **`src/lib/quo/events.ts`**: `readQuoEvent` reads a delivery (`message.received`, `call.completed`, `call.missed`, `call.voicemail.completed`, `call.recording.completed`, `call.transcript.completed`, `call.summary.completed`). Any other type, or a legacy `data.object` payload, reads as nothing to do with its reason. `planQuoEvent` says what the thread gets: the line keyed by its Quo id, the number's opt-out, the app's own answer and a team event. `unknownContact` makes the Who is this? contact for a number nobody knows.
- **A call is one line**: its events arrive in any order (Quo promises none), and `mergeCall` folds each into the line so every order ends on the same one. The channel goes by rank (voicemail over call over missed), the time is the earliest, and a voicemail keeps its own length. The body is Quo's summary on a call and the voicemail's words on a voicemail. Nobody of ours is named on a missed call or a voicemail. The test folds all 24 orders of an answered call's four events and all 6 of a voicemail's three.
- **STOP, START and HELP** (`keywordOf`): Quo stops a number by itself on STOP, STOPALL, UNSUBSCRIBE, CANCEL, END or QUIT sent alone, and answers it; START and UNSTOP bring it back. The app mirrors both on the number and answers only what Quo leaves: HELP and INFO (`helpAnswer`), and the FCC's REVOKE and OPT OUT or a stop word with punctuation (`stopAnswer`, once). A longer text that may ask to stop (`mayAskToStop`: "stop please", "do not text me") gets a team note to use Mark STOP; the app never stops a number on a guess.
- **`src/lib/quo/send.ts`**: `planSend` builds Quo's `POST /v1/messages` body (from the company number's id, to the number in E.164, as the sender's Quo user when they have a seat) and the sent line with its `optout_line` (first, silence or held). It refuses an empty text, a missing or unreadable number, a number that sent STOP (all but the one confirmation of a stop) and a text past Quo's 1,600 characters. The app's HELP answer and its confirmation carry their own STOP or START words, so they take no opt-out line. `sentQuoId` reads the message id from Quo's answer.
- **For later PRs**: the cut 5 migration adds `quo_id` (unique), `recording_url`, `transcript`, `summary` and `duration_seconds` to helper 1's `messages`. The `quo-webhook` edge function checks Quo's signature (`webhook-id`, `webhook-timestamp`, `webhook-signature`), drops a `webhook-id` it has seen (Quo retries for about 27 hours), finds the contact with `contact_for_channel` and calls these kernels. Quo's API sends no picture messages, so a photo on a text goes as a link line.
