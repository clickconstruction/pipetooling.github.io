# v2.7370 — Scores: a sent bid is judged by the labor it went out with (2026-11-15)

Punch list #360, the team scorecard's one residual, named in v2.7347 and again in v2.7369. The priced margin freezes when a bid is sent. The estimating score will not judge a bid whose labor was not estimated, because its kept cost then has little or no labor in it. But *estimated* was read from the Labor tab as it stands today. So a bid that went out with one labor row in ten filled, and had the rest filled in a week later, read as judged: its kept cost with no labor, divided by hours that were not there when it was priced.

## What ships

- **The stamp keeps the count** (migration `20261116090000_bid_priced_labor_rows.sql`, [its fragment](../migrations/20261116090000_bid_priced_labor_rows.md)): `stamp_bid_priced_margin` counts the bid's labor rows that are not a sub's, and those with hours, the way `bid_estimate_breakdown` does, and writes them beside the margin (`bids.priced_labor_rows_total`, `priced_labor_rows_with_hours`). Its arguments do not change, so no client is ahead of it or behind it.
- **A sent bid is judged by it** (`estimateHoldWords`, `bidScoreReadiness`): when a bid is sent and its stamp carries the count, that count decides, and the words say so in the past tense: *Labor not estimated when it went out: 1 of 10 rows had hours.* A bid still on the desk is judged by the Labor tab as it stands, since its stamp follows the next price. A stamp older than the count is judged as before.
- **Everywhere the hold is read**: the Scores tab and `team-scores` (the reads take the count in their own read), the Bid Board's chip, the Workbench's line on a sent bid, and the Cover Letter's Mark sent. Each reads the two columns apart from its other reads and fails soft to none, so a database that does not have them yet changes nothing.

## What it does not do

- It does not reach back. The sent bids stamped before today have no count, and are judged by today's Labor tab as they were.
- It does not re-stamp a bid when only its labor changes. The stamp follows a price write, as before; the count describes the labor that stamp's cost was made with.
