# v2.4194 — Pricing: the base and what the alternate adds, on the Workbench (2026-09-29)

The alternates train, PR 4 of 5 (after [v2.4191](v2.4191.md)). No migration, no edge function. Prices stay per row; the solver, the brush and the typed prices work on every row as they do. What changes is what the scoreboard says and how two rows that share a name pair up across versions.

## What changed

- **A second line under the scoreboard** ([`BidsPricingTab`](../../src/components/bids/BidsPricingTab.tsx), `data-testid="workbench-alternates"`): when the bid carries an alternate group that holds a row — *Base $13,580 · + Break room $3,220 · With the alternate $16,800 · Alternate margin 42%*. Revenue and cost per effective row (the live totals: drafts, the pending solve minus clicked-off rows) summed by the Count Sheet's scope through the same `sumByAlternate` Takeoffs and Labor use; the hover title carries the cents, our cost and the alternate's margin. With two or more alternates each gets its own stat and the last reads *With every alternate*.
- **The ALT mark** on every alternate row's fixture cell.
- **Pairing within the scope** ([`mapCountRowsByFixture`](../../src/lib/bids/mapCountRowsByFixture.ts) takes `scopeOf`): the same fixture in the base and in an alternate are two rows on purpose (v2.4188), and the cross-version pairing used to drop a name carried by more than one row as ambiguous. Both callers — copying a scenario between GC packets and the cross-version *Copy prices* — now pair WC-in-the-base with WC-in-the-base and WC-in-Break-room with its counterpart (`alternateScopeKey`); an unscoped call keeps the old rule. The book fill (`matchCountRowsToBookEntries`) needed nothing: it matches each row to an entry by name, so both WC rows take the same entry.

## Decided here

- **The scoreboard's first line is the whole bid**, as before; the second line is the split. Revenue, Profit, Margin and Multiple keep meaning what they meant.
- **The Alternate margin stat shows for one alternate**; with several, each alternate's margin rides its stat's hover — the strip already wraps on a laptop.
