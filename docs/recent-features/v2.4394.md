# v2.4394 — Materials: prices that look wrong show up so you can fix them (2026-10-02)

PR 3 of 4 to ship in the material prices plan (canvas *Material prices over time*). It is the plan's step 4, built before the bid line because it stays on Materials. v2.4391 already set big jumps aside from the number. This PR lists the prices still standing on one, so a person can fix them or say they are right. The plan named one: a Reece sink at $999,999 on BP338.

## What changed
- **The kernel** (`materialPriceIndex.ts`) adds `suspects`. A basket price is listed when it is a stand-in (≤ $0, or `PLACEHOLDER_PRICE` and up), or when its last history row was a big jump (`isBigJump`) that no later row confirmed. A confirmation is the same price again, which v2.4392's Same writes and *It’s right* now writes too. Each suspect carries:
  - the last real price before it (walking back past stand-ins), the day it changed, and why
  - the open bids' labels
  
  The list is sorted biggest spend first.
- **The reads** label each part's open bids with the trade's `service_types.ledger_bid_prefix` (`BP338`; `B` when blank or unreadable), in number order.
- **The card** gains an amber box: *N prices look wrong*, with three shown and *And N more.* Each says what the price reads, what it was before which day, and the open bids it sits on, with two buttons:
  - *Fix price* fetches the part and opens the page's own Prices window (`setViewingPartPrices`).
  - *It’s right* confirms a jump today through `confirmPriceToday`. A stand-in gets no confirm.
  
  A failed confirm says why. The page's Prices window fires `MATERIAL_PRICES_CHANGED_EVENT` from `onPricesUpdated`, and the card reads again.
- **Price History** (`PartPricesManager`) reads each change through [`priceHistoryChange.ts`](../../src/lib/materials/priceHistoryChange.ts):
  - a rise orange with ▲, a drop blue with ▼ (a rise read green before, a seller's view)
  - the same price again *Checked*, no old price *First price*
- The guide *track what your materials cost* gains *Prices that look wrong*. The Materials map and the glossary's Price History say where it lives.
