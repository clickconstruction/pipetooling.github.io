# v2.6976 — The dev seat writes a bid's Labor tab: plan_bid_labor and apply_bid_labor (2026-11-12)

PR 4 of the bid train (`to-dos/mcp-servers.md` → The bid train; the pickup card is #317), after counts (v2.6967) and takeoff (v2.6968). The owner, 2026-11-11: "fill out all of the line items for counts, takeoffs, labor, pricing."

## What was added

One plan → apply pair on `dev-mcp` (ninety-five write verbs). No wrapper RPC and no migration: the rows are the ones Bids → Labor writes, as the dev under the tables' RLS.

- **`plan_bid_labor` / `apply_bid_labor`** (`supabase/functions/_shared/devMcpBidLabor.ts`): `labor = { bid_id, estimate?, rows?, direct?, version_id? }`.
- **The estimate's boxes** (`cost_estimates`) are held to the ranges `buildCostEstimateAutosavePayload` holds them to. The estimate is made when the bid has none, as the tab makes it on a counted bid.
- **Labor rows** are keyed by the fixture's name. An existing row is changed; a counted fixture with no row yet is minted as the tab's sync mints it (its name, the summed count, after the last row); a fixture nobody counts is held. The counts are the job's once a bid is won, as `jobScopeRows` reads them.
- **Hours read as typed**: `source: typed` and the queue's own note. The database's fill on a first sent date (`mint_labor_rows_from_book`) leaves a row with a source alone, so the agent's hours survive the send.
- **Direct cost lines** (the five tables) are dollars a stage, found again by kind and note.
- **The plan** states the tab's "Direct cost of this bid" before and after through `computeBidCostBreakdown` from the kernel copy, at the bid's own rate.

## Three choices worth knowing

1. **No labor dollars without a rate.** The New view prices a bid with no rate at the company crew rate, a ninety-day figure from paid hours and a burden factor. The seat does not compute a second opinion of it: the plan states hours and leaves labor dollars out, in words.
2. **Never the labor book.** The tab's queue can add a book entry or learn an alias when a person saves hours. Those are the tab's presses; the verb writes the bid's rows alone.
3. **A bid with no creator is refused.** `can_access_bid_for_pricing` answers false for a bid whose `created_by` is null, even to a dev, so the plan says so before anything is tried.
