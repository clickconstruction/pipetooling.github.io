# v2.8431 — A customer's first Try again asks the network too (2026-11-24)

Punch list #85. v2.8379 made the second **Try again** on an offline customer's page ask the network, and left the first press as the owner's call (`docs/recent-features/v2.8379.md`, *Why the first press does not ask*). The owner's yes came on 2026-11-24, relayed by Diane: build it. Client only: no migration and no edge function.

## Before

On a page a customer opens from a link (`PublicPage`) that lost its code while the browser said it was offline, the first press on **Try again** only answered in words, *Still offline. Try again when you have a signal.* The second press, and each one after, asked the network and reloaded on an answer. A customer whose phone was wrong about being offline needed two presses, and nothing on the page said a second press would do more than the first.

## Now

`publicPageTryAgainAct(online)` in `src/lib/publicPageLoad.ts` decides what a press does, on the browser's word alone:

| The browser says | Press | What happens |
|---|---|---|
| online | any | Reloads, as before. |
| offline | any, the first too | Asks the network once (`probeForSignal`). Reloads only when it answers. |

What a customer on a phone that says it is offline now sees:

- **Before any press.** *You are offline. This page will load when you are back online.*, with **Try again**. Nothing is asked until someone presses.
- **A press, and the phone was wrong.** *Checking for a signal…* with the press dead, then the loading look and the reload. One press where there were two.
- **A press, and there is no network.** *Still offline. Try again when you have a signal.*, with the press live again. The page stays.

The count of presses decides nothing any more, so the rule lost its second argument. The act `'answer'` is never returned, so it is gone from `PublicPageTryAgainAct`, and its branch is gone from `PublicPageLost`. `pressedOffline` stays in the component and in `publicPageLostView`, where it still chooses the words after a no: the press sets it before it asks, so a failed ask reads *Still offline…* and not the words the page opened with. A press whose ask failed has visibly answered.

## What makes it safe has not changed

The ask is v2.8379's, untouched: `probeForSignal`, its address, what counts as an answer and its timeout are the same lines. No cache can answer it. Only the site's own answer counts. It never throws. It is a plain no after five seconds (`PUBLIC_PAGE_PROBE_TIMEOUT_MS`). A first press on a phone with no network therefore reloads nothing, exactly as a second press did, and the page never reloads on the browser's word or on a count. The test that fails a PR which tells the service worker to ignore address keys stays.

Nothing else changes. The page still reloads by itself when the browser's `online` event comes. The automatic recovery reload still never starts while the browser says it is offline.

## The cost: the first press no longer answers at once

Before, the first press drew *Still offline…* in the same turn as the press. Now it waits for the ask. How long *Checking for a signal…* shows on a first press was measured on 2026-11-24, in headless Chromium on the harness below, with a watcher inside the page that timed each change of the words:

| The network | First presses timed | *Checking for a signal…* showed for | *Still offline…* was on the page |
|---|---|---|---|
| gone, and the browser knows it | 16 | 0.4 to 5.1 ms | 2.1 to 7.7 ms after the press |
| gone, and nothing says so (the ask is never answered) | 4 | 5.00 s (5,001 to 5,004 ms) | 5.00 s after the press |

- **Gone, and the browser knows it.** The browser refuses the request at once. In 11 of the 16 presses no frame was drawn with *Checking for a signal…* at all, and in the other 5 one frame was. The customer sees the words change from *You are offline…* to *Still offline…* as fast as before.
- **Gone, and nothing says so.** A signal too weak to answer, or a network that swallows the request. The page says *Checking for a signal…* with the press dead for the whole five seconds, then *Still offline…* with the press live. This is the longest a first press can take to answer. The timeout was not shortened: a signal that cannot bring 2 kB in five seconds cannot bring the page either.
- **The phone was wrong and the network is there.** The reload left 8 to 69 ms after the press, on 16 presses, against the dev server on the same machine. A real network adds its own round trip.

## What is left

- Between the answer and the reload the signal can still drop, as v2.8379 says. That window is unchanged.
- With the network gone and the browser knowing it, the check is over in about a millisecond. A second press then goes from *Still offline…* to *Still offline…* with nothing a person can see in between. That has been so for the second press and each one after since v2.8379, and it is unchanged. The first press still changes the words.

## Seen

On 2026-11-24, on a throwaway harness page on the dev server (never committed, removed before the commit), with a headless Chromium at 390 by 844 and 1,280 by 800, in light and in dark, on the portal's paper and on the landing's photo and white box: eight combinations, each run twice. The harness drew `PublicPage` around a page whose import is refused once the browser says it is offline.

- **The first press, the network gone.** The browser was put offline for real, so its own word was offline and every request failed. The page read *You are offline…* with the press copper. After one press it read *Still offline…* with the press copper and live, 44 px tall. One ask left per press, five presses sent five asks, and the page never navigated.
- **The first press, the network there.** The browser was told to say offline while the network worked. The ask got 200 `image/svg+xml` from the dev server, and the page left for its reload. With the answer held a second and a half the page read *Checking for a signal…* with the press disabled, grey fill and dark words, then the loading look with no press, then the reload's own request.
- **The first press, the ask never answered.** *Checking for a signal…* for five seconds, then *Still offline…*. The page stayed.
- Nothing was wider than the screen in any of them. The dark theme changes nothing on these pages, since both frames are held light.
- The dead press takes 0.12 s to fade from copper to grey, the app's own transition on every button, and its words turn dark at once. So for a moment the words are dark on copper. That is v2.8379's dead press, not changed here.

## Not seen

- No live page was opened, and no real phone was used. The wait was measured in headless Chromium only: Safari and Firefox are not installed for the headless runner on this machine. How fast a phone's own browser refuses a request with its radio off was not measured.
- The built app with its service worker in control was not proved again. v2.8379 proved there that the ask cannot be answered with no network. Nothing in the ask or in `src/sw.ts` is changed by this PR.

## Tests

- `publicPageLoad.test.ts`, 25. The rule's four tests are now three: online reloads, a browser that cannot say counts as online, and an offline press asks. One new test holds the words of a first press in order: *You are offline…*, *Checking for a signal…*, then *Still offline…* on a no, three different sentences. The tests of the ask are unchanged, the one that reads `src/sw.ts` included.
- `PublicPage.render.test.tsx`, 17, was 15. Offline, nothing is asked until a press, and a first press whose ask fails reads *Still offline…*. The phone was wrong: the first press asks once, the page says *Checking for a signal…* with the press dead, two more presses while the ask is out send no second ask, and the answer reloads. The phone was right: the first press's ask fails, nothing reloads, the press is live, and three more presses ask three more times. New: a first press whose ask hangs is given up at five seconds, and a phone that came back online before the press reloads and asks nothing. A sign-in wall's answer and an answer after the page has gone still do nothing.
