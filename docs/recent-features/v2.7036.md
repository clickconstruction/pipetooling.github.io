# v2.7036 — Pay calendar: one job on its own calendar, and the day a payer accepted a bill (2026-11-12)

Punch list #323, PR 4 of seven, stacked on v2.7031. Two things keep a due day from being wrong: a job whose contract is not the payer's usual one, and a bill the payer accepted in a later month than it went in. Both were named on the card's second pass as the reason this PR moved up from sixth.

## What changed

- **A job's own calendar.** `PayCalendarModal` takes the job its press sat on (`PayCalendarJob`, through `openPayCalendar(payer, { job })`). A radio pair, **This calendar is for**, picks *Every job <payer> pays* or *Only <job>*. On the job: the form starts from the payer's calendar, **This job has no pay calendar** writes the off switch, and a job that already carries its own row opens on the job with **Follow <payer> again** to clear it (no question asked: the job reads the payer's again and nothing is lost for good). The table and `set_payer_pay_calendar` already held job rows since PR 1; this is their first writer.
- **The development's other jobs.** For a job in a development the window reads the development's jobs (`loadDevelopmentJobIds`) and offers *Also set on the other N jobs in <development>*. The button then reads *Save for N jobs*. Each job is saved on its own and the run goes past one that fails: the toast is `batchOutcomeWords`' line with the first reason, the window stays open on a partial save, and the board reads again either way.
- **The row presses carry the job**: the calendar's words on a billed row and the *Send by* line on a Ready to Bill row (`payCalendarJobOf` in `JobsStagesTab.tsx`). The line on a customer's profile and in Pay speeds still opens the payer's alone.
- **The accepted day.** Migration `20261112190000_invoice_payer_acceptances.sql` (see `docs/migrations/`). `billedPayModel` takes `acceptedYmd` on its row (`BilledPayRowInput`) and counts the cutoff from it when it is a day (`payClockFor`); the model carries `clockYmd` and `clockFrom`, and the hover says *They accepted it Dec 2, and the calendar counts from that day.* With no calendar the accepted day changes nothing.
  - `useBilledMoneyData` loads the accepted days beside the calendars (`acceptedByInvoice`); the dates block, the phone rows, the Payment forecast and the lien runway pass each bill's.
  - Under a billed row's dates, for a payer on a calendar and an office role: *Counted from Nov 24* (or *Accepted Dec 2*), a press that opens **They accepted it on…** (`PayerAcceptedModal`). The window shows both readings before a save, amber when they differ: *In on Nov 24, it is due Dec 10. Accepted Dec 2, it is due Jan 10.* **Clear the accepted day** when one is on record.
- **Guides**: *One job on a different calendar* and *When they accepted a bill later* in *set a payer's pay calendar*.

## Not changed

- A payer or a job with no calendar. A bill with no accepted day.
- The payer's record (lateness) is still measured from the day each past bill went in: the pay-speeds read has no accepted days, and past bills have none recorded.

## Deploy

Push `20261112190000` right after the merge, as with PR 1's. Either side of the push the board stands: the read answers none, and a Save says the function is missing.

## Is this the best we can do?

- **Neither save has run against a real database.** Both tables are not on prod. The two windows were walked on the local app against the live data with every write refused and the two reads answered with made-up rows: the scope switch, the job's form, the accepted-day press on 43 billed rows and its preview all drew. The saves are covered by the render tests and the SQL bed; their first real run is after the pushes.
- **The accepted day is asked for, not offered.** Nothing tells the office a bill was sent back. A bill sent in the last days before a cutoff is the likely case, and the row could say *Sent 2 days before their cutoff. Did they accept it in time?* That is a small follow-up once real accepted days show whether it happens.
- **A set across a development is a copy, not a link.** Twelve job rows are written; a thirteenth job added to the development later gets none and follows the payer. The card's call 6 (a level for developments) is the real fix if that bites.
- **Lateness and the accepted day read different clocks.** A payer whose past bills were all accepted late looks later than it is, because the record counts from the day each went in.
