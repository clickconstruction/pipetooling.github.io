# v2.5622 — Intro to electrical: the fourteen-year-old pass (2026-10-18)

Punch list #120, after the five build PRs (v2.5614–v2.5618) and the four more drawings (v2.5620). The owner asked whether a fourteen-year-old could understand the plumbing course; the same read over `src/content/training/intro-electrical/` found the sentences fine and the trade words not, so this PR reworks the words and the lesson load without adding a lesson. Thirty lessons stay thirty; every figure stays. No migration; no edge function.

- **The measurement, before and after** (a python over the lesson bodies with the frontmatter, the figure lines and the markdown stripped; the Flesch-Kincaid grade): before, 2,059 sentences at 7.8 words each, grade 2.6 over the course, lesson grades 0.4 (Switches and lights) to 4.7 (The water heater's circuit), the longest sentence 20 words. After, 2,002 sentences at 8.0 words, grade 2.7, lessons 0.6 to 4.7, longest 20. The sentences were never the problem.
- **The term scan**: for every trade word, the lesson and the sentence where it first appears. Before: twelve words first met in a figure caption (hot, neutral, ground and fault in lesson 1's caption above the lines that name them; bus bar and lug in lesson 3's; short and overload in lesson 10's; line side in lesson 12's; thermostat and disconnect in lesson 15's) and eight first met in a Check yourself option or ask (switch leg and voltage in lesson 1, branch circuit and circuit in lesson 2, cable in lesson 3, ohms in lesson 4, GFCI in lesson 5, the bonding jumper and the rod in lesson 6, ground rod in lesson 7's ask, low voltage in lesson 10). Never explained anywhere: fault, circuit, load, the lugs, a bus bar, two-pole, AWG, the whip, element, thermostat, disconnect, terminal, transformer, float switch, conduit, hasp, yoke, kick plate, lineman, array, milliamp, mA, the code and its cycle, upstream and downstream, tagged out, T&P. After: no word is first met in an option, an ask or a caption, and each of those gets its plain words in the sentence where it first appears (*a circuit*, one loop; *a fault*, current going somewhere it should not; *the lugs*, the big screw clamps above the main breaker; *a two-pole breaker*, two breakers joined under one handle; *the whip*, the short bendable cable into the heater; *an element*, a metal rod that gets hot inside the tank; *a thermostat*, a switch that reads the water's heat; *a disconnect*, a switch box or a pull-out near the heater that cuts its power; *a transformer*, a small box that turns 120 V into 24 V; *a hasp*, a clip with holes for several locks; and so on). Where the word could wait, the sentence was reworded instead: lesson 3's breaker number is *its limit* and the main is *100 to 200*, not amps, until lesson 4 teaches amps; *upstream* and *downstream* are *between the panel and the disconnect* and *past the GFCI*; *tagged out* is *leaves the job* until lesson 27 teaches tagout; *T&P* is *the relief valve*.
- **Four captions moved under the lines that name their words** (lessons 1, 3, 10, 12, 15), so the drawing follows the sentence and never leads it; the drawings themselves are unchanged.
- **One subject and three Numbers a lesson** (the plumbing course sits at three or four; the parser's floor is three). Every Numbers section is cut to the three that are the lesson's own fact; what was cut is still in the Must know or in the lesson that owns it. Lesson 3 drops the 36 in clearance (lesson 29's) and asks about the bare bus bars under the cover instead; lesson 5 drops the 240 V plug face (lesson 11's) and moves *the second wire is not a neutral, test both* into Traps; lesson 12 drops the LED bulb from Must know into a Traps line and asks about the ceiling fan that can start on someone else's switch; lesson 15 is re-cut around the company's line at the disconnect, with the high-limit named in the rule's own sentence and the elements, the thermostats and the disconnect each defined before the drawing; lesson 16 folds the air switch into its first line. The 36 in, the 60 W bulb, the 4,500 W element, the two kitchen circuits, the pressure switch's 30 and 50 psi and the shock scale's seven steps all stay in the Must know lines or the figures that hold them.
- **The course's rule holds**: every lesson still ends at what it does, how you know it is live, what you do not touch and who you call; the content test's no-instruction rule (v2.5614) is green on every rewritten line.
