# v2.5949 — The AR call card reads an evening touch on the right day (2026-10-27)

The Dashboard AR Customers view's call-sheet kernel, `src/lib/arCustomerChase.ts`, took a Payment Chase touch's day from the first ten characters of its `created_at`, which the chase log (`list_payment_chase_touches`) returns as a UTC instant. From 7 PM to midnight Central the UTC day is already tomorrow. So on the call card a touch made in the evening read tomorrow's date with no age (`arLastTouchLine` drops the age when the count comes out negative), and the chase pill (`arCustomerChasePill`) named tomorrow on *Touched*, counted a could-not-reach snooze one day long (*back in 8d* for a seven-day snooze) and kept the three quiet days one day past their end. The Dashboard already counts the AR card's today on the company calendar (`todayYmdInAppTz`), so only the touch side was off.

- **`touchYmd` counts on the company calendar**: `calendarYmdInAppTzFromIso(createdAt)`, as the Pipeline's call mode (`paymentChase.ts`) and Quickfill's lean read (v2.5947) already do.
- **Touches sort newest first as instants** (`Date.parse`), not as strings, so two spellings of one moment cannot reorder them.

Tests: `arCustomerChase.test.ts` gains four evening cases (the line reads *Oct 26 (today)*, *Touched Oct 26*, *back in 7d*, the quiet days ending on the third day, and the newest touch picked by instant). All four fail against the old kernel; the nine existing cases, all morning UTC times, pass either way, and the file passes under `TZ=UTC`. `DashboardArCustomersView.render.test.tsx` reads no clock and stays as it was.

Not here: the Bridge's Collect desk (`src/lib/bridge/collect/`) slices the same string in four files; the Bridge session was told on 2026-10-27.

## Verify

After 7 PM Central, Dashboard → AR → Customers: open a customer, press *Couldn't reach* on the call card. The card's last touch reads *couldn't reach · <today> (today)*, and the row's pill reads *Can't reach — back in 7d*.
