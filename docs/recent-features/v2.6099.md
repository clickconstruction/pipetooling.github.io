# v2.6099 — Offline in the field, PR 2: the store, the bar and the clock (punch list #163) (2026-10-29)

Punch list #163 ([`to-dos/offline-field-queue/README.md`](../../to-dos/offline-field-queue/README.md)), the second of five PRs, on PR 1's kernel (v2.6074). With no signal a clock press used to show Retry and keep nothing. Now the phone keeps it with the moment it was pressed, sends it in order when the signal returns, and the strip at the top of every screen says so the whole way: the owner's bar of 2026-10-24, and the press's life on it of 2026-10-26 (`to-dos/honest-and-fast/README.md`, part 3).

## What changed

- **The store on the phone** [`src/lib/offline/outboxStore.ts`](../../src/lib/offline/outboxStore.ts) (IndexedDB, `pipetooling-outbox`, the shape of `keptReadStore.ts`, best-effort) under [`outboxState.ts`](../../src/lib/offline/outboxState.ts), the one in-memory store the bar, the list, the clock and the replay read and write (+6 tests): every change written through, `load` reading the device's presses for the signed-in person pruned by the kernel, `clear` on sign-out and an account switch (`useOutboxClearedOnAccountChange`, mounted in `useAuth` beside the kept reads' clear).
- **The send** [`landFieldPress.ts`](../../src/lib/offline/landFieldPress.ts): one press to the `land_field_press` RPC with its id, its kind, its pressed time and its payload; the outcome classed by `errorKindOf`, never by text: landed, refused (the server's reason), or failed (the fetch layer, or a timeout whose request may still land, which the id makes safe to resend). **A press through the outbox** [`pressThroughOutbox.ts`](../../src/lib/offline/pressThroughOutbox.ts) (+7 tests): the surface hands over what was pressed; offline it is kept without a try, online it is sent at once and kept only when the send fails or times out; a refusal comes back to the surface as it does today; a press made while impersonating is never kept. `forceQueue` keeps a press behind one already waiting (a clock-out on a clock-in still on the phone).
- **The replay** [`src/hooks/useOutboxReplay.ts`](../../src/hooks/useOutboxReplay.ts) (+5 tests), mounted once in `Layout`: sends the queue in order, one press at a time, on every change of the queue, the browser's `online` event, the app-health kernel turning fine, the page coming to the front, and the oldest press's due time by the retry rule; a failed try stops the run (one lost signal costs one request), a refusal keeps its press and the run goes on, a landing fires `pipetooling:outbox-landed` on `window`. **Try now** on the bar makes every waiting press due at once.
- **The bar** is the second segment of [`AppHealthStrip`](../../src/components/AppHealthStrip.tsx) (+6 render cases), so a phone never carries two bars and no new fixed layer exists (`check:status-bar`, `check:window-z` see nothing). While the app is fine the strip is the bar alone: amber *Clock in from 7:58 AM · waiting to send · last tried 10:14 · trying again in 12 s* with **Try now** (several: *2 waiting to send*); amber pulsing *Sending clock in from 7:58 AM…*; green *Clock in from 7:58 AM sent ✓* with **Got it** (several: *3 sent ✓ · see them*); red *Clock in from 7:58 AM could not be sent.* with the reason and **Fix**, which opens the press's door and takes the red off, or *… has waited since yesterday.* with the list as its door. While the app is slow or unreachable the health words lead and the queue follows: *The app is slow right now. Nothing you pressed is lost. 2 waiting to send ›*, or *1 press needs you ›*; with no signal, *No signal. 2 waiting to send ›* and no countdown, since the resend fires on the online event. A tap on the words opens the list, [`OutboxDoor`](../../src/components/OutboxDoor.tsx) (+ render smoke): *Needs you* (the reason, **Fix**, **Send now**, **Drop it**), *Waiting* (when pressed, the last try, **Send now**, **Drop it**), *Sent today* (pressed and sent times, newest first, **Got it**), so a tech who missed the green line can still see that 7:58 went out at 8:11.
- **Clock in and out through the queue** in [`ClockInOutButton`](../../src/components/ClockInOutButton.tsx): the Dashboard's Clock In, the clock-out review, and Job Mode's direct apply (a new session, and the hourly switch, which is two presses, the clock-out then the clock-in behind it). A clock-in still on the phone is the open session until it lands (`pendingSession.ts`: its id under `press:`, the pressed moment as the start, counted in today's time), so the timer runs and Clock out is offered; a clock-out on it goes after it, never ahead, and is refused with it if the office refuses the clock-in. A queued press reads *Kept on the phone. It goes out when the signal is back.* The line beside the clock flips with the strip ([`ClockPressLine`](../../src/components/clock/ClockPressLine.tsx) over `clockPressLine.ts`, +5 tests): amber *Clock in pressed at 7:58 AM · waiting to send*, green *Clock in 7:58 AM · sent 8:11 AM ✓* for the rest of the day, red *could not be sent · Fix*. The Job Mode card ([`DashboardJobModeCard`](../../src/components/jobMode/DashboardJobModeCard.tsx)) is clocked in on the pending press too, its leg from the press's stamp (*On site since 7:58 · kept on the phone*), and reads the day again when a queued clock press lands, is refused or is dropped; I'm here waits for the clock-in to land. A try that failed changes nothing on either surface, so a lost signal never has them re-reading every 30 s.
- **The server side**, migration [`20261029230000_field_presses.sql`](../migrations/20261029230000_field_presses.md): the `field_presses` table keyed by the phone-minted id, and the `land_field_press` RPC (`SECURITY INVOKER`, the caller's own policies) that lands `clock_in` and `clock_out` and answers the first landing's result again on a repeat. **Rule B**: a late clock-in is inserted at the pressed time, so the typed-hours trigger (v2.4242) files the stretch older than its ten-minute slack as a typed entry by the person, which waits for approval like any typed hours, with the sent-late mark in the session's notes; a late clock-out closes at the pressed time. One row, not two.
- **Docs**: `GLOSSARY.md` (*The waiting bar*), `PROJECT_DOCUMENTATION.md` (Layout), `ACCESS_CONTROL.md` (the table and the RPC), the card's front matter.

## Decisions

- **One session row for a late clock-in, not the mock-up's two.** The decision says the live session runs from the moment the press lands and the stretch before it is a typed entry; the existing trigger gives exactly that from one row at the pressed time, and one row is the only shape under which a clock-out pressed before its clock-in landed can still close it at its own time.
- **Try first, then keep.** A press made online lands as it does today and the bar stays gone; the queue takes over only when the send fails at the fetch layer or times out, or when the browser already says offline. Every ordinary clock-in would otherwise end on a green line asking for Got it.
- **The bar and the list read the kernel; the surfaces read the store.** The clock and the Job Mode card re-read only when a press's state crosses to sent, refused or gone, so the countdown and a failed try never make a read.
