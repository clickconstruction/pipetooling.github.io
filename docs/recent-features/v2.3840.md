# v2.3840 — Calendar: the month arrows stay on the month you picked (2026-09-25)

Hazard #1 (and #7) of [`CALENDAR_PAGE_ARCHITECTURE.md`](../CALENDAR_PAGE_ARCHITECTURE.md), found by the 2026-09-25 map sweep: step 0 of that map's extraction order. Client only, no migration.

## The bug

The "My Day scrubbed past the grid" effect in [`Calendar.tsx`](../../src/pages/Calendar.tsx) had deps `[myDayKey, currentMonth]`, so it also ran after `prevMonth` / `nextMonth`. My Day sits on today, and today is not a cell of next month's or last month's grid (with today = 2026-09-25: October's grid is 09-27…10-31, August's 07-26…09-05), so the effect reset `currentMonth` to today's month on every arrow press — the arrows snapped straight back.

## The change

- The grid helpers (`formatDateKey`, `getDaysInMonth`, `getVisibleGridDateRange`) move verbatim from the page to [`lib/calendarMonthGrid.ts`](../../src/lib/calendarMonthGrid.ts), plus the bump itself as `monthAnchorForMyDay(currentMonth, myDayKey)` — the same anchor while My Day is a grid cell (padding days count), else the 1st of My Day's month.
- The effect is keyed on `myDayKey` alone and reads the month through the setter: `setCurrentMonth((month) => monthAnchorForMyDay(month, myDayKey))`. The month arrows move `currentMonth` without moving My Day, so they no longer trigger it; My Day's ← / → still pull the grid along; Today sets both.
- Hazard #7: the Upcoming list's time-off rows linked to the legacy `/settings#settings-time-off` anchor, which only lands on the Account tab since the section became a modal (v2.1544). They are now buttons that open `PersonalTimeOffModal` in place, like the grid's time-off chip. The `settingsDeepLink.ts` mapping stays for old bookmarks.

## Verified

- `calendarMonthGrid.test.ts` — grid bounds with padding weeks, the anchor kept inside the grid, the bump past either edge and across a year, and the snap-back case itself (an October or August anchor with My Day on Sep 25 resolves to September — why the page must not run it on arrow presses).
