# v2.7955 — Prospects: you can take over a card a colleague left more than 30 minutes ago (2026-11-19)

A fix, and the close of quirk 38 in `docs/PROSPECTS_TABS_ARCHITECTURE.md`. One migration, no client change, no edge function. The map had read the defect in the migrations and never tested it; the owner asked for proof first and a fix if it was real.

## What was wrong

A calling lock (v2.2850) is one row of `prospect_calling_locks` per prospect: who is on it right now. It is advisory. A live row held by somebody else hides the prospect from the other callers' Follow Up deck, and a row older than 30 minutes is stale, ignored by every reader and meant to be taken over by the next caller. `takeCallingLock` (`src/components/prospects/useCallingLock.ts`) takes over with an upsert on `prospect_id`, which updates the old holder's row.

The table's update policy passed only a caller's own row. So the database refused the takeover, `takeCallingLock` caught the error as it catches any (the lock is advisory), and the caller went on with no lock. The card was never marked theirs, so it stayed on the other callers' decks while they were on it. Until v2.7656 no release was ever sent, so every prospect anyone dialed kept a row, and on each of those only the first caller could ever hold the lock again.

## Proven before the fix

- **The refusal.** On a throwaway copy of the whole schema (every migration on main), as a second signed-in caller, the upsert over a row three weeks old came back `42501 new row violates row-level security policy (USING expression) for table "prospect_calling_locks"`. The same upsert on a prospect nobody held passed, and the second caller's delete of the stale row removed nothing.
- **Prod's policies**, read from the catalog on 2026-11-19, were the baseline's four with the three fences over them: nothing had fixed it since.
- **Prod's rows**, read through the dev seat the same day: 23 rows, all 23 older than the 30 minutes, the newest two weeks old, all held by one caller whose account is active. So nobody had met the refusal yet: one person has done the dialing. The second caller to work the queue would have met it on every one of those cards.

## What shipped

- **`supabase/migrations/20261119170000_calling_lock_stale_takeover.sql`.** The update policy becomes `Callers refresh their own calling lock or take a stale one`: `USING` passes the caller's own row, a row older than 30 minutes or a row with no time, and `WITH CHECK` holds the row written to the caller's own name. A colleague's live row is refused, now by the database too. The rows already stale are deleted. The select, insert and delete policies and the three fences are untouched.
- **A policy, not a function.** The client's upsert was already right, so with the policy changed it works as it stands: nothing to deploy in order, no new name for the client to call ahead of the push, no grants to close.
- **A delete alone would not have held.** It frees the 23 cards, and v2.7656 releases a lock when the caller moves on. A closed tab or a lost signal still leaves a row, and each such row would shut that card to every other caller again.
- **A refused upsert is still let go.** A colleague who dials in the same second, or a clock ahead of the server's, gets no lock and no chip, and the next sign of intent tries again.

## Checked

- **The bed** `supabase/tests/calling_lock_takeover` (`npm run test:pg:calling-lock-takeover`, in `scripts/sql-beds.txt`), run locally: 27 checks. The migration's second run keeps one update policy and deletes only the stale rows; a second caller takes a row three weeks old, one with no time and one 31 minutes old, and is refused one 29 minutes old; nobody writes a row in another caller's name; the old holder is refused the row once it is live again; delete is still your own row only; an archived caller, a read-only caller and a signed-out visitor take nothing.
- **`src/lib/prospects/callingLock.test.ts`** reads the newest migration that states the policy and holds its minutes to `CALLING_LOCK_TTL_MS`, and its `WITH CHECK` to the caller's own row.
- **`src/pages/Prospects.render.test.tsx`** (125 cases): the takeover case now holds the upsert to the caller's own name and the row to theirs, and a new case refuses the upsert as the database did and holds that no lock is claimed, no chip shows and the next sign of intent tries again.
- `docs/migrations/20261119170000_calling_lock_stale_takeover.md` and quirk 38 of the map, amended in place.
