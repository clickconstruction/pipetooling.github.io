# v2.8461 — The mode row shows for five seconds on each visit, then slides away (2026-11-24)

Punch list [#433](../../to-dos/mode-row-five-seconds/README.md), PR 2 of the train. Client only: no migration and no edge function.

## The ask

The owner, 2026-11-24, after a look at the mode row on a phone:

> I want it to appear at the top for only five seconds at a time when a user goes to the dashboard and then I want it to slide up and out away from the page where a user can no longer see it. I also want to see a timer of sorts, like a bar that slides across the screen to let the user know when it's going to go away.

It was drawn twice (`to-dos/mode-row-five-seconds/mockup.html`). Word for word, the page jumped under a thumb when the row left, the row could leave under a finger, and Task Mode lost its way out. The second draw laid the row over the page. He liked it, asked that the bar at the top of an iPhone that holds the camera be allowed for, and then said *build it all*.

## Before

The row showed on a person's first five opens of the page, or until a third was pressed, as a line of the page. After that the page opened with a 24 px handle in its place, and a tap or a pull down brought the row back for the visit. The phone kept `mode_row_pressed_<user>` and `mode_row_opens_<user>` to decide. A sub or a helper off Job Mode kept the row in sight.

## Now

- **Five seconds a visit.** On the Dashboard the row shows each time the page becomes the Dashboard: the app opening on it, or an arrival from another page. A press on a third gets five fresh seconds. The handle and the pull bring it back for five more.
- **The count is a kernel.** `src/lib/modeRowCount.ts` is one state (`showing`, `held`, `leaving`, `gone`) and one step function over *arrive*, *touch*, *release*, *press*, *swipe*, *time* and *hide*. `ModeRow` holds the state in a reducer, feeds it each event with the time, and sets one timer for the moment the state names. Nothing else decides when the row goes.
- **The bar** sits on the row's lower edge, the width of the screen. It starts full and its end runs to the left. It is `var(--text-muted)`. It is a CSS animation of the kernel's own length, handed over as `--mode-row-show-ms`, and it is drawn again for every whole start.
- **A hold.** A finger on the row, or a keyboard's focus inside it, holds the count. It starts whole when the last hold lifts, never where it stopped. A swipe up of 16 px on the row sends it away at once.
- **The row lies over the page.** The page's first line on the Dashboard is always the handle, an `AppTopBanner`. The row is drawn over that line and the header under it, in `.modeRowClip`, and never pushes the page. Walked at 390 px: the header's top edge and the page's are at the same place while the row shows and after it has gone.
- **The camera bar.** The handle's line pays `env(safe-area-inset-top)` and paints the band under an iPhone's clock and camera, as the first line always has. The clip names no `top`, so it starts where that line's own content starts. The row starts under the phone's bar and slides away behind it. No second expression of the inset has to agree with the first.
- **The dead strip.** `leaving` lasts the 240 ms slide and 300 ms more. The clip stays for that long and takes the presses, so a thumb aimed at a third does not land on the header under it. A press on a third of a row on its way out does nothing.
- **Less motion.** With `prefers-reduced-motion` the row does not slide. It is drawn in place and fades out in 150 ms. The bar is five blocks, and one goes out each second, from the right.
- **Task Mode** keeps the row as a line of the page, with no count, no bar and no handle (`modeRowHeldOpen`). The mock-up drew it lying over the page there too. The build does not: a row that lay over the page for good would cover the header.
- **One rule for everyone.** The guard that kept the row in sight for a sub or a helper off Job Mode is gone. Their ways back are the handle, the pull and the next visit.
- **Gone:** `modeRowFirstVisits`, `MODE_ROW_FIRST_VISITS`, `modeRowCountOpen`, `MODE_ROW_OPEN_DWELL_MS` and the phone's memory (`ModeRowMemory` and its reads and writes). The two `localStorage` keys are no longer read or written. A phone that holds them keeps them, unread.

`modeRowDraw` now answers `none`, `held` or `counted`.

## What the row still costs

For five seconds on each visit the row covers the top of the header: the menu, the search and the gear. A swipe up clears it. That is the price of a page that never jumps, and the owner saw it on the mock-up.

## Usage rows

`ui_nav_clicks`: `mode_row_sent` with `#swipe` for a row sent away by a swipe. `mode_row` and `mode_row_shown` are as before. A visit records nothing.

## Tests

`modeRowCount.test.ts` (15): every event from every state with the clock passed in, a touch at 4.9 s, two holds at once, the numbering of whole starts. `modeRow.test.ts` (16): counted on the Dashboard for the eight roles in every mode drawn there, held in Task Mode, and held for nobody else. `ModeRow.render.test.tsx` (21), with fake timers: the row at 4.9 s, leaving at 5 s, the handle alone 540 ms later, the same on a second visit with nothing kept on the phone, a finger and a focus that hold, a swipe, the handle, the pull, a phone put away, Task Mode at 30 s, and a press that restarts. A control run with the hold switched off in the kernel failed nine cases.

## Looked at

Walked headless on the dev server through the lens frame, over invented data, with real touch events.

- **A superintendent at 390 px, light:** the row over the handle's line in place on the first paint, the bar at 0.84 of its width 600 ms in, the row sliding at 5 s, then the handle alone. The header and the page did not move by a pixel.
- **The handle:** a tap brought the row down, sliding. **A finger** held it for six seconds. **A swipe up** sent it away and pressed nothing. **A pull down** from the page brought it back.
- **Job Mode** from the row: the page switched and the row was still there three and a half seconds later, with Job Mode filled. **Task Mode:** the checklist with the row as a line of the page seven seconds later. **Dashboard** from there: the counted row again, drawn in place.
- **The camera bar:** with the handle's line padded by 59 px, as a Home Screen iPhone pads it, the clip, the row and the handle all started at 59 px.
- **A leader in the dark look with less motion:** five blocks, three lit at 2.6 s, no bar, and the handle alone after.
- **A sub at 320 px:** the row over the page with Job Mode filled, three thirds of 100 px, no sideways scroll.

Not tried on a real phone. What is left for a hand: an iPhone on its Home Screen for the status bar, a resting thumb on real glass, and the swipe and the pull beside the phone's own gestures.
