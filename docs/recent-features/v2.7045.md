# v2.7045 — Bids: the pricing text you share with a teammate reads in whole dollars (2026-11-12)

Grace's ask, 2026-11-12, with a screenshot of the text an estimator sends a master for a look at a bid before it goes out: "I love this, but I don't want to see cents on the amounts."

## Where the text is built

- **The press:** Bids → Pricing → **Share with a teammate** → **Copy for text**, in `PackageAndSendBidPricingModal.tsx`. It puts the text on the clipboard and the estimator pastes it into their own text app. The app sends nothing.
- **The builder:** `buildBidPricingPackageSmsText` in `src/lib/buildBidPricingPackageSmsText.ts`. It feeds this one press. The window's other two ways to send (**Send for me**, **Send via my mail**) and its preview table are built by `buildBidPricingPackageHtml.ts` and its Deno mirror, and were not touched.

## What changed

The text's three money figures (unit price, line total, Total) now go through `formatUsdNoCents` from `src/lib/jobs/jobFormatting.ts`, the whole-dollar formatter the Stages lines already use. No new formatter. Before, all three used `formatPackageCurrency`, which the email table still uses.

## The rounding rule

- **A line total is the true amount rounded**, never the count times a rounded price.
- **The Total is the true total rounded**, never the rounded lines added up. So the Total can sit a dollar or two off what a reader gets adding the lines by eye (the lines above add to $18,841). No note is added to the text: a dollar on a five-figure bid does not read as a mistake, and a note on every text would cost more than it saves.
- **A count is not money** and is printed as stored: 61.37 feet stays 61.37.
- **A unit price drops its cents only when the line still adds up without them.** Pricing does produce unit prices with cents: per-foot rates, and one-off lines worked out from a margin. Two ways were weighed for those. Dropping the unit price from the line loses the one figure a master most wants to check on a per-foot row. Keeping the cents on every unit price that has them leaves cents on the common one-off line (`1 × $6,412.50`), which is exactly what the ask wanted gone. So the rule is by the line: the cents go when the count times the whole-dollar price lands on the line total shown, or when the price is the line total itself (a fixed price). Otherwise they stay, because $12.50 a foot printed as $13 would misstate the price and make `180.4 × $13 = $2,255` read wrong by $90.
- **An amount under 50 cents keeps its cents**, so a real charge never reads `$0`.
- An amount is rounded to the cent first and then to the dollar, so a float a hair under a half dollar (0.7 × 15 is 10.4999…) rounds as the $10.50 it is.

## Left alone

The bid's stored prices, the Pricing tab's figures, the window's preview table, both email lanes, the print, the CSV, the cover letter and everything the GC receives. `formatPackageCurrency` is unchanged.

## How it was checked

- `buildBidPricingPackageSmsText.test.ts`: ten new cases (round prices, a count that is not whole, .50 and over, the float half, cents dropped, cents kept, a fixed price, the true total against the rounded lines, under 50 cents, zero and not-a-number). The older cases' expectations moved to whole dollars.
- `PackageAndSendBidPricingModal.render.test.tsx`: the press copies whole-dollar lines and totals.
- Walked on the local app against production data with every write refused (2026-11-12): two real priced bids, 40 and 27 rows. Every line total and both totals read in whole dollars and matched the window's own figures rounded. Two per-foot rates that truly have cents kept them. Two one-off lines with cents dropped them. The only requests refused were the page's own activity pings.

## Is this the best we can do?

For the ask, yes: the text has no cents wherever they add nothing, and keeps them only where dropping them would make a line wrong. Two things are left for a person to decide, and neither was done here:

- **The email shows cents and the text does not.** The same window sends both to the same teammate. If the email should match, it is a second change with an edge function redeploy (`send-bid-pricing-package` mirrors the table).
- **A fixed-price row with a count over 1 reads oddly, and did before this change:** `3 × $5,000 = $5,000`. The count is on the line but the price is for the lot.
