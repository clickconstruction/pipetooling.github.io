# v2.6457 — The Scoreboard opens a job to show who put what into it (2026-11-03)

Punch list #250, PR 2 of 4, after PR 1 (v2.6407, #6886) put the Scoreboard on the Development page's Money look. The owner took draft 2 of the mock-up and its seven defaults; this PR is the game Squad's member rows under an open squad.

- **The rows** (`scoreboardMembers` in `src/lib/developments/scoreboard.ts`, drawn by `DevelopmentScoreboard.tsx`): under an open job's nine figures, the people from the clock (a **Lead** led the crew on at least one of the days, `people_crew_jobs.crew_lead_person_name`, the small words *led 40 of 74 days*; the rest **Crew**, by hours), each **Sub** by the names on the sheets (`assigned_to_name`, *2 sheets*), each **Supplier** by house (*11 invoices*), each **Card** holder (*34 charges*), the **Bank** payments with no card on them (an ACH, a wire, a check: *3 payments*), **The tally**, **Other job charges** and the overhead **Day share** (*118 days charged*), then the **Office** line: who sent the bills (`jobs_ledger_invoices.sent_by_user_id`) and who booked the payments (`jobs_ledger_payments.created_by`), words only. Each row fills its own kind's cell and **Cost**, with a thin bar for its share of the job's cost, so the rows add up to the job. A cell that is not the row's to fill is blank.
- **Adding up**: the costs read (`useSiteCosts` → `jobCostsFromRows`) keeps its totals and gains a `breakdown` per job from the rows it already reads: the sheets by name with the shared per-sheet cost (`laborJobSubCost`), the supply allocations by house (read before, unused, now handed in), the card lines the total counts (`jobCardLineStatus` = *counts*: an internal transfer and a charge also on a supply invoice stay out, as the total leaves them), and the tally and other lines counted. A split that falls short of its total (an allocation read cut at its limit, the card rule's clamp) gets one reconciling row, *Other supply invoices* or *Card charges not split*, so the sum holds.
- **The card holders** (`lib/developments/cardHolders.ts`, `fetchCardHolders.ts`, `hooks/useSiteCardHolders.ts`): the card id alone out of each bank record (`raw->details->debitCardInfo->>id`, a few bytes a row, never the whole record the Gray Fox spike took off the page's read), then the card's linked person (`mercury_debit_card_user_links`), else its nickname (`mercury_debit_card_nicknames`), else its short id. A line whose bank record names no card and whose `kind` is not a card kind (`isCardReviewCardKind`) is the bank's: one *Bank payments* row (`BANK_PAYMENTS_WORDS`), found by the live walk, where the big job's ACH payments read as a bare *Card*. Read only while the board is open, for the finance seats; until it lands, or if it fails, the job keeps one *Card* row.
- **The gold row**: the viewer's own rows, amber with a *You* chip: a person by login (`people.account_user_id`, read with the clock as `SiteLaborPerson.userId`), a person with no login by name (`useAuth().profileName`), the viewer's own card, the viewer's office line.
- **Who sees what**: dev and controller every kind with its dollars; a master or an assistant the people alone, with hours and days and no dollar (the lead's three-column board). The page's read gains `sent_by_user_id` on bills and `created_by` on payments, and their names.
- **Phone**: an open card lists the rows as lines under its nine, each with its figure (a person's *$21,420 · 612 h · 74 d*) and its share bar.
- **The middle widths** (found by the live walk across widths): at 768 the page ran 790 px wide with twelve figures clipped, at 1024 two px wide with eight. When the board itself is under 1,200 px wide (`SCOREBOARD_NARROW_BELOW_PX`, measured on the board, so a side panel or a zoom counts; the window's 1180 px query, `SCOREBOARD_COMPACT_QUERY`, guesses for the first paint) the full board now writes money short, the Gray Fox phone strip's rule with one more step (`scoreboardCompactWords`: *$2.4M*, *$712k*, one decimal from $1,000 since a 57 px column cannot hold a bold *−$6,840*, *$9.4k*, whole dollars under $1,000), with the whole figure on each cell's title, a member row's Cost title included; the percent beside Profit and the *50%* note beside Earned move into the titles; the state words step aside for the job's name (the status dot's title keeps them) and the flags read short; a member row's small words move into its title; the tiles take a smaller figure, a plain-case label and a smaller icon; the Job column narrows to 26%; the narrow cells pad 4 px a side; and the table scrolls inside its card rather than pushing the page (at 768 it now fits with nothing to scroll). The leader's two- or three-column board keeps whole dollars at every desk width, and the legend names only the groups the board shows. At whole dollars the columns are weighted (`scoreboardColumnWidths`): the Job column a quarter, Hours and Days 6.5% each, Profit 12% and the other money alike, since nine equal columns left the site's bold total and its percent 14 px short at 1280 wide.
- **A leader the clock is closed to** (found by the same walk, as the sample master): PR 1's leader board read *0 h* on every job, because `people_crew_jobs` and `people_hours_recorded` are closed to a master without payroll approval. An empty crew read now falls back to `get_man_hours_by_job` (a definer read open to dev, master, assistant and controller, the same allocation as the clock read), filtered to the site's jobs on the server (`siteLaborFromManHours`); it has no days, so the board leaves the Days column out (`scoreboardColumnsFor(seesCosts, daysKnown)`) and nobody reads as a Lead, never a false zero. An assistant and a controller read the clock as before.
- **Guide**: *Read the scoreboard* gains how to open a job and read its rows.

**Verified live** on the real site through the dev server: as the dev at 375, 768, 1024, 1280, 1440 and 1920 wide in light and dark, and as the sample master at 375, 768, 1024 and 1280. All sixteen passes fit the window, with nothing past the edge, no figure clipped and nothing to scroll inside the board; a probe of the board alone at 1200, 1366 and 1600 found nothing sticking out either. The board settles in about four seconds, the big job opens to twenty rows that add up to its Cost within a dollar of rounding, and its hours match the dev seat's `get_job` to the hour; pressing Profit sorts the losses to the bottom. Gray Fox's clock rows name no crew lead, so every person there reads *Crew*. The dev seat's `get_job` counts 200 card lines on that job where the page counts 498, so the card-charge gap first seen on PR 1 looks like that read stopping at 200 rows.
