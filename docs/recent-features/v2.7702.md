# v2.7702 — Trips: what a trip cost, the Scoreboard cut by trip, and a printed trip log (2026-11-17)

Punch list #379, PR 8 of nine: **What it cost** on an open trip, the Scoreboard's cut by trip, and **Print the trip log**. Client only. No migration, no edge function, and no read the page did not already make.

A trip stays a reading laid over the clock. Nothing here changes a clock row, a bill or a cost, and nothing is allocated: a figure that cannot honestly be laid over a trip's days is left blank.

## What landed

- **What it cost**, on an open trip that has gone out, under *What shows it*, for a reader who may see the site's costs (`canSeeSiteCosts`: a dev or a controller, the Scoreboard's own rule). Three rows (`lib/trips/tripCost.ts`):
  - **The crew's hours**: the labor dollars of the hours inside the trip. A person's day on the site's jobs (`SiteLabor` → `byWorkDate[].costUsd`) goes to the trip that person was on that day, under the hours split's own key (`tripPersonKey`), so the dollars cover exactly the hours the trip's table counts. A day off in the middle, and a person who did not go, stay out.
  - **Card and bank charges in the days**: the charges in the site's card bucket that the job costs count, dated on a day anyone was on the trip, with how many. They are read from the cost rows the page already holds (`SiteCostRows.cardAlloc` with `cardExclusions`, the lines `jobCardLineStatus` calls *counts*: no internal transfer, no charge already on a supply invoice), each dated by its bank stamp the way the Scoreboard's window dates it. The bucket also holds a payment out of the bank with no card on it (an ACH, a check), and the rows the page holds cannot tell the two apart, so the row is named for both. A charge split over two of the site's jobs is one charge.
  - **The trip**: the two together.
  A day two trips both hold goes to the trip that went out first, as an hour does. Each row says in words when its read is still out (*reading the clock…*, *reading the costs…*), closed or unread, and the sum is a dash until both figures stand. Everyone else is handed no costs at all: the look draws no table and holds no dollar.
- **The Scoreboard, cut by trip.** *Cut by* gains **trip** after job, part and person, offered only on a site with a trip (`SCOREBOARD_CUTS` in `scoreboardCuts.ts`, the rows in `lib/trips/tripScoreboardCut.ts`, drawn by `components/trips/ScoreboardTripRows.tsx`). A row for each trip that has gone out, newest first, its days in words and what it was for, and a last row **At home for the site**. A device that remembered the cut on a site with no trip reads by job.
  - A trip's row fills **Hours**, **Days**, **Labor**, **Parts** and **Cost**. Hours and Labor are its people's hours and wages on their own trip days. Days is the Scoreboard's own measure, the days with clocked work, each once, inside the trip; the cell's hover line says how long the trip itself ran. Parts is the card and bank charges dated in its days, the bucket *Card and bank charges in the days* names, and its hover line says so with the count. Cost is Labor and Parts together, which is *The trip*. So a trip's Cost on the board and on the open trip are one figure, and it does not wait on the overhead read.
  - **Subs** and **Overhead** are not cut by trip, on any row, and **Earned** and **Profit** are blank on every row. A sub's sheet and the office day share are not known to be a trip's or at home, and a job's price, its percent, what it billed and what it was paid belong to the job and not to days. Nothing is allocated.
  - The last row fills **Hours**, **Days**, **Labor** and **Parts**: the site less its trips, so Hours, Labor and Parts add up to the site's own totals, the ones the cut by job shows, and the days are the site's days counted once. Its Subs, Overhead and Cost cells are empty, so the board never says a sub who went on a trip was at home, and Cost is not claimed to add up. The line under the board says it in plain words: a trip's cost is its crew's hours and the card and bank charges in its days, and subs and overhead are not cut by trip.
  - In a window the rows carry the days inside it alone. The leader's board fills Hours and Days and no dollar. The rows do not sort by a tile and do not fold: newest first, and a trip has no rows under it.
- **Print the trip log**, a quiet link beside **+ Trip**, for everyone who sees the look. One portrait page, light whatever the theme (`lib/trips/tripLog.ts`: `buildTripLog` makes the lines from the look's own model, `tripLogPrintHtml` the page): the site, the customer when the page has one, when it was printed and by whom, then a block a trip, oldest first, with its days and where it stands, what it was for, its days by kind, who asked and how, how it went, the jobs its work goes on, its billing, its hours in the four trip figures when the clock is read, and who went with each person's days by kind. A totals line closes it: the trips, the days at the site, and the site's five figures when read. A trip's days always say their year on paper. **No dollars are on the page, whoever prints it.**
- **The print is a send.** It opens through `printAndFile`, as the Plans by date print does, so the page that printed is filed in Documents (kind `trip_log`, the development as its source, under its customer). A blocked window says so beside the link and files nothing.

## What the page reads

Nothing new. The cost rows are the ones `useSiteCosts` already reads for a dev or a controller on every Development page, for the Jobs & bids table; the labor is the read the open Trips look and the Scoreboard already ask for; the trips and the roster are `useSiteTrips`'. A reader who may not see costs still triggers no cost read.

## Left out, and why

- **The Print press on an open trip**, drawn on the mock-up, is not built. The log is the one print.
- **The Scoreboard's print** (*Print cost summary*) is by job and over the site's whole life, whatever the cut on the screen. The cut by trip is not on it, as the cuts by part and person are not.
- **Subs, supply invoices, the tally, other charges and overhead by trip.** Their rows carry dates, and the Scoreboard's window already cuts them by day, but a dated charge on a trip's day is not known to be the trip's. Only the card bucket is laid over a trip, to match *Card and bank charges in the days*. Supply invoices, the tally and other charges stay in the last row's Parts; subs and overhead are on no row of the cut.
- **Telling a card charge from a bank payment.** A payment out of the bank with no card on it (an ACH, a check) sits in the same bucket as a card charge in the cost rows, as it does in a job's card charges total. Telling the two apart takes the card holders read, which runs only while the Scoreboard is open, so the row counts both and is named for both.
