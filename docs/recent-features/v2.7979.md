## The rule

`src/lib/paintedMap/paintedMapProvider.ts`, `resolvePaintedMapProvider`. It answers `google` or `osm`, the same two words every other map in the app answers with. Google only when every line below says yes.

1. **The app's own rule first** (`resolveDashboardMapProvider`): a browser key, and no failure of Google on this page.
2. **The screen.** `window` is the painting window, staff only. `way` is the public page, opened by every outside reader a link reaches.
3. **The use.** `looking`, `drawing`, `placing` or `walking`. The Google canvas is built a step at a time, and a use it does not carry yet draws on Leaflet however good the key is.
4. **None of Esri's photographs on the screen.** Time's archive is Esri's, and so is a picture of the map made by the Share sheet. While either shows, the Google canvas is down and the Leaflet canvas takes its place, as on every Time map since v2.7794.

The Leaflet canvas is never retired. It is the fall back, and it is the canvas Time draws on (v2.7814).

## The window's modes

`paintedWindowUse` turns the painting window's five modes into a use: `view` and `review` are looking, `draw` is drawing, `place` is placing and `walk` is walking. It reads the mode and not what the window hands the canvas. A paused walk hands the canvas no `follow`, so read from the props it would look like a map being read, and the map would change under the walker each time they stopped.

## The picture's own rule

`PAINTED_PICTURE_PROVIDER` answers Esri whatever the canvas draws on. A picture and a printed page are map tiles stitched into one image, and the image leaves the app by text, on paper and into a job's folder. Google's terms keep its tiles inside its own map. So a map read over Google's photograph is still pictured over Esri's, and the two photographs of one spot can sit a few feet apart. A test ties the rule to the address the picture's tiles are really read from.

## Read on Google's own pages, 2026-11-19

The card asked for the terms to be read at this step. They were read in a browser on the day, at `cloud.google.com/maps-platform/terms`, section 3.2.3.

- **(a) and (b), no scraping and no caching.** Tiles are not downloaded in bulk, kept, or shared outside Google's services. This is why the picture and the print cannot be made from Google's tiles.
- **(e), no use with maps that are not Google's.** Google's services are not used with or near another provider's map in one application, and linking a Google map to one is a named example. This is the clause v2.7686 read. It is why the kernel takes the Google canvas down while Time or a picture is up.
- **(c), no content made from Google's content.** This one is new to the card. The clause forbids making content based on Google's content, and its first example is to "trace or digitize roadways, building outlines, utility posts, or electrical lines" from the satellite view of the Maps JavaScript API. A line drawn by hand along the road in a photograph is close to that example. So is a meter, a shutoff or a cleanout placed by dragging a dot onto the spot in the photograph. The line is then kept in our own tables, shown on maps that are not Google's, printed, and sent out as a file. This is a reading and not legal advice. It is the owner's call, and it is call 6 on the card.

A walked line comes from the phone's own readings and not from the photograph, so (c) reads differently for it. Looking at a saved map over Google's map makes nothing new and is the plain use of the service.

What Google bills, from its pricing and SKU pages the same day:

| The product | Dynamic Maps, in the Essentials group |

Google's pages do not say in words whether a second map made on one page is a second load. The plan reads it as one load for each map made. So each change from the Leaflet canvas back to Google's is one more load, and step 2 has to keep those changes few.
