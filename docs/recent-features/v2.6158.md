# v2.6158 — The interest bill shows each late bill as a row (punch list #217, outside-5) (2026-10-30)

Punch list #217, *Show it, don't say it*: the outside scan's fifth finding (`.claude/sessions/scans/show-not-tell/findings/outside.md` → outside-5). The GC customer's interest email read *Some of your bills on Sample Retail Shell went past the day they were due by the contract. The interest on them comes to $285, at 1.5% a month.*, and the reminder *Interest of 1.5% a month runs on it from Oct 2. $184 has built up so far.* A charge a stranger is asked to pay, made by a rule from bills and days the email never named. Taunya marked it **Build** on the mock-up (2026-10-30).

## What changed

- **The rows** (`src/lib/gc/ownerBillingInterest.ts`): `interestRows(state, project)` turns `ownerInterest().bills` into one `InterestRow` per late bill — the bill, the day it fell due by the contract (`from`), the day it was paid or null, the days late, what was open when it fell due, a month's interest on that, the interest it came to, and `straight` (nothing paid while it ran, so the amount is monthly × days ÷ 30.4). `interestRowCells` writes the row under `INTEREST_TABLE_HEAD` (*Bill · Due · Paid · Days late · Open · Interest*), the last cell the arithmetic a customer can redo (*$675 × 32⁄30.4 = $710*) or the figure alone for a bill paid in parts, which `interestRowNotes` names under the table; `interestSumCells` is the total under a rule; `straightInterestRow` writes a made-up row with the same arithmetic for the samples. `INTEREST_MONTH_DAYS` is 365 ÷ 12, the rate the kernel has always run at.
- **The email** (`src/lib/gc/customerEmail.ts`): `InterestBillMailFacts` carries `rows`; `interestBillMail` says *Two bills on Sample Retail Shell went past the day the contract made them due. Interest runs at 1.5% a month from that day.*, then the table, *A month counts as 30.4 days, a twelfth of a year.*, the part-payment lines, and when earlier interest bills took their share, *Our earlier interest bills asked for $X of it. This bill asks for $Y.* With no rows (the rate gone from the job) the old sentence stands. `interestBillMailFacts` reads the rows off the job, so Bill the customer's **Bill the interest** needs no change.
- **The reminder** (`src/lib/gc/ownerBillingRemind.ts`): `PayReminderMailFacts.interest.row` is the bill's own row; `payReminderMail` keeps its sentence and puts the one-row table under it. `payReminderEmail` fills it from `interestRowOf`.
- **The frame** (`supabase/functions/_shared/gcCustomerEmails.ts`): a table rides inside `lines` as plain strings — `| a | b |` is a row, `|= …` the sum row, a run of them one table, its first row the head (`gcEmailTableLines`, `gcEmailTableRow`, `gcEmailBlocks`). `buildGcCustomerEmail` draws the run as one `<table>` in the GC statement email's layout (a two-point rule under the head, a hairline under each row, figures on the right, the sum row bold under a rule with its empty cells folded into its label); the text part joins each row's cells with dots. `gcCustomerEmailMaxLines('interest_bill')` is 80, a row a late bill. The lines stay strings so `gc_owner_pay_reminders.lines` holds a reminder's row with no migration.
- **The office's preview** (`src/components/gc/GcEmailLines.tsx`, used by `GcBillRemind`): the same lines drawn the same way before the send.
- **What customers see** (`src/lib/customerSampleEmails.ts`): the interest bill sample carries two made-up late bills (pay application 2, due 60 days ago and paid 28 days ago, $45,000; pay application 3, due 28 days ago and open, $39,400) and the reminder sample its one row (`sampleInterestRows`).
- **Guides**: `bill-the-customer-on-a-gc-job.md` → Bill the interest shows the rows; `remind-a-customer-to-pay-a-gc-bill.md` names the row. `docs/EDGE_FUNCTIONS.md` → gc-customer-email: the table rule under `lines`.

## Not done, and why

- **The portal's bill shows the same rows** (the finding's last line): the customer's portal lists no interest bill today — `customer-portal` reads `gc_owner_pay_apps` and never `gc_owner_interest_bills`, and the email's portal line lands on the job's page, not on the bill. Putting the interest bill on the portal is a `customer-portal` read and a page of its own, a deploy and a design the finding did not size; it is left for a card of its own.
- The mock-up's arithmetic read *× 32⁄30* (a 30-day month); the kernel's month is a twelfth of a year, so the rows read *÷ 30.4* and say so under the table, rather than printing a sum that does not reproduce the figure.
