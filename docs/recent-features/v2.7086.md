# v2.7086 — Demand letter: the whole letter on page 1, the account behind it (2026-11-12)

Punch list #333. The owner asked how the demand letter could fit on one page, read the mock-up and said *Yes, build it*. All of it on one sheet was measured for the card and does not fit, so the build is the card's suggestion: the letter whole on page 1, signed, and the account on the page behind it.

## What changed

- **A mark on the letter's fields.** `DemandLetterFields.layout: 'letter-first'`, set by `buildDemandLetterPrefill`. The Lien window stores the fields with each recorded letter, so a letter recorded before has no mark and every renderer draws it as it was sent. The short form (no statement) has no account to move, and the mark changes nothing there.
- **The model** (`buildDemandLetterModel`). With the mark: letterhead, address, subject, amount box, the opening, the *Demand* section, the signature (and the notary block when ticked), then a new `pageBreak` block, then the statement of account, its closing sentence, the notice history, the enclosures and *Copies to*. The *Demand* heading drops its 72 mm keep: nothing sits above it to break from.
- **The pay code.** One code rides in the amount box as a third cell (`amountBox.code`). Two or more keep the band, moved above the statement on the account page.
- **The signature** drops a phone or an email the letterhead already shows.
- **One phrase.** *for the work below at …* reads *for the work on the statement of account behind this letter, at …*. No other word changes. The wording is the owner's and counsel's to change: it is one constant in the model.
- **The PDF** (`drawDemandLetterPdf`). The box grows to hold a 20 mm code, the break starts a page, and the fit of v2.7080 counts the pages through the signature only, so a long account never tightens the letter. The band's address is wrapped to its column: a bill's own address is longer than a column and two ran into each other.
- **The page and the email** (`buildDemandLetterEmailHtml`). The same order. The break is a hairline on a screen and a new sheet in print. The box's pay cell wraps its words under the code on a narrow screen, so the box never runs wider than a phone. The print page of a letter-first letter sets its lines at `DEMAND_LETTER_PRINT_LEADING` (1.4) from the sheet's own margin, so the letter holds its sheet.
- **Plain text** (`buildDemandLetterText`): the code's address under the amount line, the same order.
- **The Lien window's tick** says where the codes sit.
- **The sample** (`sampleDemandLetterFields`). Settings → What customers see now shows the letter the Lien window builds: a statement of account, the fee and interest lines by their own rules, a notice history, two exhibits and a pay code. The code is `SAMPLE_PAY_CODE_SVG`, the app's own drawing for the sample bill's address kept as a constant (a code is drawn async, a sample is framed at once); `samplePayCode.test.ts` draws it again and fails if the two differ.

No migration, no edge function.

## Not changed, named

- The pay code is a picture in the PDF because of v2.7090, found here and shipped by itself: before it, every PDF drew an empty square where a code belongs.
- The mock-up of punch list #326 still shows the short sample letter. It is that card's picture of its day.
- The statement table on a phone-wide page squeezes its *For* column to a word a line. It did before this.
- Not checked: a real job's letter (the app holds one recorded demand letter, voided), the notary block and the theft-of-services line on a letter-first letter, and a code scanned from paper.
