# v2.7377 — Box labels: the link prints in letters made of whole dots (2026-11-15)

Taunya's question of 2026-11-15, looking at v2.7373's shorter strip: "Do you think we are using a font for the link that is easy to read in low pixel?" The answer was no, and she picked the dot font from a comparison of three. Client only: no migration, no edge function. The QR, the link and the code are what they were.

## What was wrong

The link was drawn in bold Menlo at 17 dots (`tapeStripCanvas.ts`), a screen font laid down smooth and then forced to black or white, since the printer has no grey. Its capitals were about twelve dots tall. At that size the bold strokes filled the insides of the letters: in `8B0D-D08B` the 8, the B and the slashed 0 came out as near-identical blobs with uneven strokes. Those are the look-alikes of a code, which is only 0 to 9 and A to F, and the code is what a person types when a scan fails. Menlo is also a Mac's font. The list fell back to Consolas or Courier New elsewhere, so another computer printed other letters at another width.

## What changed

- **`src/lib/materials/tapeDotFont.ts`** (pure, new): `DOT_FONT`, the capitals, the digits and `. / - :` as seven rows of five squares each. The zero is slashed, the B is square at its three left corners where the 8 is round. `dotFontCovers`, `dotFontSquare` (the printer's dots to a square: 2 on 18 and 24 mm tape), `dotTextWidth`, `dotTextHeight` and `dotTextCells` (a line's ink as squares for the canvas to fill).
- **`tapeStrip.ts`**: the layout carries `codeSquare`, and `codePx` is the dot font's line, 17 dots on both tapes (it was 15 on 18 mm tape).
- **`tapeStripCanvas.ts`** fills the address's squares instead of drawing text: every edge on a dot, every stroke two dots, as the QR is drawn. A line with a character the font lacks is drawn in the old face, so nothing is ever left blank; an address is capitals, digits, a dot, slashes and a dash, so that is the test strip's words and nothing else today.
- Print labels, Print blank stickers and **What the stickers will look like** all draw through `TapeStripBitmap`, so all three follow.

## Measured

Through `TapeStripBitmap` in Chrome, in the printer's dots at 180 an inch. Capitals 14 dots, 2.0 mm (were about 12, 1.7 mm). `PRTS.CO/B/` is 118 dots, 16.7 mm (was 102, 14.4 mm), so only a strip whose name is narrower than that grows:

|---|---|---|---|---|
| G1 | 38.1 mm | 40.5 mm | 32.0 mm | 36.1 mm |
| G81 | 41.8 mm | 41.8 mm | 35.3 mm | 36.1 mm |

| a sticker, 0001 | 45.3 mm | 45.3 mm | 38.2 mm | 38.2 mm |

The first 155 columns of each strip, the QR and its clear space, are dot for dot what they were, and Chrome's `BarcodeDetector` reads `HTTPS://PRTS.CO/B/<CODE>` off each. The lines sit at dots 79 to 92 and 100 to 113 of 128, nine dots under the name and seven apart, the block centred.

## The picks

- **The dot font over a lighter weight of Menlo.** Regular Menlo at the same size opened the insides and cost no length, but left strokes one dot wide, 0.14 mm, which may print faint on thermal tape, and it kept the Mac-only font. Whole dots at two a square rely on neither.
- **Two dots a square on both tapes.** A square of one dot is a 1 mm capital; three is a 3 mm capital and a first line 25 mm wide, wider than most names, so nearly every strip would grow.
- **The print-window route keeps the browser's own type.** There the strip is page text the printer's driver draws at its own dots, in regular weight, and a canvas font cannot be handed to it.
