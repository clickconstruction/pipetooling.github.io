# v2.8291 — Materials on a phone: the desk is three figures and one row a job (2026-11-22)

The Materials desk at a phone's width, with no job open. The owner sent a picture of it from his phone and said he did not like how it looked, then asked for a second drawing before he said build. He then asked for the same list for the field roles, and then for the search's results. No migration, no edge function. The desk at a computer is not changed, and neither is Jobs → Materials at any width.

## What was wrong

The desk is a table of seven columns: Job, Containers, On site lines, Missing this week, Counts, Credits due, and a row's two presses. v2.8282 and v2.8284 reworked a job's own page for a phone and left this table as it was.

- **Five of the seven columns were off the right edge.** A phone showed Job, Containers and a cut *On site,*. Which boxes are due a count and what came up short could only be reached by sliding the table, and nothing said they were there.
- **The loudest link was the rarest act.** **Move everything on site to another job…** sat under the first site's name on two lines.
- **The sentence under the heading named columns a phone could not see.**
- **Every name was a blue link squeezed into half the width**, so most wrapped, and a site read the same as its jobs.

## What a phone gets

- **Three figures where the sentence was.** The sentence promised *what is on every site, what went missing this week, which containers have not been counted in 14 days*. The figures are those three across every job: **Boxes on site**, **Due a count**, **Missing this week**. They are the job page's own tiles (`FlowTiles`).
- **A figure narrows the list.** **Due a count** keeps the jobs with a box due, **Missing this week** the jobs that came up short. A second press, or **Boxes on site**, brings every job back. A figure with nothing behind it is grey and its press is dead. The narrowing is a visit's state and is not kept.
- **One row a job.** The number is a small tag on the left, so numbers read down a column. The name has the rest of the row. One quiet line says what the job holds: *94 boxes · 2 places*. Then a line for each thing it needs, in the words the desk already writes: *94 due a count · G1, G2, G3…* (`staleWords`), *$85.00 missing this week · 2 lines* (`missingWeekNote`). A row that needs something has an amber edge. A row that needs nothing says *all counted this fortnight*. The whole row opens the job.
- **A site is a heading.** Its name sits over its jobs with **Whole site ›** beside it, the press the site's row had. A site with more than one job here says its boxes, since no row adds them up. Jobs on no site sit under **Not on a site**.
- **Open a job** is one wide press under the list that says its own name, with the same searchable list.
- **A job that did not load** is not a press. It says *Did not load.* with its reason and offers **Read again**, as its row on the desk does. It stays in the list under every figure.

- **A site heads its jobs** with **Whole site ›**, which opens the site's screen with no money, as **Open the site** does. In the table the site was a row over a flat list of every job. Here its jobs sit under it. An estimator reads no site, so every job is a row under no heading.
- **A row names the boxes due a count**, where the table said only how many.

## The search

*Find a part or a box on any job* stands over the list for every role. Its results were a table of six columns: Part, Box, Job, On hand, Last counted and a press. A phone showed the first two.

On a phone a hit is now one row that reads down (`findPhoneRow`, `OnSiteFindPhoneList`):

- **A part that matched**: its name and maker, then the box it is in with what the box is and where it stands, then the job, then how the box's count stands. **How many are on hand** is a figure at the row's end, the number over its words, as the page's tiles read.
- **A box that matched by its own name**: the box's name with what it is and where it stands beside it, the job, and its count. Three lines, so seven boxes fit a screen.
- **A box due a count** says so in amber words. The row a delivery lands in says *delivered* and nothing about a count, as in the table.
- **The whole row is the press, and it opens the box.** For a field role that is where the table's **Open the box** leads. For the office the table's press is **Open the job**. On a phone the office's row opens the box on the Tally's screen, as a row of the job's page does since v2.8284, and the job is one Back away. A person who searched for a part wants its box.

The count beside the search, *14 boxes on 2 jobs*, the cap of sixty hits, the words when nothing matches and their **Clear** press are unchanged.

## How it is built

- `src/lib/materials/deskPhone.ts` is the kernel. `buildDeskPhone({ jobs, groups, filter, money })` takes each job's figures as the desk already computed them and the groups from `groupDeskBySite`, and answers the three tiles, the filter in force and the sections with their rows. It counts nothing itself, so the table and the list cannot disagree. A filter that would leave nothing to show falls back to every job.
- `src/components/materials/MaterialsDeskPhone.tsx` draws that model.
- `FlowTiles` in `FlowRow.tsx` takes `role`. As `filters` it is a group of presses with `aria-pressed`. The job page's tiles are tabs, as before.
- `JobsMaterialsTab` draws the phone desk when `phone` is set, which is the Materials page's mount under 640 px. The desk's rows, sort and groups are computed once and feed both layouts. **Open a job** is one definition for both.
- `MaterialsOnSiteTab` draws a field role's jobs through the same model when `phone` is set and there is a job to list, with `money: false`. Its boxes due a count are the ones the table's count is made from, named with `staleWords`.
- `src/lib/materials/onSiteFindPhone.ts`: `findPhoneRow(hit, count)` turns one of `findOnSite`'s hits into a row's lines, in the table's own words. `OnSiteFindPhoneList.tsx` draws the rows as links.

## What the look changed

Seen on the dev server as the dev, on live data, one page load: 393 × 852 in light and dark, and 375 × 812.

1. **The rail is the row's own edge.** First drawn as a bar inside the row, it floated. It is now the row's left border, flush with the card.
2. **Rows are square inside their card.** The app's buttons round their corners, which bent the line between two rows.
3. **Open a job is link blue.** The list's placeholder is grey, which read as a dead field.

At both widths `innerWidth` and `scrollWidth` were equal. At 393 px the whole desk, four jobs, is one screen. **Due a count** was pressed and the list fell to the three jobs with a box due. A row was pressed and its job opened. Nothing that writes was pressed.

The field roles' list could not be walked as one of them: the sample accounts are on no job with a box, so they see the empty words. Its layout was seen once on the dev server with the office's rows and a local switch that turned money off, which was taken out again before the commit: two figures 178 px wide, no dollar sign in the list, no overflow at 393 px. Which presses it draws and where they lead is held by its render test.

The search was seen as the dev on live data, one page load: *elbow* found one part, 9 on hand in ZZ1, and *G1* found 14 boxes on 2 jobs. Rows of boxes first stood 103 px on four lines. With what the box is and where it stands beside its name they stand 81 px. `innerWidth` and `scrollWidth` were equal at 393 and at 375 px. No row was pressed.
