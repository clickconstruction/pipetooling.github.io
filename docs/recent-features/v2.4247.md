# v2.4247 — Hours someone typed wear a pencil wherever hours are approved (2026-09-30)

Punch list #70, PR 2 — the screens for [v2.4242](v2.4242.md)'s ledger and hold. The owner's question that shaped it: *what about the other views hours are approved from?* There are nine, and all nine call one helper, so the rule lives in the database and the screens only have to show it.

## What changed

- **One stamp, three sizes** — [`TypedHoursStamp`](../../src/components/clock/TypedHoursStamp.tsx): `full` (a chip *✎ typed by Taunya · Wed 9:40 AM* and the day's *Nothing recorded → 11.0h*), `compact` (the chip, the rest in its title), `dot` (a pencil). A trim shows on the full row only, quietly. `TypedHoldNote` stands where an Approve the viewer may not press would be.
- **The words are a kernel** — [`typedHours.ts`](../../src/lib/clock/typedHours.ts): `parseTypedStampRow`, `typedByWords`, `dayChangeWords`, `holdWords`, `describeHeld`, and `splitForApproveAll`, which sorts a batch into punches (one press may take them), typed rows waiting on this viewer's look (opened one at a time) and held rows.
- **One read** — [`useTypedStamps(ids)`](../../src/hooks/useTypedStamps.ts) over `clock_typed_stamps`; a failed read is "no stamps", never an error on the list it decorates.
- **The helper** — [`approveClockSessions`](../../src/lib/approveClockSessions.ts) calls `approve_clock_sessions_v2` and falls back to the old name when it is not on the database; `heldFromApproveResult` hands each view what the approve left behind.
- **The nine views**
  - *Hours grid cell popover* — the typed line wears the stamp, its note and its own **Approve 11.00h**; the day button reads **Approve 1 punch**; the window stays open while a line is still set aside. The cell's **!N** chip shows the pencil.
  - *Approvals queue (All weeks)* — the stamp on the row; **Approve week / all / everything** take the punches and say *N of M*; a **Typed by hand · N** filter opens those rows.
  - *Dashboard My Team* — the stamp under the note; **Approve all N punches**; a line for what was set aside.
  - *Bulk approve window*, *day audit*, *pay-week sessions window* — the same split; the stamp on the row.
  - *Sessions list*, *Moneyfill queue* — the stamp and the hold in place of Approve.
  - *Clock strip* — a pencil beside the pill; the pill is not a one-press approve on a held row; Session actions shows the stamp and the hold. An approve the database held no longer paints the pill green.
- Guide *approve my team's hours* gains **Hours someone typed**; GLOSSARY gains **Typed hours**.

## Walked live (2026-09-30, prod, the switch on `test`)

With the sample accounts, on the approvals queue: the sample assistant clocked in and out on *ZZ TEST sink* (a real punch — no stamp) and her own row read *Your own hours — someone else approves them*, the batch buttons *Approve 0 of 1*. She typed an earlier start: the row wore *✎ typed by Sample assistant · own entry* and *2 min → 2.8h*, and **Typed by hand · 1** appeared. The dev had an Approve on it; he typed an hour more onto it and the stamp re-read *✎ typed by Robert · 2.8h → 3.8h* with *You typed these — waiting on a second person* in place of Approve. The sample controller approved it; the queue read *Nothing typed by hand is waiting.*

Found in the walk and fixed here: a stamp did not re-read when a row's times changed under the same id (`typedStampsVersion`, which every view now passes to the hook); a sliver of time read *0.0h* (now *2 min*); the hold note wrapped inside a pill (now a rounded rectangle); the queue's toolbar wrapped once the filter joined it.

Not walked live: the Hours grid popover, My Team, the clock strip and the other five views with a typed row in them — sample accounts are hidden from every roster, so a typed row cannot be put there without typing on a real person. They are covered by the render tests and share the kernel, the hook and the stamp that the queue walked.

Test data left in prod: one approved session for *Sample assistant* on 2026-09-30 (J1064 *ZZ TEST sink*, 8:00–11:50 AM) and its two ledger rows.
