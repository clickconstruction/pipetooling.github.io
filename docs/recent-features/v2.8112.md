# v2.8112 — Permits, Market: the call list after the owner's answers (2026-11-20)

Punch list #405 ([the card](../../to-dos/permits-market-page/README.md), *The owner's answers, 2026-11-20*). The card left four calls for the owner. He answered them on 2026-11-20 with a read of the live permits behind each. This is what two of the answers change on **Call this week**. The call list itself is [v2.7993](./v2.7993.md).

## What the office sees

- **A name with a building word leads.** Construction, constructors, contracting, contractors, builders, build, building, or GC. Then the other names that read as a company, then the rest. Inside each group the order is as it was: a job open now, then picking up or new and busy, then never bid, then the most jobs in ninety days. Nothing is removed.
- **A job is counted once.** Each row says *jobs* where it said *permits*, and a name is on the list from three jobs in twelve months.
- **An answered name comes back only with a new job.** The days stand: 90 after Pursue, a year after Not for us. After its day the name is offered again only when it has a job open now that it applied for after the answer. Until then it stays under **Answered**, which reads *Off the list until it applies for a new job*, and **Put back** still brings it back.
- The line under the list says the new order and that a job is counted once.

## Why

- **The order.** v2.7993 put a name that reads as a builder first, and read any company word as a builder: LLC, Inc, Group, Associates. Read on live San Antonio data, the first twenty held about as many design firms and trades as builders. The trades were ones that hire no plumber. A building word in the name was right on every name it picked among those twenty, and missed one, which wrote *GCs*. So GC counts as a building word too.
- **Builders only was turned down.** Too few names carry a building word to fill the weeks. The rest stay on the list behind them.
- **The count.** San Antonio's feed keeps an application and its permit as two rows. When the permit is issued the application takes the same issued day. A project of several buildings is a row a building and a row for the site work, all issued on one day, each carrying the whole project's value. The audit of 2026-11-20 found that over half of the *Picking up* and *New and busy* reasons did not survive counting a job once.
- **The return.** The order has no key for an earlier answer, so a name took its old place back on its day with nothing new to call about. At five names a week the list would have started to repeat.

## How each is decided

- **`saysItBuilds`** (`permitCallList.ts`): the office's kind *General contractor* leads. Any other kind the office gave does not lead on the name alone. With no kind, the name leads on a building word. It is the first key of `callRows`' order, ahead of `nameReads`.
- **`nameReads`** now reads a name with a building word as a builder whatever the name splitter says. *Constructors* and *Build* are no company words to `contactParts`, so such a name read *Could be a person*.
- **`marketJobs`** (`permitMarket.ts`): one name's rows of one day at one address are one job. The day is the issued day, else the applied day. The address is `permits.address_key`, which the year's read now asks for. A job is issued once any of its rows is. It takes an issued row's kind of work, its earliest applied day and its largest value. A row with no day or no address is a job by itself.
- **`callRows`** counts `marketJobs` of the year's rows: the year, both ninety-day halves, the trend, the kind of work and the typical job.
- **`backWithNewJob`**: false while the answer's day has not come. After it, true only when one of the name's jobs open now was applied for after the day of the answer.

## What this does not change

- **Builders and a builder's sheet still count rows**, as `research_builders` does. So a name's sheet may say more permits than its row on the call list says jobs. Changing that read is a migration of its own.
- **How much is being built** still counts rows. Its words say permits, and a project of ten buildings is ten permits. An application and its permit are still two there.
- **The clock** reads the rows as before. It needs the application's own two days.
- **The days**, 90 and 365, and the table `builder_call_answers`.
- **A city with no applications.** No job is ever open there, so an answered name there comes back only by **Put back**. Austin is the one such city with builders named, and the owner's answer is that Austin's list waits until 2027-02-22.
