# v2.7826 — Pay: a salaried day can be the length the person works (2026-11-18)

The database half of a per-person salaried day. The owner asked on 2026-11-18. No screen changes.

## Why

A salaried person is paid, costed and scheduled as a flat 8 hours on each weekday at the hourly wage. A person on salary for a 30-hour week, 6 hours on five days, could only be entered as 8 hours at a lower wage. The pay came out right. The wage on the card, the man-hours on a job, the week's target and the end of the shift came out wrong.

A salary is three facts: the weekly pay, the hours in the day and the days worked. The app held one shape for all three.

## What ships

Migration `20261118160000_salaried_day_hours.sql` ([its page](../migrations/20261118160000_salaried_day_hours.md)):

- `people_pay_config.salaried_day_hours`, the length of the person's salaried day in hours, 8 by default.
- `people_pay_config_salaried_day_hours_check`, which holds it above 0, at most 16 and in quarter hours.

Every row reads 8, so nothing changes for anyone until a day is set. Nobody new reads or writes anything.

## The method, in three steps

1. This column, then the shared rule, payroll and the Pay & schedule card. The card takes the weekly amount and the day's hours and works out the wage. The wage and the day's hours are stored, and the weekly figure is always worked out from them.
2. The cost readers and the five functions that carry the 8.
3. The workday schedule window, whose block becomes as long as the person's day.

Left out on purpose: different hours on different days, and a pay change that starts on a date. The second is the larger hole, since a change to a wage re-prices past weeks, and it is its own piece of work.

## Order

Additive and idempotent. The push can come any time after this merges. The client that follows must not merge before the push: it names the column in the read of the pay row.
