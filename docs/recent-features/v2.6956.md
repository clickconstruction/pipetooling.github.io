## What a size is

Another setting of the card's **back**. A card is 3.5 by 2 inches, and between the safe margins the photo and the words share about 3.15 inches of width, so a size is a trade (`src/lib/brandKit/sizes.ts`):

| Size | The name | A line | The photo |
|---|---|---|---|
| Standard | 12.5 pt | 8.4 pt | 0.86 in |
| Larger photo | 14.9 pt | 10.1 pt | 1.15 in |
| Larger text | 15.8 pt | 10.6 pt | 1.00 in |

*Larger text* gets its room by moving the seam between the panel and the ground to the left. In both larger sizes the words are centred on the card's middle, so a person with fewer lines still sits right. The front and the footer do not change.

**A size is not a look.** A look only recolours. A size moves things, so each carries its own numbers, and `cardBackFor(look, size)` draws any size in any look: the standard size is the look's own back, the same object as before.

## Who does what

- **The owner offers**: Settings → People & teams → *Card and footer looks* has a second part, *Card sizes*, with each size drawn as the back of his own card, a switch, a default and how many people use it. The default cannot be switched off.
- **A person picks**: *Your card and footer* gets a *Card size* row under *Business card* when more than one size is offered, each drawn in their own look with their own facts. The pick saves at once (`users.card_size`) and the back redraws.
- **The office picks for a teammate** on that teammate's card. *Cards and footers for the team* draws each person's back in their own size.
- **A size switched off** moves nobody's row. `resolveSize` draws a pick that is no longer offered as the default.

## A setting of its own

The sizes on offer are one `app_settings` row, `card_sizes_v1`, apart from the looks' `card_looks_v1`. The mock-up said the same row. Two rows mean saving the looks never rewrites the sizes, so a tab left open on an older build cannot drop them.

## The rules, held by tests

`sizes.test.ts` (15): in every size and every look, the longest person the card is promised to hold (a name of 24 letters, a long title, an email of 36, both phones) and the shortest stay inside the safe margin; the photo sits inside the safe margin and its ring inside the cut; the words clear the ring; a larger size is larger than the standard card on both counts; nothing moves between looks; the setting parses back safely.

## A long line shrinks as far as on the standard card

A line that will not fit shrinks, but never under 55% of its own size. A larger size has a larger own size, so its floor was higher and a long email overran sooner than on the standard card. A row may now name the smallest it shrinks to (`KitRow.min`), and each larger size sets it to the standard card's floor. So a larger size holds every line the standard card holds. The standard layouts name no `min` and draw as they did.
