# v2.5767 — Customers: a row that breathes, and how fast they pay (2026-10-23)

The owner, with a screenshot of the Customers list: "I would like to show the average time to pay after bill… very tastefully and very subtly. One thing I notice is all of the rows are two lines, yet we scrunched together *1 project · 55 bids · 1 open job · owes $4,675 · payment · Oct 20 · notes* all on one line, maybe we can make this two lines. Come up with some mock-ups and then ask yourself is this the best we can do?"

Three placements were drawn (pays-in on the money line · under the rail's PAID figure · as a chip beside owes); the first was built.

- **Two lines** (`Customers.tsx`, the `customers-projects-bids-links` block): line one is what they have — projects, bids, open jobs (or job history), notes; line two is where the money stands — *owes $4,675*, the last touch (*payment · Oct 20*, *job · Sep 2*, *quiet since May 20*), and **pays in ~30d**. The second line is drawn only when it has something to say. On a phone (`index.css`) both lines left-align under the name.
- **Pays in** (`src/lib/customers/customerRowPaysIn.ts`, tested): `paysInWords` lowercases the Pipeline's `formatPaysIn` (*pays in ~30d*, *pays in 9–41d*, null with no history), `paysInTitle` says where the number comes from (*Typical days from a bill to its payment, from 3 payments in the last 12 months…*). The page reads `get_billed_customer_pay_speeds` once per list load, best effort, for the office roles only (`moneyHiddenByRls` hides it with the rest of the money), and `paySpeedSpread` per row — the same read and kernel the Pipeline's expected pay dates and the Bid Board's customer review use.
- Unchanged: every chip's door, the money rail, the sort and filters.

## Is this the best we can do?

1. **Two lines that mean two things**: what they have, where the money stands — the way the office reads a customer, not a bag of chips.
2. **The quietest fact in the quietest type**: pays-in is a muted caption on the money line, beside the last touch it belongs with, never a button.
3. **One figure, many surfaces**: the Pipeline, the Bid Board and now the list read the same spread, so a customer's pay speed never disagrees with itself.
4. **Say nothing rather than guess**: no history, no words.
5. **Cut**: a chip for pays-in (a fact is not a door), a company-average fallback (the rail already says what they paid), a sort by pay speed (AR already has *slowest*).
