# v2.7429 — CI gives the checks 60 minutes, so a slow run no longer drops a good pull request from the merge queue (2026-11-15)

`ci.yml`'s `checks` job had `timeout-minutes: 40`, set by v2.6333 when the job took 20 to 30 minutes. The job now sits right under that limit again. Taunya asked why a small Lien desk fix (#8284) had taken three hours to merge, and the limit was half of the answer.

- **Measured over the last 80 completed `ci.yml` runs on the night of 2026-11-15**: of the 63 that passed with the full suite, the job took a median of 29.7 minutes and a maximum of 39.5, with one in ten at 38.2 or more. The Test step took a median of 21.8 and a maximum of 29.0.
- **What it cost**: two merge-queue builds were cancelled at 40 minutes. #8284's was cut off with its tests still running. #8296's was cut off after its Test step had passed. The queue reads a cancelled `checks` as failed, removes the pull request and switches its auto-merge off, so the pull request waits out of line until someone arms it again, then builds from the start.
- **The change**: `timeout-minutes: 60`, and the comment above it now gives today's numbers. A stalled check still fails rather than holding the queue. The merge queue's own `check_response_timeout_minutes` is 240, so it never fires first.
- **What it does not change**: the suite's length, or the queue's order. GitHub can move one pull request to the front of the queue (`enqueuePullRequest` with `jump: true`), but a jump rebuilds every build in flight, so it stays a rare act by hand and nothing here wraps it. Shortening the Test step is separate work, and this limit will need another look if the job's median passes 45 minutes.
