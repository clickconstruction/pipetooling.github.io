# v2.8267 — A thread on a phone: the top stays while you type (punch list #348, the walk of 2026-11-21) (2026-11-21)

The walk of 2026-11-21 saw a person's thread window lose its header under the keyboard: the way back, the name and ⋯ left the top of the screen. v2.8232 wrote up why following the keyboard alone was not enough. This is the fix, on the owner's word the same day. Client only: no migration and no function. Card: [`to-dos/task-windows-phone-leftovers.md`](../../to-dos/task-windows-phone-leftovers.md).

## The cause, in two halves

1. **The window was pinned to the page** (`inset: 0`). An iPhone does not shrink the page for the keyboard. It shrinks the part that shows and slides it down to the field being typed in, so a window pinned to the page's top leaves the screen's top.
2. **Following the part that shows cut the reply box instead.** The keyboard leaves an iPhone 17 about 375 px. The whole header is 149, the lines kept a floor of 112 and an open reply box is about 230. The window cut what was over, and that was the foot of the box.

## What changed

All of it is behind the window's own phone flag, in [`src/lib/messages/threadWindowFrame.ts`](../../src/lib/messages/threadWindowFrame.ts):

- **`threadWindowPlace(phone, band)`**: on a phone the backdrop starts where the part that shows starts and is as tall (`visibleBand`), as the task windows (v2.7155) and `PhoneSheet` (v2.7188) do.
- **`threadRoom(phone, band)`** is the order for what gives way. Under 480 px showing (`THREAD_TIGHT_BAND_PX`), the room is tight:
  1. The reply box stays whole.
  2. The header's first row stays: the way back, the name, who has it, call and ⋯.
  3. The header's other rows and the flow strip wait: the numbers behind the arrow, the chips, the sentence and the jobs.
  4. The lines keep no floor and take what is left.
- **What waits is hidden, not taken out.** Each row wears `data-thread-tight-fold` and one rule in `index.css` hides it, so a row keeps what it holds. The rule skips a row that holds the field being typed in (`:not(:focus-within)`), so nothing can vanish under a person's own typing.
- **The lines and the box scroll as one** on a phone (`columnScrolls`). Where the box is taller than the room, on a small phone, its foot is reached by a scroll and is never cut.
- **A page pinched larger keeps the old pin** (`isPagePinched`). What shows is then a corner of the page under two fingers, and a window that followed it would shrink as a person read.
- On a phone the jobs and the flow strip each sit in a wrapper that draws nothing of its own (`display: contents`), so the rule has something to hide.

What a cut draws above the lines, the sort card, is left as it was. It already gives way to the box and scrolls by itself.

## Walked

**iOS Simulator** (iPhone 17, iOS 26.2, Safari), a ZZ Test person's thread, nothing typed and nothing sent:

- **Text, keyboard up**: the header's first row is on screen. Under it sit the sort card, a strip of the lines and the whole box, with **Send text…** above the keyboard.
- **Email, keyboard up**: the same, with **Send email…** above the keyboard.
- **Keyboard down**: every row of the header is back.

## What I would not claim

- **A small phone was walked in a browser's room, not in a simulator**, and no phone in a hand. Android and the app opened from a home screen were not walked.
- **A room of 480 px or more that is still too short** keeps every row and its floor, and scrolls. That is a home-screen app on a large phone with the keyboard up, writing an email. It was reasoned, not seen.
- **A small phone on its side** also reads as tight, keyboard or not. Its header keeps the one row there too.
- **The pinch rule** is held by a unit test. The simulator cannot pinch.

## Seen, and not fixed

At 320 px wide the header's first row is 37 px wider than its room. The name gets no width and ⋯ sits half off the right edge. It is the same before this change, keyboard or not: the who-has-it words, call and ⋯ take the row. Which of them gives way is a pick, so it is written on the card.
