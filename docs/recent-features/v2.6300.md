# v2.6300 — Line budgets: what each line should cost, the owner tunes it in place, and the Costs tab reads where we are (2026-11-02)

Punch list #234 PR 1 (`to-dos/superintendent-ahead-of-schedule/README.md`), built from the 2026-11-01 design spike. One migration (`20261102003938_fixture_line_budgets.sql`, five nullable columns on `jobs_ledger_fixtures`); no function.

- **The budget is born on the line.** `src/lib/jobs/lineBudgets.ts` is the kernel: a line's split (labor %, materials %, the margin as what is left), its source (◆ workbench · ≈ assumed · ✎ tuned), the company's default split per stage kind (Rough In, Top Out, Trim Set, any other line) at a burdened rate, `calcLineBudget` (hours = labor dollars ÷ the rate), `applyLineSplit` (labor moves and materials gives way), `sumLineBudgets` (the job's budget as the sum, earned at each line's own percent). Nothing is stored until the owner tunes a line, so a later change to the company split moves every line still assumed. The workbench source is read from the columns only; writing it from the bid's workbench at award is PR 1a.
- **On the row, not beside it.** The spike kept budgets in a store keyed on the grid's row id, and a reload lost a tuning while the line stayed — the form mints that id and the save hands back other ids. The five columns ride `FixtureRow` and the fixtures autosave writes them with the price; `JobFormModal` copies them in when it opens a job.
- **The quiet line and the sliders** (`LineBudgetQuietLine.tsx`): under each line item's name on Bill → ① Line Items, on a project (a line In order stands in for #238's kind until it lands): the source glyph, a labor | materials bar, the hours, labor dollars, materials dollars and the source word. The owner (`dev` or `master_technician`) taps it and the panel opens as a full-width row under the line: a Labor slider and a Materials slider with typed boxes, dollars and hours beside them, the read bar with the target margin as a dashed mark, the margin in red under target, *Saved · ✎ tuned by you* with **Back to** the split it opened on, or **Back to ≈ the company split**. Every drag saves through the grid's autosave. The office sees the same sliders read-only. One line open at a time; `useLineBudgets` holds only which.
- **Where we are on the Costs tab** (`JobCostsLineBudgets.tsx`, above the burn chart): spent of the whole budget with the job's percent done and *kept so far* or *spending N points ahead of the work*; a Labor bar (team + sub labor dollars against the labor budget, the clocked hours beside it) and a Materials bar (parts, cards, supply-house invoices), each with the job's percent as a dashed mark and red when spend runs ten points past it; then one row per line with the quiet line's reading, the line's own percent as a mark across its bar, and *no % of its own yet* where there is none — a line never borrows the job's percent. Spent per line waits on hours landing on a stage (PR 1b).
- **The badge is the press** (`StageKindBadgePress` in `StageKindControls.tsx`): tap the number or the diamond and the line flips between In order and Any time, the kind in very small words under it; the two-button strip under each line is gone, so every line is one row shorter. A discount row keeps its plain badge; a line on an invoice keeps the press disabled. The Multiple Segment Generator keeps its own Stage column switch.
- **Small cents**: `MoneyDecimalAmountInput` gains `smallCents` — one real `<input>` with the formatted reading drawn as an overlay at rest (the way v2.6110's Other charges box does it), lifting to plain digits on focus; the Unit price boxes use it and the Job Total line uses `MoneyWithSmallCents`.
- Tests: `lineBudgets.test.ts` (the kernel), `JobFormFixturesSection.render.test.tsx` and `JobFormFixturesSectionStagePlan.render.test.tsx` (the badge press, the quiet line and the panel row), `JobCostsLineBudgets.render.test.tsx` (the card's gate, the pace rows, the per-line rows). Guides: *split a job into stages and bill stage by stage* (the badge press), *see what a line should cost and tune it* (new), *move between a job's tabs* and *read the cost and value timeline on a job* (Where we are).
