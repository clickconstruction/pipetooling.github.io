# v2.7988 — Document builder: a place in the database for builders, sheets, links and sends (2026-11-19)

Punch list #404: step 2, and the ground under steps 3 to 5. Two migrations and nothing else: `20261120032253_document_builders.sql` and `20261120034225_document_builder_sheets.sql`. No client change and no edge function. The screens read them in the PRs after the push.

## Why

Step 1 (v2.7980) put the Sub NDA's flow in the Document builder tab, on contents that are a file in the client. The card's next steps need a builder's contents to be data: a dev types on them, publishes a numbered revision, and marks who read it. The second builder, a job's insurance requirements, needs the same, and then a sheet for each job, a link to it and a send.

## Why four tables in one PR

The card gives step 2 a table and each of steps 3, 4 and 5 a table. Each is small, and they depend on each other in a line: a sheet hangs off a builder, and a link and a send point at a sheet. A migration here merges alone and is pushed before any client reads it, so four migrations would be four rounds of merge and push before the first screen. They are laid as one ground instead. Each step's screen still ships as its own PR.

## What landed

- **`document_builders`**: one row a builder, keyed by its name in the client's registry. Its kind is `walked` or `filled`. It holds the draft being set up, the published copy everyone outside sees, that copy's revision with who published it and when, and the read mark.
- **A revision number names one set of words.** A trigger keeps three rules. A builder starts as a draft on revision 0. A revision goes up one at a time and only with its published copy. A published copy changes only with its revision.
- **The read mark** is a revision, a typed name and a day, whole or absent, and only the published revision can be marked. A builder is read when the mark's revision is the published one, so a new revision is unread again and nothing has to clear the mark.
- **`document_builder_sheets`**: a job's sheet of a filled builder, one a builder and a job. The job's own part as a draft and as published, the whole page as it read when published, its revision, and the usual's revision it went out on.
- **A job's sheet goes out only on the published usual.** The trigger refuses a publish before the usual is published, and a publish that names any usual revision but the published one. So a job's sheet never carries a draft of the usual to a sub.
- **`document_builder_links`**: a link to a published sheet or builder, for someone with no page of their own. Only the SHA-256 of the code is kept. One is live a sheet and one a builder, so a new link ends the old one.
- **`document_builder_sends`**: a sheet to a trade partner or a builder to a customer who is the GC on our jobs, with who it went to, the job it is about, the revision that went, and the day and the revision they opened. One live send of one thing to one party.
- **Who did it is the caller**: who made a builder or a sheet, published it, marked it and changed it last.
- **A dev alone** reads all four, by row security. A dev writes a builder and a job's sheet. The edge function alone writes a link and a send, and a dev may only take a send off a page, once. Nobody signed out holds a grant.
- **The migrations write no words.** A builder's first draft is loaded by a press in the app, which the next PR adds.

## How a link's code can be shown again with only its hash kept

The card says to keep only the token's hash, and its drawing shows the link on the page with **Copy the link**, any day. Both hold. The row keeps a random `nonce` beside the hash. The edge function makes the code from the row's id, the nonce and a secret only the function holds, so it can make the same code again for a dev who asks, or for a trade's own page. The database alone cannot: the secret is never in it.

## What it does not do

- No screen reads any of it yet, and step 1's flow still runs on the made-up paper in the client.
- A builder keeps its current published copy, not every past one. Nothing in the card reads an old revision's words: a send keeps the revision number a person opened, and a certificate keeps the asks it was checked against.
- A publish that races another is the client's to guard, by updating where the revision is still the one it read.
- It does not record a certificate against a job. That is step 6, which adds columns to `person_contract_documents` and waits on the owner's call.

## Checks

- Two beds, in `scripts/sql-beds.txt`, so CI runs both over the whole schema. `document-builders` plays a dev through a first draft, a saved draft, two publishes, the read mark put on and taken off, and each refusal by its words. `document-builder-sheets` plays a dev through a job's sheet, the service role through a link and a send, and a dev taking a send off a page. Both then play each of the other eight roles, a dev in training, a dev's twin and a dev who left, and someone signed out.
- Docker was not running on the Mac that wrote this, so the beds did not run there. Both migrations were applied twice and both scenarios run on a throwaway local Postgres 15 with stub tables and the four fence functions' real bodies: 95 and 112 checks passed. That proves the SQL's logic. Its fit to the whole schema on Postgres 17 is CI's beds and the dry run at the push.
