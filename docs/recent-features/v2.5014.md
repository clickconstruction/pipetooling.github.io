# v2.5014 — People → Review: overhead rates come from the same scan as People → Overhead (2026-10-09)

Client only. No migration, no edge function. This builds the owner's call of 2026-10-09 on *Review: adopt the shared overhead pool read?*. The owner chose to adopt it and take the new reads. It closes step 4 of the Review map ([`PEOPLE_REVIEW_TAB_ARCHITECTURE.md`](../PEOPLE_REVIEW_TAB_ARCHITECTURE.md)).

## The change

- Since v2.3974 the People → Review tab ran its own copy of the 90-day overhead scan, `loadReviewOverheadRates.ts`, about 200 lines. The shared `loadOverheadPoolSnapshot` serves People → Overhead, the Dashboard, the Bridge and the job day ledger.
- The two agreed to the cent. That was the parity check of 2026-09-27, over every session state, both window edges, an empty window and more than a page of sessions. They differed only in what two bad reads do.
- `loadReviewOverheadRates` now runs `loadOverheadPoolSnapshotInputs()` + `loadOverheadPoolSnapshot()`. A new `reviewOverheadRatesFromSnapshot` maps the result onto the twelve figures Review shows. The type, the empty value and the hook (`useReviewOverheadRates`) are unchanged.
- The hook's silent all-null reset on a failed read stays.

## The two bad reads, before and after

1. **The person-link read** (`people` → `account_user_id`, which finds a login's wage by the person's id).
   - Before: when it failed, Review's scan threw and every rate went blank.
   - After: the shared scan prices by name. Only a person whose login name differs from their pay row loses their wage then.
   - On prod today 26 people are linked to a login, and one of them clocks in under a name that differs from their pay row. The link reads fine for a dev, so that person is priced through the link, before and after.
2. **The pay config read** (`people_pay_config`).
   - Before: Review read it unranged, so it stopped at PostgREST's 1,000 rows.
   - After: the shared scan pages it.
   - On prod today it is 27 rows, so no row falls past the page.

So neither bad read bites on prod today. Review's twelve figures read the same before and after. They were read on 2026-10-09 from the tab's *Overhead rate decomposition* on the dev server against prod.
