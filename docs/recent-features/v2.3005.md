# v2.3005 — Test-gap audit, pass 21: the 90-day overhead scan gets its first tests (2026-09-07)

Follow-up to v2.3003. Tests only; no kernel, client, migration or edge-function change.

## Why this one

`overheadPoolSnapshot.ts` (326 lines) is the ONE 90-day overhead scan (v2.2676): the People → Overhead tab, the Dashboard's Overhead card, the Bridge and the job day ledger all compute from it. The kernels it composes (daily labor, hygiene, trailing averages, rate methods, pool trend, lens series) each had a suite; the composition — the window, the queries it sends, the parts hand-off, the error semantics, and whether the per-day maps and averages line up — did not.

## What the tests found

Nothing wrong. Contracts now pinned that were only implied:

- The window is 90 company-calendar days ending today in Chicago time, not the browser's date. All three session reads are paged (`range`) and ordered by id, and carry the assignee-name join.
- The pool read takes office-job sessions plus any bid session (`or`); the field read takes real jobs only, excluding the office job; the unassigned-salary read takes salary-schedule time with neither a job nor a bid. Without an office job configured, the pool is bid sessions only, the field read has no exclusion, and the parts loader is not called.
- Invoices are fetched a day wide on both sides and re-bucketed into their Chicago day, so an invoice sent at 11:30 PM Chicago on the 6th counts on the 6th, and Stripe test-mode invoices are excluded.
- A failed unassigned-salary read fails soft: the snapshot still comes back with the error attached and that hygiene indicator hidden. A failed core read throws. A cancelled load returns null before the invoice read.
- In a tiny scenario the numbers reconcile by hand: office 4 h × $40 and bid 2 h × $40 plus $50 of office parts give a $290 pool; field 8 h × $30 plus 2 h with no wage give $240 and 10 h; averages, per-$100-revenue and all three rate methods follow.
