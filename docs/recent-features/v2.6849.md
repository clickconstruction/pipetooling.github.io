# v2.6849 — Lien desk: a property says what a lien can still cover, what it cannot, and the whole amount owed (2026-11-10)

Taunya, 2026-11-10, on three billed jobs at one property: *these 3 tickets aren't showing up on the lien desk. Total in the lien desk is $13,439 but total owed on the project is $18,046.50.* The three were on the Deadlines tab, greyed under the property's address (v2.4526), each reading *still owed*. Their $4,607.50 was in no total a reader could see: a group's `total` counts only its jobs with a date ahead, by design, and the property's heading carried the address alone. The owner then asked for the fewest presses to put such money back when the app is the one that is wrong, *such as it actually being worked on more recently*.

## What changed

- **`lienPropertyTally(p)`** in `src/lib/jobs/lienCalendarBuckets.ts`: a property's rows as two piles and their sum. `open` is its jobs with a date ahead, `gone` its jobs whose window closed unsent, `owed` both. It returns null when nothing at the property closed, since the rows already add up to the group's total there. `words` is the desk's line, `short` the phone's, `title` the hover.
- **`lienGroupRows`**: a label row now carries `tally`. The group's `jobs`, `total`, word and *Draft the N* are unchanged, and so are Overdue's counts.
- **`LienDeskCalendarTab`**: `PropertyLabel` draws the tally at the right end of the heading, ending where the row amounts end; on a phone it sits under the address. The address keeps its own `lien-cal-property` element.
- **Worked later? ›** (`LIEN_WORKED_LATER`, `lienWorkedLaterLabel`): a press on every lien-gone row, in the grey wash above the day the window closed, and on the phone row beside its verdict. It calls the tab's existing `onOpenLastWork`, which opens the Lien window with the last day's line editing (v2.4735). Office roles only, as that door already is. The phone board now receives `onOpenLastWork`.
- No new write. The day is still set through `LienLastWorkDayLine` and its confirm window, with the reason asked there.

## Why the door is the last day of work

Every window is counted from the last day of work. A job with no clock hours takes its creation day, which is often earlier than the work. On the property that raised this, one of the three jobs read *from the job's creation*. A later day reopens the windows counted from it, so that is the mistake a press can undo. A notice that was mailed on time and never recorded has its own door already, **Already sent — record it…** in the Lien window the row opens.
