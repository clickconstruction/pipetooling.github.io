# v2.7364 — Tasks: give someone a task with a painted map (2026-11-15)

Punch list #345, *A map on a task*, step 7. Taunya asked for it on 2026-11-15: "I want to add paint a map to checklist items, I want someone to be able to assign a task with a map." Her yes to the design came the same day, every call at the card's pick. The card that shows the map went in first (v2.7363), so the person given the task already sees what this sets.

## What changed

- **A new press in the Where row: Map.** It sits beside the place and the photo of the spot, in the New task window and in Checklist → Manage → Edit. A task with no map takes the presses it took before.
- **The press opens *A map for this task*:** the painted maps the giver can open, each with its length, its places and where it hangs. The newest come first. With a place on the task, the nearest come first, each with how near it comes. Past six maps a box finds one by name.
- **One press puts the map on the task.** It then shows under the row: its name lit, a picture of its line, what it holds, and *Whoever gets the task can open this map.*
- **A press on the name opens the map. ✕ takes it off the task** and leaves the map where it is.
- **The sentence in the bar says so:** *Marcus gets it today, with a map.*
- **A map needs no place.** A trench is a line, not a dot.

## A place and a map together

The task has one map, and it gets there one of two ways.

- **With the place:** a place chip that has a way in, or **＋ Add the way in** on *Where is it?* (v2.7342). That map is the way to that place. Take the place off, or pick a place with no way in, and the map goes too.
- **By itself:** the **Map** press. That map is the giver's own pick. It stays when the place is set, moved or taken off.

A chip wears the paint's stroke only for a way in. A place with its own way in replaces a map picked by itself, since the giver just chose that place and its line.

## How it is built

- `taskMapPick.ts`, pure (+21 tests): `taskMapAfterPlace` is the rule above, `taskPlaceForRow` is what the chips see, `mapsForTask` orders the offers, `taskMapColumn` and `taskMapChanged` are the save, `sentenceWithMap` is the bar.
- `TaskMapField.tsx`: `TaskMapPress` with its sheet, and `TaskMapSet`, the strip under the row. Nothing is read until the press. The sheet reads the maps with `loadPaintedMapsEvery` and their homes with `loadPaintedMapsForSearch`; the homes failing costs their words and never the list.
- `TaskFormRows` takes `map` and `onMap` and keeps the two in step, so a window that hands in neither is the window it was. The roadmap's Add task is that window until its own PR.
- The New task window writes `painted_map_id` when the task has a place or a map. Manage's Edit reads the map with `loadTaskMapAlone` and writes it only when it changed.
- The database already holds the rule that matters: a task may point only at a map its writer can open (`task_painted_map_guard`, v2.7340). The sheet lists only such maps, so the guard is the backstop and not the message.

## Is this the best we can do

- **Pick, do not paint, in this PR.** *Paint a new one* is the next step. Until it lands a map is painted from a job's address or the search, then picked here. The empty list says so.
- **Every map the giver can open is offered,** not only the near ones. Step 5's list stops at a mile because a way in is near its place. A map by itself has no place to be near.
- **A map the editor cannot read keeps its chip.** Manage's Edit does not take a map off because its read failed. Only ✕ does.
