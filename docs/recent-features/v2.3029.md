# v2.3029 — Test-gap audit, pass 39: Apply Schedule % gets its first tests (2026-09-07)

Follow-up to v2.3028. Tests only; no kernel, client, migration or edge-function change.

## Why this one

`applyScheduleProportionsToClockSession.ts` (155 lines) is what runs when a person or a leader presses **Apply Schedule %** on a closed clock session — in the My Time day editor and on the People → Hours clock strip: it splits the session across the day's Dispatch-scheduled jobs in proportion to their scheduled time and assigns each segment. The proportion planner and the cluster persist have their own suites; the wiring between them did not.

## What the tests found

Nothing wrong. Contracts now pinned that were only implied:

- Three benign refusals come back as warnings with nothing written: an unsaved draft row, a still-open session, and a day with nothing viable to split against (no picks, or none with scheduled time).
- One scheduled job assigns the whole session without a split. Several jobs cut the session in schedule order by scheduled share (60 vs 120 minutes → one third, two thirds), hand the segments to the own-session RPC set when editing oneself and the leader set otherwise, then assign each new segment its job.
- Segment notes are the session's own note when it has one, else "HCP · name" for the job, else "Scheduled work".
- A persist that returns too few ids, a persist that throws, and a failed assign each come back as errors carrying their message, with "Could not apply schedule split" for anything unnamed.

## What shipped

- **`applyScheduleProportionsToClockSession.test.ts`** (6) — the cluster persist as a spy, the own and leader RPC sets as hoisted spies, a recording Supabase stand-in for the assigns. Covers the three warnings, the single-job assign and its failure, the two-job split hand-off with exact payloads, boundaries and RPC set, the leader path with note carry-over, the "Scheduled work" fallback, and the three error paths.
