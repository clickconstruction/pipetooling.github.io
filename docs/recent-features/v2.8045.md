# v2.8045 — Checklist maps: work is safety orange (2026-11-20)

The owner walked the app on an iPhone on 2026-11-20 and circled a blue dot on Checklist → Review, **Where the open tasks are**: *On the checklist page the icons for work should not be dark blue but safety orange like the app icons colors*. Client only: no migration and no function. It follows v2.8032, which put the person's own map on Google.

## What a person sees

- **Review, Where the open tasks are.** Every dot is safety orange with a white ring. One person's dot wears their initials, the mark beside their name on the chips above the map and in the bar a pressed dot opens. A place several people share wears its count of tasks, as it did.
- **Today and Task Mode, Where my tasks are.** The numbered dots are the same orange with the same ring. Red still means a task there is late.
- The chips above the Review map lose their coloured spot and gain the person's mark.

## The call: one colour for work, and who is told another way

The map drew a colour a person (`TASK_PLACE_PERSON_COLORS`, eight of them, the first the blue in the owner's picture) and navy where people shared a place. The owner's sentence can be read two ways.

1. **Every work dot is orange.** Who is told by something other than colour.
2. **Orange is the first person's colour and the shared colour, and the other seven stay.** A map with four people on it would still show purple, green and red dots, and the palette's own amber sat a shade from the orange.

The first was built. The sentence says the icons for work are orange, and the second keeps that true only for whoever is first in the list. What the colours did, telling one person's dots from another's, is done by the initials on the dot: letters are a person and a number is a crowd. A press on a name still shows that person alone.

To go the other way, the palette is in `taskPlace.ts` as v2.8032 left it.

## The shade

`TASK_PLACE_WORK_COLOR` is `ORANGE_UNDER_WORDS`, `#c75000`: the Clock In bar's fill, safety orange's deep shade, where white words read 4.59:1 (v2.6186, *the owner likes white on orange*). Every dot on these two maps carries words, a number or initials, so the dot is orange under words and takes that rule. Bright `#FF6600` under the same white words read 2.94:1.

A deep orange on a photograph of brown ground needs an edge. Every work dot wears a white ring (`TASK_PLACE_RING_COLOR`), 3 px on both canvases through the pin's own `ringColor`. The ring also holds the dot apart from an orange main road on the OpenStreetMap map and from Google's yellow blocks.

## Older tasks on the person's map

v2.7536 drew an older task that is not late in amber `#b45309`, *since red is for late*. That amber is a shade from `#c75000`: two colours nobody could tell apart would have claimed to mean two things. `taskPlaceDotColor` now knows two colours, red for late and orange for every other open task. The kernel still carries `old` on a dot, for a screen that wants it.

## How it is built

- `src/lib/tasks/taskPlace.ts`: `TASK_PLACE_WORK_COLOR`, `TASK_PLACE_LATE_COLOR`, `TASK_PLACE_RING_COLOR`, `taskPlaceDotColor` and `taskPlaceTeamDotLabel`. `TASK_PLACE_PERSON_COLORS`, `taskPlacePersonColor` and `TASK_PLACE_SHARED_COLOR` are gone.
- `TaskPlacesTeamMapCard.tsx`: the dots, the chips' marks and the bar's marks. Initials come from `initialsFor`, the board's own, so a dot reads as the avatar beside the person's name under the map.
- `TaskPlacesMapCard.tsx`: the dots and the numbers in the bar take `taskPlaceDotColor`. The person's own dot is as it was, dark with a white ring.
- The hex is written nowhere new. `bidStyles.test.ts` holds `#c75000` to the stylesheet and `orangeUnderWords.ts`.
