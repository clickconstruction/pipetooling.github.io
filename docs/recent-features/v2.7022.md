# v2.7022 — Messages: a developer can take back a conversation they tagged onto the wrong job (2026-11-12)

The dev seat can tag a conversation out of any login's box onto a job (`plan_mail_tag` → `apply_mail_tag`). It could not take one back. Only the mailbox's owner sees **Untag**, in Messages → My email, and the seat's door on `gmail-mine` said so in words: only a tag comes through, and the seat never untags. So the seat could not undo its own work: a conversation it tagged onto the wrong job stayed there until that person pressed Untag. The owner, 2026-11-12: *Untag is a useful feature … that we should still offer.* This is that feature: the seat can undo a tag it made.

## The two verbs (server 0.79.0)

`plan_mail_untag(untag)` → `apply_mail_untag(untag, plan_hash)`, with `untag = { account, thread_id }`: the box and the conversation an `apply_mail_tag` named. They run `gmail-mine`'s own `untag` as that login through the same seat door (`as`, `plan`), so what comes off is what the person's press takes off:

- that person's lines of the conversation go, each with its copied files and its raw copy;
- a line the office keeps something on stays, with its files (a promise, a highlight, or what we owe done by it);
- a document a legal matter made from a line keeps its own copy;
- a line the bids or team box wrote stays in Messages, and comes off the job only when the tag tied it;
- the grey line and the `email_tag_threads` row go.

The mail itself is never touched: it stays in the person's Gmail. Since v2.7035 a tag puts no label on and an untag reads nothing from Gmail, so nothing there changes at all.

## The gate: only a tag the seat made

`gmail-mine` holds it, so a dev who calls the function directly meets the same refusal. For the box and the conversation it reads the ok `apply_mail_tag` rows of `dev_mcp_calls` (`seatTagsOf`) and takes the untag only when one of them put the conversation on the job it is on now (`seatMayUntag`). Refused, with words:

- a conversation with no tag row;
- one the app placed from the person's Sent by itself;
- one whose row is on a job no such call named. That is a tag the person pressed, or one the person has since moved to another job, which made it theirs.

A wider gate was not built. A person's own tag is their own statement about their own mail; taking it off for them is a different act from undoing the seat's own work, and nobody asked for it.

The rest is as on the tag: a `dev`'s own key with the seat header, never a training account, a sample, a twin or the walk tester, never through `view_as`. One difference: an archived login's box is taken, since an untag only takes off, so a tag the seat made in the box of a teammate who has left since can still be undone.

## The plan writes nothing

The live untag marks the row `untagging_at` first, so the follow passes it by. The plan does not mark, delete or untie. It reads back (`untagPlanPreview` → `MyMailUntagPlanOk`): the lines that go, the lines held with why each, the boxes' lines that come off the job, the copied files, the documents a matter keeps, whether the grey line stands, and the sweep below. *What we owe* holds a line through a foreign key the delete discovers; the plan reads `contact_owed` so it can say so beforehand.

The hash commits to the box, the conversation, the job's key and the id of every line, tie and contact the untag would touch (`_shared/devMcpMailUntag.ts`). Apply plans again, compares, asks the log whether that plan was applied already, and only then untags. A mail that landed since, or something the office kept on a line since, is another plan.

## What an untag used to leave behind, and what the seat's untag does about it

The person's untag takes the lines and leaves two things that still show who was written to:

- **The tie that put the sender on the job's people** (`contact_jobs`). The tag makes it for each person it writes a line on, and `tie_message` makes it for each line it ties. After the untag the person still stands on the job with no line there.
- **The contact the tag made for a sender nobody knew** (kind `unknown`, with its address). After the untag it is still listed in Messages, under *Who is this?*, with a name and an address and no line.

The seat's untag clears both, and only where the tag made them (`planUntagSweep`). Nothing on a tag's row records what it made, so the rule reads the database's own stamps, for the contacts of the lines that go or come off the job:

- a tie onto the tag's job goes when it was made at or after the tag (60 seconds of slack between the function's clock and the database's), it is not the person's star, and the person keeps no line on that job;
- a contact goes when it is still of kind `unknown`, was made at or after the tag, keeps no line anywhere and no tie but the ones that go. The database refuses a contact a line stands on, so a wrong guess cannot take a thread with it.

Everything else stays and is **counted in the plan and in the answer**: a tie made before the tag or starred (`tiesLeft`), and a contact with no line that was known before the tag or named since (`contactsLeftEmpty`). A second tag moves the row's `tagged_at` forward, so after one the rule takes less, never more.

Named plainly as left, and said in the plan's `left` line:

- a held line stays readable on the job with its files, until what the office keeps on it is cleared and the untag is planned again;
- a line the boxes wrote stays in its person's thread, off the job;
- each thread's state (open or done, who has it) stays as it is;
- a notification already sent is not recalled;
- the job's activity held nothing of the tag but these lines, so nothing else is there to take.

A person's own untag is unchanged. It clears no tie and no contact. That is a real gap for an ordinary untag too, left for its own change.

## Afterwards

- The row is gone, so the follow reads nothing.
- The seat's untag always leaves the tombstone (`email_tag_threads_off`), so the app's own Sent read never puts the conversation back by itself. A tag by hand lifts it.
- One target a conversation: with the job tag gone, `plan_mail_tag` with `person` is taken.

## A row tagged before v2.7020

Until v2.7020 a seat tag stamped the person on the row and the dev on each line it tied, so the untag did not know those lines as the tag's own. The seat's untag reads the dev off the call log (`seatTagRecord`) and unties them. It does not need the one-time correction of the old rows proposed in v2.7020's fragment.
