# v2.4245 — Procurement log: the printed sheet names its columns on every stage, and on every page in Chrome (2026-09-30)

Grace, with a photo of page 2 of B375's log printed from Safari: "when printed to PDF, on additional pages, it is unclear what the dates are, how could I have the header appear on every page?" The sheet was one table with one `<thead>`. Chromium and Firefox repeat a table's header on a page break; Safari repeats none (WebKit bug 17205), and it ignores `@page { size: landscape }`, so her page 2 was a portrait run of dashes under no labels.

- [`procurementLog.ts`](../../src/lib/submittals/procurementLog.ts) → `buildProcurementUpdateHtml`: **each stage is its own table** (`<table class="stage">`) whose `<thead>` is the stage's band (*Trim Set · needed on site Nov 17 · 8 items*) and the column labels. So the labels stand over every stage in every browser, and a browser that repeats headers prints the band and the labels at the top of every page a stage runs onto.
- For Safari: every stage after the first carries `class="keep"` (`break-inside: avoid`), so a stage that fits on a page moves there whole, with its labels. The first stage starts under the letter's opening and may run on — keeping it whole would leave page 1 as the letterhead and the asks alone. Rows and the signature lines do not split across a page either.
- The columns are fixed (`SHEET_COLUMNS`: Item 32 · Submittal 13 · Ordered 13 · Lead time 7 · Expected on site 12 · Schedule 11 · Notes 12, one `<colgroup>` per table, `table-layout: fixed`) so the stage tables line up with each other, and the date cells wrap instead of holding one line — which is also what keeps a portrait print in its columns.
- An empty log prints one table with the labels and *No items on the log.*
- Guide *build a submittal package* → the sheet paragraph says where the column names stand and to pick Landscape.

Read as PDFs from headless Chromium, 38 sample rows over four stages: landscape (4 pages) and portrait with the `@page` size removed, the way Safari lays it out — every page opens on a stage band and the labels; *Rough In* repeats both on its second page; the 21-row *Trim Set* starts a fresh page and repeats them on the next.

Left as it is: in Safari a stage longer than one page still runs onto a page with no labels — nothing in CSS makes WebKit repeat them, and paginating by hand would mean guessing the paper and the row heights. Not seen: a real print from Safari — the `break-inside` hold there is expected, not looked at. Keeping a stage whole can leave white space at the foot of the page before it.
