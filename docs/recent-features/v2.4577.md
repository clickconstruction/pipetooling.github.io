# v2.4577 — Final demand letter: a cleaner page, and exhibits that agree with it (2026-10-05)

Client only; no migration, no edge function. The owner's ask, on a letter to a GC covering four invoices: *"I don't think this is a very beautiful letter, but it is accurate. What can we do to make it more visually attractive?"* Reading that packet also turned up five places where the letter and its own exhibits disagreed.

## The page

[`demandLetter.ts`](../../src/lib/jobsDocuments/demandLetter.ts). The wording of every paragraph is unchanged; the blocks around it are new.

- **Model.** `DemandLetterBlock` drops `meta` and `reLine` and gains `addressBlock` (`recipientLines`: the addressee as an envelope reads it, the date beside it), `subject` (a kicker, `demandSubject`'s two lines, and the old one-line `re` for the plain-text letter), `amountBox` (balance due, pay in full by) and `enclosures` (one item per exhibit). `listItem` takes `n` (the remedies are numbered) and `lead` (a notice's day, drawn as its own column) and `heading` takes `keepMm` (the Demand section starts a new page rather than split). Notice history moved under the demand, above the signature.
- **Statement.** `statementTable` is one row per invoice: Invoice, For, Sent, Due, Balance. When anything was paid it adds Billed and Paid and moves the sent day under the invoice number. A bill of several lines names each line in its row, the charge at the right of the For column. `statementRows` stays for the plain-text letter and the window's read-only *What the letter claims*.
- **Renderers.** `buildDemandLetterEmailHtml` and `buildDemandLetterPdfBlob` draw the same blocks: a heavy letterhead rule, small spaced labels, the boxed amount, the table, room for ink over the signer's name. The PDF writes a hyphen for U+2212, which the built-in fonts do not have. The window's preview is now the HTML renderer's output (`dangerouslySetInnerHTML` of escaped text), so it cannot drift from the paper.

- **No dashes on the page.** The owner, on the first draft: *"remove the '--' across the bill, use other visuals."* Where the draft joined two things with a dash, the page now uses position: the subject is two lines, a notice's day and an exhibit's label are columns (`enclosureEntries`), a line's charge is right-aligned. A collection call reads *Collection call: no answer*. The plain-text letter keeps its dashes, having no columns.

## The exhibits

- **Labels in order.** `exhibitLabels` in [`demandLetterPacket.ts`](../../src/lib/jobsDocuments/demandLetterPacket.ts): one invoice is A, several are A-1 … A-n, and the agreement and the delivery record take the next letters. Before, every invoice was stamped *Exhibit A* and a packet with no agreement went A, C. `DemandExhibit` carries `kind`; `exhibitKind` reads a letter recorded before this by its old letter, so reprints of recorded letters are unchanged. `exhibitsSentence` says *The invoices are enclosed as Exhibits A-1 to A-4*.
- **One number, one due day.** `exhibitInvoiceDocument` gives the invoice exhibit the statement row's invoice number and due day. The app's rendering of a Stripe-hosted bill knew only its sequence (*#2*) and fell back to the send day when no due day was recorded, under a letter citing Stripe's number and due day.
- **Order.** `demandableInvoices` sorts by the day the bill went out, then its sequence.

## Every invoice PDF

[`physicalInvoicePdf.ts`](../../src/lib/physicalInvoicePdf.ts): the unit price column moved 10 mm right (*1$15,200.00* was the quantity touching it). The page stamp and the ClickTooling line were drawn at 287 mm and 280 mm on a 279.4 mm letter sheet; both now sit 10 mm above the bottom edge, the stamp at the right and reading *Page 1 of 1* in place of *-- 1 of 1 --*.

## Not changed

The exhibit's *Service date* is the job's last work day, which can fall after a bill's due day. It is a fact about the job and was left alone.
