# v2.8357 — Working hours: a dev or the controller sets the usual workday (2026-11-23)

Punch list #429, the last lock of the working hours train. The card's rule is that a person's standing hours are a dev's or the controller's to set. Working hours had one door from the start (`set_working_hours`). The usual day of the workday schedule was still open to the person themself and to anyone with payroll access, and it is the other standing week a clock reads.

## What changed

- **The database.** `20261123093000_working_hours_schedule_lock.sql` narrows the insert and the update policies on `salary_work_schedule_templates` to `is_dev() OR is_controller()`, with `ALTER POLICY`, so neither policy is ever absent. Reading the row, deleting it and every policy on `salary_work_schedule_day_overrides` stay as they were. [The migration's page](../migrations/20261123093000_working_hours_schedule_lock.md) says why each stays.
- **The Workday schedule window** (`SalaryWorkScheduleSettings.tsx`, mounted in Settings, on the person's sheet, on the Pay lens, on Employment and in the bulk window). The usual day's fields sit in one `fieldset`, off for anyone but a dev or the controller, under a line that says who sets it. The custom schedule for one date sits outside it and works for everyone it worked for. The one Save press wrote the usual day and then the date; it now writes the usual day only for a dev or the controller, and reads **Save this date** for everyone else.
- **Hire** (`hireWrites.ts`, `HirePersonModal.tsx`). `HireCaps` gains `canSetSchedule`. The workday step and the start field are a dev's and the controller's. For another hirer a salaried hire gets no workday row, and the form says *A dev or the controller sets their hours after. Until then they clock in and out.*
- **One rule, one name.** `canSetStandingSchedule(role)` in `workingHours.ts` is the client's reading of it.

## Why the doors stayed

The card drew this as hiding the Pay lens's **Workday…** and the sheet's **Workday schedule…** from everyone else. Those presses also open the custom schedule for one date, which pay staff still use. So the presses stay and the window holds the rule.

## A salaried hire by an owner

Before this, an owner's hire of a salaried person saved an 8:00 workday under the owner's own access. Now it saves none. The person has no schedule row, so the app shows them **Clock in** until a dev or the controller sets their hours or their usual day.

## Order

The client first. An older client's Save in the window writes the usual day before the one date, so for anyone but a dev or the controller it would stop at the usual day with the database's reason and the date would not be saved.
