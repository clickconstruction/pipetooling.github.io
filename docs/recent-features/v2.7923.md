# v2.7923 — Permit news on the Bid Board is your own, and only when it is true of the bid (2026-11-19)

Punch list #401, PR 1 of the train in [`to-dos/permit-news-on-the-bids/`](../../to-dos/permit-news-on-the-bids/README.md). No migration.

## Why

An estimator asked for the strip to move off the top of the Bid Board. The live lines on 2026-11-19 said the place was the smaller problem. The strip listed every person's bids, so she held one of its ten lines. Of the eight "another name" lines, four were about a different suite, one was an older job and one was site work. One sat on an electrical bid. Nobody had answered a line in nine days, because the only answer was Mark lost…, and on five of the seven lines that offered it, it would have been false.

The owner chose to move the news to Followup (PRs 2 to 4). These rules come first because they are true wherever the news is drawn.

## What changed

- **Another suite** (`src/lib/research/bidSite.ts`). `unitsOf(line)` reads the suite a line names: a bid's *Suite 255*, *#4* or *Unit A*, a feed's *UNIT 143* or *STE 113*, and San Antonio's *Unit No: 130* in the project line. *N/A*, a building and Austin's whole floor (*UNIT FL17*) name none. `onAnotherUnit` is true only when both sides name a suite and they share none; *214* and *214B* are one door, and a run (*100-110*) holds the numbers inside it. `readBidAddress` puts the bid's suites on the site, and `permitsOnSite` leaves a permit on another suite out. Every reader of that function follows: the board's news, **This site**, the GC's panel and the name finder. `permitsOnOtherUnits` is what was left out, and **This site** says how many (`otherSuiteWords`).
- **There before the bid** (`src/lib/research/bidPermitNews.ts`). A plumbing permit under another name dated before the bid's `created_at` gives no line. For `BEFORE_BID_DAYS` (183) before the bid it leaves the grey chip *Plumbing permit here since Jul 11* (`beforeBidChip`), unless a building permit on the address is news, whose chip the row then carries. Older than that it gives nothing. A bid with no start day, or a permit with no day, keeps its line.
- **The bid's trade.** `bidPermitNews` takes the Plumbing type's id (`plumbingServiceTypeIdOf`, passed by `Bids.tsx`). A bid of another trade reads no plumbing permit. Its building permit news stands. Null leaves the trade unchecked, which is the read before the trades load.
- **The reader's own** (`BidBoardPermitNews.tsx`). `newsForReader` splits the lines by the board's own `isMyBid`. The strip lists and counts the reader's lines. The rest fold behind *Permit news on other people's bids*, with **See them ›**. A reader with none of their own sees no strip, save the office (`othersAlone`, any `canResearchPermits` role but estimator), who keep that one line. The chips on the rows are unchanged: every reader sees every row's chip.

## Checked live, read only (2026-11-19)

The kernel run in the dev server's page over the live reads, as the dev login. Nothing was pressed.

- 296 undecided bids, 30 with a suite in the address. Twelve lines before this change.
- The suite rule alone leaves nine: three of the eight "another name" lines are on another suite.
- All three rules leave six: two "another name" lines, three building permits issued and one application. One bid carries the grey chip. Mark lost… is offered on two lines, down from seven.
- On the Plumbing board the five lines belong to two people, three and two. The estimator who asked has none.
- The strip for an office reader with no bid of their own is one line: 42 px at a desk 1,400 wide, where the strip of four lines stood 315, and 69 px at 375 with no sideways scroll.

## Not in this PR

A building is not read as a suite. Two permits on *Unit 100* of different buildings both stay on a bid for *Bldg 7, Suite 100*.
