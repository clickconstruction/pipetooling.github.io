# v2.8616 — Short Dashboard: the office's count and the door's count wait until every read is in (2026-11-26)

Punch list #365, finding 150 of the simulator's sitting 4 (2026-11-25), briefed by the fixing thread as `365-short-dashboard-counts-wait-for-every-source.md` after the small-fixes thread's send-back of 2026-11-25. Client only. No migration, no edge function. Nothing owed after the merge.

**Read.** As the sample leader on Short, the page read *23 other things* and *4 more of yours*; ten seconds later *24* and *6*. v2.8581 made the two counts wait for the read, but the read it waited for was the two lien reads alone (`shortDashboardRead`): the counts are over every source of the Needs you card, about sixty hooks, and each one that landed after the lien reads moved a number under the reader.

**The two ways the brief offered, and the one built.** Way A, the brief's pick: every hook says when its read is in, and the page waits for all of them. A survey of the 67 hooks `useNeedsYouInputs` reads, made before building: seven hand back a flag, twenty-six are null until their read lands (and most stay null when it fails), and **thirty-four say nothing at all**: their first value is the same as an empty one (an empty list, a zero, a null that also means *nothing to show*). Way A is a `loading` flag on those thirty-four, each a different shape (wrappers, module stores, values worked out in a `useMemo`), and their tests: L, not the brief's M. Way B, the brief's fallback, was built: the one fact every read shares is that it is a request through the Supabase client.

**The mend.**

- `src/lib/supabaseInFlight.ts`: a fifth wrapper on the client's `fetch`, outermost, counts every request while it runs, reads and writes, auth, storage and functions alike. The sibling of `wrapFetchCountingWrites`. `SUPABASE_QUIET_MS` is 300: how long nothing may be in flight before a page calls its reads in, which covers the render between a hook's first read landing and its second starting. It is a guess about that gap, so the page keeps a cap too.
- `useSupabaseQuiet()`: true once the client has been quiet for the window, false the moment a request starts.
- `shortDashboardCountsRead` (`lib/dashboard/shortDashboard.ts`), pure: the two counts are `ready` when the list's read is in and nothing is being read, or once the cap has passed; once ready they stay ready for the visit. The cap, `SHORT_DASHBOARD_COUNTS_CAP_MS`, is 8,000 ms, the watchdog's number in `useAuth`. The list keeps its own rule, `shortDashboardRead`: a paper must not wait on a slow source.
- `DashboardShortView.tsx`: `ShortDashboardBody` takes `countsRead` beside `read`, and the office's line and the door's count draw only when both are ready; `DashboardShortHome` works `countsRead` out from the quiet word, a timer at the cap, and *settled*. The root carries `data-counts-read` for a walker's read.
- The guide *see only what waits on me on the dashboard* says the two counts wait until every read is in.

**What it costs on the screen.** The two numbers draw a moment after the last read lands, together, once. The list, the greeting, the door's words and Training draw as before. Every request holds the counts, the schedule's and Training's with the card's, so the word comes a moment after the last of them. On a slow phone the numbers may take a few seconds; the cap holds that to eight. On the sitting that met the fault the slowest source took ten seconds on a starved Mac with five deploys landing, so the cap would have drawn the counts at eight seconds and they would have moved once at ten. That is the honest limit of any client mend.

**Where it differs from the brief:** the brief's pick was way A. Way B was built, for the size above; the kernels take any *still reading* signal, so way A's per-source word can replace the client's count later with no change to the view.

## What did not change

- The full Dashboard's Needs you card and the hooks it reads. No hook was touched.
