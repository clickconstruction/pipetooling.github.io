# v2.8637 — Clock out: Your day starts at the minute you clocked in (2026-11-26)

Punch list #445, finding 237 of the walking thread's sitting 23 (2026-11-26), briefed by the fixing thread as `445-strip-start-reads-the-beats-bucket.md`. Client only, one pure kernel. No migration, no edge function. Nothing owed after the merge.

**Read.** A person clocked in at 10:22 PM and opened a ZZ Test bid a minute later. At clock out the sheet read *Your day · 10:20 PM · 10:28 PM*: a start two minutes before the clock in. In `src/lib/clock/bidDay.ts`, `readBidDay` takes the strip's start as the least of every session's clock in and the first trace, and a bid's first trace is the floor of its first five-minute bucket. A beat at 10:23 sits in the bucket that opens at 10:20, so the floor is earlier than the clock in that came before the beat. The other end has the same shape on a day whose last session is closed: the last trace is its bucket's floor plus five minutes, so the strip could end up to five minutes after the last clock out. With a session open the end is *now*, so the sheet at clock out never showed that one.

**The rule kept.** The strip runs from the day's first clock in, or from the first trace when that is earlier. A trace before the first clock in is real: a person who works a bid for ten minutes and then clocks in started at the trace. A bucket's floor is a rounding and proves nothing about the minutes before the first beat in it.

**The mend.** In `readBidDay`, a first bucket that holds a clock in no longer moves the start: the strip starts at the clock in. A last bucket that holds a clock out no longer moves the end of a closed day: the strip ends at the clock out. A bucket that is over before the first clock in still starts the strip at its floor, and one that opens after the last clock out still ends it, both as before. The blocks were already cut to the strip's two ends, so a block in the shared bucket starts at the clock in with it.

The guide *clock out with the bids of the day* already says the strip runs *from your clock in to now*, which is now what it does. No words on the sheet change.

## What did not change

- A row's size. Its seconds are summed from the buckets, never from the strip's ends.
- Which bids the list holds, their order, their colours and what the press keeps.
- A day with no clock session: the strip runs from the first trace's bucket to the last one's end.
- `to-dos/clock-out-bids-of-the-day/README.md`: card #445's line is the fixing thread's.
