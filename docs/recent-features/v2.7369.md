# v2.7369 — Bid Board: your own estimating score, and what a closed job made against its bid (2026-11-15)

Punch list #360, PR 5, the last of the team scorecard's train. The estimating score was the owner's to read: the Scores tab sits behind the scoreboard's gate because its read was built from wages. The people the number is about met it only as a chip on a bid they could not finish. `team-scores` (v2.7365) can score a bid on the server and send no wage, so it can answer them.

## What ships

- **Your score**, a row at the top of the Bid Board ([`MyScoreStrip.tsx`](../../src/components/bids/MyScoreStrip.tsx), words in [`myScore.ts`](../../src/lib/scorecard/myScore.ts)) for an estimator, an assistant or a controller with a bid in the last 8 weeks: *$42,300 · won above the hurdle on 2 bids · 1 job closed and moved it by +$2,300*, or until a bid of theirs has scored, *2 of 5 of your priced bids can be scored · 3 need their Labor tab finished*. A press opens a sheet (a sheet from the bottom on a phone): the hurdle as one figure, then their bids by what happened: to finish (each with **Open Labor**), won above the hurdle, closed, counting zero, out. Nothing draws for a person with no bid in the window, or before the function answers.
- **A closeout's chip on the bid** ([`BidCloseoutChip.tsx`](../../src/components/bids/BidCloseoutChip.tsx)): on a won bid whose job was billed or paid in the window, *Closed +$2,300* (green), *Closed −$1,100* (amber) or *Closed on its estimate*, on the list row, the phone card and the opened row. Its hover says both numbers: *The job closed on 2026-11-10. It made $22,300 against the $20,000 the bid was priced to make. The estimating score moved by +$2,300.* An estimator sees it on their own bids; the scoreboard's gate sees it on every bid.
- **Two views on `team-scores`**: `bids` (the gate) and `mine` (any real account), both the estimating score alone through `readEstimatorScores`, which reads the hurdle and the bids and none of the field score's materials, subs or earned revenue. `mine` filters to the bids that name the caller as estimator or account manager; the id is the token's, never the body's. The answer (`EstimatorScoresWire`) carries the hurdle per hour and the target, not the overhead dollars or the hours behind them.
- **A closeout counts whatever its award day.** The kernel has always said so, but the loader only read bids awarded inside the window, so a bid won in August whose job was billed this month never moved the number. The reads now also take the won bids whose job was billed or paid in the window (`job_status_events`). No bid awarded before the stamp existed can be judged, so nothing moves today; it is right from the first judged award on.
- Guide *read your estimating score*; EDGE_FUNCTIONS, ACCESS_CONTROL and APP_DIRECTORY amended. No migration.

## Order

Merge, then `supabase functions deploy team-scores` (the function gained the two views). Until the deploy the row and the chips do not draw: an older function answers a view it does not know with the whole scorecard to the gate and a refusal to everyone else, and the client reads both as nothing to draw.

## What it does not do

- **The labor count is not kept with the priced margin.** A sent bid whose Labor tab is filled afterwards reads as judged while its kept cost has no labor in it. It needs two columns on `bids` and a new argument on `stamp_bid_priced_margin`, which is a migration the Pricing tab calls ahead of. Left on the card as the train's one residual, with the reason.
- The row's window is fixed at 8 weeks, the Scores tab's default. The tab's other windows are the owner's.
- The owner and a dev get the chips and no row: their number is the Scores tab's.

## Is this the best we can do?

- **Doubted: the hurdle in front of an estimator.** Break-even is the company's overhead on each field hour. Kept, as one figure: the score asks an estimator to price above it, and the target half is already on the Pricing rules for the whole board. The overhead dollars and the hours it is divided from stay behind the gate.
- **Doubted: a full read for a chip.** The Bid Board's chips need closeouts, and the full scorecard read is the field score's too. Changed: the board asks for the estimating score alone, which skips the materials, subs and earned-revenue reads.
- **Doubted: where the row goes.** The card said *on the phone*. The Bid Board is where an estimator already is on a phone, and its cards are the bids the row is about. A Dashboard card would have been a second place to keep. Kept the Bid Board.
- **Left for Micah, with my pick:** whether the hurdle's one figure is fine in front of an estimator. My pick is yes; the alternative is a chip that says *clears* or *does not* with no number, which gives an estimator nothing to price toward.
- **Left for Micah, with my pick:** whether an assistant who is an account manager on bids should see the row. My pick is yes, since the owner's rule is one shared number for the estimator and the account manager.
