# v2.5247 — Messages: the rules for contracts built from clauses (2026-10-12)

Wave 1 of the Messages build, cluster 9's first half (GC Reviews helper 9, directed by the lead session GC Review): the kernels of cut 4c, *Contracts from clauses* (`to-dos/gc-mode/messages/BUILD_PLAN.md` on `spike/gc-mode`), lifted from the Messages prototype v1.178 (`paperDoc`, `CLAUSES`, `canPublish`, `editClause`, `approveClause`, `declineClause`, `clauseNeeds`). No screen, no table, no edge function; cut 4c's SQL and Settings → Contracts and clauses come in wave 2.

- **`src/lib/contracts/assemble.ts`**: a paper (`ContractPaper`: title, name, the between line, its revision) and its clauses (`Clause`: id, order, English, Spanish, scope, its own version `v`, `retiredAt`/`retiredBy`). `assemblePaper(paper, clauses, { company, trades, job, lang })` is the paper one company signs: the live clauses for everyone numbered from 1, then the exhibit, the clauses naming any of the company's trades, each once, numbered on from the base, with `{company}` and `{job}` filled. A scope is `'all'` or a list of trade names, matched trimmed and case-blind on whole names, so nothing inherits (Electrical, low voltage is not Electrical). A master agreement takes every trade the company has; a statement of work its package's one trade. Spanish falls back to the English clause by clause. `problem` holds a draft (revision 0), a paper that needs a job and has none, or one with no clauses. `versionLine` (*Version 3, with the Sitework exhibit.*, and in Spanish), `exhibitHeading`, `settingsView` (the one numbering: the base as every paper numbers it, an exhibit per trade A to Z numbered as that trade's paper does, the retired clauses newest first), `clauseLabel` (*clause 2*, *clause 6 on the Sitework exhibit*), `clauseVersionsOf` and `signedCopyOf`, which freezes the words, the revision and each clause's id and version, sharing nothing with the clauses it came from.
- **`src/lib/contracts/approval.ts`**: `editClause` takes a new clause, an edit or a retire. A dev's or a controller's publishes at once (`publishChange`: the paper's revision one up, the clause's `v` one up, a new clause at the end as version 1, a retired one kept); anyone else's in the office becomes a `ClauseProposal` carrying the clause's version it was made against (`baseV`); anyone outside the office is refused. `approveProposal` publishes it as written and refuses once the clause has moved on or been retired since (*Clause 2 changed since Rosa proposed this.*), so a newer publish is never overwritten; `declineProposal` needs a why. `clauseNeeds` gives a controller or a dev the waiting rows, the longest waiting first, with the title (*Rosa changed clause 2 of the master agreement.*), the badge and the facts as separate pieces; nobody else gets a row. `changeProblem` refuses an empty clause, a scope with no trade, a clause not on the paper, a retired one and an edit that changes nothing.
- **`src/lib/gc/access.ts`**: `GC_CLAUSE_EDIT_TEAM` (the office team, `GC_OFFICE_TEAM`) and `GC_CLAUSE_APPROVE_TEAM` (dev and controller), with `canEditClause` and `canApproveClause` (the owner, 2026-10-12). `docs/ACCESS_CONTROL.md` names them.
- **Changed from the prototype, and why**: the paper's version is a revision counter, since the highest clause version let two texts share a number and a new clause never moved it; clauses are ids with an order, not array places; a stale proposal is refused rather than applied over a newer publish; one numbering in Settings and on the sign page, where the prototype's Settings numbered by array place; Spanish per clause; scope as a list of trades; retire, never delete; the words split to plain words (`plainWordsFailures` holds every toast, refusal and row).
- **Tests**: `assemble.test.ts` (24) and `approval.test.ts` (21) over the prototype's papers and people (`src/test/fixtures/clauseBookDemo.ts`: Greg Paulk at Hillside Excavation, Sitework; Bill Sorrell at Tejas Power, Electrical, low voltage; Tom Riddle at Bexar Steel Erectors; Dr. Priya Raman; Robert the dev, Rosa the office, Maria the controller, Wendi the superintendent), with a signed copy that keeps its words, its revision and each clause's version after a clause changes and another retires; `access.test.ts` gains the two lists.
