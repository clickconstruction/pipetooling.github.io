# v2.3026 — Test-gap audit, pass 36: the IP-to-map helpers get their first tests (2026-09-07)

Follow-up to v2.3025. Tests only, plus one hygiene to-do line; no kernel, client, migration or edge-function change.

## Why this one

`ipGeolocationMaps.ts` (159 lines) decides whether a clock punch or estimate view gets a map pin from its IP: the routable-IP gate (mirroring the edge function's), the Google Maps link, and the authorised call to `resolve-ip-geolocation` with its 24-hour session cache. Three callers, no test.

## What the tests found

Nothing wrong in the client. One gap recorded: the edge twin `_shared/ipGeoValidation.ts` does not typecheck under the app's strict tsconfig, so the client test could not import it for a client↔edge parity guard the way the accounting-label parity tests do. Noted in `to-dos/engineering-hygiene.md`; the fix is type-narrowing only, followed by a redeploy of the function.

Contracts now pinned that were only implied:

- The gate hides the map for private (10/8, 172.16/12, 192.168/16), loopback, link-local, carrier-grade NAT (100.64/10), 0/8 and malformed IPv4, and for IPv6 loopback, link-local and unique-local, bracketed or not; public addresses pass.
- The Maps link is `https://www.google.com/maps?q=<lat>,<lng>` with the pair encoded, opened in a new tab with `noopener,noreferrer`.
- The resolve call sends the encoded IP, the session JWT and the anon key, caches the answer for 24 hours under the trimmed IP, refetches past expiry or on a corrupt or malformed entry, and caches nothing on failure. The caller variant sends no IP and never uses the cache.
- Failures are named: missing IP, not signed in, the service's own error, a bare failure, an invalid payload.
