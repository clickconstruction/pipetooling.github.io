# v2.7116 — What we buy: a part's price over time, bill by bill (2026-11-12)

Punch list #298 PR 4b (`to-dos/supply-bill-lines/README.md`, frame H of its mock-up), over What we buy ([v2.7114](v2.7114.md)). The book keeps one price per part per house, the last one checked. The bills' lines are the real price history: every purchase is a point with its day, its house and its quantity.

## What a person sees

On Materials → What we buy, a row's name is now a press, and the page has a second view, **Price over time**.

- **The chart**: every purchase of the row a point, the day across and the price for one up. Each of the company's three biggest houses has its own colour; every other house shares the muted point. The book's price is a dashed line with the day it was checked. A point says its price, day, house and quantity on hover and on focus.
- **Last paid** at each house: the newest price, its day, how it moved since the purchase before (*up 2% since Sep 18*), and *cheapest these 90 days* on the lowest of two or more houses.
- **The book**: its price, the day checked, the house, and *stale* past ninety days.
- **Every purchase as a row**: day, house, bill, quantity, the price for one, the job.
- **With no row picked**: a search by name or code, the most spent offered first.

## How it is built

- **`src/lib/materials/lines/priceOverTime.ts`** (pure, `priceOverTime.test.ts`, 13 tests): `pricePoints` (one row's purchases, a return and an unpriced line left out, the price through the pricing unit and the crosswalk's `unit_factor`, so a stick and a foot land on one scale), `lastPaidByHouse`, `priceScale` with `dayAt` and `priceAt`, `houseSeries`, `bookLine`, `bookIsStale`.
- **`PriceOverTime.tsx`**: the view, an inline SVG. `WhatWeBuyTab.tsx` gains the strip of views and passes the read it already made.
- **`purchaseLinesIo.ts`** reads the paper's number and the crosswalk's unit factor, and `bookPrices(partId)` reads the book's prices for the one part on screen.
- **`src/index.css`**: `--house-series-1` to `-3`, one set per theme.

## The colours

A scatter has to tell every pair of series apart, not only neighbours. Three hues can; a fourth cannot, for a colour-blind reader. So three houses get a colour and the rest share the muted point, and the three are the houses with the most lines in all, so a house keeps its colour from part to part. The palette was run through the chart validator against the app's own light and dark surfaces: every pair passes for colour-blind and full-colour readers in both. One light-mode colour is under 3:1 against white, which the legend and the table under the chart answer: nothing is said by colour alone.

## Choices

- **The book's *Use $3.84* press is not here.** Frame H drew it. It writes the book's price, which is PR 2b, with the widened price-history trigger and the offer on a big move.
- **A row with no part has no book line.** The book prices parts. The chart still draws its points.

## Is this the best we can do?

It answers *what did we pay, and where is it cheaper* for one part today. Three things would make it better.

- **Most rows are one house.** Until codes are matched, a part bought at two houses is two rows, so *cheapest* rarely has two houses to compare. The matching list (2a) is what makes this view earn its place.
- **A price is for the bill's unit.** A house that sells by the box and prices by the piece is handled by the check, but the unit factor is only on the crosswalk, so an unmatched code keeps the house's own unit.
- **One part at a time.** A list of the parts whose price rose most since the purchase before would point the eye. The data is all here.
