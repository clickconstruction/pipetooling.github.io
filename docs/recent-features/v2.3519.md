# v2.3519 — A card refund nets against the job's parts cost (2026-09-16)

Client only: one sign rule in one kernel, applied to every reader, plus the display rows. No migration, no edge function. PR 2 (a migration) brings the six SQL readers to the same rule.

## Why

Found while mapping the supply-house credits train (`to-dos/supply-house-credit-residuals.md` → item 2). A refund from a pay-at-the-counter house arrives in the bank feed as money **in**. `mercury_transaction_job_allocations.amount` carries the bank's sign — the allocation rows sum to the signed transaction amount, so a purchase is negative and a refund positive — and every cost reader took `Math.abs(amount)`. A $40 refund allocated to a job therefore **added** $40 to its parts cost. The job read high by twice the refund.

The absolute value was never a decision about refunds. It was the shortest way to turn a −$37.99 debit into $37.99 of cost, and negation does that identically for every purchase; the two differ only on money coming back. The one reader whose author considered refunds — the weekly money payload (`20260807060000`) — negates, and its comment says refunds reduce the week's cost. So the code did not hold two conventions; one author thought about it and the rest did not.

Measured read-only on prod (2026-09-15): **46 positive allocations, $2,494.05** — Lowe's $916.96 (6), O'Reilly $768.39 (3), Home Depot $435.54 (5), plus small ones and 19 tiny Shell fuel adjustments. Each was counted the wrong way, so about $4,988 of parts cost was overstated across the affected jobs.

**Decision** (Grace, 2026-09-16, as the residuals note asked for): refunds net against cost. The same rule the supply-credit train applies — *a cost number nets*.

## The rule

[`cardChargeCostUsd`](../../src/lib/jobs/cardChargeAllocationFilter.ts) in the kernel that already owns the card-charge rule: `cost = −amount`, NaN and null read as 0, and `0 − n` rather than `-n` so a zero never comes back as `-0`. A $37.99 purchase is $37.99 of cost; a $40.00 refund is −$40.00 of cost.

Every client reader now goes through it — the same "change them all together or the same money disagrees between surfaces" ordering v2.3500 used:

| Reader | Feeds |
|---|---|
| `cardChargeAllocationFilter.ts` (both sums) | Job Summary's card column, the one-job refresh, People → Review (via `netCardChargesByJobId`) |
| `netCardChargesByJob.ts` | the Parts figure — the `min(gross, linked)` clamp is gone, so a job whose parts all went back reads a net credit rather than zero |
| `fetchJobMaterialsCostSnapshot.ts` `mercuryCardTotalFromLines` | the job window's Costs tab; `useJobCostSnapshot` now calls it instead of carrying its own copy |
| `partsPerPersonCostSummary.ts`, `jobSummaryPersonSummaryTable.ts` | the per-person split and its footer — these had to net too, or the "per-person totals may not match" warning would fire on every refund |
| `fetchOverheadOfficePartsByDay.ts` | People → Overhead, office and other-jobs parts by day — and the Office job's own refunds now lower the overhead pool |
| `bridge/loadBridgeData.ts` | the Bridge's materials by day |
| `people/reviewOfficeLikeCharges.ts` | Review's office-like charges card |
| `mercuryTagSplit.ts` | the per-tag cost lines (⛽ fuel & gas …) under Parts |
| `projectsJobHistoryDayCosts.ts` | Projects → Job History day modal |
| `bids/bidAssignedCosts.ts` | Bids → Bid Costs, on the mirror table |

**Display.** New [`CardChargeCostAmount`](../../src/components/jobs/CardChargeCostAmount.tsx) renders an allocation as cost: `40.00`, or `−40.00 refund` with a muted mark. Used by the Costs-tab rows (`JobDetailMaterialsCostSection`, `JobFormPartsCostSection`) and the Parts drilldown (`JobSummaryCostCellDrilldownModal`); the Job Summary expanded row keeps `AmountSmallCents` with a `−$` prefix and the same mark; the Overhead day list shows `−$` too. Before, every row printed the absolute value, so a refund looked exactly like a purchase.

**The overhead engine had to learn a negative day.** Netting the Office job's own refunds (O'Reilly, Progressive, AutoZone, a few Shell cents) means an office day can now pool *below zero* — May 9 nets −$44.69. [`overheadAllocation.ts`](../../src/lib/jobs/overheadAllocation.ts) skipped any day whose pool was not positive (`if (!(P > 0)) continue`) but still added it to the pool total, so the toolbar lit **⚠ overhead does not reconcile to the pool** on the first live look. The engine now spreads a negative pool exactly like a positive one, as a negative share: every `> 0` gate on a share became `!== 0`, the per-day `Math.max(0, landed − charged)` clamp is gone so a refund with nobody to charge books as negative unallocated, and the identity (pool + carried in = charged + unallocated + in flight) ties again under every setting. Six new tests pin it: the four-setting grid with a −$120 Sunday, the pool falling by twice the old reading with charges falling too, and the original day-share booking the quiet day's refund as negative unallocated. The 24 existing engine tests, including "1 day · 0% reproduces the original day-share to the cent", are unchanged and green.

**Left alone, on purpose.** `buildUserJobLabelBreakdown.ts` sorts one transaction's allocations by magnitude — ordering, not cost. `moneyfillWeekClose.ts` and `people/wheels.ts` read transactions, not job allocations (the week-close remainder is a to-do magnitude; Wheels is fuel spend per person, where a fuel refund is a separate question). The bank-side surfaces (`BankingMercuryTxDetailModal`, the drag-sort ledger) already print the signed amount.

## Tests

One test pinned the bug outright — `projectsJobHistoryDayCosts.test.ts` asserted that "negative (refund) and positive (debit) both add", with the signs backwards. Rewritten. Across the ten affected suites, fixtures that used a positive amount to mean a purchase now use the bank's sign, and each kernel has a refund case: `cardChargeCostUsd` edge cases, a $100 purchase + $40 refund reading $60, a net credit surviving `netCardChargesByJobId`, an unattributed refund netting in the per-person split, a refund on the bid side, the Home Depot row in the overhead loader, the CVS refund in office-like charges. 88 tests in the ten files.
