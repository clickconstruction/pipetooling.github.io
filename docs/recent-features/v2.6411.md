## Decisions

- **One template per statement, not one shape for all.** A Contract Form is a PDF plus boxes; the statement's PDF differs per row (its rows, its gap, its stated days), so each gets its own template row with its own schema. That keeps `get-contract-for-signer`, the signing page, `accept-contract` and the PDF gate untouched: the statement is just another form to them. The cost is a template row per statement, which the Form Studio's list leaves out by kind.
- **The link is minted where every person's link is minted.** `send-contract-for-signature` gained a link mode rather than the new function growing its own token path, so a statement's link rotates, expires and refuses exactly as a lien waiver's does.
- **The worker's typed days are the statement's days.** What the worker types into the stated boxes is what the paper says once signed, so `accept-contract` replaces the row's `stated_days` with the parsed boxes (the office's seeds come back through the same boxes, changed or not). The paper path does the same with what the office keys.
- **In person is a flag on the same page.** `?inperson=1` on `/contract/accept` records the consent's method as in person and says so on the page; the copy's note names who handed the phone over. No column was added for it.
- **Counsel's word is still owed.** The card's blocker — what a worker's stated days for 2025 are worth beside the company's payment records — is not answered. Diane asked for the build on 2026-11-02; the stated block ships as the design has it, marked as the worker's own statement on the paper, the form and the row. The card says so.
