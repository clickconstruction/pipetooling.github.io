# v2.5621 — Intro to plumbing: every trade word explained where you first meet it (2026-10-18)

Punch list #119, PR 6 (PR 5 v2.5619 drew the facts that were words alone). The owner asked whether a fourteen-year-old could understand the course. A read of the thirty lessons found the sentences fine (about twelve words each, Flesch-Kincaid grade 3.5 over the course and 5.2 at worst) and the trade words not: "check valve" used in lesson 4 and taught in 9, "vacuum breaker" and "backflow" used in 10 and taught in 27, "cleanout" and "hub" first met as quiz options, "stack", "lavatory", "lav" and "closet drain" never explained, and lessons 15 and 27 each carrying more than one fact. The content version hashes a lesson's words and a changed word never voids a pass (v2.5569), so every change here is a rewording. No lesson is added or removed; no migration; no edge function.

- **A word's one sentence where it is first met**: a lavatory, a lav for short, is the bathroom sink (lesson 13); the toilet is the closet in the trade, so its drain is the closet drain (20); a stack is the vertical drain pipe that the branches drop into (14, before the back-pressure sentence that used it); a hub is the bell end of a drain fitting that the pipe sits in, and cast iron has none, hence the no-hub coupling (19); a check valve is a one-way valve (4, before lesson 9 teaches it); a hose bib is the outside faucet (3); Valves at the fixture names the vacuum breaker in its Why it matters and says what backflow is in the sentence about the bucket pulled up the hose (10).
- **A quiz option is never the first time a reader meets a word**: "The cleanout cap" in lesson 12 becomes "The top of the vent", "It leaks at the hub" in 17 becomes "It leaks at the joint", and Vent types no longer asks what an AAV is. Sanitary tee vs wye names both fittings in its Why it matters so the figure caption is not the first place they appear.
- **Lesson 15, Vent types, down to one fact**: every trap has a vent and the vents leave through the roof, with the individual, common and wet vent; the stack vent and vent stack pair becomes one Traps line; Numbers keep three (6 in above the roof, 10 ft from a window or 3 ft above it, 1-1/4 in the smallest vent); the rise above the rim rides in its Must know sentence. The AAV moves to lesson 23, A blocked vent, with its 4 in riding in the sentence and its "not a vent through the roof" line in that lesson's Traps; the final bank's two AAV asks follow lesson 23 as before. Its first question now asks what a common vent is.
- **Lesson 27, Backflow and cross-connection, down to one fact**: the cross-connection, back siphonage as the street's pressure drop pulling the wrong way, the air gap and the hose bib's vacuum breaker. The backflow preventer, back pressure and the "a check valve is not a backflow preventer" line move to lesson 29, What the inspector tests, with **every year** in its Numbers; the dishwasher's gap and its `dishwasher-air-gap` drawing become a Traps line of 27. Its fourth question now asks what defeats a faucet's air gap.
- **The rule, for every course**: `src/lib/training/tradeTerms.ts` holds the short list (check valve, vacuum breaker, backflow, cleanout, hub, stack, lavatory, lav, closet drain, AAV, cross-connection, air gap, trap arm, hose bib, angle stop, wye, sanitary tee, wet vent), `tradeTermFirstUse` finds the first place a course meets a word (the lessons in order, each lesson's Why, Must know, Traps, Numbers and Check yourself in reading order, the bank last) and `tradeTermFailures` names each word met first outside a Must know or Why it matters sentence. `courseContent.test.ts` runs it over every course on disk; a course that never uses a word is not held to it. The list says `closet drain`, not `closet`, so the electrical course's clothes closet is not a hit.
- **The `vent-types` drawing** no longer draws an AAV under the kitchen sink, since the lesson no longer teaches one: the kitchen sink gets its own vent rising above its rim and turning to the stack, labelled like the lav's. Lesson 15's `10 ft` Numbers label dropped a second bold span that the page printed as literal asterisks.
