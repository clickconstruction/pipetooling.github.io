## Decisions

- **The estimator's door is the strip's sheet, not Documents.** The spike drew him on the Documents tab, but `resolveJobWindowMode` gives an estimator the read-only pane, which has no tabs. So everything he authors with is on the sheet, the arrows too, which the spike's sheet did not have.
- **The press follows the database's answer for this job.** PR 1 drew the superintendent's press for the role and let the database refuse on another job. With the estimator that would be a dead press on most jobs he opens, so both now ask first. The database still holds the line.
- **▲ ▼ move the job's own checks alone.** A company row's place is the whole company's, it would move under every other job from a press on one, and an estimator may not write it. The company's order stays on Settings until PR 4 brings authoring to Documents.
- **The promote moves the answer and leaves the plan sheet and the why.** The card says copy and retire; it does not say what happens to the answer. Left on the retired row, the stage this job already answered would read not ready, so it moves. P-201 on one job's plans means nothing on the next job's, so the plan sheet and the why stay on the job's row, and the hint says so when there are any.
- **The promote is the office's alone, and the company's list asks a thing once.** The office wrote the list. The estimator authors for his job. Words the list already asks are refused with that reason.
- **A skip closes and stays.** Put it back never deletes, so PR 3's record can say what was skipped. An answer the question had stays in its table and reads again.
- **An adder's only write is now truly the retire.** PR 1's gate let the adder of a retired item write to it again, and let a retire rewrite the words in the same statement, under the answer the item keeps. A policy cannot compare a row before and after, so a trigger does. A put back is held the same way, by granting its two columns alone. The bed pins both.
