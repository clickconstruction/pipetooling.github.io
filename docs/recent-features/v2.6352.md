# v2.6352 — Projects: one read for every role, and a line under Money in on the leader's Dashboard (punch list #242, PR 6) (2026-11-02)

Two things the owner asked for and one the walk found. The owner, 2026-11-02: *on a leader's dashboard I would like to see the projects section in a collapsible way below the finances section with Accounts Receivable and below the Money in row … where it can collapse down to the size of Money in.* And before calling the train live, a read of what each role actually gets showed the board worked only for payroll.

## What the walk found

Measured on the sample accounts: a superintendent reads no working job and no crew row, so his board was empty and PR 4's phone card (#6776, pulled from the queue for this) would never draw; an assistant reads hours but no wage, so every person read $0 and the money ran short of the leader's. The spike never showed this: it gated its page to the office and ran as the dev. Nothing leaked, because the wage policies held; the feature just did not work for those roles.

## What landed

- **`list_project_board`** (migration `20261102163008_project_board_read.sql`, `docs/migrations/20261102163008_project_board_read.md`): one definer read for every role on the board. The wages stay inside it: what leaves is hours per person per day and the blended spend as a whole percent of the money budget. No labor dollar leaves it for anyone. A superintendent now reads every project (the card's default on open question 2) and every target (`job_expected_hours_select`). Its arithmetic mirrors the Costs tab: checked read-only on prod against the tab's own client read, every component equal to the cent on 35 of 35 Working jobs and the percent equal on 37 of 37.
- **`lib/jobs/projectBoardRead.ts`** parses the read; **`projectPace`** now takes a job from it, so the board, the Dashboard line and PR 4's phone card cannot disagree. **`hooks/useProjectsBoard.ts`** holds the read and the cards (lifted out of the page; `useJobBudgetRows` goes). A payroll viewer's own read of each job's events (`useBidVsActualBurnInputs`) now only fills in the dollars on a day; it never decides a number.
- **The day card reads `hoursOnly`** for anyone whose read carries no wages: each person's hours and the report, no dollar, and no subs or purchases to misread, since their RLS may hide them and *Nothing bought* would be false.
- **`DashboardProjectsStrip`** under Money in, for the leader and a dev, in its frame, padding and type, so closed it is the same 44 px line: *Projects · 13 working · 5 behind · 5 ahead · 1 on pace · 2 no reading yet · <the worst project> · Open ▾*. Open: the board's own cards with *Open the board →* and *Close ▴*, the choice kept per device (`dashboard-projects-open`). Closed it asks for no day's detail, so the Dashboard's read is one function and two small reads, where the first cut made several requests per project on every load. It draws once the Financials section has read, so it never jumps above the cards.

## Fixed on the way, from real screens

- **The money budget was the spike's, not the Burn's.** It took any baseline's total, so a driving-only snapshot read as the job's budget (the v2.3847 mistake again): all 6 baselines on Working jobs carried no labor and materials pair, and cards read *money 2508%* and *budget?*. It now follows `budgetForBurn`. Projects reading behind fell from 8 to 5 with no job's work changing.
- **A job past its budget read *on pace*.** `hoursPct` was capped at 100, so a job 242 h over read as even. The verdict now reads the real hours, and the legend says so.
- **The done mark sat on the track, not the plan.** On a track run past the budget, or under expected hours, 80% done drew at 80% of the track. It now sits at the percent of the plan (`PaceStrip`), so the gap to the fill is ahead or behind in hours, and a drag of the slider moves it.
- **Smaller.** The large card no longer repeats the window's title; the proof line says *the crew's 90%*, not *your*; the slider gives way on a phone instead of pushing the row past the card; *Job ›* shows only to the office, since a superintendent's RLS would not open most jobs.

## Calls taken for the owner

- **The leader and a dev only** on the Dashboard: he asked for the leader's; the office has the board and the Pipeline press.
- **A superintendent's read carries each project's line prices**, because the labor hours are worked out from them; the screens show hours and a percent, and the stage bar's card its budget dollars at the cost rate. No wage.
- **One company margin (35%) on an assumed budget**, not each device's own Job Summary target, so the number is the same on every screen. A leader with his own target set may see a different Burn on the Costs tab for a job with no standing baseline.
