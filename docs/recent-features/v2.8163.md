- **The lessons** (`src/content/training/intro-hvac/`, Part 2, *Heat in and air*): 11 the gas furnace (the heat exchanger as the one wall between the flame's gas and the house's air, the order the parts start in, Category I and Category IV); 12 gas to the appliance (written as the company's own work, since the Texas Plumbing License Law counts piping that supplies gas as plumbing); 13 combustion air; 14 venting the flame (the shared vent, and the water heater left alone on it when a furnace is changed); 15 carbon monoxide; 16 ducts, supply and return; 17 the filter, the airflow and the thermostat; 18 where water meets air (the boiler, the hot water coil, the heat pump water heater, the humidifier, the evaporative cooler, and the company's rule: water in pipes is plumbing, air in ducts is HVAC, a sealed loop is a carded technician's). `course.md` reads `lessons: 18`, `minutes: 105` and gains the part; the bank grows to thirty-two.
- **The sources read on 2026-11-21.** International Fuel Gas Code 2024: 202 (Category I, Category IV, the draft hood), 303.3 (bedrooms, bathrooms, storage closets), 304.5.1 (50 cubic feet for each 1,000 Btu an hour), 304.5.3.1 (two openings, 1 sq in for each 1,000 Btu an hour, 100 sq in at the least, within 12 in of the top and of the bottom), 304.6.1 (outdoor openings), 406.4.1 and 406.4.2 (the test, 10 minutes in a house), 408.4 (the sediment trap and the appliances that need none), 409.5.1 (the shutoff in the room, within 6 ft, ahead of the connector), 411.1.3.1 and 411.1.3.3 (the connector, 6 ft, one to an appliance, never through a wall, a floor or a ceiling), 503.10.8 (1/4 in per ft, no dips or sags). International Mechanical Code 2024, 603.10 (flexible duct hung as its maker says). International Residential Code 2024, R311 (the carbon monoxide alarm; R315 in the 2021 edition). OSHA's carbon monoxide fact sheet (50 ppm over 8 hours, the first signs), the NIOSH Pocket Guide (1,200 ppm immediately dangerous to life), the Consumer Product Safety Commission's pages and its September 2026 staff memo (a home alarm at 70 ppm sounds in 60 to 240 minutes). Texas Occupations Code 1301.002 and 1302.002. As in PR 1 the code text was read on UpCodes' copy that adopts the 2024 code without amendments, and which edition and amendments the company's cities inspect under is the reader's.
