# v2.4051 — Pipeline: the lien runway under every billed row — does the money land before the lien dies? (2026-09-28)

Client only; no migration, no edge function. Mock-up: the owner's canvas (before · first try · best · compare), boards 3–4 and the anatomy board.

## The ask

The last day to lien a home was computed everywhere and shown nowhere upfront: the Payment forecast's month panel (a chevron and a scroll away), the Lien window's affidavit tab (a red footnote), the Lien desk's Timeline tab (GC jobs only — a direct homeowner job never appears), and a Dashboard card that starts 21 days out. The owner's question was sharper than "when is the date": *does the money land before the lien dies?* — the expected-pay chip and the § 53.052 clock already answered that between them, but the reader had to subtract.

## What changed

- **`src/lib/jobs/lienPayRunway.ts`** (new, 17 tests) — `buildLienPayRunway({ todayYmd, openBalance, lastWorkYmd, propertyKind, expectedPayYmd, filedYmd, releasedYmd })` returns one reading: `state` (`room` · `file_first` · `no_pay` · `closed` · `filed` · `none`), `tone`, the `words`, a `title` with the basis (last work month, kind, § 53.052), a `chipLabel` for the phone, the `marks` for the track (today → the pay dot → the flag, percent along a runway that ends a fifth past the farthest mark) and a `sortKey` (file-first first, then a flag standing alone, then room, then closed, then filed). `lienByForJob` gives the § 53.052 date and, when the property kind is unset, uses the **residential** (earlier) clock and says `kindAssumed` — a house read as commercial is a lien lost a month late. An expected pay date already past reads *pay was due Sep 20*; the flag then stands alone. A job with no approved clock hours counts from the month it was created, as the Lien desk does (`datedFromCreation`; the hover says *dated from the job's creation · no clock hours*) — on the live board that is most billed rows. `lienRunwayWantsTheChip` is the phone rule: file first and lien gone always; a flag inside 21 days.
- **`src/components/jobs/LienPayRunway.tsx`** (new, render smoke) — the track (theme tokens; the green run is `--bg-green-200`, the short run a red hatch), *today* and the end date under it, and the sentence as a `<button>` that opens the job's Lien window. Closed and filed draw the sentence alone. In the table's 13rem money column the sentence wraps at its separators (two lines) rather than clipping — a clipped verdict is no verdict; in the card layout the runway spans the card like the bar above it.
- **`src/hooks/useBilledLienClocks.ts`** (new) — for every job in Billed: the property kind from `customer_addresses` and any live `affidavit` / `release_of_record` in `job_lien_filings`, chunked, keyed on the job ids. Null while loading, empty on error, so the rows simply draw no runway.
- **`JobsStagesTab.tsx`** — `lienRunwayFor(job, inv)` reads the same `billedExpectedPayModel` the chip reads (the GC's date from the statement round, a promise on record, or the pay-speed estimate) and hands it with the clock facts to the kernel; `billedLienRunwayRenderer` draws it. `stagesUnifiedTableShared` carries it as `billedLienRunway`, so the Billed and Collections tables and the unified card list draw it under `StagesProgressPaymentCell` (`StagesUnifiedJobRow` both branches, `StagesUnifiedInvoiceRow`, `JobsStagesCardList`). The phone rows' `phoneNextInput` passes the runway; the chip's tap opens the Lien window (`action: 'lien'`).
- **`jobNextLine.ts`** — `lienRunway` on the input, `'lien'` on `JobNextChipAction`; the rule sits after the returned-check and set-%-done chips (wrong figures first) and before quiet days and the late-pay chip.

## What it does not do yet

- No sort by the runway — the kernel's `sortKey` is ready; the header's sort switch is a follow-up.
- The expected-pay chip still draws in the action stack; the mock-up moved it onto the runway. Left as is for one release so the two can be compared live.
