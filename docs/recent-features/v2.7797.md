# v2.7797 — The pace card, shown: stages as a rail, the finish day, and fewer words (2026-11-18)

Punch list #396, PR 1 of 3. Client only. No migration and no edge function.

## Why

The owner looked at the superintendent's pace card behind the short Job Mode page's pace line. His words: too many words, not enough showing instead of telling. On pace, the card read about ninety words and offered one press. It was drawn four times with him the same day, ending with the job's stages stood up as a rail, and he said to build the train.

## What landed

- **The rule** is `lib/jobMode/paceCard.ts`, pure and tested. `paceStageRail` turns the job's stages into rows, done, now and next. `paceFinish` gives the planned day and, when ahead or behind, the day this pace lands on. `paceHours` is the hours bar, `paceDaysLeftWords` the unspent budget in crew days, and `paceRowNote` the folded row's second line.
- **The rail** is `PaceRail.tsx`: a circle a row with a tick when done, the same form as the day's rail on the card's top. The stage in hand carries **Set the %**, with where the stage stands, *15% · Set the %*. The last row is **Finish** with its day as *Fri, Nov 20*. Behind or ahead, a row under it reads **At this pace** with that day.
- **The card** (`SuperintendentCard`): the headline, the days of hours left, the rail, the **Hours** bar, the move's one sentence when behind, and **Open the checklist** as before. **How this is figured** folds open over everything the card used to say: the job's label and the budget's source, the pace strip with its days, the finish sentence, the target, the money, the proof and the month's score. Nothing is removed.
- **The folded row** on the short page (`JobModeShortRow` takes a `picture`): two thin bars, blue for the work and the hours under it, and a second line that names the finish day.

## How the rail reads a stage

- When the crew reported any stage by itself, each stage reads its own number and an unreported one reads 0. A stage finished out of order keeps its tick, and the first unfinished stage is the one in hand.
- When nobody reported a stage, the job's one percent is spread over the stages in order by their share of the hours. The fold says *Stages read from the job's percent.*
- **Set the %** opens the window the card always opened. It already takes a number for each stage (punch list #130), so the rail needed no new write.
- A job with fewer than two stages has no rail. It shows a **Work** bar, the **Hours** bar, the finish day in words, and **Set the %** as a full press.

## What moved from the drawing

- A finished stage shows no date: nothing keeps the day a stage reached 100.
- The top right reads *3 days of hours left*, not *3 days left*. The figure is the unspent budget in crew days, and the shorter words would read as the calendar.
- **My finish date** and **Ask a master** are PR 3 and PR 2.

## Who it changes

A superintendent in Job Mode on a project, on the long page and the short one. The office's Projects board draws its own strip and is untouched.
