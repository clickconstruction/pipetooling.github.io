# v2.8244 — Agreements: a line says which company its paper names (2026-11-21)

Found by the audit of punch list #403 (finding 9). Asked for by Taunya on 2026-11-21. Two kernels and the two lists that draw them. No migration and no edge function.

## What was wrong

A paper keeps the business its signer signs for, in words, as it was sent (`signing_for`). The paper's party is a contact, and a contact can change: someone typed in for one company can be added to a list under another, through **Add to a list**.

The line then named the record they were added to and nothing else. Seen on prod with the ZZ test papers: the paper said *signing for* one ZZ test company, and its line read *With* another. Nothing on the line said the two differ. The company's own page, built in v2.8162, had the same gap.

## What changed

A line now says *signing for* and the business the paper names, when that business is none of the names already beside it.

- **Documents → Agreements.** After who signs: *With Ridgeway Builders · Noor Haddad signs · signing for Haddad Engineering*.
- **A supply house's Papers section, and the Customer page's papers.** Under the paper's name, after who signs.

It is said only when it adds something:

- not when the party beside it is that business, in any case or spacing;
- not when the signer signs for themselves;
- not on a paper that kept no business;
- not on a kind that keeps no signer: a job's contract, a work order, a GC project's papers.

On a company's own page the line needs the party's name to hold the paper's words against. The three hosts pass it. With no name, the line says nothing, so a page never names its own party as another's.

A party that was renamed after a paper went out reads the same way: the line names the party today, and says the name the paper carries.

## The rule

`signingForBeside(signingFor, beside)` in `src/lib/contracts/paperParty.ts`, pure. `agreementSigningForWords` (`agreementsLedger.ts`) reads it for the tab from the view's `facts`. `partyPaperLine` (`partyPapers.ts`) reads it for a company's page, and the page's read now asks for `signing_for`.

Nothing about a paper changes. Its party, its signer and its words are as they were.
