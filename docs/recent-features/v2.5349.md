# v2.5349 — Messages: reading an email that reaches bids@, behind the scenes (2026-10-12)

Cut 6 (email in), PR 1 of 4: the kernels the `gmail-sync` reader runs, pure and tested, in `src/lib/emailIn/`
(`to-dos/gc-mode/messages/BUILD_PLAN.md` → Cut 6; the mock-up `email-in-mockups.html` on branch spike/gc-mode, with the
lead's answers to its nine questions). No migration, no edge function and no screen yet.

- `gmailMessage.ts`: a Gmail message (`format=full`) read into an email: the addresses (RFC 2047 words, quoted names,
  `mailto:` brackets), the plain words or the HTML read as text, every link, and the files with their Gmail ids. A
  picture placed in the body is marked inline. Words past 100,000 characters stay in Gmail.
- `leftOut.ts`: what the reader leaves in Gmail with no label: spam and trash, anything not in the Inbox, our own
  addresses, lists, bulk and machine mail (an out-of-office reply too), no-reply senders, and Gmail's Promotions, Social,
  Updates and Forums.
- `forwarded.ts`: a copy one of us forwards to bids@ lands on the block's first sender (Gmail's, Apple Mail's and
  Outlook's blocks), with its subject and time; the forwarder's note above it is kept for a team note.
- `jobInSubject.ts`: a job's number as the app shows it, standing alone in the subject, ties the line; else a job's whole
  name among the sender's own jobs.
- `paperGuess.ts`: a trade partner's W-9 or certificate of insurance, read from the file's name, for the thread's offer.
- `fileLinks.ts`: links to Drive, Dropbox, Box, WeTransfer, OneDrive and SharePoint files, kept as links.
- `emailLine.ts`: the line the reader writes and a row per file (copied up to 25 MB, left in Gmail above it, a picture
  in the body under 30 KB left out) and per file link.

Next: the migration (`messages.gmail_id`, `message_attachments`, the private bucket, the reader's state), the
`gmail-sync` function, and the screens. Later, the team's own Gmail replies as our lines, which also closes the gap
where an estimator sees none of the emails we sent.
