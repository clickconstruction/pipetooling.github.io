## Every changed sentence, before and after

- **Lesson 1, How safety works on a site** (Unit 0, Must know)
  - Before: PPE is the last line of defense. It reduces the effect of a hazard and never removes it.
  - After: PPE is the last line of defense. It reduces the effect of a hazard and never removes it. A respirator, the mask you breathe through, is PPE too.
- **Lesson 1, How safety works on a site** (Unit 0, Must know)
  - Before: A registered professional engineer designs trench protection deeper than **20 ft**. A fire watch stays **30 minutes** after hot work ends.
  - After: A registered professional engineer designs trench protection, the protective system against falling walls, deeper than **20 ft**. A fire watch stays **30 minutes** after hot work ends.
- **Lesson 1, How safety works on a site** (Unit 0, Must know)
  - Before: Your employer owes a workplace free of recognized hazards, training you understand, and PPE at no cost. Your duties are to follow the rules, inspect your gear, and report hazards instead of fixing them yourself.
  - After: Your employer owes a workplace free of recognized hazards, training you understand, and PPE at no cost. Your duties are to follow the rules, inspect your gear, and report hazards instead of fixing them yourself. NFPA 70E, the electrical safety standard, splits the duties the same way.
- **Lesson 1, How safety works on a site** (Unit 0, Must know)
  - Before: Any employee at any level can stop unsafe work. Raise it with your supervisor first, then the next level, then OSHA.
  - After: Any employee at any level can stop unsafe work. An unsafe scaffold, the platform built up to work from, is one case. Raise it with your supervisor first, then the next level, then OSHA.
- **Lesson 2, Every day on the job** (Unit 0, Why it matters)
  - Before: Every job has the same shape. Plan it, inspect the gear, lock it out, wear the PPE, report what goes wrong. Nearly every death in the course came from rushing, a shortcut or a skipped step.
  - After: Every job has the same shape. Plan it, inspect the gear, lock it out, wear the PPE, report what goes wrong. Nearly every death in the course came from rushing, a shortcut or a skipped step. Around a machine, stay out of its swing radius, the circle its parts sweep through.
- **Lesson 3, OSHA and your rights** (Unit 1, Must know)
  - Before: **Six** hazard categories: safety, chemical, biological, physical, ergonomic and work organization. Safety hazards, unsafe conditions and unsafe acts, are the most common.
  - After: **Six** hazard categories: safety, chemical, biological, physical, ergonomic and work organization. Safety hazards, unsafe conditions and unsafe acts, are the most common. Asbestos, the fiber in old building materials, counts as chemical.
- **Lesson 5, Finding and fixing hazards** (Unit 4, Must know)
  - Before: High-risk work: heights, electricity, struck-by, trenching, confined spaces and cranes.
  - After: High-risk work: heights, electricity, struck-by, trenching, confined spaces and cranes. Caught-in or between means crushed or pinned between two things.
- **Lesson 10, Caught in or between** (Unit 9, Must know)
  - Before: A cubic yard of soil weighs **2,000 to 3,000 lb**, as much as a car. Protection goes in before anyone enters.
  - After: A cubic yard of soil weighs **2,000 to 3,000 lb**, as much as a car. A cave-in is a trench wall falling in on you. Protection goes in before anyone enters.
- **Lesson 12, PPE** (Unit 11, Must know)
  - Before: Safety glasses stop front-facing particles. Goggles seal against splashes and vapors. A face shield goes over glasses or goggles, never alone.
  - After: Safety glasses stop front-facing particles. Goggles seal against splashes and vapors. A face shield goes over glasses or goggles, never alone. Cutting rebar, the steel bar inside concrete, throws such particles.
- **Lesson 13, Respirators** (Unit 12, Why it matters)
  - Before: Bad air in a sewer, crawl space or trench kills fast. The wrong respirator is no protection at all. An N95 does nothing against a gas or low oxygen.
  - After: Bad air in a sewer, crawl space or trench kills fast. The wrong respirator is no protection at all. An N95, the common dust mask, does nothing against a gas or low oxygen.
- **Lesson 14, Chemicals, HazCom and biological hazards** (Unit 13, Must know)
  - Before: Asbestos and lead are hazardous when disturbed by scraping, grinding, demolition or renovation. Stop, do not disturb it, and report. A qualified person tests and a certified contractor abates.
  - After: Asbestos and lead are hazardous when disturbed by scraping, grinding, demolition or renovation. Stop, do not disturb it, and report. A qualified person tests and a certified contractor abates. Abatement is the name for that removal.
- **Lesson 19, Trenches: soil and protection** (Unit 19, Why it matters)
  - Before: Trench collapses kill about **40** workers a year. Most happen in trenches only **5 to 15 feet** deep. The right protection depends on reading the soil right.
  - After: Trench collapses kill about **40** workers a year. Most happen in trenches only **5 to 15 feet** deep. The right protection depends on reading the soil right. Egress means the way out of a trench.
- **Lesson 19, Trenches: soil and protection** (Unit 19, Must know)
  - Before: A trench is deeper than it is wide, and no more than **15 feet** wide at the bottom.
  - After: An excavation is any cut dug into the ground, and a trench is one kind. A trench is deeper than it is wide, and no more than **15 feet** wide at the bottom.
- **Lesson 22, Cranes, concrete and steel on someone else's site** (Units 21, 23, 24, Must know)
  - Before: A certified signal person is required whenever the operator cannot see the load or the landing. A stop signal from anyone on site stops the lift.
  - After: A certified signal person is required whenever the operator cannot see the load or the landing. A stop signal from anyone on site stops the lift. A flagger is the one who directs traffic.
- **Lesson 26, Confined spaces** (Unit 30, Must know)
  - Before: Oxygen from **19.5% to 23.5%** is acceptable. Normal air is about **21%**. Below 19.5% is oxygen-deficient and immediately dangerous. Flammable gas above **10% of the lower explosive limit** is unsafe.
  - After: Oxygen from **19.5% to 23.5%** is acceptable. Normal air is about **21%**. Below 19.5% is oxygen-deficient and immediately dangerous. Flammable gas above **10% of the lower explosive limit**, the LEL, is unsafe.
- **Lesson 28, Electrical safety and NFPA 70E** (Unit 32, Must know)
  - Before: Arc flash PPE categories: 1 is **4 cal/cm²**, 2 is **8**, 3 is **25**, 4 is **40**. Wear it over natural fibers, because synthetics melt. Read the equipment's arc flash label and never reuse yesterday's choice.
  - After: A PPE category is the heat a suit must take, in calories per square centimeter. Arc flash PPE categories: 1 is **4 cal/cm²**, 2 is **8**, 3 is **25**, 4 is **40**. Wear it over natural fibers, because synthetics melt. Read the equipment's arc flash label and never reuse yesterday's choice.
