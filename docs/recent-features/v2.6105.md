# v2.6105 — Legal desk: the money drawn as bars — the bills, the claim with every offer on it, and what is left (2026-10-29)

Punch list #214 (`to-dos/legal-desk-show-not-tell/`), PR 2 of 4, on v2.6103 (#213's read of the retainage a sent bill already carries). *How much?* and *What's left for us?* are a figure each with a sentence under it. On a subcontract account the sentence had to explain where the retainage sat, and the mock-up's first money bar showed why a sentence was the wrong shape: a bar cannot count a dollar twice, and an offer is a mark on a length, not a clause.

## What changed

- **`LegalWorth` carries the parts** ([`legalPacket.ts`](../../src/lib/legal/legalPacket.ts)): `openBills`, one row per open billed line with its job, its billed day, the ledger row's words and what it still needs (`LegalJobMoney.openByInvoice`), and `retainageOnBillsByJob`, v2.6103's read run per job (`claimRetainage`) so the bars can bracket the job whose sent bills already carry the retainage.
- **The kernel** ([`moneyBars.ts`](../../src/lib/legal/moneyBars.ts), pure, 8 tests): from `worth`, the Offers log and the settlement floor, up to four bars as fractions of a width — **On the bills** (a segment per open bill, oldest first; a bracket under a job's bills for the retainage inside them; a shell job's remainder as one quiet block), **The claim** (drawn only when it says something the bills bar does not: the retainage no bill asks for yet, a standing offer or a floor; the bills as one block, the added retainage after, and a hatched stretch to the largest standing offer or the floor named *asked beyond the claim on record*, since the desk counts only the bills and the retainage and never invents a figure; every standing offer — open, countered or accepted — and the floor as marks, two rows when two would sit within 18% of the width), **What's left, on the claim** (keep · the firm's cut · the filing cost; a figure the cut exceeds reads *short*, never a negative keep) and **What's left, on the bills alone** when the claim is larger, because the firm's cut on a disputed claim is a different decision from its cut on an undisputed balance. One sentence under the bars says the same in words (`words`).
- **The component** ([`LegalMoneyBars.tsx`](../../src/components/jobs/legal/LegalMoneyBars.tsx)) draws under the four questions when the account owes anything: the bills in blues a step darker per bill, the hatch for what is asked past the claim, green for keep, the reds for the firm and the filing cost; a segment under 9% of the width keeps its words for the hover. `LegalDesk` composes the input from `worth`, the matter's offer entries (voided ones left out) and `settlementFloorOf(matter)`.
- **Guide** *review a collections account before it goes to your attorney*: a paragraph under the three tiles on what the bars draw.
- **Tests**: `moneyBars.test.ts` (new: the bills order and bracket, the claim's scale and marks and rows, the added retainage alone, no claim bar when nothing is offered, the two what's-left bars, a short figure, a shell job, the day words and which offers stand); `legalPacket.test.ts` (+3: `openBills` foot to the balance, each with its job and day); `deskQuestions.test.ts`'s default worth carries the two new fields; `LegalDesk.render.test.tsx` (+3: three bill segments for three jobs, the keep bar, no claim bar).

## What the mock-up asked for that this does not do

The mock-up's claim bar had a *lost profit* stretch with its own figure. The app keeps no lost-profit figure — it lives in the narrative and in the offer — so the bar draws the stretch an offer or the floor reaches past the claim on record and names it as what was asked, not as a figure the desk computed.

The client deploys with the merge. No migration, no edge function.
