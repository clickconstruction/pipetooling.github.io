# v2.8532 — Global Reload: only a dev can send the signal (2026-11-24)

Punch list #85. The look behind v2.8527 found that the signal behind **Global Reload** was open to anyone, and put it to Diane. Diane, 2026-11-24: *Yes, lock it.* One migration and a client change. No edge function.

## Before

**Global Reload** (Settings → Advanced, dev only, v2.8527) makes every other open copy of the app hard-reload. It sent a broadcast on the Realtime channel `force-reload`, and every signed-in copy listened there (`ForceReloadContext`).

The channel was an open one. Supabase takes a broadcast on an open channel from anyone who holds the project's public key, and every browser that opens the app holds that key. So the dev gate was on the screen only. A person outside the app could send the same broadcast and reload every open copy, over words people had typed and not sent.

## Now

The app joins the **private** channel of the same name (`FORCE_RELOAD_CHANNEL_OPTIONS`, `src/lib/forceReloadChannel.ts`). Realtime checks a private channel against row policies on its own table, and the migration makes two (`docs/migrations/20261124215500_global_reload_dev_only.md`):

| Who | Hears the signal | Sends the signal |
|---|---|---|
| a dev | yes | yes |
| anyone else signed in | yes | no |
| nobody signed in | no | no |

A send the policy refuses is dropped by the server. It reaches nobody, and the sender is told nothing.

A private channel and an open channel of one name are two channels. A broadcast on the open `force-reload` channel no longer reaches a copy of the app that has this change.

Nothing changes on the screen. The press, its window and its words are v2.8527's.

## What a dev should know

- **A copy that has not updated yet is on the old channel.** A signal sent from an updated copy does not reach it. It takes the update by itself at a quiet moment (v2.3740), and from then on it hears the signal. Until it updates, it still listens on the open channel, where the old weakness holds.
- **The role is read when a copy joins.** Realtime keeps the answer for the life of the connection and reads it again on each join and each new sign-in token. A person made a dev sends after their next join. A person who stops being a dev stops after theirs.
- **The open channel is still there.** The project allows open channels, since every other channel the app uses is one. Nothing listens on the open `force-reload` channel once every copy has updated.

## Seen

On 2026-11-24, in the bed (`scripts/pgtest-global-reload-dev-only.sh`, Postgres 15 in Docker), with a stand-in for Realtime's table: all fifteen checks passed, and the file ran clean on a database with no such table. As a control the dev test was taken out of the write policy, and the bed failed at *a master cannot send it*.

## Not proved

- **The real Realtime service.** The bed tries the read and the write the way Realtime's documentation says it does. No join and no send were made against the live service, since the policies are not there until the push.
- **A refused send on the live service.** It will not be tried there: if the policy were wrong, the try would reload every open copy.
- A join that is refused before the push and then let in after it, without a reload. The client's own code retries a refused join, and Realtime's documentation says policies are read again on a join.

## What is left

- **Global Reload still reloads over typed words** in every copy it reaches (`docs/recent-features/v2.8527.md`). The lock changes who can send it, and nothing about what it does.
- **A copy hidden for five minutes drops the channel and does not join it again** until it reloads (`RealtimeLifecycleProvider`). That was so before this change.
- The live look after the push: a signed-in copy joins the private channel, and a signed-out browser is refused.
