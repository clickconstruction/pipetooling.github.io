# v2.8490 — GC projects: take back an ask the company already heard about (2026-11-24)

The follow-up v2.8440 named in its first call. **Take the ask back…** deleted an ask the company never heard about and refused the rest, pointing at **Will not do it** and **Cannot do it**. Those are the company's words: an ask made on the wrong company that was already emailed or opened could only be closed by writing that the company would not do it, and their record then counted a pass they never made. The owner's word of 2026-11-24: build the withdrawn answer for an ask they heard about.

## The design, as a table

| | |
| --- | --- |
| **The press** | The same **Take the ask back…** beside **Will not do it** and **Cannot do it**, on a trade's asks on the project's card and on a Follow up card. For whoever may ask: the office team. |
| **Shows when** | The ask is still open: no quote, not awarded, not closed. There is no dead state. |
| **The confirm, they heard nothing** | As before: the ask is deleted, they read as never asked, they can be asked again. |
| **The confirm, they heard** | *‹company› opened this ask in their portal.* (or *heard from us about this ask.*) *So the ask is kept, and it closes as we took it back. Their portal says we took it back, and no email goes to them. A closed ask does not open again.* The button reads **Take the ask back** in both. |
| **What changes** | The ask closes with a third answer, ours: its chip reads **we took it back**. One line on the company's Activity says who took it back, when, and what they had heard. |
| **What stays** | The ask, the company, every contact logged on the ask. Nothing is deleted on this path and nothing is sent. |
| **What the company sees** | Their portal's page for the job reads *‹we› took this invitation back. Nothing more is needed on it.*, and the home's Before list reads *‹we› took it back*. A page left open that tries to write gets the same in a refusal, never *You passed on this one*. |
| **Refused, in words** | The trade is awarded to this ask. A statement of work names it. A quote is in. They already passed. It is already taken back. |

## The calls taken on defaults

Each is the smallest choice, and each can be flipped.

1. **One press, two outcomes, said in the window.** A second press with its own name would make the office judge what the company heard. The app knows, and the server knows more (an email that went), so the press is one and the window says which will happen. A stale screen can only err toward keeping: the server closes an ask the screen thought nobody had heard about, and never deletes one they heard about.
2. **The answer sits on `declined_why`, and the ask's status stays `declined`.** Every reader of an ask as out reads it right with no change. Only the words learn the third answer, through one function, `askTakenBack`.
3. **No reason is asked for.** Will not do it and Cannot do it ask why, since the reason is the company's and is kept on their record. Ours is not about them. The company's Activity line says what they had heard, which is why the ask was kept.
4. **Nothing is sent.** The portal reads right when they next open it. An email that tells a company an invitation is withdrawn is a new message with its own wording to approve, so the guide says to call.
5. **It is not a pass.** It is left out of the bench's *passed* count and of *Times they passed* in the company's window, and the price line reads *We took the ask back from ‹company›.*
6. **A closed ask stays closed.** Nothing reopens an ask today, a pass included, and this builds no reopen. The window says so before the press.
7. **A quoted ask is still refused.** A quote is the company's work and stays on their record. So walk 1a's second test company, asked and quoted, still cannot leave the bench.
8. **The bench's Remove is not changed.** A company with an ask we took back was asked and told, so it stays.
