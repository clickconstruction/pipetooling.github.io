# v2.7850 — Stages: one rule for the stage a line's name reads as (2026-11-18)

Punch list #397, PR 6 of the train. One migration, `20261118210000_stage_kind_one_rule.sql`, and the app's readers. No edge function.

## Why

Nothing stores "this line is Rough In". Each reader works it out from the line's name, and there were three of them: the job's (`stageRank`), the bid's (`bidStageOfLine`) and the database's (`stage_checklist_kind_of`). They disagreed, which is how two screens come to say different things about one job.

The owner was shown the 35 real lines the two app readers disagreed on. The bid's reader was the wrong one: it found the word anywhere in the name, so a service line such as *installed shower trim* read as the Trim stage. He picked the fuller fix, with the migration: *B, with migration*.

## The rule

1. The **start** of the name gives the stage, after a leading *Stage 2 —* or *3.* is dropped.
2. ***Set trim*** is the trim stage said the other way round.
3. A name that also holds the word of a **different** bid stage stands for none. *Rough in, top out and trim* is the whole job on one line. Words of the same bid stage may sit together: *Rough in and underground*, *Trim and final*.

## What changed

- **`stageRank`** (`src/lib/jobs/stageRecognition.ts`) carries rules 2 and 3. `stageGroupOfRank` names the bid stage a rank falls in.
- **`bidStageOfLine`** (`src/lib/bids/stageEarnedValue.ts`) reads through `stageRank`. Its own pattern is gone. Final and Underground now fall in Trim and Rough, as the pay application already read them (`bidStageOfJobLine`, which uses the same grouping now).
- **`stage_checklist_kind_of`** in the database is replaced to match, and now drops a leading *Stage 2 —* as the app does. `docs/migrations/20261118210000_stage_kind_one_rule.md` has the before and after.
- **`STAGE_NAME_TABLE`** (`src/lib/jobs/stageNameTable.ts`): 37 made-up names in the shapes real lines take, with the stage each must read as. The app's test runs the table through `stageRank` and checks the SQL scenario asserts every row against the database's function, so the two cannot drift again.

## Measured on the live lines, 2026-11-18

979 real lines were read through the old rule and the new one, counts only:

- **On the job's side, 7 lines read differently.** Five are named *Set trim*, all on paid jobs, and now read as Trim. One is a Waiting job's single line that names all three stages. It read as Rough In and now reads as none, so it stops taking the Rough In checklist. One is a billed job's invoice line that starts with *Final* and names the top-out. It reads as none.
- **On the bid's side, 2 lines read differently**, both named *Final*, on jobs that came from a bid. Bid vs actual now counts them with Trim.
- The bid's reader had also matched 25 service lines by a word in mid-sentence. None of those sits on a job from a bid, so no screen showed them. They can no longer match.
