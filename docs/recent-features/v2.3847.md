# v2.3847 — Burn: a half-empty bid figure no longer stands as the whole job's budget (2026-09-26)

The owner's screenshot 2026-09-26: the Pipeline's *3 jobs burning* card read **J892 Megan Connell · 1355% spent at 30% done** on a $37,745 job. Client only, no migration.

## The bug

`snapshot_job_budget_from_bid` (v2.3297) writes whatever the bid's estimate carries. J892's row: 47 hours on 3 of 12 rows, **no labor rate** (labor $0), **no materials** (`materials_source: none`), $608.65 of driving and travel — `total_direct_usd` **$608.65**, `completeness.usable` false. Three readers took that total as the job's direct budget:

- [`useJobBudgetFootings`](../../src/hooks/useJobBudgetFootings.ts) (the Pipeline burn card and Job Summary's Burn column) read `kind` + `total_direct_usd` and set the ◆ footing on any positive total;
- `budgetForBurn` in [`jobBudget.ts`](../../src/lib/jobs/jobBudget.ts) (the Costs tab's Burn model and verdict) returned `directUsd` for any bid / typed row;

so J892's $8.2k of spend read as 1355 % of budget, while the Budget card under it promised that "components without a figure on the bid burn against the assumption". On prod every one of the 18 bid snapshots is unusable; four open jobs carried a non-zero partial total and read a wrong whole-job burn — J892 ($609, driving), J879 ($689, driving), J775 ($369, driving), J1007 ($64,168, materials only).

## The rule

A bid or typed footing stands for the **whole-job** burn only when it carries **both a labor figure and a materials figure** (`budgetStandsForJob`). Subs and other may be 0 — many jobs have neither — but labor or materials at 0 means the snapshot never found them, and spend on them would read against nothing. Otherwise the job burns against the assumption (price × (1 − target)), glyph ≈, and the components keep reading against what the bid did carry. A nearly complete bid (hours on 88 % of rows, a rate, a takeoff — the kernel's own example) still stands; `completeness.usable` is the Labor tab's stricter head-of-tab rule and was left alone.

## The change

- [`jobBudget.ts`](../../src/lib/jobs/jobBudget.ts): `budgetStandsForJob(resolved)`; `budgetForBurn` returns the direct cost only when it stands; `jobBudgetFootingFromRow(row)` — the many-jobs form, resolve + the rule, for the hook.
- [`useJobBudgetFootings`](../../src/hooks/useJobBudgetFootings.ts) selects the component columns and completeness and calls `jobBudgetFootingFromRow`, so the Pipeline card and Job Summary fall to ≈ on a partial row.
- [`jobCostsVerdict.ts`](../../src/lib/jobs/jobCostsVerdict.ts): the Direct cost row's *≈ $X assumed (65 % of price)* reads off the burn model's budget, so it shows whenever the model runs on the assumption — not only when there is no row.
- [`JobCostsBurnSection`](../../src/components/jobs/JobCostsBurnSection.tsx): the cumulative chart draws the dashed *◆ bid figure* line only when the footing stands (was: any non-assumed row, so a $609 line sat at the floor of the chart).
- [`JobBudgetCard`](../../src/components/jobs/JobBudgetCard.tsx): the *Why* sentence adds "…and so does the job as a whole until labor and materials both have one".

## Not changed (noted)

Every prod snapshot has `labor_rate: null` — `bid_estimate_breakdown` finds no rate on any of these bids, so no bid can stand until the rate lands (the Labor tab's *rate set* chip). That is the linking backlog the ≈ chip already counts, not this fix.
