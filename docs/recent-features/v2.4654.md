## The day of a charge is the day of the swipe

Found on the live look: Mercury's `posted_at`, which the app keys "the day" on everywhere, is the settlement time. It comes a median 8.2 hours after the swipe (`raw.createdAt`), 32 hours at the 90th percentile, and on a later company day for two charges in five; swipes fall in working hours, postings overnight. The queue now keys each charge's day and the time on its line on `raw.createdAt`, falling back to `posted_at`. The date floor, the over-2-days count and the staff RPC stay on `posted_at`. History keeps the posting day until `list_card_charges_window` carries the swipe time.

In the 90 days, where the swipe day and the posting day each had one clocked job and the two differed, the office chose the posting day's job 48 times and the swipe day's 6. The Assign window shows "their clock sessions that day" from the posting day, so it most likely led there: about 48 charges in 90 days may sit on the next day's job. That fix is its own PR (below).

## Kernel: rule set v2

`TALLY_SUGGESTION_RULES_VERSION = 2`:

- A refund is not history: a sorted charge whose splits sum to 0 or above feeds no store run and no same-day chip (`list_card_charges_window` returns refunds since v2.4611).
- The split by hours is no longer a chip (the office split evenly 35 times and by hours 0); its amounts stay on every line of a two-job day.
- The day carries the facts its evidence sentence reads: each clocked job's sessions, the bid or no-job time, the scheduled jobs, the neighbouring days.
- `TallyCharge.madeAt` (the swipe time) replaces `postedAt` on unsorted charges.

Replayed 2026-10-06 over the last 90 days of sorted card charges (839 charges, 542 sorting visits) as the queue reads them: each charge on its swipe day, history on its posting day, each day rebuilt as it stood when it was sorted. Keyed on the posting day, as rule set v1 was, `clock-one-job` agrees 72% (268 of 370). **Agreement by the swipe day is lower because the past sorts followed the posting day the old window showed; the 60 misses to a job clocked the day before or after are that.** Agreement is with one sorter's choices (one person sorted 98.6% of the 839), not correctness, and clock sessions are read as stored today. Nothing is `sure`; the levels are unchanged.

**The chips** held the answer for 605 of the 839 at the cap of 5 (577 at 4, 610 at 6, 616 uncapped); the median day shows 4.
