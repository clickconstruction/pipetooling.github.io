# v2.6441 — Charges by crew, PR 1: a person's standing, the rate card by crew, the markup on materials, and the crew-day kernels (punch list #254) (2026-11-03)

The owner, 2026-11-03, after #251's charge rate landed as one number: the rate is really per crew-hour and depends on who was out that day. A master with a helper is $285, two techs together are $285, a tech alone is $185, a helper alone is $75; for the Gray Fox development the master-with-a-helper way was used throughout, whoever clocked. Materials want a markup over cost the same way. The card `to-dos/charges-by-crew.md` holds the whole train and its canvas; this first PR gives the facts a home and the kernels their rules. Nothing prints yet.

## What changed

- **A person's standing** (migration `20261103070000_charges_by_crew.sql`): `users.charge_standing` — master · tech · helper · none, null reading as none — a plain profile field the office roles may set, on the person's desk under Access as **Charged as**, with a line under it saying what the standing means on the card (`PersonDeskAccessSection.tsx`).
- **The rate card** (`RatesChargedByCrewSettingsBlock.tsx`, Settings → Jobs & billing): seven rows for the crew's hour — master with a helper (the v2.6431 key `hourly_charge_rate_v1`, so a rate saved there stands), two techs, master alone, a tech alone, two helpers, a helper alone, and each person beyond two — self-contained over `app_settings` like the trip charge amounts. The migration seeds the owner's four numbers; the three other rows are hers and ship blank.
- **Markup on materials** (`MaterialsMarkupSettingsBlock.tsx`): one percent over cost in `app_settings.materials_markup_pct_v1`, the line under the field reading it both ways; blank means materials at cost, and the kernel says so.
- **A job's rule** (same migration, no screen yet): `jobs_ledger.charge_rule` crew · shape · flat, `charge_shape`, `materials_markup_pct`; the existing `hourly_charge_rate` is the flat rate. The Charges window (PR 3) and Edit Job write them.
- **The kernels** (`src/lib/charges/`, pure, tested): `rateCard.ts` (the standings, the shapes, the keys, `crewShapeOf` — a master with anyone is a master with a helper, a tech with anyone below a master is two techs, two helpers are two helpers, everyone past two an extra person — and `crewRateFor`, which names a missing row instead of pricing); `crewDays.ts` (`buildCrewDays`: sessions by job and day into crew-days, the crew's hours the longest day of anyone charged, priced from the card or the job's rule; `crewDaysByWeek` for the paper's lines; `jobChargeRuleFrom`); `materialsCharged.ts` (`chargeMaterials`: cost plus the markup to the cent, pass-through and left-off adjustments, `marginOfMarkup`); `moneyParts.ts` (the owner's rule that the cents print smaller than the dollars: one split for every surface).
- **Settings search** finds both cards. **Tests**: `rateCard.test.ts`, `crewDays.test.ts`, `materialsCharged.test.ts` on the owner's examples and a master-with-a-helper job rule; render smokes on both cards.

## Decisions

- **Rates by shape, not by person.** Two techs out together are one $285 crew-hour, each alone $185: the rate belongs to the crew on the day. A person carries only a standing.
- **The two highest standings decide, and a blank row is named.** The owner's rows for a master alone, two helpers and each person beyond two are not guessed; a day they would price reads *The rate card has no rate for …* until she fills them.
- **The crew's hours are the longest day of anyone charged.** A finer overlap rule waits until the owner wants one.
- **Half a cent rounds up**, whatever floating point made of the product: $806.98 at 25% is $1,008.73.
