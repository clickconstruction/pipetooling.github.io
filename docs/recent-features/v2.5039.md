# v2.5039 — Bids and Crew P&L: what the trucks cost per field hour, wear included (2026-10-09)

Client, plus migration `20261010008000_fleet_truck_rate.sql`, which Punchlist pushes after merge. No edge function. Wheels PR 3 from `to-dos/crew-pnl-and-wheels.md`, the owner's call of **2026-10-09**, sent by Punchlist, with the four choices Punchlist confirmed:

1. **The rate is Review's.** A person's vehicle rate is People → Vehicles' `fixedRate`: the override, else a company truck's insurance + registration + service + wear ÷ its holder's field hours, $0 on their own vehicle. Fuel stays on the jobs.
2. **Wear** is the truck's latest replacement value on or before today spread over a five-year life, pro-rated to the 90-day window. No value adds $0, and the Wheels row says *no replacement value on file*. A latest reading of $0 ends the wear.
3. **Crew P&L** gains a Vehicle part per person: the rate × their field hours in the range (any job but the office job, Review's field hours), at today's 90-day rate. A Vehicle column, a total and a footnote; profit nets it, Labor Cost stays wages.
4. **Bids** shows *trucks $X/field h*: every vehicle's fixed costs and wear ÷ the crew's field hours over 90 days. **Shown, not added**: the burden factor and the driving line already carry the truck, so adding it would count it twice. A small `SECURITY DEFINER` function returns the number so estimators, who cannot read the fleet, see it.

**The $50 default.** `DEFAULT_SUB_LABOR_EQUIVALENT_RATE` (Crew P&L's sub-equivalent rate) is untouched and stays $50. It was never a per-hour vehicle fallback; plan item 2 (deriving it from the loaded field average) waits until vehicle records exist.

## What changed

- **The kernel** (`src/lib/people/wheels.ts`):
  - `WHEELS_WEAR_LIFE_YEARS = 5` and `truckWearForWindow(value, days)`.
  - `truckRunningCost` takes `replacementValueUsd`, returns `wear` and `hasReplacementValue`, and puts wear in `total` and `fixedRatePerFieldHour`.
  - `fleetTruckRate(trucks, crewFieldHours, days)` sums every vehicle's insurance, registration, service and wear and divides by the crew's field hours.
  - The Wheels note says *no replacement value on file* when a truck has none.
- **The loader** (`src/lib/people/wheelsData.ts`):
  - `loadReplacementValues` reads each vehicle's latest value on or before today, fail-soft.
  - The snapshot carries `fleet` (`{fixedUsd, ratePerFieldHour, fieldHours, trucks}`).
  - `loadWheelsFixedRates` gives the people's rates without the card-fuel read, for Crew P&L.
- **The migration** (see `docs/migrations/20261010008000_fleet_truck_rate.md`): `fleet_truck_rate_per_field_hour(p_today)` returns `{rate, fixed_usd, field_hours, trucks, days}` to the office and estimators. On a private local Postgres it gave $3,245.30 ÷ 18 h = $180.29 over the same fixture the loader test holds; `fleetTruckRateSql.test.ts` pins its window, wear life, session filter and rules to the kernel. Its arithmetic, run as a plain read on prod for 2026-10-09, gave $4,754.85 over 13 vehicles ÷ 3,905.2 h = $1.22, the line the dev server's Wheels showed that day.
- **Bids → Labor** (`useFleetTruckRate`, `BidsLaborNewView`): the crew-rate card's *trucks $X/field h = $F insurance, registration, service and wear on N trucks ÷ H field h (90 d) · shown, not added*. Hidden when the read fails, the caller is refused, or the crew has no field hours. The bottom line is unchanged.
- **Crew P&L** (`crewPnlSummary.ts`, `JobsCrewPnlTab`):
  - `crewPnlVehicleRatesFromWheels` keeps every deal but None with a rate, as Review does.
  - `buildCrewPnlSummary` takes `vehicleRates` and `officeJobId`; each row gains `fieldHours`, `vehicleRate` and `vehicleCost`, totals gain `vehicleCost`, and `'vehicleCost'` sorts.
  - The tab reads the rates and the office job on mount. A failed read leaves no Vehicle part and the footnote says so.
- **People → Vehicles → Wheels**: a Wear column on the trucks table (a dash with no value on file), parked trucks carry their wear, and the fleet line under the trucks names the number Bids shows.
- **People → Review**: the math drawer's empty-truck words read *no insurance, registration, service or replacement value on file for the truck*.
