# v2.8433 — Document builder: the send button names who gets the email by their whole name (2026-11-24)

A small fault the #416 walk found and left (`to-dos/document-builder-whats-left.md`, walk 1i): the press that sends to one party named the contact by the first word of its name, *Send to ZZ*. Client only: no migration, no edge function.

## The cause

`sendBox` in `src/lib/documentBuilder/send.ts` built the press from `firstName(to.name)`, the name's first word. A first word is a first name only when the name is a person's, and the record does not say which a name is. A GC's first contact is the customer's own record, named for the company (`builderPartiesFrom` hands `contactsOf` the customer's name), and a contact person can be a desk.

Reproduced on made-up rows before the change: a GC named *ZZ TEST GC On Notice* with a contact *ZZ Test Sink* and a contact *Accounts Payable* drew *Send to ZZ*, *Send to ZZ* and *Send to Accounts*.

## What changed

- `pressName` replaces `firstName` in `send.ts`: the contact's whole name with its spaces collapsed, or the address when the record keeps no name. The press reads *Send to Dana Whitlock*, *Send to Northgate Builders*, *Send to Accounts Payable*.
- No rule tries to tell a person from a company by the look of a name. Any such rule prints a meaningless word for some name, and the whole name never does.
- The role stays where it was, in the **To** list (*Cy Doyle, office*). The one press under the list and its count words are unchanged.
- Help: *use the document builder* and *set the insurance a job's subs must carry* show the press with a whole name.

## Tests

- `send.test.ts`: a person, a company's own record, a desk and a test row each read whole; doubled spaces collapse; a record with no name reads by its address.
- `BuilderSendList.render.test.tsx`: the box on a GC whose first contact is the company's own record reads the company's name, the second contact reads whole, and no press reads *Send to ZZ*.
- Not measured: the press's width on a phone with a long name. The press sits in a row that wraps, and a button's words wrap by default; no harness was drawn for it.
