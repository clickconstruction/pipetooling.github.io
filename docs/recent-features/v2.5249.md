# v2.5249 — Messages: who a contact is, whose move it is, and the stamps, behind the scenes (2026-10-12)

The Messages build's cut 1 kernels (the spine), lifted from the Messages prototype (branch `spike/gc-mode`,
`to-dos/gc-mode/messages/prototype.html` v1.178) per `BUILD_PLAN.md` and `BUILD_HELPERS.md` (cluster 1). Pure `.ts`
with unit tests; no table, no screen, no edge function, no migration. Cut 1's SQL follows as its own PR.

- **`src/lib/contacts/kernel.ts`**: a contact is a view over a person the app already has. `ContactRecord` points at
  exactly one of `customers` (a customer who is one person), `customer_contact_persons` (a person at a customer or an
  architect's office), `gc_companies` (a trade partner's main contact), `gc_company_people` (anyone else the trade
  partner named) or `people` (the roster), or at none for a number nobody has named. `customer_contacts` and
  `gc_customer_contacts` are call logs, not people, so nothing points at them (`BUILD_PLAN.md`'s `customer_contact_id`
  meant `customer_contact_persons`). `Contact.name` names the person when the record is a company with no row for
  them. `firstName` takes `goesBy` first, else `greetingName`, main's titled rule ("Dr. Priya Raman" is "Dr. Raman"),
  now kept here and read by Owner Billing's `customerGreeting`. `matchChannel` is the matching rule by number and
  address, compared normalized through `customerSimilarity`'s `normalizeCustomerPhone` / `normalizeCustomerEmail`; a
  number two people share lands on the one it last carried a message for. Opting out is per number:
  `ContactChannel.optedOutAt`, `textableNumbers`, `isOptedOut` (every number stopped and no address). `nameContact` /
  `applyNamePlan` are *Who is this?*: re-point the contact and keep its thread, or, when someone already is that
  person, fold the number and the thread into their contact. A person's row is one contact; a company's row (a
  customer, a trade partner) is one contact per name, so a second person there with no row of her own (Dee at Alamo
  Concrete, known only by a cell) keeps her own contact under her own name.
- **`src/lib/messages/thread.ts`**: `ThreadMessage` (with the `contact_channels` id it came from or went to, the
  opt-out line's three values, first, silence and held, and a call's length in seconds), `threadOf`, `threadRows` (a
  divider at each new Chicago day, events as their own rows, a repeated job within a day as its number alone, v1.158),
  `lastIn` / `lastOut` / `lastMessage`, `courtOf` (owe and a promised day as optional inputs, cut 2's), `agoWords`,
  `whenWords`, `snippetOf`, `channelWord`, `callLength`, `isLong`, and the Chicago clock words (`clockTime`,
  `dayLabel`, `lineWhen`, `fullWhen`) through `utils/dateUtils`.
- **`src/lib/messages/stamps.ts`**: Them · Us (`stampPair`, `stampShort`, the `(+6)` `agoMark`, `stampFull`), the
  folded header's `stampSentence` as parts with the when marked strong, and the hover card's `stampCalendar`.
- **`src/lib/messages/messagesDemo.ts`**: the shared fixture every cluster imports: the prototype's eight people, six
  jobs and twenty messages at Fri Oct 2 2026, 11:45 AM Chicago, made-up records, `555-01xx` numbers and `.example`
  addresses only.

Changed from the prototype, on purpose: the stamp sentence is two sentences where the prototype joined them with a
semicolon (the plain-words rule); `whenWords` counts weeks back rounded up, so last week's Sunday reads "last week"
(the prototype read it "two weeks ago"); a date in another year carries its year; days are Chicago's, never the
browser's; an event row under its day divider carries its time alone.

Tests: `contacts/kernel.test.ts`, `messages/thread.test.ts`, `messages/stamps.test.ts`, `messages/messagesDemo.test.ts`
(48), green under UTC, Tokyo and Honolulu clocks too. Six pins were broken on purpose and caught: the week rounding,
an event breaking a job's run, the semicolon, the shared-number ranking, a UTC day, a dropped title.
