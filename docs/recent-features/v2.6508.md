# v2.6508 — A web address in an email’s words is one press, and the sender’s bold reads bold (2026-11-04)

Grace, 2026-11-04, with a screenshot of the message window on Glenn Gelwin's *Northeast Bible Church Elementary Building @ San Antonio - Invitation to Bid*: "when looking at an email, I would like the option to see it as rich text or in the current format, and I would like to see a link icon in blue that if clicked takes a user to that link with one click to save them time". The viewer drew the body as plain text: the sender's bold as literal stars (`*Marrex Builders*`, `*Scope of Work*:`), the plans link written twice, once bare and once in angle brackets (`**https://maxxbuilders.box.com/s/… <https://maxxbuilders.box.com/s/…>*`), none of it pressable, while the same link sat above under Attachments as *Box link · a link, not a file · Open*. No migration, no edge function.

## Whether the HTML part exists today — it does not

The first thing to find out, and the answer that shaped the PR. The bids@ reader (`gmail-sync` over `_shared/gcKernels/lib/emailIn/gmailMessage.ts`'s `readGmailMessage`) walks every MIME part, reads both `text/plain` and `text/html` into memory, and keeps **the text alone**: the plain part when there is one, else the HTML part read as text (`htmlToText`), cut at `EMAIL_TEXT_MAX`. The HTML part is read once more for its `href`s, which join the body's own URLs as the line's `links` and become `message_attachments` rows of kind `link` (the Attachments chip). Then it is dropped. `messages.body` is text; `messages` has no HTML column; no bucket holds the raw message for bids@ mail. The one place a raw email is kept is the My email tag (v2.6266, `gmail-mine`), which files `mine/<line id>.eml` for a conversation a person tags onto a job or a bid — not the path an invitation to bids@ takes, and a MIME file the client has no reader for. So there is nothing to draw a rich view from, and this PR does not invent it: it builds the plain view's two improvements and names the HTML question as the owner's card.

## What changed

- **One kernel, `src/lib/messages/bodyLinks.ts`** (pure, 9 tests on Glenn's words and the edge cases): `bodyPieces(body)` reads the words into text and link pieces, each bold or not. A web address is `https?://…` with the sentence's trailing punctuation left outside it; the mail client's doubled form `url <url>` and a bare `<url>` fold to one link; two different addresses stay two. Bold is `*words*` as a mail client writes it (the opening mark at a start or after a space, the closing one before a space or punctuation, no star and no blank line inside, one or two stars so Markdown's `**words**` reads the same), so `2*3*4`, a bullet's `* `, `*** CONFIDENTIAL ***` and an unclosed mark stay as they are; a span may cross one line break, as the project name in Glenn's email does. `bodyLinks` lists the addresses once each; `bodyHtml` writes the print page's words. Words with no mark and no address come back as one piece, so every thread line that read plain before reads the same.
- **`MessageBody.tsx`** draws the pieces: a link is the address's own words in `var(--text-link)` (`#2563eb` light, the house token's `#60a5fa` dark) with a small chain-link SVG beside it, one `<a target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">`, `overflow-wrap: anywhere` so a Box address never scrolls a phone sideways; bold is `<strong>`. The message window (`MessageWindow.tsx`) draws its words and its print page through it (`bodyHtml`); the thread's line (`ThreadLine.tsx`) draws its words through it in both of its branches, and the line's own press already steps aside for an `a` as it does for a button, so the link opens without the window opening over it. Copy and Share still carry the words as they came. The Attachments chip stays: it is the filed copy; the body's link is the fast door.
- **The guide** *find every message with a person in one place* gains *Open a link in an email's words* (the press, the doubled address shown once, the bold, the chip beside it, the thread's line, and that an email reads as plain words because the layout is not kept).
- **Punch list #259**, `to-dos/email-rich-text.md`: the rich-text switch Grace asked for, held for the owner's call — what keeping the HTML part would store, where, and why.

## The Attachments row (Grace's two more points, the same day)

"I would like to move the open button to the left and better understand what the blue arrow is for." The blue ↗ square was `FileBadge`'s kind mark for a link (`aria-hidden`, no press, no title): the row had one door, **Open**, and one mark nobody could read. Now:

- **One mark means a link everywhere in the viewer**: the badge for a link is the same chain link the words draw beside a web address (`LinkMark.tsx`, in `var(--text-link)`), on the Attachments row and on the chip under the line, with its meaning in its title, *A link, not a file. Open goes to its address.* (`LINK_MARK_TITLE`). The PDF, picture and file badges stay as they were.
- **Open sits right after the words.** The row's words took the row's whole width (`flex: 1`), which pinned Open, Cut it off and Save to Drive to the window's far edge. The words now take only their width, so each row's presses follow its words on the left and wrap under them on a phone, never off the edge; every file row moves the same way.

## Tests

- `bodyLinks.test.ts` (9): Glenn's body folds to one link and six bold spans with no star but the sender's own stray one; the colon after a span; Markdown's double star; arithmetic, bullets and unclosed marks untouched; trailing punctuation and bare brackets; two different addresses; plain words as one piece; `bodyLinks` and `bodyHtml`.
- `MessageWindow.render.test.tsx` (+1, and the Attachments case widened): the window with Glenn's words draws one link with the mark, target and rel, the four bold spans, no star; its print page carries the `<a>` and `<b>`; Copy writes the words as they came; the link row's mark is the chain link with its title, the PDF badge still reads PDF, and on every row the words take no extra width and Open is the element right after them.
- `ThreadLine.render.test.tsx` (new, 2): the line draws the same; a press on the link does not open the message and a press on the words does.

## Left out, on purpose

- No rich-text switch, no sanitizer, no DOMPurify: nothing to feed them (above). The guide says so in one sentence.
- A bare `www.` address with no scheme, and a `mailto:`, stay plain words: the reader's own URL rule is `https?://`, and a guessed address is a wrong press.
- A line a later cut's `lineBody` slot replaces (the estimate email's **Open estimate #N** door, v2.5698) keeps that cut's body.
