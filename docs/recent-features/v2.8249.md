# v2.8249 — GC mode: a trade's draw is due 10 days after the customer pays (2026-11-21)

Taunya's call, 2026-11-21. The agreement a trade signs in GC mode says a trade is paid only after the customer pays us, within 10 days. GC mode said something else: an approved draw was due 10 days after we approved it, whether or not the customer had paid (`PAY_WITHIN_DAYS`, the call of 2026-10-03). A trade could hold the app's day against the paper. The app now counts as the paper does. No migration, no function, no deploy.

## The rule

`src/lib/gc/drawPayClock.ts`, pure:

- **Which bill a draw rides on.** The first pay application we sent the customer on or after the day we approved the draw (`ownerAppForDraw`). The work we approved by then is the work that bill asks for. It reads what happened, so it needs no cut-off setting. The final pay application, which asks for retainage, is never the one.
- **The day.** `PAY_WITHIN_DAYS` after that bill's `paidOn` (`drawClock`).
- **No day yet.** While no bill has gone since the approval the draw waits on `ourBill`. While the bill is unpaid it waits on `customer`. Either way it has no pay-by day and is never late.
- **A job that keeps no bills.** With `ownerBilling` null, or with no `payApps` list, there is no payment to count from. Those draws keep the day counted from approval (`fromApproval`).
- **Retainage is as it was.** A final draw keeps `tradeCloseout().opensOn`, which already waited on the customer's final payment.

## Where it shows

| Place | Before | Now |
|---|---|---|
| Draws → **To pay**, and each draw's row | *pay by* approval + 10 | *pay by* the customer's payment + 10, or *due 10 days after the customer pays us*, or *… pays our next bill* |
| The card's sentences (`progress.ts`) | *Pay it by …* | The same when it has a day, else *It is due 10 days after the customer pays us.* |
| Bill day → From the trades (`rowMove`, `TradeDraw.due`) | *we pay by …* | The same when it has a day, else the waiting words |
| Bill day's dates | *Within 10 days. Of each draw's approval.* | *Within 10 days. After the customer pays us for the draw.* |
| Money ahead (`cashMoves`) | An approved draw on approval + 10. A draw asked and not approved on today + 10. | An approved draw with a day, as before. One waiting on the customer on that bill's expected day + 10 (`afterCustomer`, a new kind of move). One asked and not approved on our next bill's expected day + 10. |
| Money ahead, expected (`expectedDraws`) | Each next draw on bill day + 10 | On the day that customer should pay that bill, + 10. Bill day + 10 when the customer's usual days are not known. |

A bill with no day to be paid counts from today in `cashMoves`, so the money out is never left off the weeks.

## What it does to the forecast

On the made-up jobs the weeks of money lose their low point. Counted from approval, the trades' draws fell before either customer paid and the weeks went under. Counted from the customer's payment they fall after it, most of them past the six weeks shown. That is the rule doing what it says: we no longer plan to pay a trade before we are paid.

## Left

- **The trade's portal does not say the rule.** Its only line on an approved draw is *approved, payment coming*, with no day. `portal.ts`'s `payByOf` and `portalPay`, and `portalI18n.ts`'s `payIntro`, `payBy` and `payWasDue`, still read the day from approval. Nothing draws them. They are copied to the edge functions, so changing them means redeploying `gc-trade-portal` and `customer-portal`. The session that puts a pay table on the portal changes them then.
- **A part payment.** A bill's `paidOn` is set when the customer has paid it in full. A draw waits until then, even when the customer has paid most of it.
- **Texas sets 7 days.** The prompt-pay law gives a contractor 7 days to pay a sub after the owner pays. Whether an agreement can make it 10 is a question for the attorney. The day is one constant, `PAY_WITHIN_DAYS`.
- **Not walked on prod.** No GC-mode job on prod has an approved draw waiting, so the screens were read through their render tests.
