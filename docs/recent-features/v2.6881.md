# v2.6881 — Labels: a run of tape strips shares one blank lead-in (punch list #304) (2026-11-10)

The owner, with strips coming off his P-touch: *right now when we print there are white squares of paper that are being cut off and are not being used. Is there any way to make our printing any more efficient?* The cutter sits about an inch past the print head. Every strip was its own job that fed out and cut, so the inch of blank tape between the head and the cutter was thrown away once a strip.

## What changed

- **`lib/materials/ptouchRaster.ts`**: `cmdChainToNext` (`ESC i K` with bit 3 clear, chain printing) and `ptouchLabelJob(lines, tapeMm, chain)`. A chained label is printed and not fed out, so the next label prints straight against it and the automatic cut falls on their seam. `chainsToNext(index, total)` chains every label of a run but its last, which is sent as before and feeds the run out.
- **`lib/materials/ptouchUsb.ts`**: `print` sends each label's job with that flag. Each label is still its own job, gated by the status request and waited out by its own time: the one byte is the whole difference from the sequence v2.6865 proved.
- **`LabelsPrintModal`**: a run that stops part way adds that a printed strip may still be inside the printer (`PTOUCH_STRIP_INSIDE`), since a chained strip stops short of the cutter.

## Why this way

v2.6865 tried one job of several pages (`0C` between pages) and it left the printer deaf. Chain printing keeps the proven one-label job and changes one mode byte, and it is what Brother's own software does when it prints label after label.

## Checked

Unit tests for the byte, which labels chain, and the job differing by that one byte; the window's smokes. **The owner printed a run on his PT-D610BT, 24 mm tape, Chrome on macOS: one white square for the run, every strip cut.** Not checked: 18 mm tape, a run that stops part way on the real printer, and what the printer does with a chained strip left inside when the next run starts later.
