# v2.6810 — A printed lien paper keeps the signature with its statement, and nothing of the browser prints on it (2026-11-09)

Reported by the office on 2026-11-09 with a two-page scan of a printed Mechanic's and Materialman's Lien Affidavit: "Is there a way to space out the notice better? [The] signature is going to be at the very top part of the 2nd page. Also, do we want the date and time to be printed on the top left corner of the pages?" Page 1 held the paper through item 10 and ended in white space; page 2 opened with the signature rule and the signer's name alone, then the notary's block and the statute line. Every sheet also carried the browser's own header and footer: the date and time, the document title, `about:blank` and `1/2`.

No, the date and time should not print, least of all `about:blank` on an instrument a county clerk records.

## Cause

`filingDocPrintHtml` (`src/lib/jobsDocuments/lienFilingDocuments.ts`) is the print document for the § 53.056 notice, the § 53.054 affidavit and the release of record from the Lien window's tabs (`LienFilingTabs`, **Print for notarization**), and for the legal statements (`legal/statementPrint.ts`).

- It had no page rule, so Chrome printed at its default margins with its *Headers and footers* on, and the window is opened blank, hence `about:blank`.
- Nothing tied the signature to what it swears to. The blocks flowed, and the page broke wherever the text ran out. The PDF twin (`filingDocPdfBlob`, **Download PDF** and the emailed PDF) broke the same way: line by line, with no look ahead.

## What changed

- **`filingKeepTogetherRuns(blocks)`** (pure): the runs a page must not split. A signature, the block just above it when that is a numbered item, a paragraph or a form line, and the notarial block or a second signature under it. The affidavit's run is item 10, the signature and the jurat; the release's is its last paragraph, the signature and the jurat; the notice's is the last form line and the signature (the § 53.254(g) statement under it is not part of the run).
- **The print page.** `@page { size: letter; margin: 0; }` leaves Chrome no room for its header and footer: its print window offers *Headers and footers* only when the page's top or bottom margin is above zero. The margins are the sheet's own instead. The sides are the body's padding (0.85 in, the old column width). The head and foot of every page (0.6 in) are a table header and footer the print engine repeats, blank on screen. Each run prints inside one `break-inside: avoid` block, and the statute footer line stays with the last run.
- **`appendToFilingPrintHtml(html, extra)`**: pages added behind a paper (the notice's pay page and its enclosed invoices) go inside the sheet, so they keep its page margins. `LienFilingTabs` called `html.replace('</body>', …)`, which would have put them outside it.
- **The PDF.** `filingDocPdfBlob` draws the paper, and where a run fell across two pages draws it again with that run starting a page (`drawFilingDocPdf`, at most one more pass per run). A run taller than a page still splits.

The words on every paper are the same blocks in the same order. No statutory wording changed.

## Decisions

- **Two clean pages, not one squeezed page.** A typical affidavit runs about a third over one Letter page at the paper's type size and leading. Fitting it would mean smaller type on a recorded instrument, and a long legal description would still spill. The rule is the keep-together run.
- **No page number drawn.** With zero page margins there is no margin box to draw the app's own "Page 1 of 2" in, and a counter of pages is not available to the body in Chromium. The PDF carries "Page 1 of 2" as it did.
- **The demand letter is not on this path.** Its **Print packet** opens a PDF, so it never carried the browser's header and footer; its pagination was not part of the report and was not measured.

## Proof

Chromium's print engine through the repo's Playwright (`page.pdf`, `preferCSSPageSize`), then `pdfinfo` and `pdftotext` per page; sample papers with made-up names.

- **Before (the builder on `main`, Chrome's default margins):** a typical affidavit prints item 10 on page 1 and the signature, the jurat and the footer on page 2, as the scan shows.
- **After:** item 10, the signature, the jurat and the footer on page 2; page 1 ends after item 9.
- **The sweep:** 41 affidavits, each one printed line longer than the last, two and three pages: the closing is on one page in all 41. The PDF: 31 lengths, 11 split before, 0 after.
- The notice still prints on one page, the notice with the homestead statement on two as before, the release on one; enclosed pages keep their margins.

Not observed from a session: Chrome's own print window (a session cannot open it). That it drops *Headers and footers* at zero top and bottom margins is its print preview's rule, and the stopgap below covers a browser that keeps them. Nothing was printed on a real job, since a print there files a sent copy.

## Stopgap until this is live

In the print window, open **More settings** and untick **Headers and footers**.
