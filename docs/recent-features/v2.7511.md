# v2.7511 — Lien filings from a scan: an assistant can record a filed lien and its stamped copy (2026-11-16)

PR 2 of the dev seat's lien filing verbs: **`plan_lien_filing`** → **`apply_lien_filing`** on `dev-mcp` (server 0.93.0). The owner, 2026-11-16, after the office filed eleven lien affidavits at two county clerks and scanned what came back: "long term I would like to not have to use chrome and I would like to build the verbs". The design was shown to him the same day and he picked: a staged upload for the scan, a hold when the paper and the desk differ, and filing, service and a read in the seat.

## What the pair does

It records a filing as the job's Lien window records one (Lien affidavit → Record the filing):

1. One `job_lien_filings` row, as the dev under RLS: kind `affidavit`, the claim, the county, the recording number, `filed_at`, `serve_due` (`serveDueForFiling`), and `fields` by the desk's own recipe (`buildLienDeskAffidavitFields`).
2. The desk's affidavit item is marked sent with the filing, as `syncLienDeskAfterRecord` marks it.
3. The scan goes into the job's Drive folder under Team only › Sent by us, through `drive-link`'s `put`, under the scan press's own name (`lienFiledScanName`), and its link goes onto the row as the saved copy, as the Filings list's own upload sets it.

The staged file is checked before step 1. The row comes before the file on purpose: a Drive door that fails then leaves a filing with no saved copy, which the next plan takes the scan for, and never a file with no filing.

The table's trigger writes the job's Activity line. Nothing is mailed and nobody is told. No wrapper RPC and no new migration: the bucket came with v2.7510.

## How a scan reaches the server

A staged upload, the route `plan_file_plans` opened. The plan commits to the file's name, size and sha256, and hands back a signed upload address in the private `dev-seat-staging` bucket, inside the dev's own folder. The agent PUTs the file. Apply reads the staged copy back as the dev, checks its size, its sha256 and its header, files it, and removes it.

Why this route: a scan is about 485 KB. As base64 in a call it is 650,000 characters the agent would have to write. A file in Drive or on a mail needs a person to put it there first. One file is one lien: a packet is split on the agent's machine, where the agent already opens the pages to read them.

## One plan, many filings

`filings` takes a visit to the clerk, at most 20. Each entry is shaped, read and judged on its own: `ready` with its own `plan_hash`, `held`, or `refused` in words. An apply takes one filing and its hash. So a filing that fails never stops the rest, and the reply to each apply names what landed. A job, a recording number or a file named twice in one call refuses both entries.

## What the plan checks

The caller reads the scan first and sends what the paper says (`paper_states`) and why it matched the job (`matched_because`). The plan leads with the job as a person would check it, then reads the paper against the desk:

- the claim, against today's reading of what the sent bills owe less a claim set by hand
- the months, and the day notice went to the owner
- the property address, the legal description and the owner
- the clerk's county against the property record's
- any job at the same street address that sits on another property record

Each difference names the door that fixes it. A held entry carries no hash. It is planned again once the desk is fixed, or with `accept_differences: true`, which records the filing as the desk reads it and says so.

It refuses: a job the desk lists no affidavit window for, a job with no approved affidavit unless `filed_outside_the_desk: true`, a second filing on the job, a recording number already on another job, a day ahead of today, and a file that is not a scan or is over 10 MB. A filing already on record with the same number and no saved copy takes the scan alone.
