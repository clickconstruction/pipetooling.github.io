# v2.7231 — Subs Ledger: by sub, one sub's statement, and on People → Subs (2026-11-13)

Punch list #347, PR 3. PR 2 (v2.7174) listed every sub payment by date on Jobs → Subs → Ledger. This is the same money by person, the statement for one person, and the door on People → Subs the owner asked for.

## What it is

- **By date | By sub** chips at the top of the Ledger. By sub is one row per person: payments in the period, their first and last day, paid, back-charged, and **We owe today**.
- **We owe today** is what is still open on their sheets, whatever the period. It is the Pay view's *Owed to subs* and People → Subs' Balance due, sheet by sheet. A sheet paid past its total is noted under the amount (*over by … on a sheet*) and taken off nothing, because nothing moves it to another sheet today. That note is where the credit the owner asked for will land.
- A person with nothing paid in the period keeps a row while we owe them, so the column foots to the Pay view. A crew sheet reads *payroll* and is in no total.
- **A name opens the statement** (so does picking the person in Paid to while By sub is on). Oldest first: *Sheet agreed* adds, a payment or back-charge takes away, and the last column is what we owed after each line. With a period picked the first row is *Brought forward*.
- **The foot is checked.** The statement's open-on-sheets figure is held against the roster's Balance due when the caller hands it in (People → Subs does). It reads *✓ checks*, or says both figures and why they can differ.
- **Print the statement** and **Download CSV** take one person's statement out. The print is the office's own report: nothing is sent to the sub.
- **People → Subs** has a **Roster | Ledger** switch. Ledger is this screen opened on By sub, at `/people?tab=subs&view=ledger`, and `&sub=<people id>` opens one person.
- **How paid** is a column on the by-date list, on the phone card, in the CSV (two columns, method and reference) and in the print. The search finds a check by its number.

## How it is read

- `src/lib/subWorkOrders/subPaymentsLedger.ts` gains `ledgerBySub`, `ledgerBySubTotals`, `buildSubStatement`, `statementCheckWords`, `subStatementCsv`, `subStatementHtml`, and the address pair `ledgerSubParam` / `ledgerSubKeyFromParam`. A line gains `method` and `methodRef`.
- **A sheet is dated no later than its first payment.** A sheet's day is its job date, else the day it was made. Where a payment went out before that day, the sheet leads on the payment's day, so a deposit never reads as money paid for nothing.
- **A statement of every date ends on what the sheets owe.** The kernel test holds `closing` and `owedToday` to the same sum `subLaborJobBalance` makes, for every person in the fixtures.
- **The search never trims a statement.** A line left out would break the running figure, so the statement reads the period and nothing else, and the search box gives way to the person's name.
- `sheetPaymentDates.ts` reads a payment's day through the ledger's `paymentDay`. The two were separate copies of one rule.

## Wiring

- `SubLedgerBySub.tsx`, new: the list and the statement, a table at a desk and cards on a phone. `subLedgerStyles.ts` holds the looks both files share.
- `SubPaymentsLedger` takes `by` / `onByChange` / `initialBy`, `sub` / `onSubChange` and `balanceDueByPersonId`, all optional. Jobs keeps the grouping and the person in the address (`by=sub`, `sub=`).
- `PeopleSubsLedger.tsx`, new: loads the sheets through `useSubLaborLedger`, read only, and only while the Ledger view is open. A sheet opens on Jobs through the existing `editLabor=<sheet id>` link.
- `PeopleSubsTab` draws the switch and hands the roster's Balance due by person to the ledger.
- No migration. No new query on Jobs.
