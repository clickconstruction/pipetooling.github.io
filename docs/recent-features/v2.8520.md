# v2.8520 — New Job and Edit Job: the address box shows addresses already on file (2026-11-24)

Punch list #439, piece 1 of seven. Client only: no migration, no edge function.

## What was there

The Job Address box asked Google alone. It never showed an address the company already had, so one place was typed two ways and its jobs stopped matching. A read of the jobs on file on 2026-11-24 found 24 pairs with the same house number, a street written alike and the same spot on the map.

## What changed

- **Already on file.** While someone types, the dropdown lists the addresses on file that match, above Google's rows, three at most. Each row shows the address as most of its jobs write it, how many jobs and bids are there, and the development when every one that has a development has the same one. What was typed is bold when the address begins that way. A street typed with no house number matches too, after the ones that begin as typed.
- **One list, one set of keys.** The on-file rows take the first indexes of the one listbox. The arrows and Enter run through both groups. Google's rows sit under *A new address*, and *powered by Google* shows only when Google's rows do.
- **A pick takes the spelling on file**, not a title-cased copy of what was typed. Under the box a green line then reads *On file. 25 jobs are at this address, in the development …*, its count and name drawn as values (`ValueSentence`).
- **One letter off.** An address that is not on file, with the same house number as one that is and a street that differs by a letter or two, a swap of two letters or a space (`FM1117` for `FM 1117`), asks once after the box is left: the two spellings side by side with the differing words marked, **Use the one on file** and **Keep mine**. **Keep mine** settles it for that address. Nothing blocks a save.
- **What it stays quiet about.** Another house number, a unit, a building, or a different number in the street is a different place, not a slip. So is an address with no house number.

## How it is built

- `src/lib/addresses/addressesOnFile.ts` (pure). One rule decides *the same address*: `normalizeAddressKey` from `developments/notInIt.ts`, the cleaned form the Development page already matches by. `addressesOnFile` groups jobs and bids under that key and leaves out the job being edited. `onFileMatches` finds what is being typed, treating the last typed word as half a word so `40 st` still finds Stone Rd. `closeOnFile` is the slip rule. `onFileSentence` and `closeOnFileFoot` are the words.
- `src/hooks/useAddressesOnFile.ts` reads `jobs_ledger`, `bids` and `developments` once the box is first focused, a thousand rows a page, shared by every box and kept for a minute. A read that fails answers an empty list and the box works as before.
- `JobAddressSuggestions.tsx` draws the group. `JobFormAddressOnFile.tsx` draws the line and the nudge. `JobFormIdentityFields.tsx` wires them, and `JobFormModal.tsx` hands in the job being edited.

## Design calls

- **Read on first focus, not when the form opens.** The Edit region is mounted with every job window. A read of every address on each open would be waste. The cost: a job opened for edit shows its *On file* line only once the box has been focused.
- **Words only, no map.** An address written differently at the same spot on the map is piece 2's question, with its own answers kept.
- **A unit is not a slip.** *Building 16* never offers *Building 20*. Whether they are one site is asked once, in piece 2, not guessed here.

## Tested

- `addressesOnFile.test.ts`, 23 cases: the grouping, the spelling kept, the job being edited, the typed match and its half word, the slip rule and what it stays quiet about, and the words against the plain-words rules.
- `JobFormIdentityFields.onFile.render.test.tsx`, 9 smokes: nothing is read before the first focus, the rows and their counts, a press and the line under the box, the keys through both groups, the on-file rows alone, the nudge and both its presses, the job being edited, and a refused read.
