# v2.7008 — Permits: a San Antonio loop address no longer names a lot on the opposite stretch (2026-11-12)

Punch list #320. The last thing v2.6997 left: a direction written behind the street was never compared. Grace asked to look at it, the look said what to build, and she said to build it.

## What was measured first (read-only, counts only, 2026-11-12)

The referee is the lot under the permit's own point. 40,000 San Antonio permit rows were read (most of them history), 27,639 addresses.

- 282 addresses carry a direction behind the street, about 1 in 100. Among commercial building permits it is 85 of 1,133. Every one is a numbered loop or highway.
- 196 of the 282 find a lot by their address. The picked row's directions against the permit's:

| the county's row against the permit | addresses | the lot under the point | another lot | no point |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| the directions agree | 158 | 152 | 5 | 1 |
| the county's row has no direction | 13 | 10 | 3 | 0 |
| one shared, written on the other side ("N 1604 W" for "W LOOP 1604") | 12 | 11 | 0 | 1 |
| the same two, swapped ("W 1604 N" for "N LOOP 1604 W") | 3 | 3 | 0 | 0 |
| one the same, one opposite (W for E) | 4 | 0 | 3 | 1 |
| nothing in common | 6 | 2 | 4 | 0 |

So "the directions must match" would be wrong: the city and the county write one stretch of a loop two ways 15 times here, and all 15 are the right lot. What marks another stretch is an **opposite** direction.

## What changed

- **Kernel** (`supabase/functions/_shared/permitParcels.ts`): `streetDirections` reads the direction in front of a street and the one behind it, off the same split `streetParts` uses (`splitStreet`; `StreetParts` itself is unchanged, so no kept question changes). `oppositeDirections` is true when a row carries the opposite of one of the asked directions and not that direction itself, letter by letter (NE against NW is opposite). `parcelFactsFromBexarAddress` takes a fourth argument, the asked directions, and passes by a row that is opposite unless its lot is under the permit's point. `bexarNeedsPoint` says when the point has to be asked first: several lots at the address, or a row there that is opposite.
- **`permit-lookup`**, Bexar's branch: the point is asked when `bexarNeedsPoint` says so, with the full columns, and that answer is read again when every row at the address was passed by, so the point is asked once. The card then says the owner was read at the permit's point. With no point, an address whose only rows are opposite says the county has no parcel.
- **Guide** `research-permits-for-work-to-bid.md`: two sentences on a loop's stretches.

## Measured through the new kernel, before the deploy

The same 282, the lookup's steps before and after:

| | before | after |
|---|---|---|
| by the address, the lot under the point | 178 | 178 |
| by the address, another lot | 15 | 8 |
| by the address, no point to referee | 3 | 2 |
| at the permit's point | 76 | 83 |
| no parcel | 10 | 11 |

Seven wrong lots become the lot under the point, each with another owner, 3 of them commercial building permits. One address with no point now says no parcel. No right lot changes and no card changes its words only. The point is asked for the direction alone once in 282.

300 more addresses with a direction in front only (a sample of 2,235): nothing changes on any of them.

Without the "unless it is under the point" part, a first simulation turned one right lot wrong: its row carries an opposite direction and the point says it is the lot all the same.

Not done: a kept answer is not asked again, as in v2.6997. The other counties' pick (`pickParcel`) is untouched: it was not measured.

Tests: `permitParcels.test.ts` (the directions around a street, what is opposite and what is one stretch written two ways, the row passed by, the row kept under the point, when the point is asked). Every name and number is made up.
