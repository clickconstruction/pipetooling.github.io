# v2.7463 — Lien desk: By job draws sooner (2026-11-16)

A look at the live desk found By job (`/lien-desk?tab=timeline`) blank for five to ten seconds on a cold open, under the words *Reading every unpaid job…*. This PR is the measured answer and the part of the wait that was By job's own.

## What the wait was made of

Timed on the local dev server against the live database, by the browser's own resource timings: three opens by address, three presses of **By job** from To do, and To do itself as the yardstick.

| | before | after |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **By job** pressed from another view (the desk already read) | 1.05, 1.06, 1.23 s | 0.56, 0.63, 0.63 s |
| By job by its address, from the desk's first read to the first row | 4.5 s | 3.1 s |
| To do by its address, the same span | 3.0 to 3.5 s | unchanged |

- **The work in the browser was never the wait.** The first row drew 20 to 50 ms after the last answer came back. The fold (`buildLienTimelineBook`) is not worth memoising.
- **No read was slow.** Every one took a tenth to a third of a second, the round trip to the database. The wait was the number of reads made one after another.
- **By job waited on the whole desk.** `useLienTimelineBook` was enabled only once `useLienDeskData` had handed over `data`, and it needs one thing from it, the desk's items. The desk's own read is about thirteen steps in a row, three seconds and more. Then By job made five steps of its own, another 1.1 s.
- **Part of what the look saw was the dev server's.** The page's code took 1.5 to 4.5 s to arrive before any read began, and the dev build runs each first read twice. The deployed app does neither.

## What changed

- **The book's reads run in three steps, where there were five** ([`lienTimelineBookRead.ts`](../../src/lib/jobs/lienTimelineBookRead.ts), `readLienBookRows`). Step 1 is the two RPCs, which name the jobs. Step 2 reads everything keyed by the job together: `jobs_ledger`, `job_property_owners`, `job_lien_filings`, `job_demand_letters`. Step 3 reads what the jobs' own rows name: `customers` and `customer_addresses`. The same eight reads with the same filters, and none added.
- **A job that leaves the desk is dropped after step 2, not before it.** ZZ test jobs when hidden and jobs an open in-kind deal pays are known only once the jobs are back, so step 2 asks by every job the RPCs named, and the owner, filing and letter rows of a job that leaves are dropped in the kernel. The rows handed to `assembleLienBookInput` are the rows the five steps handed it.
- **The read starts with the tab, beside the desk's.** `LienDesk.tsx` passes `bookOpened` alone, and the hook folds the book (`useMemo`) once its rows and the desk's items are both in hand. `loading` is true from the first moment, so an open by address reads *Reading every unpaid job…* where the status line was empty.
- **The desk's refetch still reads the book again.** A new list of items after the first one bumps the read. The first hand-over does not, or every open by address would read the book twice.

## What it does not change

An open by By job's own address still waits for the desk's read to end, because the book cannot be folded without the desk's items. That read is the larger part of the wait on every view of the desk, and it is not touched here.

## Tests

- `lienTimelineBookRead.test.ts`: the three steps hand on the rows the five steps did, and the same book, with ZZ test jobs hidden and shown. The order of the reads is pinned. No read is added. A job that leaves takes its rows with it.
- `useLienTimelineBook.render.test.tsx`: the reads are made before the desk's items come, the book folds when they do with no second read, and a new list of items reads again.
