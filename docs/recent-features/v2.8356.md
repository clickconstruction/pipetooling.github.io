# v2.8356 — Working hours: the clock follows a person's own start and end (2026-11-23)

The fourth of five PRs for punch list #429. The sessions the schedule makes for a salaried person take their start and end from the person's working hours.

## Why

`salary_sync_one_user_clock_sessions` made every day from the workday template: one start and a fixed 8 hours. A person on a 30-hour set week who had a template got 8-hour sessions. Pay read 6 hours a day, since payroll never reads the sessions, while the clock strip, the day's split over jobs and Utilization read 8. This is the gap the whole card started from.

## What ships

**The sync.** Migration `20261123083429_working_hours_clock.sql` ([its page](../migrations/20261123083429_working_hours_clock.md)) replaces the function with the body from `20261014200000` and three changes:

1. The day's ranges come from the person's set week: the newest save among the set rows that had started and were saved before the day began.
2. A day with no range makes nothing, as an excluded weekend does. The template's own weekend rule is asked only when there is no set week, so a Saturday with hours is made.
3. One range is a continuous day. Two are the two slots of a split day, with nothing on the clock over the break.

A meaningful override for the day still wins, so one day's move is as it was. With no working hours the template stands, 8 hours and all.

**Next day, not this minute.** Hours saved during a day reach the clock from the next day. A session that is already running keeps the start it has and the end it was given.

**The template row stays the switch.** It still says *the schedule clocks this person in and out*, and it keeps the job or bid the sessions land on. A person with working hours and no template clocks in themself, and the sync makes them nothing.

**One day's move.** The override's two checks that held a day to 8 hours are dropped. Its quarter-hour checks stay.

**The readers.** `workingDayForClock` in `workingHours.ts` reads a day the way the sync does. `resolveCalendarWorkday.ts` and `salaryOnShift.ts` take that day and draw it: the Calendar's chips, My Time's hint for an empty salaried day, and the team strip's on-shift line. Each screen reads the person's rows through `availabilityIo`, and a read that fails or is refused leaves the template's day, as before.

**The Workday schedule window** says when working hours stand: the clock follows them, and the start, the length and the weekend choice in the window are not read.

## What does not change

Pay and cost. They read the seven hour numbers, which the door has kept equal to the hours since v2.8345. Who may write the schedule tables is unchanged in this PR.

## Order

The client first, then the push. Pushed alone, the Calendar would draw 8 hours beside sessions that run the set hours. Nobody has both a template and working hours until someone sets them, so the order only matters from that first save on.
