# v2.8440 — GC projects: take back an ask the company has not heard about (2026-11-24)

The second of two undo tools the walks of punch list #416 asked for (`to-dos/document-builder-whats-left.md`, Part 1; the first is v2.8434, taking an award back). Walk 1c makes a test trade company and asks it on a trade, and nothing took the ask back: an ask only closed with the company's own answer, and the bench's Remove keeps any company that was asked. An ask made on the wrong company had the same dead end. The owner's word of 2026-11-24: add the way to delete it.

## The design, as a table

| | |
| --- | --- |
| **The press** | **Take the ask back…** beside **Will not do it** and **Cannot do it**, on a trade's asks on the project's card and on a Follow up card. For whoever may ask: the office team. |
| **Shows when** | The company has not heard about the ask: not opened, no quote, no contact logged, not awarded. There is no dead state. An ask they heard about shows no press and keeps its own two closes, as the bench's Remove leaves a company with history to them. |
| **The confirm** | *Take the Electrical ask back from ‹company›?* It says they have not opened it, quoted or heard from us, that the ask is deleted and they read as never asked, and that they can be asked again. The button reads **Take the ask back**. |
| **What goes** | The ask's row, and the office's own notes on it. |
| **What stays** | The company, and a row in `gc_asks_taken_back`: who asked and when, who took it back and when. |
| **Refused, in words** | The trade is awarded to this ask. A statement of work names it. A quote is in. They already passed. They opened it. We emailed them about it. A contact with them is logged on it. Each reads *‹company› stays asked on ‹trade›, since …*, and the last three say to close it with Will not do it or Cannot do it. |

## The calls taken on defaults

Each is the smallest choice, and each can be flipped.

1. **Deleted, not marked withdrawn, and only when they never heard.** The code decides it: an ask's answers are `invited`, `opened`, `bid` and `declined`, and every reader of an ask, the trade's portal and the copy of the kernels that a dozen edge functions carry, would have to learn a fifth. A trade that opened the page or was sent anything is therefore never un-asked here: the function refuses, and the ask closes with **Will not do it** or **Cannot do it**, with the reason in your words. A real *we took it back* answer for an ask they heard about is the follow-up if the office wants one.
2. **No dead press.** The press is absent where it cannot work, as **Remove from the bench…** is. A stale screen gets the refusal in words beside the press.
3. **The office team may take an ask back**, since the office team may ask. This is wider than taking an award back, which is a dev's until call W.
4. **The bench's Remove is not changed.** A company whose only history was the ask can leave after it. A company we emailed, or whose portal was opened, still stays.
5. **The trace is a table**, the only one this pair of tools makes. The ask is deleted and its company may be removed, so a note on either would go with them, and a note on the company's log would itself keep the company on the bench. No screen reads `gc_asks_taken_back` yet: the office team and the dev seat can.
6. **An ask that was passed on stays.** Their answer is history, even though nothing else stands on it.
7. **A test company that quoted still cannot leave.** Walk 1a's second test company is asked, quoted and awarded. Taking its award back leaves an ask with a quote, which this tool refuses and the bench's Remove keeps. The smallest dev-only removal would be a function that takes a dev, refuses any company whose name is not `ZZ Test … delete me`, refuses one that holds a statement of work, and deletes the row with its asks and quotes by cascade. It is not built here: it deletes quotes, and the name is the only fence between it and a real company.
