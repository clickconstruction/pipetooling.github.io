# v2.7032 — My email: a tag says what it did to Follow up, with one press to say otherwise (2026-11-12)

Punch list #324, PR 5 of 5: who decides that a mail is history. No migration.

## Why

v2.7027 files a mail by its age. Age is not intent: a mail three weeks old that truly went unanswered lands silent, and a mail from last week filed only for catching up opens a thread. The card's call was that the age decides and a person overrules in one press, with no tick before a tag, since the common tag is today's mail.

## What changed

- **The line after a tag** (`afterTag` in `myMail/reader.ts`, drawn in the reader's green bar and added to the toast): when every mail the tag wrote was old and the thread is not open, *6 old mails were filed for the record. Nothing went on Follow up.* with **Put on Follow up**. When the thread is open, *This conversation is on Follow up.* with **No reply needed**. Once the press went, the line says so and offers no more.
- **The presses** (`MyMailIo.afterTag`): Put on Follow up is `set_state` to waiting, which takes the mark off the thread's mail (v2.7026). No reply needed is `FollowUpIo.noReplyNeeded` (v2.7029), with the grey line naming the person signed in.
- **The function's answer** carries `state`, the conversation's state once the tag is done, read best effort. With no state the count of filed mail still draws.
- **The seat** (`plan_mail_tag`, server 0.80.0): `tag.for_record: true` files every mail of the tag whatever its age. It rides to `gmail-mine` as `forRecord`, reaches `planTag` as `MyMailKnown.forRecord`, and is kept in the plan's commitment, so an apply without it is another plan. The plan's `lands` sentence now says what the tag would do to Follow up. A tag onto an HR file refuses the argument: entries ask nobody already.
