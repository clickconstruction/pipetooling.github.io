# v2.6159 — The Project Board's bidding price as a bar, with the words under it (punch list #217, gc-messages-5, a prototype) (2026-10-30)

Punch list #217, *Show it, don't say it*: eight scans found 131 places where the app tells instead of shows, and a mock-up page draws each as a before/after pair for the owner to pick from. Of gc-messages-5, the Project Board's Bidding heading and each bidding row's price, the owner marked Build on 2026-10-30 and wrote: "I think this has potential, but I would need to see it based on data from bids we've been sending out and how it could look. Otherwise I'm worried that it's not going to be as informative as we make it look to be here. Would you be able to help me see what real data would look like on here?" This is that prototype, a draft PR with no auto-merge, behind a per-person switch that is off by default, with a dev-only side by side on the live board. No migration, no edge function.

## The finding

A bidding row said its price in prose three times — *$612,400 + ?*, *so far, with 2 holes*, *about $1.42M with every trade in* — and the heading added the rows up as *priced so far* with no mark that a hole counts $0 and a budget counts as a quote. The mock-up's whole-board page is the spec: each bidding row gets a bar on its own line carrying only the numbers (quoted solid, our budget hatched, each hole's estimate dashed, general conditions and fee grey), a legend under the bar naming each part and its trades, the price column saying two short lines, the heading saying the section's two sums in words, and every segment a door to the price card on those trades.

## What changed

- **`boardPriceBar.ts`** (`src/lib/gc/`, new): the pure kernel from `proposalTotals` and `priceStanding` to the bar. `boardPriceBar(state, project, { moneyShown })` returns the segments as fractions of the price once every trade is in (`priceStanding.likely`, or the price so far with no hole): *quoted* (the trades with a real number, a gap or a quote that ran out), *budget* (the trades carrying our budget), *hole* (each hole's estimate, from the lowest good quote, our budget or our own figure), *overhead* (general conditions with contingency and fee, one grey tail); the legend's words (*quoted · 5 trades · 1 with a cost missing*, *our budget · 2 trades carrying ours*, *holes · Concrete, Roofing · from the lowest good quote*, *GC $12,000 + contingency 3% + fee 8%*); the two short lines for the price column (*quoted · we stand behind it* when every trade is a real number, else *so far, with 2 holes, our budget on 1*, then *about $1.42M with every trade in*); and one sentence for the hover and the screen reader. `BAR_STANDINGS` maps each segment to the price card's groups. `boardSectionBarWords` is the heading's line (*$977,823 priced so far · about $1.9M with every trade in*), and `boardBarsSaySomething` the rule below. Ten tests in `boardPriceBar.test.ts` over the clinic fixture.
- **`GcBoardPriceBar.tsx`** (new): the bar on the row's own line under the row (`gridColumn: 1 / -1`), the numbers inside the segments the only words it carries, the legend under it with a swatch per part. Every segment is a `GcPriceTrigger`, so a hover, a focus or a press opens the row's price card as the *so far, with N holes* line does; `onArm` tells the card which group it came from, and the card (`GcPriceCard`, a new `focus` prop) lights that group — the quoted part lights *A real number*, the budget *Our guess*, a hole the four hole groups, the overhead the totals block.
- **`GcBoard.tsx`**: a `priceBars` prop (the switch, `ShapeView`); **Show the price bars** / **Show today's look** beside By stage | By customer in the strip's lead; each bidding row draws its bar when the switch is on (`bar` on `ProjectRow`), its price column then two short lines, and the Bidding heading says the two sums. With `?sbs=1` and a dev signed in, the Bidding section draws **Today** (the rows as they are) above **With the bars** (the same live rows with their bars and the heading's words) and no switch. `GcProjects.tsx` holds the switch (`useShapeView('gc-board-price-bars')`, remembered in localStorage per person, off by default) and hands it to the board.
- **`src/components/prototypes/SideBySide.tsx`** (new here): the shared #217 helper — `useShapeView(key)`, `SideBySide`, `ShapeSwitch` — copied as is from PR #6528 (v2.6138, the office round's columns), since the rhythm-strips branch that was writing it first was not pushed when this PR got there. Whichever of the #217 PRs lands first carries it; the rest take the one on main.
- **Smoke** `GcBoard.render.test.tsx`: six new cases — the switch off reads as today and the press asks for the bars; on, the bar's segments, legend, lines and heading; a segment lights its group in the card and the overhead the totals; outside the money team the trades alone; a board of plain quoted rows keeps its numbers and the side by side says so; the side by side draws both shapes with no switch.
- **Guide** *see where every GC project stands*: a section **See the price as a bar** names the switch and the parts.

## What the real board makes of the bar (2026-10-30)

Read on a dev server from this branch at `/gc?sbs=1` as the dev, on the live rows:

- **One project is bidding**, and it carries **one trade** — a quote carried with a cost missing, no general conditions, contingency or fee set. Its bar is one solid quoted part the full width of the row, and its legend reads *quoted · 1 trade · 1 with a cost missing*. The heading reads *$48,000 priced so far* with no second figure, since nothing is a hole. The row's figure already said all of that in *$48,000 + ?*.
- **One project is building** with five trades, none carried; a bidding row like it would draw five dashed guesses and nothing solid — the other extreme, and no more informative.
- So on today's data the bars **do not differ from row to row**: there is one row, and it is all quoted. The mock-up's three rows (two holes and two budgets, one hole, every trade quoted) are the shape the board takes once a few real bids carry a mix of quotes, budgets and holes; GC mode has not had that bid yet.

**What the bar should show when most rows are all-quoted.** A bar with one solid part is the number drawn again, so it should not draw. This prototype keeps that rule: with the switch on, the bars draw only while some bidding row's bar says something its figure does not — a hole, our budget, or the general conditions and fee set on the project (`boardBarsSaySomething`) — and then every bidding row draws one, so an all-quoted row reads as plain *against its neighbours*. A board of plain quoted rows keeps its numbers; the side by side draws the bars regardless and says in one muted line that they would stay off. Of the three shapes the brief offered for an all-quoted board — the GC and fee share, the row's standing against the others, or nothing but the number — the GC and fee share is the one worth drawing once Our number is set on a project (it is a second segment, so the rule draws it), the standing against the others is what the bars give by being drawn together, and nothing but the number is what a lone quoted row gets.

## What the mock-up asked for that this does not do

- **The quoted figure as the row's big number.** The mock-up's first row shows *$612,400* as the quoted part alone, while its third shows the whole price; the row keeps today's figure (`proposalTotals.price`, the number the heading sums and the card reads) and the bar's solid part carries its own amount inside it, so one figure keeps one meaning.
- **A bar on By customer's rows for a dev's side by side.** The switch draws bars on By customer too; the side by side is drawn under By stage only.
- **The mock-up's colours.** The quoted part takes the Bid Board's blue and the overhead the muted grey; the mock-up's hatch and dashes are kept.
