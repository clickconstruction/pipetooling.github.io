# v2.4371 — Pricing: your win and loss history counts Combined materials (2026-10-01)

The second By Stage leftover found in the 2026-10-01 review (the first, the Approval PDF, is v2.4368). The Pricing tab's win/loss strip, *This number vs your history* (`PricingMarginHistory` over `usePricingMarginHistory`), reads the RPC `bid_pricing_history`. Its `materials` CTE summed the items of the three By Stage stage POs only. A Combined bid (`materials_model = 'rough'`, every bid since May 2026) prices materials from its takeoff's part lines, so it entered the history with $0 of materials. Its estimated margin read 97–99.9% on every decided Combined bid but one, past the strip's 95% sanity band, so the strip left them out. On 2026-10-01 Plumbing had no usable dot, and the strip never drew.

## What changed
- **Migration `20261002070000_bid_pricing_history_combined_materials`** (`CREATE OR REPLACE`, same return type). Materials follow `bid_estimate_breakdown`'s rule: the stage POs when they hold anything, else Σ line quantity × unit price × the fixture count.
- **Version scope: the version the Workbench priced.** The rule is `pickActiveVersion` in SQL: the saved active version, else the first by `sort_order`; an unsplit bid reads its NULL-version base. A split bid keeps no NULL-version rows, so a base-only read would give every versioned bid $0. Its one cost estimate's labor follows the active version, and so does the strip's ▼, so a dot and the ▼ compare like with like.
- Counts read as on Pricing: a 0 count or an out-of-version line counts ×1.
- **Not done in SQL:** the order rounding (no part line on prod carries a Sold in rule yet), and model-first picking (the rule matches the job budget; no decided bid holds both stores). The reasons are in `docs/migrations/20261002070000_bid_pricing_history_combined_materials.md`.

## Verified
`BEGIN … ROLLBACK` dry run on prod over every service type: 73 rows before and after. 24 Combined rows gained their materials (+$502,577) and one By Stage row (BP159) gained $14.96 of parked lines. BP190 +$8,368.34 and BP483 +$47,211.84 equal their Labor tabs on a worktree dev server; BP483's figure is its selected base version, not the $46,282.85 alternate. Plumbing's strip goes from 0 dots to 6 (2 won, 4 lost on price), so it now draws. Plumbing runs in about 21 ms.

## Left as it was
The RPC's labor hours still use the pre-v2.3291 rule, and it still adds the estimator time that v2.3293 took out of the Workbench's cost. 71 of the 73 decided bids with an estimate carry a $0 labor rate, so the first barely moves the dots today. Both stay a separate follow-up.

Apply with `supabase db push` after merge. No client change; the types do not change.
