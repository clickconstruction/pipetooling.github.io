## What was wrong

A plan the dev seat made lived only in the session that made it. With many sessions working one HR file, the owner said *Apply it* in each of them, and a plan was lost when its session ended.

## What changed

- **Migration `20261113120000_dev_seat_kept_plans.sql`**. Page: [`docs/migrations/20261113120000_dev_seat_kept_plans.md`](../migrations/20261113120000_dev_seat_kept_plans.md).
  - `dev_seat_kept_plans`: the person, the plan verb, its arguments, the plan hash, the dry run's reply as shown, one paragraph of words, who kept it and from which session. Decided once: applied, dropped with a reason, or stale. Never deleted.
  - `dev_kept_plan_keep` and `dev_kept_plan_decide`, the only writers.
- **Kernel** `supabase/functions/_shared/keptPlans.ts` (door `src/lib/people/keptPlans.ts`, 7 tests): which plans may be kept and why another may not, a row in words, how long it has waited, the list in the order it is read.
- **Docs**: `HR_FILES.md`, `ACCESS_CONTROL.md`, `GLOSSARY.md`.

## Access

A dev reads the table. Nobody writes it directly. The HR agent's key has no grant and nothing files a row to Drive. Three things are never kept, so they stay in the session that made them: a plan that carries a file's bytes, a plan marked counsel-only, and a change to a counsel-only document.

## Is this the best we can do?

The card kept every plan about a person's file. The stored arguments are the words of the plan, so a kept counsel-only plan would put counsel-only words in a second table. Refusing those at the door costs the owner one *Apply it* in a session for that kind of plan and keeps the mark's meaning whole, so that is what was built.

The hash rule is untouched. A kept plan is the same arguments and the same hash. Nothing in this PR applies a plan, and nothing in the app can.

## The owner's calls, with the default built

- **Who may press Apply in the app** (any dev, or him alone): no Apply press is built. A plan is applied in a session on his word, as today.
- **Whether a kept plan expires**: not by the clock. It turns stale only when its dry run no longer matches.
