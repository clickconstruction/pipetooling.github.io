# v2.8222 — Lien desk: the last day of work and the days that follow it, as values in place (punch list #399, the first screen) (2026-11-21)

Punch list #399 (`to-dos/values-in-place/README.md`), the first screen of its Lien desk group. No migration, no edge function, no change to a kernel that works out a date.

## Why

The owner asked for values in place across the app: every date, count and name drawn apart from the words, bold where the app works it out. The shared piece shipped in v2.7985 and no screen on the card used it. The Lien desk goes first, because its days all hang on one day, the last day of work.

## What changed

- **The last-day line** (`LienLastWorkDayLine.tsx`) is one fact a line (`ValueLines`): *Last day of work*, then *Why* when a reason was typed, then *Later hours* when approved hours follow a day set by hand. The day, who set it, when, and the later hours' last day are each a bold value. A job with no day reads *none yet* in quiet words, with no value.
- **The timeline** (`LienTimelineStrip.tsx`) draws every day it prints as a bold value: each stop's date, the days inside a stop's two small lines (*open since Aug 1*, *22 days · to draft*), the verdict band's sentence, its aside and its *Waiting on* line, the fold's tray, and the calendar's captions, name column and closing sentence. A word in front of a date (*filed*, *served*, *reply by*) is now a quiet word and the date is the value. A date line with no day in it (`—`, *now*, *when paid*, *4 windows closed*) reads as it always has.
- **A value stays on one line.** `Jan 15, 2027` and `35 days` wrap before them and never inside.
- **The chip on the last-day line wraps.** It sits in the values column now, and a long *set by hand · name · day* ran past its box at 375 px until it could wrap.

## How the days are marked

`src/lib/jobs/lienDayValues.ts`, pure, with `val` from `src/lib/valuesInPlace.ts`:

- `lienDayValues(sentence)` marks a day (`Oct 15`, `Dec 15, 2027`), a work month (`Aug 2026`) and a count of days (`22 days`, `+5 days`, `21 of 75 days`) inside a sentence a lien kernel wrote. These are the shapes `lienDateWords`, `formatYmdMonthDay` and `daysWords` write, and the ones `keepDatesWhole` already reads.
- `lienLastWorkSourceValues` marks who set the day and when inside the source line.
- `lienHoursAfterHandDayValues` marks the later hours' last day inside `lienHoursAfterHandDayWords`'s own sentence.

The timeline's sentences have other readers that want plain words: a tooltip, the folded summary, the Dashboard, the firm's page. So the kernels' fields are untouched, and the marks are made on the sentence they already wrote. `plainSentence` of every answer is the sentence that went in. The tests hold that over six timelines the desk builds and over every case of the last-day line. The marked forms of the two last-day sentences live outside `lienLastWorkDay.ts`, which is mirrored into the edge functions and has no reader for screen words there.

## What did not change, on purpose

- **No date and no word.** Nothing the desk computes moved. Every sentence reads the same.
- **How the last day of work is set.** It is still *Change ›* on the line, or the press under LAST WORK, then the window that shows what moves and asks why. The day moves every lien deadline, and the card's first open call is whether a change to such a date saves at once or asks first. So the day is drawn bold with no press of its own, and the question is the owner's (below).
- **The mini row.** Its short dates were bold already.
- **Other counts.** The fold's *3 noted · 1 to note*, *4 windows closed*, money and percents are not days and stay words. They belong to the card's timeline row.

## The owner's call this leaves

May a press on the last day of work itself open its field in place, with **Keep** opening today's window that shows what moves and asks why? That would retire the *Change ›* link, as the rule asks, with the same window and the same write behind it. Until he says so the link stays.

## Seen in passing, not fixed

On a strip under about 330 px wide, the calendar's captions run past the strip's right edge. It is the same with plain words on `main`: a caption is capped at the width of its column from where its bar starts.
