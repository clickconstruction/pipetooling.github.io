# v2.4677 — Legal: the statement of account adds up to the balance (2026-10-05)

Client only; no migration, no edge function. Item 5 of punch list #85 ([`to-dos/legal-portal-firm-ready/`](../../to-dos/legal-portal-firm-ready/README.md)), stacked on item 1 (v2.4615, the firm's print packet). Mockup: `mockup-5-money-that-foots.html` beside the card, drawn from the sample matter and a harder copy of it through the real parser and kernel.

## The defect

The firm's Account tab printed a statement whose rows did not add up to the Balance under it.

- **An agreed write-down counted twice.** The invoice row carried the already-reduced `amount`, and a separate `−(previous − amount)` row followed, so the column summed to balance minus the write-down.
- **A payment with no bill never reduced the balance.** `jobOpenBalance` subtracted only payments linked to an open billed line, so a payment recorded on the job (`invoice_id` null) sat in the ledger and the Balance ignored it. That overstated the claim the firm would sue on.
- **The firm's demand rose when the office applied a recovery.** The matter card's total demand added every `fee` and `cost` entry, including the `cost` row Mark applied writes for the firm's contingency (`Contingency 33% of $X`).
- **Raw system words** rode into ledger text: `sent stripe`, `· uncollectible`, `· open`, `Cheque`.
- The Balance line under the table was a separate number, so nothing guaranteed it matched the rows.

## What changed

- **One money rule per job**, `legalJobMoney(job)` in [`legalPacket.ts`](../../src/lib/legal/legalPacket.ts) (`jobOpenBalance` reads it). It runs the app's shared payment rule, `attributeJobPayments` (v2.3592 oldest bill first, v2.4534 work on no bill first), so the packet, the bill tab and the customer's portal agree on what a payment with no bill pays. The job total handed to the rule is `revenue` less the jobs' write-downs: the written-down part is no longer owed and is not work on no bill. Money beyond every sent bill is a credit against the job. A job with no billed line still owes its job-level remainder.
- **The statement** lists each sent bill at what was billed (`agreed_write_down_previous_amount` when written down), the write-down beneath it, and every payment (a negative payment reads *Refund*). Then rows that explain the rest of the job's balance: *Work on no bill, covered by the payments on this job* (`off_bill`), *Marked paid, no payment recorded for this part* (`settled`), *Job total, not split into bills* (the no-line shell), and as a last resort one *Difference the records do not explain* row (`unexplained`). The column therefore reaches the Balance by construction, and a gap the records cannot explain is said out loud on the desk before the firm sees it.
- **A shared money kernel**, [`legalMoney.ts`](../../src/lib/legal/legalMoney.ts): `legalRunningLedger` (the running balance), `firmFeeEntries` / `contingencyEntries` / `isContingencyEntry` / `firmDemand` (moved from item 1's print so the page uses it too), `CONTINGENCY_ENTRY_META` and `contingencyEntryBody` (what Mark applied writes), `invoiceSentWords` and `paymentHowWords` (the plain words).
- **The ledger text** drops the job number (each view prints it in a Job column) and reads *Invoice · sent 2026-06-03 through Stripe*, *Payment · check no. 2291*, *Payment · ACH*. Stripe's status is gone: the running balance already says what is open.
- **The firm's Account tab** gains Job and Balance columns; the line under it reads *Balance owed*. The matter card's demand is `firmDemand` and its subline reads *balance $X + fees and costs $Y*. The Fees & steps tab lists only the fees and costs in the demand, with their total, and the contingency on a line of its own (*not in the demand*). The firm's print does the same.
- **The office desk and the office print** draw the same Job and Balance columns from the same ledger. **Mark applied** now writes the contingency row with `meta: { contingency: true }`; rows written before the tag are still caught by their body.
- The guide *share your attorney their portal* says the contingency does not count toward the total demand.

## Is this the best we can do?

- **Should a payment with no bill just be labelled "unapplied" instead of lowering the balance?** No. The app already has the owner's rule for such money, and the bill tab and the customer's own page use it. A statement that ignores a payment the debtor holds a receipt for overstates the claim. The packet now uses the same rule.
- **What if the rows still cannot meet the balance?** The kernel names the known causes as rows of their own. Anything else becomes one row per job saying the records do not explain it. The column always reaches the Balance and nothing is hidden.
- **Should the write-down sit directly under its invoice, out of date order?** No. A statement of account runs by date, and a running balance only reads in date order. The Job column pairs them.
- **Drop the contingency row from the firm's view entirely?** No. The firm should still see what it earned, on its own line, outside the demand.
- **Found while drawing it:** the write-down made the shared payment rule treat the written-down part as work on no bill, so a later payment with no bill would have paid that phantom first. The packet hands the rule the job total less the write-downs.
