# v2.8047 — Job Mode: a row of named squares that follows your day (2026-11-20)

The owner walked the app on a phone on 2026-11-20 and circled the row under the header in Job Mode: *"in Job mode I don't think that paint a map should be this big on the screen, is there more things that we can offer in the header on mobile that would be useful?"* The row was a blue square with no name and a Paint a map card as wide as the screen. It was the full Dashboard's row with the clock and the report taken out, never drawn for Job Mode. Three arrangements were mocked up beside it, and he picked the third, the day's row. No migration and no edge function.

## What a person sees

Equal squares, each with its name, 62 px tall.

- **A stop still owed, or on the way to it:** **Directions**, **Job details**.
- **On the job:** **Photo**, **Job details**. On a site that holds boxes a fifth square, **On site**, with the site's box count on its corner.
- **No job left today:** **This week** with the hours so far, **Tomorrow** with the count of jobs.
- **Always, at the right end:** **Paint a map**, **My spend**. My spend carries the count of card charges with no job yet.

The job's two squares are blue. The person's own are outlined, so the job's pair reads first. Job details keeps its place while there is a job, so only the first square swaps. While the day is being read the job's pair is two empty squares, and nothing moves when the read lands. A read that fails draws This week and Tomorrow, which need no day.

Directions opens the phone's maps on the job's address. Job details opens the job's window. Photo opens the job's photos window, the one the card's Photos line opens. On site opens the Tally's On site tab on the job. This week scrolls to the hours card. Tomorrow opens the Schedule tab.

## Why these squares

The press record for the field roles over 31 days showed the job's window as the door opened most (92 opens by 5 people), a scroll down the card, and the two things the old row held as the two opened least (My spend on 4 person-days, Paint a map once). The person's own quick buttons were weighed and left out: only a leader, an assistant and a dev have any. Materials on site is not a fixed square because nearly every working job holds no boxes.

## How it is built

- `src/lib/jobMode/topRow.ts` (pure, 21 tests): `topRowJobState` picks the job from the open clock and its leg, else the first of today's stops with no closed session today, never the office's job or a billing-only one. `topRowSquares` gives the squares in order. The words, the screen reader's names and the press record's targets live there too.
- `src/lib/jobMode/topRowIo.ts`: today's stops, the day's clock sessions with the two travel stamps, and the site's live box count, read only for the job the person stands on.
- `src/components/jobMode/JobModeTopRow.tsx` (12 render tests) draws the row. `DashboardPinnedQuickRow` mounts it where the classic row had no clock slot and `hideJobReport`. The Dashboard hands it `jobModeDay`: this week's hours, which the hours card now reports through `onWeek` so the week is read once, and tomorrow's stops from the schedule read.
- `PaintAMapPress` trades its `wide` shape for `tile`, which takes the row's own look and reads the day on the press like the other two shapes. `paintPressLine` and the wide press's second-line words are gone with it.
- Each square records its press as `job-mode-row` with `#<square>`, so the next look at this row has counts. Paint a map keeps its own record, now `#dashboard-tile`.

## Walked

On the dev server at 390 and 320 px wide, light and dark, in Job Mode and on the full Dashboard, with the row's own reads handed a made-up day for the job states and no write leaving the browser. Every square measured the same width and height in each state, and the page did not scroll sideways. The full Dashboard's row is unchanged.

## Not in this change

The Short view of Job Mode draws no row, as before. A leader's quick buttons and pins stay on the line under the row.
