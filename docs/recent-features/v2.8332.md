# v2.8332 — Dashboard: one row at the top switches between Task Mode, the Dashboard and Job Mode (2026-11-23)

Punch list [#425](../../to-dos/job-mode-offer/README.md). Client only: no migration and no function.

## The ask

The owner, 2026-11-23, with a phone screenshot of the green card *Working in the field? Turn on Job Mode.*: he likes that it is on top of many people's Dashboard, but nobody reads its small text. How could it work for someone impulsive?

The small grey text was the only place the card said how to get back. **Turn on Job Mode** swapped the whole page and the way back was in the gear menu. **Not now** removed the offer from that phone for good. Four ways were drawn (`to-dos/job-mode-offer/mockup.html`). He picked the switch with no card, and turned it: the switch lives above the top of the page, a person who scrolls all the way up can pull it down, and Task Mode is offered too, on the left, since it is pressed least. A second drawing let him try three ways to hide the row (`switcher-mockup.html`). He picked **Tucked**.

## What changed

- **The mode row.** `ModeRow` (`src/components/layout/ModeRow.tsx`) draws **Task Mode · Dashboard · Job Mode** as three thirds, the first line above the header, inside an `AppTopBanner`. The third of the mode drawn is filled. It is drawn for a leader, a superintendent, a sub and a helper, on the Dashboard in any mode and on the checklist while Task Mode is on. The office keeps the gear menu's *Modes* rows.
- **The rule** is `src/lib/modeRow.ts`, pure, with every word on the row. A press goes through `usePageMode`'s `set`, the one door that switches a mode. Task Mode and Job Mode each switch on. The middle third switches both off and never writes Dispatch Mode, so a leader keeps the Dispatch Mode bar and the third reads **Dashboard** for both.
- **Tucked on a phone.** The row shows on the first five opens of the page, or until a third is pressed. After that the page opens with the row out of sight and a 24 px handle in its place. A pull down at the top of the page, or a tap on the handle, brings the row down for that visit. Leaving the page or putting the phone away tucks it again. The row also shows after every switch, so the way back is where the thumb is.
- **Two guards hold it open.** In Task Mode the row is the way out, as the green strip was, so it never tucks there. A sub or a helper who is off Job Mode keeps it in sight until they are back on it, since Job Mode is their first screen.
- **On a desk** the row always shows, at 32 px.
- **Gone:** `DashboardJobModeFirstRunCard`, its smoke, `showJobModeFirstRunCard` and the put-away key. A phone that stored `'card'` still reads Job Mode on.
- **Out of the gear menu** (the owner, the same day: *since we're adding this, we can remove the job mode and task mode from the gear wheel dropdown*): a role with the row has no **Job Mode** or **Task Mode** row in the gear menu, nor in the phone dock's More sheet. `gearModesRows` (`src/lib/gearMenuSections.ts`) is the rule. A leader keeps **Dispatch Mode** and its PO tab under *Modes*; a superintendent, a sub and a helper have no *Modes* group at all, and no label over nothing.
- **Kept for everyone else:** the gear menu's Job Mode and Task Mode rows, the *Task Mode · Exit* strip and **Show full dashboard** at the foot of the Job Mode page are drawn only for the roles the row is not drawn for. Taking the gear rows from the office too would leave it no way into either mode, so that waits on the row going to everyone.
- The Dashboard clears its *full page for this visit* flag whenever Job Mode goes off, so switching back on opens the Job Mode card.

## How the pull differs from the drawing

The drawing tucked the row by opening the page scrolled just past it. The build does not. The header is not sticky, and on a Home Screen iPhone the first line of the page pays the status bar. A page that opens scrolled would put the header under the clock, and every jump to the top of a page would land on the row.

So the tucked row is not on the page at all. `index.css` already sets `overscroll-behavior-y: none`, which leaves a downward drag at the top of a page with nothing to do. `ModeRow` listens for that drag, 36 px of it (`modeRowPull`), and for a wheel turned up there. A drag that starts inside something scrolled, on a map or while a window holds the page still is left alone. To the thumb it is the same move: at the top, pull down.

## Usage rows

`ui_nav_clicks`: control `mode_row` with target `#task`, `#full` or `#job` for a press, and `mode_row_shown` with `#pull` or `#handle` for a tucked row brought down. Job Mode switched on from the row records `job_mode_enabled` with `#row`.

## Defaults the owner did not rule on

Three calls were drawn with a default and built that way. Each is one line in `modeRow.ts`.

- The row starts to tuck after the first press or five opens: `MODE_ROW_FIRST_VISITS`.
- The handle is drawn: `MODE_ROW_HANDLE`.
- The four field roles get the row: `modeRowOfferedToRole`.

## Tests

`gearMenuSections.test.ts` gains the Modes rows for all nine roles, and `Layout.gearSwitches.render.test.tsx` pins a leader's menu with neither row. `modeRow.test.ts` (21): the roles, the pages, a press from every state the three toggles can be in, the first visits, the two guards, the pull and the phone's memory. `ModeRow.render.test.tsx` (14): the thirds in order, the tuck, the handle, a pull by touch events, and what each press sends through the door. `topBannerInset.test.ts` now starts its reading of the lines above the header at the row. `jobModeToggle.test.ts` loses the card's cases and gains the row's door.

## Looked at

Walked on the dev server through the lens frame, over invented data, at a phone's width (390 px and 320 px) and a desk's (1280 px), in the light and dark looks.

- **A leader on a phone:** ten presses in a row across the three thirds each landed on the page they named, with the stored values to match and Dispatch Mode's value never written. The gear menu listed Dispatch Mode and neither other mode.
- **The tuck:** leaving the Dashboard and coming back left only the 24 px handle. A real pull by touch events, a wheel turned up and a tap on the handle each brought the row down.
- **What must not pull:** a drag with the page scrolled, a drag that went up first, one that went sideways first, a short one, two fingers, and a drag under the open Clock In window. None brought the row down.
- **A sub at 320 px:** three thirds of 100 px by 44 px with no sideways scroll. After **Dashboard** the row held open through a trip to another page. After **Job Mode** the same trip tucked it. No *Modes* group in the gear menu and no **Show full dashboard**.
- **A superintendent:** the green *New* on Job Mode until the first press, the Job Mode tabs after it, the checklist with the row and no strip in Task Mode, and the Dashboard again from there.
- **A leader on a desk:** the row always showing at 32 px, no handle, still there after a trip away.
- **An assistant:** no row, all three rows in the gear menu, the green strip in Task Mode and its Exit.

The lens frame empties the phone's memory on every load, so the tuck on the sixth open with no press is held by the tests alone. It was not tried on a real phone. The pull under a thumb, and the row under a Home Screen iPhone's status bar, are what is left.
