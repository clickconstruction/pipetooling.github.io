# v2.4826 — Lien desk: the § Rules window redrawn — a rail, folded rules with one-line answers, the open job's dates, counsel marks (2026-10-07)

Client only. Punch list #99 ([`to-dos/lien-rules-window/`](../../to-dos/lien-rules-window/README.md)): Grace asked 2026-10-07 whether the Lien desk's rules were easy to read and well structured, and what would help a user. Mocked up as Now vs Proposed ([`mockup.html`](../../to-dos/lien-rules-window/mockup.html), canvas https://claude.ai/artifact/4755Z3N8cF5sgEZxq2uKv1), held against "is this the best we can do", then built.

## What was wrong

The § Rules window (v2.4655) opened the guide *read the Texas lien rules the app follows* at one rule, a third of the way down a 7,100 px page of 2,300 words, with the index 1,900 px above. The headings were the statutes' names (*The § 53.056 notice*, *Attorney's fees need presentment*), the dates were prose, *What the app does* was an inventory of screens, seven rules counsel had not read were only listed at the foot, and nothing said what the rules meant for the job the desk had open.

## What changed

- **The guide** ([`texas-lien-rules-the-app-follows.md`](../../src/content/help/texas-lien-rules-the-app-follows.md)): every rule is retitled as the question it answers (*When each date falls*, *Does this job need a monthly notice?*, *What the affidavit needs before it can be filed*, *Interest at 1.5 percent a month on an unpaid bill*); a one-sentence lead under each heading is the short answer; *What the app does* became *What you do*, with a door to the screen it names; the clock rule carries a dates table (work month → notice and lien dates, commercial and a house, 2026 as the example); the homestead statement and the late-notice reading are rules of their own, the latter with a **Counsel has not read this.** line; Taunya's ask the same afternoon (*"Need to make clear in the Rules that you can lien as soon as work is complete as long as proper steps are taken"*) is the rule *How soon the lien can be filed* under *The lien*: the 15th dates are the last day, not the first, a sub files as soon as its notice is mailed (counsel confirmed 2026-09-22), and filing while still on the job for finished months carries the counsel line; trade words get their meaning in the sentence where they first appear. The *Find a rule* index and the section order are unchanged. `LIEN_RULE_CITES` carries the new headings (the test pins each to the guide).
- **The window** ([`LienRulesModal.tsx`](../../src/components/jobs/LienRulesModal.tsx)): the rendered guide is read once ([`lienRulesFold.ts`](../../src/lib/jobs/lienRulesFold.ts) — `indexLienRules`, `dressLienRules`, `setLienRulesOpen`, `lienRulesHit`, `lienRuleAt`, `swapLienRuleDates`, 7 tests over the real guide): each `h3` becomes a button with its cite as a tag, its members fold under `data-folded` (never `hidden`, which the find's `narrowToMarked` owns), the lead and the counsel line stay out, the page's own sections and every *Back to Find a rule* line are folded away. Only the door's rule opens; a press on a question toggles it; *Unfold all* / *Fold all* in the find row; a find unfolds every rule it hit for as long as it lasts. A rail on a computer (groups and rules, the current one lit on scroll), a chip row on a phone. The window is 1,040 px wide.
- **The job strip** ([`lienRulesDates.ts`](../../src/lib/jobs/lienRulesDates.ts), 8 tests): the desk hands `LienRulesDoor` the picked job (`lienRulesJobFrom` over the queue entry: the label, the kind, sub or direct, the last work month, the earliest open § 53.056 date, the § 53.052 date from `lienDeadlines.ts`); the window names it with both dates above the rules, and `lienRuleDateRows` rebuilds the clock table around its work month with that row lit. With no job (the Lien window's door) the table shows the three months around today and no strip is drawn. `todayYmd` comes from the desk, else `todayYmdInAppTz()`.

## Is this the best we can do

The first draft had a rail and folded rules and stopped there; a folded list of statute names is still a list of statute names, so the one-line answer became part of the fold. The rules are general and the reader has a job open, so the strip and the lit row were added. The lawyer-facing half stays, folded, because the Lien window's demand letter opens the window at those rules. Hover tooltips for terms were dropped: a phone has no hover, so the meaning goes in the sentence.
