## Why

The owner's words: *so I can know at any point in time when to update them.* A date on a card answers half of that. The other half is wording that never changes and is never read — the consent, the signing sentences, the built-in bid terms — which no date flags. A review does.

## The rules

- **A Settings text's first record dates the history, not the wording.** It reads *On record since Sep 28, 2026. Changes before that were not kept.* and does not start the review clock. A Book document's first record carries the day the document was last saved, and does.
- **A change counts as a read.** A card is due `REVIEW_EVERY_MONTHS` (12) after the later of its last review and its last change on record. A Book document with no history yet falls back to its own version date.
- **One review per catalog entry.** A Book holding two customer documents shows the review on both cards and counts it once.
- **The day is the company's.** Records are stamped in UTC and read in the company's zone; a review is filed under today's company date.
- **The history is read-only here.** Nothing in the app edits or deletes a record.

## Found on the way (not changed)

- The interval is a constant. If the office wants six months for one text and two years for another, it becomes a column on the review or a Setting.
- Nothing outside the tab says a review is due — no Dashboard card, no email. The count is at the top of the tab.
- The fixed wording (the consent, the signing sentences, the payment line) has no history table; its history is the app's own release notes. A review is the only date it carries.
