# v2.4591 — Job Parts Tally: groundwork for suggesting where a card charge goes (2026-10-05)

Punch list #72 (`to-dos/tally-transactions-refresh.md`), PR 1 of 5: the pure kernel the team queue (PR 2) and the holder's phone card (PR 4) will read. No screen change, no migration, no edge function.

## What it is

- **[`src/lib/tally/tallySortSuggestion.ts`](../../src/lib/tally/tallySortSuggestion.ts)** — `suggestTallyDay(input)`: one holder's day (its unsorted charges) + the holder's clock sessions and schedule rows from the day −3 to +3 + the holder's sorted charges 30 days back → the day's chips in a fixed order, capped at `TALLY_DAY_CHIP_CAP` (5); each line's `best` (its own suggestion at `likely` or above, else the day's) and its `own` (category, store); the even and by-hours splits on a day with two or more clocked jobs; `allSure` (every line sure; never true in rule set v1). Every suggestion is `{ choice, rule, confidence, facts }`: a stable rule id, the rule's one level from `TALLY_RULE_CONFIDENCE`, and the facts (hours, store, count, category, days, posted times). Also `groupTallyChargesByDay` (holder × company day, newest first), `splitTallyAmountByHours` (largest remainder in whole cents on integer seconds, the sign kept, always summing to the charge), `tallyRowsForChoice` (the rows to write for a job or a split), `tallyStoreKey`, `tallyDayKey`, `TALLY_SUGGESTION_RULES_VERSION = 1`.
- **[`src/lib/tally/tallySuggestionWords.ts`](../../src/lib/tally/tallySuggestionWords.ts)** — `tallySuggestionWhy(s)`, the one place facts become words ("only job clocked that day", "Ridge Supply, last 4 times", "worked Fri and Mon"), so the office queue and the phone say the same sentence; held to the plain-words rules (`src/lib/plainWords.ts`).
- **[`src/lib/mercuryOfficeLikeCategories.ts`](../../src/lib/mercuryOfficeLikeCategories.ts)** — `OFFICE_LIKE_MERCURY_CATEGORIES` and `mercuryCategoryString` moved out of `people/reviewOfficeLikeCharges.ts` (which imports Supabase) and re-exported there unchanged; the kernel's office categories are tested as a subset of the list.
- Days are company days through `calendarYmdInAppTzFromIso` / `todayYmdInAppTz` (`docs/TIME_AND_ZONES.md`), the calendar `clock_sessions.work_date` uses, so a charge posted at 8:30 PM keeps its day.

## Rule set v1, as measured

The rules were replayed over the last 90 days of sorted card charges before any of them got a level: 839 charges in 500 sorting visits on 377 person-days. Each day was rebuilt as it stood when it was sorted. The history is only the charges sorted before that visit, never the charge's own split or a later one.

| Rule | Level | n | Right | Precision | Where the misses went |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `clock-one-job`, the only job clocked that day, a field job | likely | 370 | 268 | 72% | 42 split, 16 Office, 16 a job clocked the day before or after, 15 a job scheduled that day, 13 another job |
| `schedule-one-job`, no clock, the only job scheduled | likely | 30 | 22 | 73% | 5 another job, 2 Office, 1 a job clocked nearby |
| `clock-one-job-mixed`, one job plus bid time | likely | 9 | 9 | n too small | none |
| `office-category`, Advertising, Insurance, InternetAndTelephone | likely | 16 | 14 | n too small | 2 to jobs |
| `clock-office`, the only job clocked is Office, if it were a guess | none | 103 | 45 | 44% | 54 to jobs, 4 split |
| a store's run of 3 or more, chosen over the day as drawn | none | 68 | 35 | 51% | |
| a store's last 1 or 2 charges, on a line with no other guess | none | 90 | 21 | 23% | |
| the holder's previous charge, as a candidate rule | no rule | 729 | 232 | 32% | |

**Nothing is sure in rule set v1.** A rule earns `sure` at 95% or better on 40 or more charges, measured the same way; none came close (the best combined signal, the day's earlier charges agreeing, reached 85% on 97). The drawn one-press bar would have sorted the "obvious" days all right on only 186 of 309.

**What the replay cannot know.** The truth is one sorter's final choice (one person sorted 98.6% of the 839), so a precision is agreement with that sorter, not correctness; People → Review calls some of those choices wrong (Software and Utilities on field jobs). Clock sessions are read as stored today, after later corrections, not as the sorter saw them. Both make `sure` harder to earn, not easier. Rules built from the sorter's own earlier sorts (the store runs, the day's sorted charges, the previous charge) can only echo the sorter's habits, so they stay offered whatever they score. The replay measures agreement with the final split, not "accepted vs overridden" on what a screen showed; that difference is an open question for PR 5's plan.

## What the data overruled in the plan and the drawing

- **The order of the rules.** The plan put "only job clocked that day" before "category → Office", which files a Software charge on the field job. Now a line's own rules come first and the day's second.
- **"Telecom."** Mercury's category is `InternetAndTelephone`. Of the six office-type categories only Advertising, Insurance and InternetAndTelephone went to Office in practice (14 of 16); Software went 2 of 8 and Utilities 6 of 14.
- **A store keeping its own pick under the day.** Against a clocked job a store's run was right 2 times in 12. Store rules are offered on the line and never chosen; fuel gets none, since it follows the job.
- **The day ±1.** A Saturday's neighbours are Friday and Monday, so an empty day looks up to 3 days each side; a worked day offers the jobs clocked the day before and after.
- **Split by hours.** Of the 37 splits across exactly a day's clocked jobs, 35 were even and none by hours. Even leads; by hours is the last chip.
- **A day clocked only on Office** is not a guess (44%): office staff clock Office and buy for jobs. It gets `clock-office`, offered only.

## The day's chips

Fixed order: the day's likely chip; the jobs clocked that day; the even split; the jobs scheduled but not clocked; the jobs worked on the neighbouring days; where the day's sorted charges went; Office; the split by hours. A job appears once, at its first place. The chips held the answer for 640 of the 839 at the cap of 5, against 625 at 4, 643 at 6 and 647 uncapped (612 without the scheduled-but-not-clocked and neighbouring-day chips). The median day shows 4 chips; no day shows more than 5; 175 of the 500 visits reach the cap. The rest of a day's options stay behind *Another job…*.

## Also counted

- 35 card charges in the window have neither a split nor a payroll mark.
