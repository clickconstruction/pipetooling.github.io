## The read

`list_card_charges_window(p_start_ymd, p_end_ymd)`, over the `LANGUAGE sql` rows function `_card_charges_window_rows` (checked in full when the push creates it; callable only by the wrapper) — every card charge posted in the company days asked for (at most 366), one row each: the attribution, the card, its holder and nickname, the bank category, the accounting label, the payroll mark, the real job splits, the invoice links, who sorted it last, and whether the viewer's split write would take it. Office roles only; definer, because the card-holder links are not office-readable through RLS. The payroll mark keeps its own rule (`has_payroll_access()`): without it, a charge settled by a payroll mark alone is left out, as the Team purchases queue leaves it out, so Spending's totals differ by role by exactly those charges. Agreed with #72 (the Tally team queue) as its history read too, so one function serves both screens; its splits and invoice links carry the v2.4566 Sorted RPC's keys.

`src/lib/banking/cardChargesWindow.ts` — the row type, the mapper (the Sorted parsers for the splits and invoice links) and `fetchCardChargesWindow`, paged past PostgREST's 1,000-row cap.

## The kernel

`src/lib/people/spendingRollup.ts` — `buildSpendingRollup`: one row per person, plus *Company cards* and *Not tied to anyone*, with card spend, ⛽ fuel, other, on jobs, Office, payroll, not on a job, and before sorting began; each person's jobs (spend and fuel), their charges on supply invoices, and their charges on no job, each with how *Put on a job* saves it and whether this viewer can. It calls the job screens' rules instead of restating them: `cardChargeCostUsd` (a refund comes off — once it reaches the kernel: until v2.4611 the read dropped card refunds, which Mercury files as kind `other`), `cardChargeAllocationCounts` (no Internal Transfers), `categoryTagForCharge` against the fuel family's tag, the one rule's invoice-linked test. A charge on a supply-house invoice counts On jobs but is listed under the invoice, never under a job, so each job cell is the Job window's card line; the Office job is its own bucket; a payroll mark and a charge before Tally's sorting floor are not work to do. Who: the attribution (a person record with a login is that login), else Company cards on a company card, else the card's holder (counted "by card"), else Not tied to anyone.
