# v2.3542 — People → Day book: what the office got done, any day you look back at (2026-09-16)

Client + one migration (one RPC, no table). PRs 1 and 2 of `to-dos/day-book/` shipped together, the way the tip strip did (v2.3496): the tab handles the RPC not being live yet, so the client can merge before the push.

## Why

Grace, on the owner's behalf: *"When people in the office clock in and clock out they don't want to record everything they do every day, and management would like some oversight as to what they are doing. I don't think presenting every click is valuable, but presenting the outcomes is — 'Billed X, Y, and Z' in a daily summary."* Then: a People tab so it can be looked back at through time, and the "is this the best we can do?" pass that turned a person-volume month grid into a coverage grid and took "quiet days" out of every total.

The app already stamps who did what. `job_activity_events` carries the actor on every bill marked, payment applied and status move; contract sends, clock approvals, dispatch answers and deletions each have an actor column. What was missing was a surface that read them per person per day. This adds that surface and nothing new for anyone to fill in.

## What shipped

- **Migration [`20260917003402_day_book_payload.sql`](../migrations/20260917003402_day_book_payload.md)** — `get_day_book_payload(p_from, p_to, p_person default null)`, SECURITY DEFINER, gated inline the way `get_crew_day_payload` is. Dev, controller and pay-approved masters (`is_dev() OR has_payroll_access()`) may pick any person and receive amounts; assistants, unapproved masters and estimators are pinned to themselves and every `amount_usd` is emitted NULL, so the client never holds a figure it may not show. Range capped at 93 days. Returns raw rows in one shape — `sessions` (Office job and bid sessions, with the clock-out note), `events` (`actor_user_id · day · kind · ref · amount_usd · detail`) and `system_counts` — plus the users and jobs the rows point at.
- **Kernel [`src/lib/people/dayBook.ts`](../../src/lib/people/dayBook.ts)** (+18 tests) — `buildDayBookView` groups by day then person and writes the lines: *Billed 3 · J102 J258 J273 · $14,200*, *Applied 4 deposits · 3 jobs*, *Moved 2 jobs to Billed*, *Sent 2 contracts*, *Filed 1 signed contract*, *Approved 12 clock sessions · 5 people · 61.5h*, *Reviewed hours*, *Answered 3 dispatch requests*, *Deleted 4 records · recoverable* (muted). An invoice both marked billed and sent counts once. A day with clock time and no lines is `quiet` and counts in no total. Notes ride on the row. Days come newest first; people with more lines first.
- **Door [`dayBookDoor.ts`](../../src/lib/people/dayBookDoor.ts)** — `/people?tab=day_book&dayb_from=…&dayb_to=…[&dayb_person=…]`, namespaced like the Review door because a bare `person=` is the Person desk's.
- **Tab [`PeopleDayBookTab.tsx`](../../src/components/people/PeopleDayBookTab.tsx)** — toolbar (week stepper, *This week*, Week / Month pills with Month disabled until PR 3, the person select for payroll viewers, kind chips Everything · Billing · Deposits · Contracts · Approvals · Schedule, the last disabled until the schedule keeps a ledger), a six-tile range strip with no "quiet days" figure, then each day with its people. Today's lines end with what is left from the live counts the Dashboard already reads: *48 still waiting* on approvals, *1 left to match* on deposits, *N jobs still without one* on contracts. A day header adds *and N more by the system* for rows written with no actor. Registered in `peopleTabGroups.ts` under the People group (18 → 19 tabs), gated by two new `usePeopleAccess` flags, rendered inline in `People.tsx` like every other tab.
- **Help guide** `see-what-the-office-got-done.md`; the People page guide lists the new view.

## What the census changed (to-dos/day-book/census-2026-09.md)

The plan's PR 0 ran first, read-only against prod, and two of its findings changed the build:

- **94 % of `invoice_sent` rows carry no actor** — the Stripe and email send functions write the send stamp under the service role. So the *Billed* line reads **`invoice_billed`** ("Marked billed", 105 of 106 attributed), plus any in-app send, deduped per invoice; unattributed sends are counted on the day header as system rows. A follow-up (PR 1b in the to-do) should have the send functions stamp the caller so the send itself becomes attributable.
- **Deletions dominate the ledger** (7,091 rows in 30 days, 989 in a nine-day payload), so the RPC aggregates them per person, day and table and the kernel sums `detail.n`.

Also learned: the quiet-day rate is 23 %, not the 40 % that would have sent the design back; 56 of 57 office days carry a real clock-out note ("bids", "Office", "Walmart and office"), which is why the note is worth a line; `hours_reviewed` has not been used in 30 days, so that line will read zero for now.
