# v2.8120 — Job pace and the Projects board: a small job can no longer hide a large gap (2026-11-20)

Punch list #406, the fault it had left alone. Client only. No migration and no edge function.

## What was wrong

Ahead or behind was decided in crew days: the hours over or under the work done, divided by the job's crew day, a day or more either way. A job with fewer than two worked days takes an assumed crew day of 24 hours. So on a small job, a gap that is most of the budget can still be under half a crew day, and the job read *On pace.*

The assessment of 2026-11-20 found one on the board: hours at 140% of the budget, the work at 80%, and the words *on pace*. The owner's word: fix it.

## The rule now

`hoursVerdict` in `src/lib/jobs/superintendentLine.ts`, pure and tested, the one rule the office's line and the crew's card both read:

- a crew day or more either way decides it, as before;
- under a crew day, a gap of **a tenth of the hours budget** or more decides it too (`PACE_SHARE_PTS`, 10). Ten is the line the money reading already uses.

A gap under a crew day is no day, so it is said in hours:

- the line's headline reads *6 hours behind.* or *9 hours ahead at this pace.*;
- the strip's chip reads *-6 h* or *+9 h* (`paceGapShort`);
- the Dashboard's Projects line names its worst project as *6 h behind* (`worstWords`);
- the crew's card (`paceReading.ts`) reads *Hours are ahead of the work.* with *The gap is about 6 hours.*, or *The work is ahead of the hours.*

The line carries the gap as `hoursAhead`, whole hours, signed. `days` stays 0 for such a gap, so the finish day at this pace does not move.

## What it changes

- A job under a crew day off its hours, by a tenth of its budget or more, leaves *on pace* for ahead or behind. On the board's data of the day that was two of eighteen projects: the small job above, and the test project, which reads ahead.
- A large job is decided by its crew days long before a tenth of its budget, so nothing changes for it.
- A day count that rounded to minus zero is a plain zero.

## Not changed

- Under 8 hours on a job is still too early to call.
- The money reading, and that it outranks the hours on the office's line.

## Checked

- `superintendentLine.test.ts`: the small job both ways, one hour in the singular, a gap under a tenth staying on pace, a large job unchanged, `hoursVerdict` at its edges, and `paceGapShort`.
- `paceReading.test.ts`: the same small job on the crew's card.
- `projectsBoard.test.ts`: the worst project's words in hours.
- Three earlier tests read a new, correct answer and were updated: the test project's 9 of 70 hours at 25% now reads ahead.
