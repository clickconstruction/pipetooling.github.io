# v2.4956 — GC mode: the schedule read back from its rows (2026-10-08)

PR 6a of the schedule's real build (`to-dos/gc-mode/SCHEDULE_REAL_BUILD.md`; its plan `to-dos/gc-mode/mockups/schedule-pr6.md`, both on branch `spike/gc-mode`): the mapper and the reads. No migration and no screen.

- `src/lib/gc/schedule/rows.ts`, pure: `scheduleFieldsFromRows` reads one job's schedule rows (the schedule's PRs 2 to 4) back as the kernels' shapes: the bars with their waits, gaps, limits, parts and inspections; the dates to meet; the baselines (the one at Start with no one's name on it); the moves newest first by the version they made, with their pushes, tells and answers; the walks, marks and late notices; the waits, crew counts, sends, the reader's own what-if copy (its places read from the real bars) and the rough. `templatesFromRows` reads the templates.
- A bar's `lineId` is its row's id; a line's bar has its scope line's id (the schedule's PR 5). A row that names a bar that is gone is left out, never guessed.
- The job itself is the board's: `boardProjectFromView` (`src/lib/gc/boardRows.ts`, B3-b), the one project mapper. `withScheduleRows` lays one job's schedule fields over that project in the board's state (`boardStateFromRows`), sets the company's templates on the state, and gives the schedule's version beside it; every other project stays as the board read it. The schedule never builds a project of its own.
- `src/lib/gc/scheduleIo.ts`, beside `gcIo.ts`: `loadSchedule(state, projectId)` reads only the schedule's rows in two rounds (child rows a hundred ids at a time), the names of the people they name and the templates, then lays them over the board's state. It never re-reads the board.
- `rows.direct.test.ts`: Fair Oaks Shops, Building D, given one of every record through the kernels, written as rows and read back equal, field for field; the same read from shuffled rows; a bar taken away; the rows laid over the board's own job, every other project untouched; and a job with nothing drawn.
