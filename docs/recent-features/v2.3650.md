# v2.3650 — Hiring, the helper try-out loop PR 2: the leader's verdict — "take them again?" at the helper's clock-out, by name (2026-09-20)

PR 2 of [`to-dos/helper-tryout-loop`](../../to-dos/helper-tryout-loop/README.md). PR 1 (v2.3627) made Try-out a stage; this is the half that gives the stage a purpose: the people who worked with the helper say, the same day and by name, whether they want them back. One migration.

## Who is asked — the Supervision rule, not the to-do's first draft

The to-do was written before the Supervision train (v2.3611–v2.3616) and named `derivedLead` ("the master or sub on the helper's linked block, else one who clocked the same job"). Supervision replaced that with one rule the whole app now shares, and `whosWhere.ts` already said *the helper try-out loop asks these people* of `crewSupervisors`. So the card goes to **everyone who could run a job the helper worked that day**: a master, or a helper / sub the office switched *needs supervision* off for, **listed** (`job_schedule_blocks`) or **clocked** (`clock_sessions`) on the same job and day. A helper who still needs supervision is never asked. Several leaders on one job are each asked; the table is unique per card, leader and day. Nothing reads or writes the Team leads list.

## Left to PR 3, on purpose

The office does not see the verdicts on the board yet — the tally, the nudge, *Keep trying*, and the Try-out card's *no lead listed — ask Dispatch to put a master on the block* line (a day with nobody who could run the job asks nobody) are PR 3. The rows are being collected from this release on.

## Verify

A master is on a block with a trial helper; the helper clocks in and out on that job → the master's Dashboard shows *Take <name> again?* and the push arrives; Yes + a word → the card collapses to *You said yes · "…"* with Change. A cleared sub who clocked the same job and clocks out sees the prompt before the crew deck. A sub who still needs supervision sees nothing. A board holder reads both rows in `team_prospect_trial_verdicts`.
