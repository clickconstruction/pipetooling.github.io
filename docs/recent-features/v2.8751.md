# v2.8751 — The trade portal's job page fits its shell on a small phone (2026-11-27)

Punch list #205, retired. Finding 334 of the fixing thread's ledger, from the simulator thread's sitting 3 of 2026-11-27 (SE, 375 by 667, iOS 26.4, Safari, `/t/sample`, reads only). Client only. No migration, no edge function. Nothing owed after the merge.

**As seen.** The shell's right border stood at 358 points. Every card on the sample job's page ran under it with no right edge of its own. A sideways swipe moved nothing, so the cut part could not be reached. The portal's home did not do this.

**Read.** The shell hides what runs past it (`overflow: hidden`, `GcTradePortalView.tsx`). Every page of the portal is a stack of grids that name no column, over a hundred of them across its files. Such a grid has one column as wide as its widest child wants to be. So one child that cannot shrink widens every card at once. Messages had the same fault (`lib/messages/pageFit.ts`).

**The child.** The brief could not find it. On a scratch page with the repo's own sample answers, at 375 px in a desk Chrome, each piece of the job's page was asked for its least width. One stood out: the browser's own file picker under *Your quote file*, 258 px, 25 px inside the limit. Every row of set-width boxes in the quote form already wraps. Safari on an iPhone draws a file picker wider than Chrome does. Given a picker 340 px wide on the same scratch page, the desk showed what the simulator saw: all four sections past the shell.

**The mend.** `src/lib/gc/tradePortalFit.ts`, one rule in the shell's own `<style>`:

- A grid inside the shell that names no column gets `minmax(0, 1fr)`, the column that takes the room it is given and no more. The rule reads a grid by its drawn style, so it holds every grid of the portal at once, and one added later. A grid that names its columns, or flows sideways, is left alone.
- The file picker takes `max-width: 100%`, so it keeps to the card it stands in.

The first rule stops the cut whichever child is the wide one. The second keeps the picker itself from sticking out of its card.

**Measured on a desk, with a made-up wide picker.** No desk browser draws the picker as an iPhone does, so the script gave it a width of 340 px to stand in. The real page over the sample answers, a headless Chrome, no host asked beyond the page.

**Not proved here.** That the picker is the child on a real iPhone. The rule does not depend on it.

**Tests.** `tradePortalFit.test.ts`: the rule's words, the column Messages took, nothing outside the shell. `GcTradePortal.render.test.tsx`: the shell carries the rule, and every grid on the job's page is one the rule reaches or one that names its own columns. That holds the rule's reading of a drawn style. No test holds what a browser does with it.
