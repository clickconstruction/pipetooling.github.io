# v2.8306 — The main chunk's split, step 3: a public page loads by itself (2026-11-22)

Steps 1 and 2 (v2.8098, v2.8104) took nine windows out of the entry's chunk. This takes the pages someone opens from a link with no sign-in: the estimate, the account page, pay, the agreement, the rating, the portals, the rooms and the short links. A customer's link no longer downloads every other public page with it. It also mends a fault in `lazyWindow` that steps 1 and 2 shipped. No migration and no function.

Twenty-three pages, on twenty-six routes. Fifteen were imported eagerly and are lazy now: `EstimateAccept`, `EstimatePublicTerms`, `HazmatNoticePublic`, `CustomerPortal`, `PayLink`, `CustomerRating`, `JobContractSign`, `ContractAccept`, `SubPortal`, `BidRoom`, `SubmittalRoom`, `SupplyHouseQuotePage`, `LegalPortal`, `LegalConfirm` and `GcWordAsk`. ## What changed

- **On demand.** `App.tsx` holds each page through `lazyWindow` (`src/lib/lazyWindow.ts`), the helper the windows use, and draws it inside `PublicPage` (`src/components/PublicPage.tsx`), which carries the page's own `Suspense` and its own chunk boundary.
- **While it loads.** `PublicPageLoading` draws the page's own ground, pinned light, in one of three tones. On the *landing* (the estimate, the agreement, the sub's contract, the bid room) it is the same photo, title and white box with *Loading…* that those pages draw while they read their data, so the customer sees one picture from the first paint to the estimate. On *paper* (the portals, pay, the short links) and on a *plain* light page (the rating, the terms, the rooms) it is the bare ground, and *Loading…* after a quarter second, by a timer and not a CSS delay: a tab in the background holds its animations still.
- **A wait that runs long.** A fetch that hangs never fails. Past ten seconds the page says *This is taking a while. Your signal may be weak.* with **Try again**, which reloads.
- **A file that did not come.** A lost chunk still gets the one guarded reload every lazy page gets (`chunkLoadRecovery`), which is what carries an old tab onto a new deploy, and the page keeps its loading look meanwhile, not *Updating app…*. When that reload is spent it says *This page did not load. Check your signal, then try again.* with **Try again**. No staff words and no repair link.
- **Offline, no reload.** `startChunkRecoveryReload` now refuses while the browser says it is offline, and leaves its one reload unclaimed. A reload then has nothing to fetch: it emptied the caches and landed on the browser's own error page. The public page says *You are offline. This page will load when you are back online.*, the press answers in words, and the page reloads by itself when the signal is back. The staff boundary (`RouteChunkBoundary`) and `main.tsx`'s preload handler go through the same kernel, so a signed-in phone that loses its signal keeps its caches and gets the fallback with its **Reload** press.
- **`lazyWindow` asked without end.** When a load failed, the helper made its next ask ready at once. The render that was waiting drew again, took the new ask, failed again, and so on: no pause, and no error for any boundary to catch. A test counted 116,720 asks in five seconds. On the live site the window-level reload hid it the first time, and a second failure inside a minute would have spun on *Opening…*. Now the failure is kept for two seconds (`FAILED_LOAD_HELD_MS`), which hands it to the waiting render and its boundary, and the next open after that asks afresh. A warm-up that failed with nobody waiting still leaves the first open free to ask. The nine windows of steps 1 and 2 get the same mend.
- **The guards.** `ON_DEMAND_PAGES` in `src/lib/entryGraph.ts` lists the twenty-three, and `entryGraph.repo.test.ts` fails a PR that makes one reachable from `src/main.tsx` again, with the chain of imports that did it. `ENTRY_SOURCE_BUDGET_BYTES` comes down from 12.5 MB to 9 MB. `lazyRouteBoundaries.ts` reads `lazyWindow(…)` as lazy and `<PublicPage>` as a `Suspense`, so a public route with neither still fails.

## Seen

Each of the twenty-three was opened on the dev server at 393 by 852 and at 1,280 wide, one at a time, through its `sample` link where it has one and through a made-up code where it has none (`/r`, `/f`, `/b/<code>`, `/ask`, `/hazmat-notice`, which answer with their own *this link opens nothing* page). The estimate and the account page were read before the change and after it, and the words on each matched to the letter; the estimate matched again on the built app. The pay page drew the same picture with the same count of letters.

The three pictures a customer may now see were looked at by holding a loader open, and by making one fail: *Loading…* on paper and on the landing, the ten-second line with its press, and *This page did not load.* on both grounds. The failing one took the real road: one reload from the root, the address put back, then the words.

## What is left

- **A customer's link still downloads the staff shell.** `Layout` and the signed-in providers are imported by `App.tsx`, so 3.4 MB comes before any public page. By the import graph the entry reaches 7.73 MB of source, and 2.45 MB without them (279 files against 989). Loading the shell on demand is the step that makes a pay link small.
- **The service worker registers on a public page.** `UpdatePrompt` mounts for every route, so a customer's phone precaches the whole app after the page draws: 1,515 files, 50 MB. Seen on the built app, where the second public page was served by the worker.
- **A weak signal the browser still calls online.** The first failure reloads once, as before. If that reload cannot fetch `index.html` the browser's own error page shows.
- **The Settings page's chunk**, 5.76 MB. Three tabs hold most of it by the import graph: What the team sees, What customers see and Contracts.
- `PartnerStatement`, `Logbook` and `ContractBookPreview` are signed-in pages still imported eagerly, 0.15 MB of source together.
