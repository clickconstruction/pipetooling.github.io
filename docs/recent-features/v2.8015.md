# v2.8015 — Before we start: a bullet of ours reads the app (2026-11-19)

Punch list #382, PR 8 of eleven: our bullets read the app. No migration and no edge function: `start_check_bullets.reads` has held the four kinds since v2.7997. Nothing here reaches the GC.

## What a person sees

A bullet of ours carries its tag as a value in place: *ours, **ticked by hand***. A press turns it into a choice with **Keep** and **Cancel**: ticked by hand, read from the order log, read from Trips, read from the city's permits.

A bullet the app reads shows the fact and a door to the screen that holds it, in place of the hand tick.

| Read from | Ready when | The fact, for example | The door |
|---|---|---|---|
| The order log | Every line of the stage is on site, or is ordered and lands before our first day | *1 of 3 lines on site. The last lands Nov 25, 6 days ahead.* | **Open the order log** (`/bids?tab=submittals&bidId=`) |
| Trips | A trip on the job with a person on it has days that cover our first day | *Trip 4: Ana, Bo and Cy, Nov 30 to Dec 4.* | **Open Trips** (`/developments/:id?look=trips`) |
| The city's permits | A plumbing permit in our name at the job's address is issued | *Plumbing permit P-100 was issued Nov 3.* | **Open Permits** (`/prospects?tab=permits`) |

A reader never guesses. Where the app holds nothing to read, the row says so and reads open, and the same value turns the bullet back to a hand tick:

- a job that came from no bid has no order log, and a stage whose name is not Rough In, Top Out or Trim Set has no lines in it;
- a line with no order, or no day to land, is open, and so is one that lands on our first day or after it;
- trips are planned on a development, so a job on none has no trip to read;
- the city's records cover the cities the Permits tab reads, and only where the address reads as a street number in one of them.

## What the app does not record

**Nothing in the app records our own permit for a job.** The permit reader reads the city's public feed, as the bid form's site panel does: a plumbing permit at the job's address whose contact is one of our names (Settings → Company → the names on our permits). It is the only permit fact the app holds. A job in a city we do not read stays a hand tick.

## Who reads what

Each read goes the way its own screen goes, under the reader's own row security, so a bullet never shows a fact its reader could not open. The cost is that two seats can read one bullet differently:

- a superintendent reads the order log only on a bid tied to their master, and reads only the trips they are on;
- only the office and the estimators read the city's permits.

A seat that cannot read a source is told so on the row (*Only the office and the people on a trip can read it.*), and the bullet reads open for that seat. A read that fails says *The app could not read this just now.* on its own bullets, and the other kinds still read.

## How it is built

- `src/lib/startChecks/startCheckReaders.ts`: the three readers, pure, each from the few fields it asks (`OrderLine`, `TripFact`, `PermitFact`); `readsWanted` names the jobs that use each kind; `readerFor` is the `readUs` seam `stageFromRows` has taken since PR 4.
- `src/lib/startChecks/startCheckReadersIo.ts`: the order log built as Bids → Submittals builds it (`buildProcurementLog` over the newest revision's rows, the rows that stand approved on an earlier one, the records and each tag's stage), the site's trips (`readSiteTrips`, `tripsOnJob`), and the city's permits at the address (`readBidAddress`, `loadPermitsByHouseNumber`, `permitsOnSite`, `isOurName`).
- `src/hooks/useStartCheckReads.ts`: asks only for the kinds a live bullet uses, on the jobs that use them. A list with every bullet ticked by hand reads nothing more than it did.
- `useJobStartChecks` and `useSiteStartChecks` run it, so the Documents tab and the Schedule look read alike.
- `changeBullet` takes `reads`. A bullet turned to the app drops its hand tick, as the table's own rule asks.

The starter's bullet of ours, *Our material lands before day one.*, still starts as a hand tick: a job with no bid would read open on day one otherwise. One press turns it to the order log.
