# v2.5395 — Messages: where a residential customer is on their job, read from what the app keeps, behind the scenes (2026-10-12)

R4 · 2 of 4 of the residential build (`to-dos/gc-mode/messages/RESIDENTIAL_BUILD_PLAN.md` on `spike/gc-mode`): the residential flow's loader. `residentialStampsFor(rows, now)` in `src/lib/flows/load.ts` sits beside `flowStampsFor` and turns one residential customer's rows into `FlowStamps` for `readFlow`, one `JobStamps` per Pipeline job (`jobs_ledger.id`). Pure, no client: the query layer selects the customer's rows and hands them in. No migration, no screen. It stacks on R0 (the `residential` kind) and R4 · 1 (the eleven facts and the seed), and reads no column main lacks.

**The facts, per job** (one family function each, exported for tests):

- `visitStamps`: `booked` is dispatch's first `job_schedule_blocks` row for the job, by whoever made it. `on_way` is the first On our way text. `arrived` is the first `clock_sessions` clock-in there that was not revoked or rejected. `found` is the first `reports` row whose words read through `jobReportPreviewLine` (a percent or a blank alone is not a finding).
- `estimateStamps`: `estimate_sent` is the newest `estimates.sent_at` on the job, a change order and a draft aside. `estimate_approved` is `status = 'customer_accepted'` with `acceptor_consented_at`, which accept-estimate writes on the row. There is no separate acceptance table.
- `statusStamps`: `working` is the first `job_status_events` move to Working, else the job standing past Waiting with no move kept (no day). `done` holds while the job stands at Ready to Bill or past, dated by its first move there after its last move back. A job moved back to Working drops it.
- `reviewStamps`: `review_asked` is the first review ask text.
- **The bills are loops** (`billedRounds`, `paidRounds`), one round per `jobs_ledger_invoices` row keyed by the invoice id, so They owe us and the bill reminder read the same round. A draft (`ready_to_bill`) is an open Billed round until it goes. Each sent bill is a Paid round. It reads paid when main's one rule (`attributeJobPayments`: its own payments in full, then the job's unlinked money oldest bill first, given the job's revenue) covers it, or when it is marked paid, dated by the last payment it took. Otherwise it waits on them from the day it went. Its due day is the expected pay chip's (`billedExpectedPayModel` with the bill's payer from `paySpeedPayer`, or the job's promised day), the same call the Stages tab makes. Helper 9's `billDueDay` replaces it once it lands.

**Two guesses from the words** stand in for the quick reply's step on a message, which main does not keep yet (R3's screens PR adds it). Each is one exported function so the swap is one line. `isOnOurWayText(body, firstName)` needs their first name, "this is", and "on our way", "on the way" or "minutes out", because the Booked text greets them the same way. `isReviewAskText(body)` needs "a review" or "review us", because the estimate text says "Review and approve it".

**Not kept on main**: a customer's days to pay. `customers.payment_terms` is a policy key, not a count of days, so the Paid round's due day is the pay speed model, not their terms (recorded in the plan for R8 · 6).

**Tests**: `load.residential.test.ts` (24) builds Dana Whitfield, the prototype's demo customer, on two jobs. Her finished August job stamps every fact and reads finished. Her water heater job, the star, is read after each stage: on Estimate sent after the report, on Approved once the estimate went, on Working after her yes, on Paid once billed, on Review asked once paid, finished once asked. Also covered: a job with a block alone reads booked, an unpaid bill waits with its due day, a promise wins over her pace, unlinked money pays the bill, a second bill is its own round, a revoked clock-in and a wordless report count for nothing, and done drops on a move back. Three pins were broken on purpose before pushing: counting revoked clock-ins, counting linked payments only, and dropping the road words. Each failed the tests that pin it.
