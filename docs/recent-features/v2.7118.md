# v2.7118 — What we buy: What to stock, the parts we keep going back for (2026-11-13)

Punch list #298 PR 4c (`to-dos/supply-bill-lines/README.md`, frame I of its mock-up), over What we buy ([v2.7114](v2.7114.md)) and Price over time ([v2.7116](v2.7116.md)). The owner's ask was what to stack up on. A bill cannot say what was used. It can say what the company keeps going back for.

## What a person sees

The third view of Materials → What we buy, **What to stock**.

- **A row per part bought on three or more separate days** in the window, the most trips first: trips, how many at a time (*2 to 4 EA*), how many jobs, what was spent, where (*the counter, both houses* or *delivered, ‹house›*) and one line to read it as (*a trip every 18 days for a $3.10 part*, *one at a time, 3 times*, *on big orders, not a trip to the counter*).
- **Window**: the last 30, 90 or 180 days. A window with nothing in it offers the longest.
- **A row's name** opens its prices bill by bill.
- **The two limits, on the page**: a bill is a purchase and not a use, and big-box card purchases are not here.

## How it is built

- **`src/lib/materials/lines/whatToStock.ts`** (pure, `whatToStock.test.ts`, 7 tests): `whatToStock` groups lines the way What we buy does (`lineIdentity`), counts a trip as a day and not a bill, leaves a return out, and reads a trip as to the counter when its bill carries a PO Generator code or the whole bill, charges with it, is under `SMALL_BILL_CENTS` ($250). A row is a counter part when half its trips or more were.
- **`purchaseLinesIo.ts`** answers which counted bills carry a PO Generator code: the bill's PO text through `parsePoGeneratorCodeFromPurchaseOrderName`, held to the ledger's codes.
- **`WhatToStock.tsx`**: the view. `WhatWeBuyTab.tsx` gains the third entry in its strip.

## Choices

- **Three trips to be listed**, and **$250 for a small bill**. Both are constants. Neither was on the card; they are the owner's to move once the list is read by the person who buys at the counter.
- **The count of card charges with no lines is not on the page.** The mock-up printed one. It would need the bank's card charges matched to material houses, which punch list #295 owns. The limit is stated in words.

## Is this the best we can do?

Honestly, the list is thin today, and the page says why. On the live data the last 90 days list 22 rows and no row has more than 4 trips.

- **Big-box purchases have no lines.** They are the small, frequent buys this list most wants. A photo of the receipt (punch list #295) is their door.
- **A part at two houses is two rows**, each with half the trips, until its codes are matched. The matching list (2a) fixes that and is next.
- **Where** is a guess from the bill's size and its PO code. A bill that says *will call* or *delivered* in its own words would be better evidence, and the reader does not ask for it yet.
