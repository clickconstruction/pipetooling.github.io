# v2.5252 — Messages: the groundwork for what customers and trade partners see on their own page (2026-10-12)

Cut 8 of the Messages build (`to-dos/gc-mode/messages/BUILD_PLAN.md` on `spike/gc-mode`), wave 1, by GC Reviews helper 8, directed by GC Review: the kernels of the portals, lifted from the prototype's v1.178 (`prototype.html`: `portalStepHtml`, `portalHtml`, `portalWhereHtml`, `portalMove`, `portalMoneyHtml`, `portalPapersHtml`, `portalReady`, `portalWords`, and the `PT`, `PB` and `PW` tables; the pieces landed in v1.97, v1.162 to v1.165). Pure `.ts` with unit tests. No screen, no table, no edge function. Dev only.

- **The words** (`src/lib/portal/pageWords.ts`): every sentence of ours on a person's own page, as one table in English and Spanish (`say(lang, key, vars)`), with what each paper asks (`PAPER_WORDS`: its name, why, its press, what to expect while we are on it, when it comes). Dates are blanks filled by main's `pDate` / `pWeekday`. The company, its phone, the superintendent, the days to pay and the card's fee are blanks too. The Spanish trade words follow main's trade portal (`portalI18n.ts`): *renuncia de gravamen*, *documento para aprobación*, *pago* for a draw. The page passes main's `portalShownLang(lang)`, so Spanish stays behind `PORTAL_SPANISH_ON` until a native speaker reads it.
- **Their first page** (`src/lib/flows/portalPage.ts`): `portalStep` reads one step from their side as done (with its day), yours (why, the press, due or late), decide, ran out, waiting, *We are on it* (with what to expect) or *Later* (with when it comes). `portalPage` lays a trade partner's or an architect's page out as Getting set up, one section per job they are asked on, and Open. `customerWhere` is the customer's *Where you are* on Home: a bar and three rows that open to all. A step comes in as the flow's state already read it (`PortalStepIn`), so this waits on nothing in `flows/state.ts`.
- **Your move** (`src/lib/flows/customerMove.ts`): the first thing we need from the customer in the flow's order. That is a decision of theirs, the lowest open change order, or a paper of ours waiting on their signature. Otherwise one line: *Nothing from you today. We are on Start date.*
- **Money** (`src/lib/portal/moneyLines.ts`, `moneyStory.ts`): the six lines, and the Money tab as the owner picked it. The win comes first, then one card per pay application, *After that*, the change orders waiting, the newest paid card with the older ones folded, *History* and *The statement*. `CustomerChange` is the one change-order shape Your move, Money, Papers and readiness all read.
- **Papers** (`src/lib/portal/papers.ts`): `customerPapers` (Signed, Waiting on you, Comes later), and `portalWords`, the office's read-back of what the customer opened. `PaperView` is one shape for the thread pill, the step card's *So far*, Follow up's portal rows and By company's Portal column.
- **Readiness** (`src/lib/portal/readiness.ts`): `portalReady`, the side panel's *Ready for Priya?*, one row per piece, each with the page that fixes it.

## What changed from the prototype, and why

1. **A decision shows only when it is theirs.** The prototype drew our *Compared* on a trade partner's page, with *Accept the bid* and *Not this time*, while it was on now. Its decision branch never asked who moves. Our decisions never show, and a decision of ours is never the customer's move.
2. **Waiting on someone else names who.** It says the architect (with the certificate when that is what we wait for), the owner, or someone else. The prototype said "your architect for the certificate" whoever it was.
3. **No English inside the Spanish.** Paper names have both languages. Days are formatted per language, and the waiver's "Mon Oct 19" and "October" are blanks. A done step shows its day, never the office's history sentence ("She called, Tue Sep 1").
4. **What main keeps elsewhere is a blank**: the company, the phone, the superintendent, the days to pay (the job's terms, not a fixed 30) and the card's fee. With no card on the job, no card is offered.
5. **Typed lines need their Spanish.** The week line, a pay application's covers line, a change order's description, a milestone, a move and its why, and a person's role are typed by the office. A typed line with no Spanish reads in English, and readiness's *Words* row names each gap. The prototype's row was always true. What we owe them reads as its day only, "We send it to you by Mon Oct 5.", so the office's typed owed line never reaches their page.
6. **Change orders read from the order.** A credit reads "a credit of $500" and the days read "adds 3 working days". The prototype always said "adds no days". The contract takes *Read and sign* only, since main's portal declines only change orders.
7. **A view belongs to one paper, by its id.** With two change orders out, the office can tell which one was opened. The last open is main's `OfficeViewStats.lastOpenedAt`.
8. ***Comes later* drops a kind once one has come.** A job with three paid applications no longer promises pay applications later.
9. ***Built* is the paid share of the revised sum**, as the plan says. With retainage held it reads a little under the work in place. If the owner wants retainage counted, that is one line in `paidPct`.
10. ***After that* is sized by rule**: the forecast's next bill, ten percent down rounded down to $5,000 and ten percent up rounded up ($130,000 reads $115,000 to $145,000). Under $20,000 it reads one "about" rounded to $1,000. The prototype typed its ranges.
11. **One paid card shows**, the older ones folded, as the prototype's code does. Its comment said two. The v1.162 words nothing reads (*stands*, *next*, *how*, *each*, *nopay*, *receipt*, the question words) stayed behind.

Also:
- A their-move step whose own paper is already in waits on us, so it reads *We are on it*. The prototype told the architect *Your move* on an addendum they had filed.
- A pay application sent on a shared step reads as theirs to pay (*Pay by the day on it*). The prototype's code never reached those words.
- A ran-out step names the paper and how to renew it. The prototype kept the first-time why, which reads wrong once they have an agreement.
- *Where you are* counts the rows it can show, so *All 8 steps* lists eight.
- Readiness gives a customer off the flow one row, since the page reads only a thank-you line. Its counts read in the singular when there is one.
- Sentences with a semicolon or parentheses became two sentences, held by the plain-words rules.
