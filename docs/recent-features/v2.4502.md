# v2.4502 — AIA G702-G703: the lines come from the bid's schedule of values (2026-10-04)

The second half of the lines work, after [v2.4498](v2.4498.md) (an application carries lines). The owner's four calls, 2026-10-04 (*"I agree with all four, build it"*): the lines are the bid's schedule, with the job's reported percents offered on it; labor and material rows are a switch per job, starting from the bid; a job price that is not the bid's total is shown and scaled, never an adjustment line; more than the sheet holds is grouped (v2.4498's 34-row message). Client only: no migration, no edge function.

## The change

- **Kernel** [`aiaBidSchedule.ts`](../../src/lib/aiaBidSchedule.ts) (new):
  - `linesFromBidSovLines(sovLines, ruleLaborPct)` — the estimator's `bid_sov_lines` in their order, an empty row left behind, labor the typed figure else the company share (`lineSplit`), the stage kept.
  - `linesFromStageSplits(splits)` — Rough In / Top Out / Trim Set as three lines with their labor part; fewer than two stages with a value is no schedule (a half-staged bid puts the whole contract on one stage).
  - `scheduleGap(lines, contractSumToDate)` and `scaleLinesToAmount(lines, amount)` — the bid tab's own scaling rule (`scaleLinesToContract`), exact to the cent; work already claimed is left as it is.
  - `bidStageOfJobLine(name)` over `stageRank` (ground and rough → Rough In, top out → Top Out, trim and final → Trim Set); `crewPercentByStage(fixtures)` — each stage's percent from the job's own stage lines (`jobs_ledger_fixtures.progress_pct`), weighted by price when a stage has several; `crewOfferForLine(line, crew)` — the stage's percent only when it is ahead of what the line claims.
- **IO** [`aiaBidScheduleIo.ts`](../../src/lib/aiaBidScheduleIo.ts) `loadBidScheduleForJob(bidId, contractAmount)`: `sov_shape = 'lines'` with lines → those; otherwise the three stages through the doors the Cover Letter tab and the Approval PDF use (`pickActiveVersion` → `loadMaterialsByStageForBid` → `scheduleOfValuesLetter(summary, the job's price)` → `splitStageValues`). `sov_split_labor_material` is the job's starting switch. Anything that fails, or a bid with fewer than two stages staged, is null: the job keeps its one line.
- **Window**: the schedule is read once per opening, only when the job has a bid and nothing saved; application 1 starts from it and the line under the list says so. After a save the bid is not read again — the lines carry from the saved application. Under a line of a stage: *The crew reported Top Out at 90%.* **Use 90%**. Above the lines, when they do not add to the contract to date: how far off, and **Scale the lines to $…**.

## On live data, 2026-10-04

The loader was run over the 18 jobs that carry a `bid_id`. None of their bids has its own lines, 17 have nothing staged and one (job 1007) has one fixture of 22 staged, which is what the two-stage rule is for. So every job on the books today still starts with one line; the schedule arrives with bids staged or lined from here on.
