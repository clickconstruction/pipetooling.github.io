# v2.7494 — Development page: the plans by date, every set of drawings against time (2026-11-16)

The owner's ask of 2026-11-16: a one-page chart had been drawn by hand for one site, its drawings set against time with the bid, the crews' days and a letter on the same axis, and the app should offer it. Punch list #367 holds the mock-up and the train. This is PR 1, the view itself. No migration, no edge function: it reads only what the page already holds.

## Where it lives

The Plans look has two views, as Money has its own: **By part** (the parts table, unchanged) and **By date** (the chart). The switch sits at the right of the Plans card's head and takes a full row on a phone. The device remembers the view (`readPlansView` / `writePlansView` in `lib/developments/devicePrefs.ts`, key `pt.development.<id>.plans.view`; the table leaves no key). It is not called a timeline, so it cannot be mixed up with Money's Timeline, which is a cost chart for the finance seats.

## What it draws

One lane each, time left to right, a dashed line for today:

- **Bids**: a diamond on the day each bid was sent (`bids.bid_date_sent`), a small open diamond on the date of the plans it was priced from (`design_drawing_plan_date`) with a dotted join, and the day it was won or lost in its line. The robot estimators' shadow bids are left out.
- **One lane for each part**, in the parts table's order: a dot for every drawing set on the date printed on its sheets (`plan_sets.dated`), a ring and a bold name on the set the part builds from (`plan_parts.current_set_id`), an open dot on the day it came for a set with no date, a square for one of ours (an as-built or an assembled set). Papers are not drawings and are left out. A part's line begins on the day of its first set. A set that replaces a set on another part (`replaces_set_id`) draws a dotted line between the two lanes: the day one design became two parts.
- **Crews on site**: the clock's days on the site's jobs joined into bars, a day two jobs share counted once, a new bar after more than seven days with nobody on site (`crewRuns`). A job the clock has no day for draws a dashed open bar from its first work day to its last.
- **History**: a tick for every kept entry (`development_history_entries`), a flag and the word *Letter* for paper.

Four figures sit on top: **First drawings**, **Sets since the bid** (the bid is the first one won, else the first sent; the figure names it), **Newest set**, **Days on site**. Our own as-builts are left out of the counts, since they were not issued to us.

Under the chart every mark reads again as a dated line, oldest first, a bid's line tinted and today last. Past 40 lines the list opens on the newest and folds the older ones behind a press. A bid with no sent day has nowhere to stand, so a quiet line names it.

## A mark's line

The chart lays itself out at the width it really has (a `ResizeObserver` on its box hands `layoutPlansByDate` the measure), so a word is drawn at its true size on a tablet and a wide screen alike, and the names that fit are worked out for the room there is. Under 620 px the lanes are named over themselves, as on a phone. Three or more sets closer than 7 px are drawn as one bar with their count (*14 sets*), and the bar's line names them; with room to tell them apart each is its own dot again, and the set a part builds from is never folded in. A History lane with more than three letters drops the word *Letter* from each flag.

Every mark and bar takes a hover, a keyboard focus and, on a phone, a press, and shows one line: *Permit set · the GC · dated May 8, 2026 · came May 12 · Building A builds from it*. A name is drawn on the chart only where it fits: the set being built from first, then the newest, above its mark, else below it, else left to the hover and the list (`placePlansByDateLabels`). On a phone the chart names only the bids, the letters and the sets being built from.

## The print

**Print or save as PDF** opens one landscape page (`plansByDatePrintHtml` through `openHtmlPrintWindow`): the site and its customer, the four figures, the chart at its desktop shape whatever the screen, the key and the list in three columns. Paper has no theme, so its colours are literal.

## Who sees what

The chart carries dates and drawings and no money, so both doors draw it. The office door reads the clock's days (`useSiteLabor`, now also while By date is open) and `jobs_ledger.first_work_date`, a column the page did not read before. The overview door (an estimator) has no clock read and no first work day, so its crew lane shows each job's last work day and says so under the chart. A leader without payroll approval reads the same way. The crew does not open the Development page.

## How it is built

- `src/lib/plans/plansByDate.ts`, pure, in three steps. `buildPlansByDate` turns rows into lanes, marks, the list, the figures and the key. `layoutPlansByDate` turns those into shapes at a width (desktop, phone or paper). `plansByDatePrintHtml` is the page. One paint table (`PBD_PAINT`) serves the screen and the paper, so a mark means the same on both.
- `src/components/developments/DevelopmentPlansByDate.tsx` draws the shapes in theme tokens: `--house-series-2` for the sets, `--bid-mark` for the bids, `--man-hours-field` for the crews. Identity never rests on colour alone: each kind of mark has its own shape, every lane is named, and the key lists only the marks on the chart. Blue for the sets was tried and dropped, because beside the bid's violet it fails for a red-green colour-blind reader.

## Not in this PR

The rest of #367: a card on every item with its details and its presses, a schedule on a bid with a typed start and end, and sheets in hand.

## Verify after deploy

The component was looked at with made-up rows at 375, 768, 1024, 1280 and 1600 px, in light and dark, on a quiet site and on a busy one (28 sets, 11 bids, 67 History lines), and the print page in a frame. The busy site is what led to the bar for a burst of sets, the taller lane on a phone and the quieter letters. The live page on a real site was not walked. Open a development with several parts, press **By date**, and check the set each part builds from wears the ring, the crew bars match the Scoreboard's days, and the print fits one page.
