# v2.5795 — Messages: Who is this? offers the customers we know, and makes a new one in one press (2026-10-23)

Punch list #149, PR 2 of 3 (the owner's yes on the mock-up's §2; the to-do and its mock-up were retired once the three PRs shipped, `git show 7050ad052^:to-dos/messages-who-is-this/mockup.html` shows it). The gap, seen live on the real New row: an Invitation to Bid from a general contractor on bids@ got three guesses that each said *Add … first* and nothing about a customer, though a general contractor is a `customers` row in this app; and *A new customer* on the team line waited until someone added them on Customers.

- **The kernel** ([`customerLink.ts`](../../src/lib/followUp/customerLink.ts), [its test](../../src/lib/followUp/customerLink.test.ts)): `customerMatches(sender, known)`, the customers the sender may be, one guess per record with its reasons joined and the strongest first: the address or the number on a customer's record or on a person at one (`customer_contact_persons`); a bid or a Pipeline job that names them (`bids.gc_contact_email` / `gc_contact_name` / `gc_contact_phone`, `jobs_ledger.customer_email` / `customer_phone`), which carries its key so the press lands the thread on it while it is under way; the whole name; the last name as the team line guessed before; the company's own domain (`companyDomain`, never an open mail service) as someone new at that customer. A person already matched at a customer owns that customer's job and domain hits, so one sender never draws twice. `samePerson` says whether the sender's name reads as the customer's own ("Kevin" in "Kevin Alvarez") or a person at a company ("Dale Ferris" at "Ridgeway Builders"), which sets `newPerson` and the name `name_contact` keeps. `newCustomerGuess` is *A new customer* as one live press, with `makes` (the field's name, commercial on bids@ or residential on the team number, the hint). [`newLane.ts`](../../src/lib/followUp/newLane.ts) gains `senderFacts` (the address and the number from the contact's channels, the name from *this is …* or the email's From line, the domain) and puts the block first on both boxes; `tradeGuesses` reads the domain from the sender's address rather than the contact's name, so an email with a display name matches its trade partner again. [`newLaneRows.ts`](../../src/lib/followUp/newLaneRows.ts): a guess with `makes` never waits, and `namedWords` says *Ridgeway Builders is a customer now.*
- **The reads and the press** ([`newLaneCardIo.ts`](../../src/lib/followUp/newLaneCardIo.ts)): `loadKnown` reads customers and the people at them by the address, by the number's last four (settled exactly in the kernel), by the name's last word and by the domain, bids by the contact's address, number and name, Pipeline jobs by the customer's address and number, each read failing soft so a door a role cannot open leaves its matches out and the card stands; `name` passes a guess's `jobKey` to `name_contact`; `makeCustomer` is the lead doors' pattern (`customerPayloadFrom` with the box's `customer_type`, then `name_contact`), the sender a person at the company when the names differ.
- **The card** ([`NewLaneCard.tsx`](../../src/components/followUp/NewLaneCard.tsx), `WhoBody`): **A customer we know** over the matches, **A new customer** with the Customer field and **Make the customer** (dead while the field is empty), **Or** over the rest. [`NewLaneSlot.tsx`](../../src/components/followUp/NewLaneSlot.tsx) hands the contact's channels to the reads and the guesses, and routes a `makes` press through `makeCustomer` with the address, the number, the sender's name and today. Sort all draws the same card.
