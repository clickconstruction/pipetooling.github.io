## The idea

Nobody types a quantity over another. A press names what happened to some of a part in a container on a job, and the number on the screen is the sum of the rows. A count is a row like any other: a point in time that resets the line and leaves the difference on record, so *report when they are missing* is the same rows read a second way. Built 2026-10-29 in four PRs (v2.6039 the ground, v2.6041 the phone, v2.6043 the office, v2.6045 the photo sweep); the card is [`to-dos/materials-on-site/README.md`](../to-dos/materials-on-site/README.md), the owner's six calls and how each went are under its table.

## The objects

Five tables, one migration ([`20261029010000_materials_on_site.sql`](./migrations/20261029010000_materials_on_site.md)), all `ON DELETE CASCADE` from the job, and since v2.7072 four more for stickers ([`20261113020000_box_stickers.sql`](./migrations/20261113020000_box_stickers.md), the last four rows below; a sticker outlives its job and its box, on no box again). Two more hold no material: `on_site_part_ties` (v2.7076), a person's answer that two part names on a site are one part, and `on_site_prices` (v2.7159), a price a person accepted for a row of the site's materials or the answer that it is not a part, both scoped to the site and read by its screen and, since v2.7159, by the store for every job's prices (The screens). `job_part_renames` (v2.7212, the last row below) is the one update of the ledger, a typed name's spelling. A job combine carries them to the target instead (v2.6423), and the dev seat moves them to another job with the source left standing (v2.6808, below). Both run one function, `on_site_carry_job` ([`20261109160000`](./migrations/20261109160000_on_site_move_job.md)): a place of the same name becomes one, the source's Unsorted folds into the target's with its movements, moves and photos, a live cage or box whose label the target already holds keeps its row and printed code and takes the site's next free name (`on_site_next_box_name`, v2.7276; `A1 (2)` before, which the name rule refuses), and since v2.7265 ([`20261114070000`](./migrations/20261114070000_on_site_carry_job_renames_ties_prices_runs.md)) the job's part renames and sticker runs take the target job and its own ties and prices go to the target's scope, the target job or its site, a pair both answered keeping the newer answer. A box's own moves name no job and follow their box.

|---|---|---|---|

| `returned` | −1 | — | *Back to the supply house*; the office expects a credit |

## The arithmetic

One pure kernel, [`src/lib/materials/onSite.ts`](../src/lib/materials/onSite.ts) (tests beside it), everything else reads it:

**A press taken back is in no sum** (v2.7827). No sum knows about it: the ledger's own SELECT policy closes the row, and the kernel, `on_site_job_summary`, `on_site_line_on_hand` and the dev seat each read under the caller's RLS. A new sum written as its owner (`SECURITY DEFINER`) must add `taken_back_at IS NULL` itself.

- **`partKey`** — a part's one identity on a job: `id:<part_id>`, or `text:<name folded>` for a typed name (trimmed, lower-cased, spaces collapsed).
- **`containerLines(movements)`** — the lines of every container, from the rows in `(at, id)` order: a `counted` row sets the line to the number counted and records `lastCountDiff = counted − on hand`; every other row adds to its `to` side and takes from its `from` side; `cannotFindSinceCount` sums Can't find presses since the last count. **On hand is the sum. Expected at a count is the sum before it. Missing is a negative difference; found a positive one.**
- **`linesOf(lines, container)`** — what a crew sees: a line with something on hand, a Can't find since the count, or a shortfall at the last count.
- **`countDiffs(movements)`** — every count with its expected, counted and difference, newest first: the Missing section and the money read these.
- **`lastCountedAt`, `countIsDue`, `STALE_COUNT_DAYS = 14`** — a box with no count, or one older than 14 days, is due.
- **Words**: `REASON_WORDS`, `pressLabel` (*Mark 4 used*, *Move 10 to A1*, *Save 12*), `leftWords` (*A2 will have 10 left.*), `containerWords`, `nextLabel` (A1 → A2), `compareLabels` (A1, A2, A10), `groupByPlace` (Unsorted first on its own, then the places in name order, no-place last).
- **Money**: `missingMoney(diff, unitPrice)` prices a shortfall; [`onSiteSummary.ts`](../src/lib/materials/onSiteSummary.ts) gives the desk, the Job window's tab, the ⋯ menu and the Needs you line one job's facts (live containers, lines on site, the week's missing lines and money, the stale boxes, credits due). Since v2.7246 (punch list #344 PR 6) it also counts `missingUnpricedLines`, the short lines the money leaves out for want of a price: the desk's *Missing this week* cell, its site row, the site screen's tile and the Job window's chip read `missingWeekNote` ([`missingWeekWords.ts`](../src/lib/materials/missingWeekWords.ts): *3 lines · 2 with no price*) under the money, a row is amber and sorts up on short lines and not on money alone, and the Track badge names the short lines when none has a price. The price is the parts book's (call 5, v2.6673, [`bookPrice.ts`](../src/lib/materials/bookPrice.ts)): the store reads `material_part_prices` for the parts a ledger names, `pickBookPrice` takes the row checked most recently (a same-day tie to the lower price) and flags it stale past 90 days, never a zero or the book's stand-in (`isRealPrice`: above zero and under `PLACEHOLDER_PRICE`, the invoice price held to the same rule; v2.7249, punch list #344 PR 7), and `priceLookup` falls back to the invoice price the office typed when the part landed (`rememberPrice`, kept on the device) where the book's is stale or missing; a typed name only ever carries the invoice price. `priceFor` is the pick with its checked day, `priceOf` its number for the sums.

The same arithmetic lives once more, in SQL, as `on_site_line_on_hand(container, part_id, part_name)` ([`20261102100000`](./migrations/20261102100000_dev_on_site_write.md)), so the agent's plans can say a line's before and after without a round trip; `read_on_site` reads the kernel, so the two can be held against each other, and on 2026-11-03 they agreed on all 118 live lines of JP972, the job that held the count. Both part arguments default to null since v2.6456, so the seat's `call_read` names just one. Since v2.6743 the whole arithmetic lives in SQL too, as **`on_site_job_summary(job, since)`** ([`20261109010000`](./migrations/20261109010000_on_site_job_summary.md)): every line, the last count per container, the counts and Can't find presses in the window and the returns, in one pass of window functions; [`onSiteServerSummary.ts`](../src/lib/materials/onSiteServerSummary.ts) reads its answer into the kernel's shapes and its tests hold the two equal over one ledger. The store calls it only for a job whose ledger read came back capped.

Beside the kernel: [`flowRow.ts`](../src/lib/materials/flowRow.ts) (the four figures came in → on site → used → missing, and the phone's three), [`labels.ts`](../src/lib/materials/labels.ts) (`boxCode`, the QR's `HTTPS://PRTS.CO/B/<CODE>`, with its scheme since v2.6862 because an iPhone's camera searched the web for the bare address, the label's ruled rows), [`stickers.ts`](../src/lib/materials/stickers.ts) (v2.7072: what a sticker is called (its name, or its code since v2.7438), a run's counts, the placing verdicts, and `codeLookup`, what a scanned code names), [`stickerRun.ts`](../src/lib/materials/stickerRun.ts) (v2.7272: who may mint, the run request's checks, the press's words, what one sticker prints, a run's title and counts, the rest after a stop, the CSV), [`manifest.ts`](../src/lib/materials/manifest.ts) (an invoice's text read into lines; the three-way comparison of bought, reported and left), [`boxSearch.ts`](../src/lib/materials/boxSearch.ts) (the Go anywhere group *Boxes on site*), [`photo.ts`](../src/lib/materials/photo.ts) and [`photoShrink.ts`](../src/lib/materials/photoShrink.ts).

## The writes and their rules

The screens write the tables **directly** through [`onSiteStore.ts`](../src/lib/materials/onSiteStore.ts): a cache of each job (places, containers, moves, movements, photos; `loadJob`, `loadDesk` for every job with a live container, `MOVEMENTS_LIMIT = 5000` — the ledger read newest first since v2.6743 and, since v2.7160, a page of 1,000 at a time through `fetchAllRows`, as the places, boxes, photos, box moves and the desk's list of jobs are (PostgREST cuts an un-ranged answer at 1,000 rows without a word, so until then the read stopped at the newest 1,000 and no job could ever read as capped), and a read that comes back full marks the job `capped` and fetches `on_site_job_summary`, which `jobMaterialsSummaryFor` in `onSiteSummary.ts` hands every summary reader in place of the device's sums), reads as plain functions so a screen renders without waiting, a job read again by itself since v2.7177 (punch list #344 PR 4: `refreshJob` and `refreshDesk`, asked by `useOnSiteJob` and `useOnSiteDesk` when the page comes back to the front, the window takes the focus, the signal returns and every five minutes while it stays in front, and by the phone when a box is opened, each only when the last read is older than its age in [`onSiteFresh.ts`](../src/lib/materials/onSiteFresh.ts); a read made this way is quiet, so nothing reads as loading and a failure leaves the last good read with no error; before it a job was read once a page session), and each write landing in the cache first and going to the table behind it. Since v2.7173 (punch list #344 PR 1) a write is plain data, `OnSiteWrite` in [`onSiteWrites.ts`](../src/lib/materials/onSiteWrites.ts): an insert under an id minted on the phone, or a few columns changed on a box or a photo. `commit` draws it on the job through `applyOnSiteWrites`, the one place a write becomes what the screen shows, and hands it to [`onSiteSend.ts`](../src/lib/materials/onSiteSend.ts), which keeps it on the phone's store before the first send, sends one press at a time in the order pressed, and with no signal, or behind a press that still waits, puts it in the outbox (`on-site-press`, punch list #163's queue) for the replay. A resend carries the same ids, and an insert that meets its own row is a landing (`insertAlreadyLanded`, read from the primary key's name, since the key's detail is hidden under row level security). `loadJob` draws the presses still on their way over every read (`withPending`), so a read never takes a kept press off the screen; a press the office refuses, at once or on a later try, is said with its reason and the job read again, and a press whose rows stand alone (`rowsStandAlone`, v2.7267: a delivery's lines) is answered row by row, a refused row kept with its reason while the rows after it land, so the refusal names what landed (`OnSiteLanding`) and the same rows sent again count once. There is no RPC for a press; **the rules are the tables' own** — the CHECKs above, the gate below, the training-mode blocks and the digital-twin fence — and the store's few shapes:

the code is cut from the id, so a printed label still opens the box, and the database's own trigger keeps the old name in `job_container_names`; the one form is `EditBoxForm.tsx`, in the phone's sheet and the office's `EditBoxWindow`; since v2.7348 the form holds **What is in it**, the box's line, and the phone's box screen has **Say it better** over the same column through `sayBoxLine`, a person's words over the app's line and cleared words handing it back, `boxLine.ts`; since v2.7199 the form also holds **Which place on site**, planned with the fields by `editBoxPlan` and saved through `moveContainer`, and on Jobs → Materials a box's name opens the window; the window is two tabs, `BoxTabs.tsx`: **Cage** with the form, the code and the box's own history, **Contents** with each line's first row here, the place the box stood then and that line's history, all readings of kept rows by `boxHistory.ts`, the old names read from `job_container_names` by `boxHistoryIo.ts`), `retireContainer` (`retired_at = now()`; the phone refuses while any line has parts on hand; since v2.7303 the office's retire-all press (it reads its count, *Retire 3 empty boxes*; its confirm window is headed *Retire every box on this job?*), under the job's boxes on Jobs → Materials beside the Retired fold, runs it on every empty box of the job at once, one box a call, after `retireAllPlan` in [`lib/materials/retireAll.ts`](../src/lib/materials/retireAll.ts) sorts the live boxes into the empty ones that go and the ones with parts, held with their reason and named beside the press, Unsorted never among them, under the batch rule: the press counts what it will do, the confirm window lists what goes and what stays, a box that fails is named afterwards with its reason, and the toast's Undo brings every retired box back for a minute through `unretireContainer` behind `unretireVerdict`, as the phone's single Retire does;
