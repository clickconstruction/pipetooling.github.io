## The idea

Nobody types a quantity over another. A press names what happened to some of a part in a container on a job, and the number on the screen is the sum of the rows. A count is a row like any other: a point in time that resets the line and leaves the difference on record, so *report when they are missing* is the same rows read a second way. Built 2026-10-29 in four PRs (v2.6039 the ground, v2.6041 the phone, v2.6043 the office, v2.6045 the photo sweep); the card is [`to-dos/materials-on-site/README.md`](../to-dos/materials-on-site/README.md), the owner's six calls and how each went are under its table.

## The objects

Five tables, one migration ([`20261029010000_materials_on_site.sql`](./migrations/20261029010000_materials_on_site.md)), all `ON DELETE CASCADE` from the job, and since v2.7072 four more for stickers ([`20261113020000_box_stickers.sql`](./migrations/20261113020000_box_stickers.md), the last four rows below; a sticker outlives its job and its box, blank again). One more, `on_site_part_ties` (v2.7076), holds no material: it is a person's answer that two part names on a site are one part, read by the site's screen alone (The screens). A job combine carries them to the target instead (v2.6423), and the dev seat moves them to another job with the source left standing (v2.6808, below). Both run one function, `on_site_carry_job` ([`20261109160000`](./migrations/20261109160000_on_site_move_job.md)): a place of the same name becomes one, the source's Unsorted folds into the target's with its movements, moves and photos, and a live cage or box whose label the target already holds keeps its row and printed code and takes a numbered label (`A1 (2)`). A box's own moves name no job and follow their box.

| Object | Table | What one row is | Changes |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Place** | `job_places` | A place on the site a box can stand in: *Level 2*, *the laydown yard*. One per (job, name), case-insensitive. | insert only |
| **Container** | `job_containers` | A **cage**, **box** or **loose** pile with its **stencil** (`label`: A1, B7 — one live label per job), a `where_note` in the crew's words, its `place_id`, a map pin (`lat`, `lng`), `retired_at`. The generated eight-character `code` (the id's first hex characters, upper-cased) is the short address a printed label's QR carries: `prts.co/b/<code>` → `/b/:code`. The job's **Unsorted** row (`kind = 'unsorted'`, label *Unsorted*) is where a delivery lands until a crew presses *Put in…*; the app makes it on first use. | insert; update (a move, a pin, a retirement) |
| **Container move** | `job_container_moves` | The box itself moved from one place to another, by whom and when — the box's own history, apart from its parts. | insert only |
| **Movement** | `job_material_movements` | **The ledger.** One row per press: the part (`part_id` into `material_parts`, or `part_name` typed on site with `part_id` null), `manufacturer`, a positive `quantity`, a `reason` of eight (`delivered`, `moved`, `used`, `returned`, `taken`, `cannot_find`, `damaged`, `counted`), `from_container_id` and `to_container_id`, a `note` (a taken part's destination, a delivery's unit, *office correction*), a `source` (the invoice or PO behind a delivery), `by_user_id` and `by_name`, `at`, and `item_id` (V3's engraved item, null from day one). Checks: a delivery has a `to`, a count has a `from`, only a count may be zero. | **insert only — never edited; a wrong press gets a correcting row** |
| **Box photo** | `job_box_photos` | A photo of a box: shrunk on the phone to 1,600 px, named as the sweep names it (*2026-10-29 Jordan · A1 after Count.jpg*), sent to the private `box-photos` bucket as `<job>/<photo>.jpg`; `photo-save` (v2.6045) copies it into the job's Drive photo folder, writes `drive_file_id` and `filed_at`, deletes the bucket copy. `fail_why` carries every reason it could not be filed. `refile_due_at` (v2.6915) is set when a filed photo's job changes: its file is due a move into the new job's photo folder, and the sweep clears it once the file is there. | insert; update by the one who took it |
| **Sticker run** | `sticker_runs` | A run of stickers printed at once (v2.7072, punch list #329): the `job_id` a new box made from one of its stickers lands on (set null when that job is deleted, and the stickers stay good), `sticker_count`, `first_number` and `last_number`, `copies` of each, who printed and when. | insert by the stamp and mint triggers' rules; update of `job_id` alone |
| **Sticker** | `box_stickers` | A printed code and a short number that point at one box, or at nothing yet. `number` is one company-wide count, printed `0147`. `code` is cut from the sticker's own id by the container code's rule, so `prts.co/b/<code>` → `/b/:code` reads both, and no box carries a sticker's code. `container_id` is null while **blank**; `placed_by`, `placed_by_name`, `placed_at`; `lost_at`. A box may carry several stickers and its own label. | **made by the mint trigger and moved by the events trigger; no client writes it** |
| **Sticker event** | `box_sticker_events` | What happened to a sticker: `placed`, `moved`, `taken_off` or `lost`, with the box it left and the box it went to, who and when. **Inserting the row is the act**: a trigger refuses one that does not fit where the sticker is, fills the box it left, and moves the sticker. | insert only |
| **Container name** | `job_container_names` | A box's old name, its new name, who and when, written by a trigger when `job_containers.label` changes, so every client is covered at once. | insert only, by the trigger |

Which rows a reason writes (`reasonSides` in `onSite.ts`):

| Reason | from | to | The press |
|---|---|---|---|
| `delivered` | — | +1 | a line landed by the office (Deliveries), *Add a part* on the phone (note *added on site*), or the agent's `plan_delivery` |
| `moved` | −1 | +1 | *Put in…* from Unsorted, *Move to…* another box |
| `used` | −1 | — | the daily press |
| `returned` | −1 | — | *Back to the supply house*; the office expects a credit |
| `taken` | −1 | — | *Taken* to a truck or the shop, named in the note |
| `cannot_find` | −1 | — | *Can't find*: the line reads amber for the office until the next count |
| `damaged` | −1 | — | *Damaged, scrap* |
| `counted` | reset | — | *Count this box* (Right · a number · Gone), the office's *Correct…*; `quantity` is the number counted, not a change |

## The arithmetic

One pure kernel, [`src/lib/materials/onSite.ts`](../src/lib/materials/onSite.ts) (tests beside it), everything else reads it:

- **`partKey`** — a part's one identity on a job: `id:<part_id>`, or `text:<name folded>` for a typed name (trimmed, lower-cased, spaces collapsed).
- **`containerLines(movements)`** — the lines of every container, from the rows in `(at, id)` order: a `counted` row sets the line to the number counted and records `lastCountDiff = counted − on hand`; every other row adds to its `to` side and takes from its `from` side; `cannotFindSinceCount` sums Can't find presses since the last count. **On hand is the sum. Expected at a count is the sum before it. Missing is a negative difference; found a positive one.**
- **`linesOf(lines, container)`** — what a crew sees: a line with something on hand, a Can't find since the count, or a shortfall at the last count.
- **`countDiffs(movements)`** — every count with its expected, counted and difference, newest first: the Missing section and the money read these.
- **`lastCountedAt`, `countIsDue`, `STALE_COUNT_DAYS = 14`** — a box with no count, or one older than 14 days, is due.
- **Words**: `REASON_WORDS`, `pressLabel` (*Mark 4 used*, *Move 10 to A1*, *Save 12*), `leftWords` (*A2 will have 10 left.*), `containerWords`, `nextLabel` (A1 → A2), `compareLabels` (A1, A2, A10), `groupByPlace` (Unsorted first on its own, then the places in name order, no-place last).
- **Money**: `missingMoney(diff, unitPrice)` prices a shortfall; [`onSiteSummary.ts`](../src/lib/materials/onSiteSummary.ts) gives the desk, the Job window's tab, the ⋯ menu and the Needs you line one job's facts (live containers, lines on site, the week's missing lines and money, the stale boxes, credits due). The price is the parts book's (call 5, v2.6673, [`bookPrice.ts`](../src/lib/materials/bookPrice.ts)): the store reads `material_part_prices` for the parts a ledger names, `pickBookPrice` takes the row checked most recently (a same-day tie to the lower price) and flags it stale past 90 days, and `priceLookup` falls back to the invoice price the office typed when the part landed (`rememberPrice`, kept on the device) where the book's is stale or missing; a typed name only ever carries the invoice price. `priceFor` is the pick with its checked day, `priceOf` its number for the sums.

The same arithmetic lives once more, in SQL, as `on_site_line_on_hand(container, part_id, part_name)` ([`20261102100000`](./migrations/20261102100000_dev_on_site_write.md)), so the agent's plans can say a line's before and after without a round trip; `read_on_site` reads the kernel, so the two can be held against each other, and on 2026-11-03 they agreed on all 118 live lines of JP972, the job that held the count. Both part arguments default to null since v2.6456, so the seat's `call_read` names just one. Since v2.6743 the whole arithmetic lives in SQL too, as **`on_site_job_summary(job, since)`** ([`20261109010000`](./migrations/20261109010000_on_site_job_summary.md)): every line, the last count per container, the counts and Can't find presses in the window and the returns, in one pass of window functions; [`onSiteServerSummary.ts`](../src/lib/materials/onSiteServerSummary.ts) reads its answer into the kernel's shapes and its tests hold the two equal over one ledger. The store calls it only for a job whose ledger read came back capped.

Beside the kernel: [`flowRow.ts`](../src/lib/materials/flowRow.ts) (the four figures came in → on site → used → missing, and the phone's three), [`labels.ts`](../src/lib/materials/labels.ts) (`boxCode`, the QR's `HTTPS://PRTS.CO/B/<CODE>`, with its scheme since v2.6862 because an iPhone's camera searched the web for the bare address, the label's ruled rows), [`stickers.ts`](../src/lib/materials/stickers.ts) (v2.7072: a sticker's number as printed, a run's range and counts, the placing verdicts, and `codeLookup`, what a scanned code names), [`manifest.ts`](../src/lib/materials/manifest.ts) (an invoice's text read into lines; the three-way comparison of bought, reported and left), [`boxSearch.ts`](../src/lib/materials/boxSearch.ts) (the Go anywhere group *Boxes on site*), [`photo.ts`](../src/lib/materials/photo.ts) and [`photoShrink.ts`](../src/lib/materials/photoShrink.ts).

## The writes and their rules

The screens write the tables **directly** through [`onSiteStore.ts`](../src/lib/materials/onSiteStore.ts): a cache of each job (places, containers, moves, movements, photos; `loadJob`, `loadDesk` for every job with a live container, `MOVEMENTS_LIMIT = 5000` — the ledger read newest first since v2.6743 and, since v2.7160, a page of 1,000 at a time through `fetchAllRows`, as the places, boxes, photos, box moves and the desk's list of jobs are (PostgREST cuts an un-ranged answer at 1,000 rows without a word, so until then the read stopped at the newest 1,000 and no job could ever read as capped), and a read that comes back full marks the job `capped` and fetches `on_site_job_summary`, which `jobMaterialsSummaryFor` in `onSiteSummary.ts` hands every summary reader in place of the device's sums), reads as plain functions so a screen renders without waiting, and each write landing in the cache first and going to the table behind it. Since v2.7173 (punch list #344 PR 1) a write is plain data, `OnSiteWrite` in [`onSiteWrites.ts`](../src/lib/materials/onSiteWrites.ts): an insert under an id minted on the phone, or a few columns changed on a box or a photo. `commit` draws it on the job through `applyOnSiteWrites`, the one place a write becomes what the screen shows, and hands it to [`onSiteSend.ts`](../src/lib/materials/onSiteSend.ts), which keeps it on the phone's store before the first send, sends one press at a time in the order pressed, and with no signal, or behind a press that still waits, puts it in the outbox (`on-site-press`, punch list #163's queue) for the replay. A resend carries the same ids, and an insert that meets its own row is a landing (`insertAlreadyLanded`, read from the primary key's name, since the key's detail is hidden under row level security). `loadJob` draws the presses still on their way over every read (`withPending`), so a read never takes a kept press off the screen; a press the office refuses, at once or on a later try, is said with its reason and the job read again. There is no RPC for a press; **the rules are the tables' own** — the CHECKs above, the gate below, the training-mode blocks and the digital-twin fence — and the store's few shapes:

- `addPlace` (the same name twice is one place), `addContainer` (the stencil upper-cased and trimmed; `created_by` filled by the database since v2.6456), `moveContainer` (the row's `place_id` and a `job_container_moves` row), `pinContainer`, `retireContainer` (`retired_at = now()`; the phone refuses while any line has parts on hand), `unretireContainer` (v2.6406: `retired_at` cleared after `unretireVerdict` in `lib/materials/unretire.ts` finds no live box carrying the stencil), `unsortedContainer` (made on first use), `addMovement` (quantity rounded, never negative), `addPhoto` / `updatePhoto`. **A code is resolved in one place** (v2.7072): `resolveBoxCode` asks `sticker_by_code` and the box's own `code` in one round trip and `codeLookup` picks, the sticker first; `fetchContainerByCode` and `findContainerByCode` answer through it. A label never depends on the sticker read: when that read fails and a box carries the code, the box opens.
- **A sticker is written by the database** (v2.7072): `mint_sticker_run` inserts a run as the caller and its triggers make the stickers, one unbroken block of numbers under a lock; `place_sticker` puts a blank sticker on a known box, or on a new box made in the same transaction and named by the sticker's number, and answers `{ ok: false, sticker }` when someone else took it first. Both are `SECURITY INVOKER`, so the gate judges the caller. No screen calls either yet: printing a run and the first scan are #329's PRs 2 and 3 ([`to-dos/stickers-before-boxes/README.md`](../to-dos/stickers-before-boxes/README.md)).
- **The phone caps a press at what the box has** (the number sheet's `max = onHand`); *Add a part* is a `delivered` row into the box, and since v2.7075 (punch list #330) its sheet lists the names the job already holds above the parts book as the person types ([`onSiteNames.ts`](../src/lib/materials/onSiteNames.ts): `matchOnHandNames` through the same-words fold, so `2 inch 90` finds `2" 90 pvc`), a pick writing that exact name and id; a delivery line's Part box in the office offers the same names; a count writes a row even when the number is right.
- **The office** lands a delivery as one `delivered` row per line into Unsorted with the invoice or PO as `source` and the unit as `note` (Deliveries: the job's Drive folder first, or pasted text read by `manifest.ts`); *Correct…* is a `counted` row in the office's name with the note *office correction*; *Add a container* is a cage with its place.
- **The writer** is the signed-in person: `by_user_id` and `by_name` from the session; the store's `onError` names a refused write.
- **A row takes its container's job** (v2.6808): a `BEFORE INSERT` trigger on `job_material_movements` and `job_box_photos` (`on_site_row_takes_container_job`) sets a new row's `job_id` to the job of the container it names, and the table's own INSERT policy then judges the row on that job. Before it, a row could name one job and a box on another, where no screen read it. It matters after a move: a phone that still holds the old job sends its press under the old id, and the press lands with the box, or is refused in the open for someone who is not on the new job's team.
- **No screen changes a row's job.** The ledger and the box moves take no update under RLS, and a container's `job_id` is never written by the store. A whole job's records change job in two places only, both through `on_site_carry_job`: the whole-job combine, and the dev seat's `plan_materials_move` → `apply_materials_move` (The agent's door).
- **Not built as drawn**: *Mark delivered* on the Procure step writes no movement; *Start from the bid's parts* is not there. **Short** (v2.6742) is the one press that writes no movement: it files the Job Parts Tally's own request — a `jobs_tally_parts` row in the presser's name and, for a book part, the draft purchase order `create_po_from_job_tally` makes — through [`shortRequest.ts`](../src/lib/materials/shortRequest.ts) and `shortRequestIo.ts`; that table takes the office set and a primary, never a helper on the job's team, so a crew's refused ask reads what to tell the office.

## Who may write

One gate for all five tables, and for a sticker on a box through its box (v2.7072), `on_site_can_touch_job(job_id)`, `SECURITY INVOKER` on purpose: the caller may read and write a job's materials when they can see the job in `jobs_ledger` under their own RLS (a primary keeps their job scope, the office its reach) **or** are on the job's team in `jobs_ledger_team_members` (how helpers and subcontractors reach a job). Movements and moves are select + insert only; containers and places allow update; a photo row is updated by its taker. A training account (`users.read_only`) and a digital twin are blocked by the restrictive policies and the statement trigger every new table gets. Depth: [`ACCESS_CONTROL.md`](./ACCESS_CONTROL.md) → *Materials on site*. A box's QR opened by someone not on the team reads the lines and offers no press. **Stickers** (v2.7072): a blank one is read by whoever may touch the job its run was printed for, and by nobody else, so no one collects codes ahead of their boxes; whoever holds a sticker's code reads what `sticker_by_code` answers; a run is printed by the office, a primary or a superintendent on a job they can reach; placing is open to whoever may touch the box's job. Moving a job's records to another job is outside the gate: `dev_on_site_move_job` is a definer open to devs alone, never a training account or a twin.

## The agent's door

Rebuilding the boxes by hand on the right job would have changed every printed code, lost the history and written false shortfall rows; this changes only the job a row sits under.

**The photos' files** follow a move (v2.6885, punch list #297): a row moves with its box, a photo still waiting in the bucket is filed for the new job by the sweep, and a file already in Drive is moved into the new job's photo folder once the apply's rows land.
