## The idea

Nobody types a quantity over another. A press names what happened to some of a part in a container on a job, and the number on the screen is the sum of the rows. A count is a row like any other: a point in time that resets the line and leaves the difference on record, so *report when they are missing* is the same rows read a second way. Built 2026-10-29 in four PRs (v2.6039 the ground, v2.6041 the phone, v2.6043 the office, v2.6045 the photo sweep); the card is [`to-dos/materials-on-site/README.md`](../to-dos/materials-on-site/README.md), the owner's six calls and how each went are under its table.

## The objects

Five tables, one migration ([`20261029010000_materials_on_site.sql`](./migrations/20261029010000_materials_on_site.md)), all `ON DELETE CASCADE` from the job. A job combine carries them to the target instead (v2.6423), and the dev seat moves them to another job with the source left standing (v2.6808, below). Both run one function, `on_site_carry_job` ([`20261109160000`](./migrations/20261109160000_on_site_move_job.md)): a place of the same name becomes one, the source's Unsorted folds into the target's with its movements, moves and photos, and a live cage or box whose label the target already holds keeps its row and printed code and takes a numbered label (`A1 (2)`). A box's own moves name no job and follow their box.

| Object | Table | What one row is | Changes |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Place** | `job_places` | A place on the site a box can stand in: *Level 2*, *the laydown yard*. One per (job, name), case-insensitive. | insert only |
| **Container** | `job_containers` | A **cage**, **box** or **loose** pile with its **stencil** (`label`: A1, B7 — one live label per job), a `where_note` in the crew's words, its `place_id`, a map pin (`lat`, `lng`), `retired_at`. The generated eight-character `code` (the id's first hex characters, upper-cased) is the short address a printed label's QR carries: `prts.co/b/<code>` → `/b/:code`. The job's **Unsorted** row (`kind = 'unsorted'`, label *Unsorted*) is where a delivery lands until a crew presses *Put in…*; the app makes it on first use. | insert; update (a move, a pin, a retirement) |
| **Container move** | `job_container_moves` | The box itself moved from one place to another, by whom and when — the box's own history, apart from its parts. | insert only |
| **Movement** | `job_material_movements` | **The ledger.** One row per press: the part (`part_id` into `material_parts`, or `part_name` typed on site with `part_id` null), `manufacturer`, a positive `quantity`, a `reason` of eight (`delivered`, `moved`, `used`, `returned`, `taken`, `cannot_find`, `damaged`, `counted`), `from_container_id` and `to_container_id`, a `note` (a taken part's destination, a delivery's unit, *office correction*), a `source` (the invoice or PO behind a delivery), `by_user_id` and `by_name`, `at`, and `item_id` (V3's engraved item, null from day one). Checks: a delivery has a `to`, a count has a `from`, only a count may be zero. | **insert only — never edited; a wrong press gets a correcting row** |
| **Box photo** | `job_box_photos` | A photo of a box: shrunk on the phone to 1,600 px, named as the sweep names it (*2026-10-29 Jordan · A1 after Count.jpg*), sent to the private `box-photos` bucket as `<job>/<photo>.jpg`; `photo-save` (v2.6045) copies it into the job's Drive photo folder, writes `drive_file_id` and `filed_at`, deletes the bucket copy. `fail_why` carries every reason it could not be filed. | insert; update by the one who took it |

Which rows a reason writes (`reasonSides` in `onSite.ts`):

| Reason | from | to | The press |
|---|---|---|---|
| `delivered` | — | +1 | a line landed by the office (Deliveries), *Add a part* on the phone (note *added on site*), or the agent's `plan_delivery` |
| `moved` | −1 | +1 | *Put in…* from Unsorted, *Move to…* another box |
| `used` | −1 | — | the daily press |
| `returned` | −1 | — | *Back to the supply house*; the office expects a credit |
| `taken` | −1 | — | *Taken* to a truck or the shop, named in the note |
| `cannot_find` | −1 | — | *Can't find*: the line reads amber for the office until the next count |
| `damaged` | −1 | — | *Damaged, scrap* |
| `counted` | reset | — | *Count this box* (Right · a number · Gone), the office's *Correct…*; `quantity` is the number counted, not a change |

## The arithmetic

One pure kernel, [`src/lib/materials/onSite.ts`](../src/lib/materials/onSite.ts) (tests beside it), everything else reads it:

- **`partKey`** — a part's one identity on a job: `id:<part_id>`, or `text:<name folded>` for a typed name (trimmed, lower-cased, spaces collapsed).
- **`containerLines(movements)`** — the lines of every container, from the rows in `(at, id)` order: a `counted` row sets the line to the number counted and records `lastCountDiff = counted − on hand`; every other row adds to its `to` side and takes from its `from` side; `cannotFindSinceCount` sums Can't find presses since the last count. **On hand is the sum. Expected at a count is the sum before it. Missing is a negative difference; found a positive one.**
- **`linesOf(lines, container)`** — what a crew sees: a line with something on hand, a Can't find since the count, or a shortfall at the last count.
- **`countDiffs(movements)`** — every count with its expected, counted and difference, newest first: the Missing section and the money read these.
- **`lastCountedAt`, `countIsDue`, `STALE_COUNT_DAYS = 14`** — a box with no count, or one older than 14 days, is due.
- **Words**: `REASON_WORDS`, `pressLabel` (*Mark 4 used*, *Move 10 to A1*, *Save 12*), `leftWords` (*A2 will have 10 left.*), `containerWords`, `nextLabel` (A1 → A2), `compareLabels` (A1, A2, A10), `groupByPlace` (Unsorted first on its own, then the places in name order, no-place last).
- **Money**: `missingMoney(diff, unitPrice)` prices a shortfall; [`onSiteSummary.ts`](../src/lib/materials/onSiteSummary.ts) gives the desk, the Job window's tab, the ⋯ menu and the Needs you line one job's facts (live containers, lines on site, the week's missing lines and money, the stale boxes, credits due). The price is the parts book's (call 5, v2.6673, [`bookPrice.ts`](../src/lib/materials/bookPrice.ts)): the store reads `material_part_prices` for the parts a ledger names, `pickBookPrice` takes the row checked most recently (a same-day tie to the lower price) and flags it stale past 90 days, and `priceLookup` falls back to the invoice price the office typed when the part landed (`rememberPrice`, kept on the device) where the book's is stale or missing; a typed name only ever carries the invoice price. `priceFor` is the pick with its checked day, `priceOf` its number for the sums.

The same arithmetic lives once more, in SQL, as `on_site_line_on_hand(container, part_id, part_name)` ([`20261102100000`](./migrations/20261102100000_dev_on_site_write.md)), so the agent's plans can say a line's before and after without a round trip; `read_on_site` reads the kernel, so the two can be held against each other, and on 2026-11-03 they agreed on all 118 live lines of JP972, the job that held the count. Both part arguments default to null since v2.6456, so the seat's `call_read` names just one. Since v2.6743 the whole arithmetic lives in SQL too, as **`on_site_job_summary(job, since)`** ([`20261109010000`](./migrations/20261109010000_on_site_job_summary.md)): every line, the last count per container, the counts and Can't find presses in the window and the returns, in one pass of window functions; [`onSiteServerSummary.ts`](../src/lib/materials/onSiteServerSummary.ts) reads its answer into the kernel's shapes and its tests hold the two equal over one ledger. The store calls it only for a job whose ledger read came back capped.

Beside the kernel: [`flowRow.ts`](../src/lib/materials/flowRow.ts) (the four figures came in → on site → used → missing, and the phone's three), [`labels.ts`](../src/lib/materials/labels.ts) (`boxCode`, the QR's `HTTPS://PRTS.CO/B/<CODE>`, with its scheme since v2.6862 because an iPhone's camera searched the web for the bare address, the label's ruled rows), [`manifest.ts`](../src/lib/materials/manifest.ts) (an invoice's text read into lines; the three-way comparison of bought, reported and left), [`boxSearch.ts`](../src/lib/materials/boxSearch.ts) (the Go anywhere group *Boxes on site*), [`photo.ts`](../src/lib/materials/photo.ts) and [`photoShrink.ts`](../src/lib/materials/photoShrink.ts).

## The writes and their rules

The screens write the tables **directly** through [`onSiteStore.ts`](../src/lib/materials/onSiteStore.ts): a cache of each job (places, containers, moves, movements, photos; `loadJob`, `loadDesk` for every job with a live container, `MOVEMENTS_LIMIT = 5000` — the ledger read newest first since v2.6743, and a read that comes back full marks the job `capped` and fetches `on_site_job_summary`, which `jobMaterialsSummaryFor` in `onSiteSummary.ts` hands every summary reader in place of the device's sums), reads as plain functions so a screen renders without waiting, and each write landing in the cache first and going to the table behind it (`afterWrite`: a refusal is said through the screen's toast and the job read again). There is no RPC for a press; **the rules are the tables' own** — the CHECKs above, the gate below, the training-mode blocks and the digital-twin fence — and the store's few shapes:

- `addPlace` (the same name twice is one place), `addContainer` (the stencil upper-cased and trimmed; `created_by` filled by the database since v2.6456), `moveContainer` (the row's `place_id` and a `job_container_moves` row), `pinContainer`, `retireContainer` (`retired_at = now()`; the phone refuses while any line has parts on hand), `unretireContainer` (v2.6406: `retired_at` cleared after `unretireVerdict` in `lib/materials/unretire.ts` finds no live box carrying the stencil), `unsortedContainer` (made on first use), `addMovement` (quantity rounded, never negative), `addPhoto` / `updatePhoto`.
- **The phone caps a press at what the box has** (the number sheet's `max = onHand`); *Add a part* is a `delivered` row into the box; a count writes a row even when the number is right.
- **The office** lands a delivery as one `delivered` row per line into Unsorted with the invoice or PO as `source` and the unit as `note` (Deliveries: the job's Drive folder first, or pasted text read by `manifest.ts`); *Correct…* is a `counted` row in the office's name with the note *office correction*; *Add a container* is a cage with its place.
- **The writer** is the signed-in person: `by_user_id` and `by_name` from the session; the store's `onError` names a refused write.
- **A row takes its container's job** (v2.6808): a `BEFORE INSERT` trigger on `job_material_movements` and `job_box_photos` (`on_site_row_takes_container_job`) sets a new row's `job_id` to the job of the container it names, and the table's own INSERT policy then judges the row on that job. Before it, a row could name one job and a box on another, where no screen read it. It matters after a move: a phone that still holds the old job sends its press under the old id, and the press lands with the box, or is refused in the open for someone who is not on the new job's team.
- **No screen changes a row's job.** The ledger and the box moves take no update under RLS, and a container's `job_id` is never written by the store. A whole job's records change job in two places only, both through `on_site_carry_job`: the whole-job combine, and the dev seat's `plan_materials_move` → `apply_materials_move` (The agent's door).
- **Not built as drawn**: *Mark delivered* on the Procure step writes no movement; *Start from the bid's parts* is not there. **Short** (v2.6742) is the one press that writes no movement: it files the Job Parts Tally's own request — a `jobs_tally_parts` row in the presser's name and, for a book part, the draft purchase order `create_po_from_job_tally` makes — through [`shortRequest.ts`](../src/lib/materials/shortRequest.ts) and `shortRequestIo.ts`; that table takes the office set and a primary, never a helper on the job's team, so a crew's refused ask reads what to tell the office.

## Who may write

One gate for all five tables, `on_site_can_touch_job(job_id)`, `SECURITY INVOKER` on purpose: the caller may read and write a job's materials when they can see the job in `jobs_ledger` under their own RLS (a primary keeps their job scope, the office its reach) **or** are on the job's team in `jobs_ledger_team_members` (how helpers and subcontractors reach a job). Movements and moves are select + insert only; containers and places allow update; a photo row is updated by its taker. A training account (`users.read_only`) and a digital twin are blocked by the restrictive policies and the statement trigger every new table gets. Depth: [`ACCESS_CONTROL.md`](./ACCESS_CONTROL.md) → *Materials on site*. A box's QR opened by someone not on the team reads the lines and offers no press. Moving a job's records to another job is outside the gate: `dev_on_site_move_job` is a definer open to devs alone, never a training account or a twin.

## The agent's door

The dev MCP seat ([`dev-mcp/README.md`](./dev-mcp/README.md)) reads and writes the surface since v2.6367 (server 0.16.0), in its plan → apply grammar:

| Verb | What it does |
|---|---|
| `read_on_site(job)` | The job as the screens read it, through the kernel copy of `onSite.ts` ([`_shared/devMcpComposites.ts`](../supabase/functions/_shared/devMcpComposites.ts) → `getOnSite`): the whole ledger 200 rows a page to the 5,000 cap, then every live box with its stencil, kind, place, code and lines (on hand, last touched, the last count's difference, Can't find since), Unsorted, the retired boxes with v2.6406's Bring back verdict, the boxes due a count, the last 30 days' shortfalls and Can't find presses, the newest 15 rows, and the words of the write verbs. Read it first: a box is named by its stencil as listed. |
| `plan_container` → `apply_container` | A cage or box added (stencil, kind, where, place — made when missing), moved to a place (the row and a `job_container_moves` row), retired when empty (a box with parts in it is refused in the phone's words), or brought back by v2.6406's verdict. Unsorted is never touched here. |
| `plan_delivery` → `apply_delivery` | An invoice's or PO's lines (a book part by id or a typed name, a whole quantity, a unit) into Unsorted — made on first use — or a named box, the source on every row. |
| `plan_movement` → `apply_movement` | One press on a line: `moved` (with `to`; Put in… from Unsorted is this), `used`, `returned`, `taken` (a note says where), `cannot_find`, `damaged`. Capped at what the box has, as the phone caps it; a box with none of the part refuses. |
| `plan_count` → `apply_count` | The Count this box walk or the office's Correct…: for each line named, what is there now. A line left out is not reset; a part not yet in the box may be counted into it. |
| `plan_materials_move` → `apply_materials_move` | Everything one job holds on site moved to another job (v2.6808, server 0.57.0), for a count recorded under the wrong job: `{ from_job, to_job }`. Places, cages and boxes, the ledger, the boxes' moves and photos take the target job; every box keeps its id and printed code, its lines, counts and history. The source job stays, with nothing on site. |

All four pairs POST one RPC, `dev_on_site_write(p, p_dry_run)` ([`20261102100000`](./migrations/20261102100000_dev_on_site_write.md)), **`SECURITY INVOKER`** on purpose: there is no office RPC to wrap, the screens write under RLS, so the wrapper runs as the dev and the database stays the judge — the gate, the CHECKs, the training blocks and the twin fence decide every row exactly as for a press on the phone, and a training account or a twin is refused in words first; since v2.6456 the plan and every refusal name the job as the app does (`JP972`, `J1085`, `formatJobLedgerNumberLabel`'s rule), not the bare number. The plan commits to every line's on hand before and after and the box's live row count (a press on the box since the plan makes apply refuse) and carries `now` or the time given, never the clock. Every row reads **`<agent_label> · MCP as <dev>`** in `by_name` with the dev as `by_user_id`, so the crew's History and the office's page say who did it. The server-side grammar is `_shared/devMcpWrites.ts` (`WRITE_VERBS`, `writeRpcBody`), tested in `src/lib/devMcp/devMcpWrites.test.ts`; the wrapper ran on a throwaway Postgres through every action and refusal before it shipped.

The fifth pair POSTs its own RPC, `dev_on_site_move_job(p, p_dry_run)` ([`20261109160000`](./migrations/20261109160000_on_site_move_job.md)), **`SECURITY DEFINER`**, the one on-site wrapper that is: the ledger has no UPDATE policy for anyone, so no caller's own RLS could make this write, and the wrapper gates `is_dev()` itself and refuses a training account and a twin in words. It writes nothing of its own to the five tables: it calls `on_site_carry_job`, the rule lifted out of the whole-job combine, so a move and a combine can never disagree. The dry run does the move, reads both jobs and unwinds. The plan names both jobs as the app does and commits to the counts moved per table, the places that became one, every label renumbered with its code, where the photos' files are, and each job's row counts and parts on hand before and after (`on_site_job_counts`); the wrapper itself refuses when the rows after do not add up to the rows before. It refuses the same job twice and a source with nothing on site, so a replayed apply is refused by the data. A note lands on both jobs' notes in the dev's name, as a combine leaves one. Rebuilding the boxes by hand on the right job would have changed every printed code, lost the history and written false shortfall rows; this changes only the job a row sits under.

Two things a move does not do. **The photos' files**: a row moves with its box, and a photo still waiting in the bucket is filed for the new job by the sweep, but a file already in Drive stays in the first job's photo folder (the plan counts them and says so; punch list #297). **The crew**: the gate is the job's team, so whoever counts on site must be on the new job's team before they can press again.

twin-mcp has no materials verb and should not: it is the estimator's seat (`docs/TWIN_MCP_SERVER_ARCHITECTURE.md`), and a twin is fenced from these tables.
