## Why it matters
The box in the yard is where the house's heat is let go. It starts by itself and it runs on 240 V. One part inside holds a charge after the power is off. Know what is in it before you work beside it.

## Must know
- The outdoor unit is called the condensing unit. It holds the compressor, the condenser coil and a fan.
- The coil wraps the sides. The fan on top pulls outdoor air through the coil and blows it up and away.
- That air comes out hot when the system cools. It is the house's heat leaving.
- The unit needs clear space all round for air. The maker's sheet gives the distance.
- It sits level on a pad. A unit that leans wears out its compressor.
- It runs on **240 V**. A disconnect, a switch box on the wall within sight of the unit, cuts its power.
- {{figure:outdoor-unit|The fan pulls air through the coil and blows the heat up and away. The disconnect on the wall cuts the power.}}
- The thermostat starts it with a **24 V** signal. It can start at any moment with nobody near it.
- A capacitor inside helps the motors start. It stores a charge and can hold it with the power off.
- Grass, lint and dirt on the coil hold the heat in. The coil is rinsed gently with a hose, with the power off.

## Traps
- A quiet unit is not an off unit. The thermostat can start it while your hand is on it.
- The disconnect pulled is not every part safe. The capacitor can still hold a charge, and it is a technician's to empty.
- A fence, a shrub or a deck built tight around the unit starves it of air. It runs hot and fails early.

## Numbers
- **240 V** runs the outdoor unit
- **24 V** is the signal that starts it
- **within sight** is where the disconnect sits

## Check yourself
1. What leaves the top of the outdoor unit when the system cools?
- [ ] Cold air
- [x] Hot air, the house's heat
- [ ] Refrigerant
- [ ] Water from the drain
> right: The fan blows the heat from the condenser coil up and away.
> wrong: The outdoor unit lets the house's heat go. The air leaving it is hot.

2. What cuts the power to the outdoor unit?
- [ ] The thermostat
- [ ] The fan
- [x] The disconnect on the wall within sight of it
- [ ] The capacitor
> right: The disconnect is the switch box by the unit. Pull it before a hand goes in.
> wrong: The thermostat only sends a signal. The disconnect on the wall is what cuts the 240 V.

3. Why can a part inside still bite with the disconnect pulled?
- [ ] The fan keeps spinning for an hour
- [ ] The refrigerant carries power
- [x] The capacitor can hold a charge
- [ ] The coil stays hot
> right: A capacitor stores a charge to start the motors and can keep it with the power off.
> wrong: The capacitor is the part that holds a charge. Emptying it is a technician's work.

4. A deck is built tight around the outdoor unit. What happens?
- [ ] Nothing, it only needs power
- [ ] It runs quieter and longer
- [x] It cannot get air, runs hot and fails early
- [ ] It makes more cold
> right: The coil has to pass air to let the heat go. Starved of air, it overheats.
> wrong: The unit needs clear space for air on every side. Boxed in, it runs hot.
