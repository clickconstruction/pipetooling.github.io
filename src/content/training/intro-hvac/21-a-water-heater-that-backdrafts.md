## Why it matters
A backdraft is the flame's gas coming down the vent and out into the room. It is how a water heater that worked for years starts making people sick. A plumber changes water heaters and their vents, so this one is ours to know.

## Must know
- A backdraft is the flame's gas spilling into the room at the draft hood. The vent is not carrying it away.
- {{figure:backdraft-strip|The vent draws and the gas goes up. Something pulls on the room. The gas spills at the draft hood instead.}}
- A blocked vent causes it. So does a vent connector with a dip or a loose joint.
- A vent too big causes it. That is the water heater left alone on a vent it shared with a furnace.
- A room pulled short of air causes it. A big exhaust fan or a clothes dryer can pull the gas back down.
- A closet with its air openings blocked causes it too.
- The signs sit on top of the heater. Melted plastic rings at the pipes, soot, rust and streaks of water.
- A technician tests the draft with smoke at the hood, a few minutes after the burner lights. The smoke should be pulled in.
- A heater that spills is shut off and stays off. The room is aired out.
- The cause is found and fixed before the heater is lit again.

## Traps
- A heater that makes hot water is not a heater that vents. It can do the first and fail the second.
- A new furnace on its own plastic vent changes the old water heater's vent. Check the heater's draft that same day.
- Warm, damp air at the top of the heater is not harmless. It is the flame's gas.

## Numbers
- **4** causes, a blocked vent, a vent too big, a room pulled short of air, a starved closet
- **the top of the heater** is where the signs sit
- **off** is how a spilling heater stays until the cause is fixed

## Check yourself
1. What is a backdraft?
- [ ] Cold water going back up the supply

- [ ] Gas leaking from the gas line
> right: The vent is not carrying the gas away, so it spills at the draft hood.
> wrong: A backdraft is the flame's gas coming out into the room at the draft hood.

2. Where do the signs of a backdraft show?
- [ ] At the drain valve
- [ ] At the gas meter
- [x] On top of the heater, as melted rings, soot, rust and water streaks
- [ ] At the thermostat
> right: The spilled gas is hot and wet, and it marks the top of the heater.
> wrong: Look at the top of the heater. Melted plastic, soot, rust and water streaks are the signs.

3. Which of these can pull the flame's gas back down the vent?
- [ ] A ceiling light
- [x] A big exhaust fan or a clothes dryer
- [ ] An open window
- [ ] The water meter
> right: A fan that pulls the room short of air can pull the vent backwards.
> wrong: An exhaust fan or a dryer takes air out of the room. The vent can run backwards to replace it.

4. A water heater is found spilling at its draft hood. What is done?
- [ ] The burner is turned up
- [ ] A window is opened and it runs on
- [x] It is shut off and stays off until the cause is fixed
- [ ] The draft hood is taped over
> right: Off, the room aired out, and the cause fixed before it is lit again.
> wrong: A spilling heater is shut off and stays off. The cause is fixed first.
