## Why it matters
The refrigerant goes round one loop and passes four parts. Each part changes it in one way. Name the four in order and you can follow the heat from the living room to the yard.

## Must know
- The refrigerant runs in a sealed loop. It goes round and round and is never used up.
- The compressor is a pump in the outdoor unit. It squeezes the refrigerant, and squeezing makes it hot.
- A coil is a long tube bent back and forth through thin metal fins. Air blown across it trades heat with the tube.
- The condenser is the coil in the outdoor unit. The hot refrigerant gives its heat to the outdoor air there.
- The metering device is a small valve at the indoor unit. It lets a little refrigerant through at a time.
- Past the metering device the pressure drops, and the refrigerant turns very cold.
- The evaporator is the coil in the indoor unit. The cold refrigerant soaks up heat from the house air there.
- The order never changes. Compressor, condenser, metering device, evaporator, and back to the compressor.
- {{figure:cooling-loop|The compressor squeezes. The condenser gives the heat up outside. The metering device drops the pressure. The evaporator soaks heat up inside.}}
- The loop has a high side and a low side. The compressor and the metering device are where one becomes the other.

## Traps
- The compressor does not pump cold. It pumps refrigerant, and the cold comes from the pressure drop.
- A system does not burn through refrigerant like fuel. If it is low, it has a leak.
- The two coils look alike and do opposite jobs. One gives heat up and the other soaks it up.

## Numbers
- **4** parts in the loop, always in the same order
- **2** sides, the high side and the low side
- **0** refrigerant used up in a tight system

## Check yourself
1. Which part squeezes the refrigerant?
- [ ] The evaporator
- [ ] The metering device
- [x] The compressor
- [ ] The condenser
> right: The compressor is the pump. Squeezing the refrigerant makes it hot.
> wrong: The squeeze happens in the compressor, in the outdoor unit.

2. Where does the refrigerant give up the house's heat?
- [x] At the condenser, the outdoor coil
- [ ] At the evaporator, the indoor coil
- [ ] At the thermostat
- [ ] In the ducts
> right: The hot refrigerant hands its heat to the outdoor air at the condenser.
> wrong: The heat leaves at the condenser outside. The evaporator inside is where it is picked up.

3. What happens to the refrigerant just past the metering device?
- [ ] It gets hot
- [x] Its pressure drops and it turns very cold
- [ ] It turns into water
- [ ] It leaves the loop
> right: The small opening drops the pressure, and low pressure makes it cold.
> wrong: The metering device lets a little through into low pressure. That drop is what makes it cold.

4. A system is low on refrigerant. What does that tell you?
- [ ] It is old and has used it up
- [ ] It needs a bigger compressor
- [x] It has a leak
- [ ] The weather is too hot
> right: The loop is sealed. Refrigerant only goes missing through a leak.
> wrong: Refrigerant is never used up. A low system has a leak somewhere in the loop.
