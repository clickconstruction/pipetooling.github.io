## Why it matters
Every gas appliance gets its gas the same way. A valve you can reach, a trap for grit, and a short connector. The furnace and the water heater follow one set of rules, and the company's plumbers pipe both.

## Must know
- Texas law counts gas piping as plumbing. On the company's jobs the gas line is the licensed plumber's work.
- Every gas appliance has its own shutoff valve. The valve is in the same room as the appliance.
- The shutoff is within **6 ft** of the appliance. It sits ahead of the connector, the short flexible line into the appliance.
- A sediment trap is a tee with a capped pipe pointing down. Grit and water in the gas drop into it.
- The sediment trap goes after the shutoff valve, as close to the appliance's inlet as it can be.
- {{figure:gas-to-appliance|The shutoff within reach, then the sediment trap, then the connector into the appliance. The furnace and the water heater are piped the same way.}}
- A furnace and a water heater each get a sediment trap. A range, a clothes dryer and an outdoor grill do not need one.
- A connector is at most **6 ft** long, and each appliance gets one.
- A connector is never hidden in a wall, a floor or a ceiling, and never runs through one.
- New gas pipe is tested with air before it is used. A house's test holds at least **10 minutes** on a gauge.
- A joint is checked with leak soap. Bubbles mean a leak. A flame is never the test.

## Traps
- A sediment trap is not a leftover stub of pipe. It is there on purpose, pointing down.
- A shutoff behind the appliance or in the next room does not count. It is in the room and within reach.
- Two connectors joined end to end are not a longer connector. Each appliance gets one, and rigid pipe covers the rest.

## Numbers
- **6 ft** or less from the appliance to its shutoff valve
- **6 ft** is the longest a connector may be
- **10 minutes** is the least a house's gas test holds

## Check yourself
1. Where is a gas appliance's shutoff valve?
- [ ] At the meter only
- [ ] In the attic
- [x] In the same room, within 6 ft of the appliance
- [ ] Inside the appliance
> right: Same room, within 6 ft, ahead of the connector.
> wrong: Each appliance has its own shutoff in the same room, within 6 ft of it.

2. What is a sediment trap for?
- [ ] Holding a water seal
- [x] Catching grit and water in the gas before the appliance
- [ ] Venting the flame
- [ ] Testing for leaks
> right: The capped pipe points down, and grit and water drop into it.
> wrong: A sediment trap catches grit and water in the gas. It is a tee with a capped pipe pointing down.

3. How long may a gas connector be?
- [ ] 1 ft
- [x] 6 ft at most
- [ ] 12 ft
- [ ] Any length
> right: Six feet at most, one to an appliance, and never through a wall, a floor or a ceiling.
> wrong: A connector is at most 6 ft long. Rigid pipe covers any longer run.

4. How is a gas joint checked for a leak?
- [ ] With a lighter
- [ ] By smell alone
- [x] With leak soap, watching for bubbles
- [ ] By listening
> right: Soap on the joint shows a leak as bubbles. A flame is never the test.
> wrong: Leak soap is the check. Bubbles mean gas is getting out.
