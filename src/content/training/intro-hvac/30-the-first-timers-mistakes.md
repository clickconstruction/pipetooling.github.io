## Why it matters
Five mistakes look fine on the day and cost a ceiling, a compressor or worse a year later. Each one is a rule from this course, drawn beside the right way. If you can name the five, the course has done its job.

## Must know
- Five mistakes a first-timer makes, each drawn beside the right way.
- {{figure:hvac-mistakes|A drain tied straight in, a crushed duct, a sagging vent, a blocked closet, a line set as a hanger. Each beside the right way.}}
- A condensate drain tied straight into a plumbing drain. The right way ends in the open, over a drain.
- A duct crushed or cut to pass a pipe. The right way sends the pipe round.
- A vent connector left sagging or loose after a water heater swap. The right way slopes up **1/4 in per ft**, tight at every joint.
- A furnace closet with its air blocked by a solid door or by boxes. The right way keeps both openings clear.
- A line set used as a hanger or a step. The right way guards it and hangs nothing from it.
- Each of the five works on the first day. Each one fails later, when nobody is looking at it.
- Three more to watch. A filter put in backwards. A float switch wired round to get the cooling back.
- And the worst one, hands in a unit that was only quiet.
- When one of these has already happened, the fix is a word to the lead. It is never a quiet patch.

## Traps
- It works is not a test. A drain tied straight into a sewer works until the trap dries.
- A float switch wired round is not a repair. It is a flooded ceiling with the warning taken off.
- A mistake nobody saw has not gone away. The sagging vent is still there next winter.

## Numbers
- **5** mistakes, each one a rule from this course
- **1/4 in per ft** is the slope a vent connector keeps
- **a word to the lead** when one has already happened

## Check yourself
1. A condensate drain is glued straight into a plumbing drain. What is the right way?
- [ ] A bigger glue joint
- [x] It ends in the open, over a drain
- [ ] It ends in the attic
- [ ] It ties into the vent instead
> right: The drain ends in the open air over a drain, so nothing can come back up it.
> wrong: A condensate drain never ties straight in. It ends in the open, over a drain.

2. A float switch has been wired round so the unit runs again. What is that?
- [ ] A good quick fix
- [x] A flooded ceiling with the warning taken off
- [ ] What the maker intends
- [ ] The inspector's choice
> right: The switch stopped the unit for a reason. The drain is the repair.
> wrong: Wiring round a float switch removes the warning and leaves the clog. The drain is the fix.

3. A water heater is swapped and its vent connector now sags. What is wrong?
- [ ] Nothing, it still connects
- [x] The flame's gas stalls at the sag and can spill into the room
- [ ] The water will be too hot
- [ ] The gas pressure drops
> right: A connector slopes up 1/4 in per ft with no dips. A sag holds the gas back.
> wrong: A sag stalls the flame's gas. The connector slopes up all the way and is tight at each joint.

4. One of the five has already happened on a job. What is the fix?
- [ ] A quiet patch
- [ ] Leave it for the next crew
- [x] A word to the lead
- [ ] Wait and see
> right: The lead hears, and the right trade puts it right.
> wrong: When a mistake has happened, tell the lead. A quiet patch hides it.
