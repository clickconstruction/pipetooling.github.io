## Why it matters
A gas leak gives one warning, the smell. What a person does in the next minute matters more than anything a trade can do later. The rule is the same for a homeowner, a helper and a master plumber. Get out first.

## Must know
- Natural gas has a smell added to it, like rotten eggs. The smell is the warning.
- A hiss or a whistle near a gas line or an appliance is a warning too.
- A nose gets used to the smell. If the smell fades while you stand there, the gas has not gone.
- The first move is to leave, on foot, and take everyone with you.
- {{figure:gas-smell-strip|The smell is the warning. Touch no switch. Everyone out on foot. Call from a safe distance.}}
- On the way out nothing is switched on or off. A light switch, a phone or a doorbell can make a spark.
- No flame, no smoking and no starting a car near the building.
- From a safe distance, call 911 and the gas utility, the company that owns the gas main.
- Never assume someone else has called.
- Nobody goes back in to hunt for the leak. The utility's crew finds it and says when the building is safe.
- The repair of the house's gas pipe comes after that. It is a licensed plumber's work, with the gas off.

## Traps
- A faint smell is not a small leak. Leave on a faint smell the same as on a strong one.
- Turning on a fan to clear the air is a switch thrown in a room full of gas.
- Finding the leak is not the first job. Getting people out is, and the call is second.

## Numbers
- **first** everyone leaves, on foot
- **0** switches, phones or flames on the way out
- **911** and the gas utility, called from a safe distance

## Check yourself
1. You smell gas at a furnace. What is the first move?
- [ ] Find the leak with soap
- [ ] Open the windows
- [x] Leave on foot and take everyone with you
- [ ] Turn off the lights
> right: People out first. Everything else comes after.
> wrong: The first move is to leave on foot with everyone. The leak is found later, by the utility's crew.

2. Why is no light switch touched on the way out?
- [ ] To save power
- [x] A switch can make a spark
- [ ] The switch may be wet
- [ ] It wakes the thermostat
> right: A switch, a phone or a doorbell can spark, and gas needs only one spark.
> wrong: A switch can spark. In a room with gas in the air, one spark is enough.

3. Where is the call made from?
- [ ] Beside the furnace
- [ ] The kitchen phone
- [x] A safe distance from the building
- [ ] The truck in the driveway, with the engine started
> right: From a safe distance, to 911 and the gas utility.
> wrong: The call is made from a safe distance, never from inside and never beside a running engine.

4. The smell fades while you are still inside. What does that mean?
- [ ] The leak has stopped
- [ ] The gas has cleared
- [x] Your nose has got used to it, and the gas is still there
- [ ] It was never gas
> right: A nose stops noticing the smell. The gas has not gone.
> wrong: The smell fading proves nothing. A nose gets used to it while the gas is still there.
