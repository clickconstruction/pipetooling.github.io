## Why it matters
Nothing in an HVAC system waits for a person. The thermostat calls and a fan, a compressor or a burner starts. A hand near a blade or a wire needs more than a quiet machine. It needs the power off, locked and proved.

## Must know
- The thermostat starts the system whenever the room drifts off its setting. Nobody has to be there.
- {{figure:starts-alone-strip|A quiet unit can start any second. Thermostat off, disconnect pulled and locked, tested dead. Then hands go in.}}
- The outdoor fan, the compressor, the blower and the inducer can each start with no warning.
- Setting the thermostat to off is the first step. It is not the last, since anyone can turn it back on.
- The outdoor unit's power is cut at its disconnect. The indoor unit's is cut at its switch or its breaker.
- Lockout is a lock on the disconnect so it cannot be put back. Tagout is a tag that says who locked it.
- The one who puts a lock on is the one who takes it off.
- Then the wires are tested dead with a tester that was just proved on a live outlet.
- The outdoor disconnect does not cut the indoor unit. The **24 V** and the blower have their own power.
- A fan coasts after the power is cut. Hands wait until the blade has stopped.
- The capacitor holds a charge after the power is off. Emptying it is a technician's step.

## Traps
- A quiet unit is not an off unit. It is a unit between two calls.
- A float switch that stopped the unit lets it start again the moment the pan drains.
- Pulling the disconnect is not the end. Pulled, locked and tested dead is the end.

## Numbers
- **3** steps, the power cut, locked, and tested dead
- **2** places to cut power, the outdoor disconnect and the indoor switch or breaker
- **24 V** stays live indoors with the outdoor disconnect pulled

## Check yourself
1. Why is setting the thermostat to off not enough?
- [ ] It uses power
- [x] Anyone can turn it back on, and the power is still there
- [ ] It breaks the thermostat
- [ ] It is enough
> right: The thermostat is a first step. The power is cut and locked after it.
> wrong: A thermostat can be turned back on by anyone. The power has to be cut and locked.

2. What does lockout mean?
- [ ] Tape over the thermostat
- [ ] A sign on the door
- [x] A lock on the disconnect so it cannot be put back
- [ ] The door of the house locked
> right: A lock keeps the power off until the one who locked it takes it off.
> wrong: Lockout is a real lock on the disconnect. Tape and signs do not hold the power off.

3. The outdoor disconnect is pulled. What is still live indoors?
- [ ] Nothing
- [x] The 24 V and the blower, which have their own power
- [ ] Only the filter
- [ ] The line set
> right: The indoor unit is fed from its own switch or breaker.
> wrong: The outdoor disconnect cuts the outdoor unit only. The indoor unit has its own power.

4. A float switch stopped the unit, and the pan has just been drained. What can happen?
- [ ] Nothing until someone resets it
- [x] The unit can start again by itself
- [ ] The breaker trips
- [ ] The thermostat turns off
> right: When the float drops, the call goes through and the unit starts.
> wrong: The float drops as the pan drains, and the unit starts again with nobody touching it.
