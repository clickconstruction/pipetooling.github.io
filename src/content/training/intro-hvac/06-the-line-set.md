## Why it matters
Two copper lines join the outdoor unit to the indoor unit. They look like water pipe and they are nothing like it. They hold refrigerant under pressure every hour of the year, and a nick lets it out.

## Must know
- The line set is the pair of copper lines between the two units. The refrigerant goes out in one and back in the other.
- The big line is the suction line. It carries cool vapor back to the compressor.
- The suction line is wrapped in foam. Bare, it sweats like a cold glass and drips inside the wall.
- The small line is the liquid line. It carries warm liquid refrigerant to the indoor coil.
- {{figure:line-set|The big wrapped line carries cool vapor back. The small bare line carries warm liquid out. Both are under pressure all the time.}}
- Both lines are under pressure with the system on or off. There is no safe time to cut one.
- The joints are brazed, which is far hotter than soldering. Making or breaking one opens the loop.
- Refrigerant copper comes clean, dry and capped. A line left open takes in damp air, and water ruins a compressor.
- A kink chokes the flow like a half-shut valve. A kinked line is replaced, never bent back.
- A line closer than **1-1/4 in** to the edge of a stud gets a steel plate over it. The plate stops a nail or a screw.
- The filling ports on the outdoor unit wear locking caps. The caps keep the refrigerant away from children.
- {{figure:line-set-care|A kinked line is replaced. An open end stays capped. The filling ports wear locking caps.}}

## Traps
- A line set is not a hanger, a step or a place to strap a pipe. Weight and rubbing wear a hole in it.
- The foam on the big line is not packing to pull off. Without it the line drips for years.
- Copper is not all one kind. Water pipe and refrigerant line are different tubes, joined in different ways.

## Numbers
- **2** lines in a line set, the big one wrapped and the small one bare
- **1-1/4 in** from a stud's edge, or closer, means a steel plate
- **always** under pressure, with the system on or off

## Check yourself
1. Which line of the line set is wrapped in foam?
- [x] The big one, the suction line
- [ ] The small one, the liquid line
- [ ] Both
- [ ] Neither
> right: The suction line is cold, so it is wrapped to stop it sweating.
> wrong: The big suction line is the cold one. It is wrapped so it does not drip.

2. When is a line set safe to cut?
- [ ] When the thermostat is off
- [ ] When the disconnect is pulled
- [ ] In winter
- [x] Never, until a technician with the federal card has taken the refrigerant out
> right: The lines hold pressure all the time. Only an emptied loop can be opened.
> wrong: Power off does not empty the lines. They are under pressure until a technician with the federal card takes the refrigerant out.

3. What is done with a kinked refrigerant line?
- [ ] It is bent back straight
- [ ] It is taped
- [x] It is replaced
- [ ] It is left alone
> right: A kink chokes the flow and weakens the copper. The line is replaced.
> wrong: A kinked line is never bent back. It chokes the flow, and it is replaced.

4. A line runs through a stud 1 in from its edge. What does it need?
- [ ] More foam
- [ ] A bigger hole
- [x] A steel plate over it
- [ ] Nothing
> right: Closer than 1-1/4 in to the edge, a steel plate stops the nail or the screw.
> wrong: A line that close to the stud's edge gets a steel plate, so a fastener cannot reach it.
