## Why it matters
What is in the loop is controlled by federal law. Opening the loop takes a card. Letting the refrigerant out is against the law. The newest kind can also burn, so the rules a crew learned ten years ago are not enough.

## Must know
- Each refrigerant has a number, like R-410A. The unit's nameplate says which one it holds.
- R-22 is the old one. None has been made or brought into the country since **2020**.
- R-410A filled most systems after it. It does not burn.
- New home systems use R-32 or R-454B. Both are in a class called A2L, named lower flammability. They can burn.
- An A2L line carries a label. It says Risk of Fire, Flammable Refrigerant.
- {{figure:the-card|Three refrigerants a crew meets. The newest ones can burn. Opening any of them takes the card.}}
- The Environmental Protection Agency, the EPA, sets the rules. Its Section 608 card is the licence to open a loop.
- Anyone who adds refrigerant, takes it out or hooks gauges to the loop holds that card.
- A helper may do that work only beside a carded technician who watches closely the whole time.
- Venting is letting refrigerant out into the air. Doing it knowingly is against federal law.
- Recovery is pumping the refrigerant into a tank before a loop is opened. It is the legal way.
- {{figure:vent-or-recover|Cutting a line lets the refrigerant out, which is venting. Pumping it into a tank first is recovery, the legal way.}}
- Only a carded technician may buy refrigerant.

## Traps
- The different refrigerants are never mixed. A system takes the one on its nameplate and no other.
- Lower flammability is not no flammability. An A2L refrigerant near a torch or a spark can light.
- The card is not a state licence. Texas has its own rules on top, and lesson 26 covers them.

## Numbers
- **2020** is the year the making of R-22 stopped
- **A2L** is the class of the new refrigerants that can burn
- **608** is the section of the law the card is named for

2. What is venting?
- [ ] Running a pipe through the roof
- [x] Letting refrigerant out into the air
- [ ] Opening a window in the attic
- [ ] Cleaning the outdoor coil
> right: Venting is releasing refrigerant. Done knowingly, it breaks federal law.
> wrong: Here venting means letting refrigerant escape to the air. The law forbids doing it knowingly.

3. Which of these refrigerants can burn?
- [ ] R-410A
- [x] R-32 and R-454B, the A2L class

- [ ] Only R-22
> right: The new A2L refrigerants have lower flammability, which still means they can light.
> wrong: R-32 and R-454B are class A2L and can burn. R-410A does not.

4. How is refrigerant taken out of a system the legal way?
- [ ] The line is cut outdoors
- [ ] It is left to leak down
- [x] It is recovered into a tank by a carded technician

> right: Recovery pumps the refrigerant into a tank before the loop is opened.
> wrong: The refrigerant is recovered into a tank. Cutting a line or letting it leak is venting.
