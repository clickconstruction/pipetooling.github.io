## Why it matters
The ducts are half the system and the half that gets hurt most. They run through the same attics and walls as the pipes. A crushed or torn duct wastes cooling a customer pays for. It is easy to do with a boot.

## Must know
- The ducts carry air between the indoor unit and the rooms. There are two kinds.
- A supply duct carries heated or cooled air out to a room. It ends at a register, the grille you feel air from.
- A return duct brings room air back to the unit. It starts at a return grille, often the big one in a hall.
- Every cubic foot the supply sends out has to come back by the return. A blocked return starves the blower.
- The plenum is the box on the unit where the ducts begin.
- {{figure:supply-and-return|The supply carries heated or cooled air out to the rooms. The return brings room air back. Block either and the unit starves.}}
- Flex duct is a wire coil in plastic, wrapped in insulation. It is light, and it crushes underfoot.
- Flex duct is hung the way its maker says, with wide straps and gentle bends.
- A kink, a sag or a crushed spot chokes the air, like a half-shut valve in a pipe.
- A leak in an attic duct blows paid-for air into the attic. A leak in a return pulls attic dust into the house.
- {{figure:duct-faults|A kink, a sag and a crushed spot each choke the air. A leak in a supply loses air, and a leak in a return pulls dust in.}}
- A duct is never cut, moved or squeezed to make room for a pipe. The pipe goes round.

## Traps
- A duct is not a shelf or a step. Weight on flex duct flattens it for good.
- Shutting registers in unused rooms does not save power. It raises the pressure and strains the blower.
- A return is not a storage nook. A box set against the return grille starves the whole system.

## Numbers
- **2** kinds of duct, supply and return
- **0** kinks, sags or crushed spots in a run of flex duct
- **the maker's sheet** says how flex duct is hung

## Check yourself
1. What does a return duct do?
- [ ] Carries cooled air out to a room
- [x] Brings room air back to the unit
- [ ] Vents the flame
- [ ] Drains the pan
> right: The return brings the room's air back to be heated or cooled again.
> wrong: The return carries room air back to the unit. The supply carries treated air out.

2. What does a crushed spot in a flex duct do?
- [ ] Nothing
- [ ] Speeds the air up
- [x] Chokes the air, like a half-shut valve
- [ ] Cools the air more
> right: Less air gets through, and the room at the end goes warm.
> wrong: A crushed or kinked duct chokes the airflow to everything past it.

3. A pipe has to cross where a duct runs. What happens?
- [ ] The duct is cut and taped
- [ ] The duct is squeezed flat
- [x] The pipe goes round the duct
- [ ] The duct is taken out
> right: The duct is never cut, moved or squeezed for a pipe.
> wrong: The pipe goes round. A duct is never cut or squeezed to make room.

4. A return duct in the attic has a leak. What does it do?
- [ ] Cools the attic
- [x] Pulls attic dust and heat into the house
- [ ] Raises the pressure
- [ ] Nothing
> right: The return is under suction, so a hole in it pulls attic air in.
> wrong: A leak in a return pulls attic dust and heat into the house's air.
