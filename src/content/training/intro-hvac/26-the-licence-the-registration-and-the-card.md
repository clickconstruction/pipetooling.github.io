## Why it matters
Three papers stand behind air conditioning work in Texas. The company's licence, each worker's registration, and the federal card. A person who knows which paper covers which work never has to guess whether a job is theirs to do.

## Must know
- Texas licenses air conditioning work through the Texas Department of Licensing and Regulation, TDLR for short.
- The contractor licence is the company's. A company that offers this work keeps a licensed contractor at each office.
- A Class A licence covers a system of any size. Class B covers cooling up to **25 tons**.
- The licence carries an endorsement for the kind of work. Homes and offices are environmental air conditioning.
- Everyone who works on the air conditioning side is registered or certified with TDLR.
- A registered technician works under the licensed contractor. Registration takes no exam and starts at age **16**.
- A worker under 18 is with the contractor or a certified technician at all times on a job.
- A certified technician has two years of supervised work or an approved course, and has passed the state's exam.
- {{figure:licence-line|The company holds the licence. Each worker holds a registration. The federal card is for the refrigerant loop.}}
- A registration lasts **one year** and is renewed.
- A plumbing licence does not cover air conditioning contracting. The law says so in plain words.
- The EPA card from lesson 7 is separate and federal. It covers the refrigerant, in every state.

## Traps
- Working for a licensed company is not a registration. Each person's own registration is on file first.
- A master plumber's licence does not reach the air conditioning side. Each trade has its own paper.
- A state registration is not the federal card. A registered technician with no card does not open a loop.

## Numbers
- **16** is the youngest age for a technician's registration
- **25 tons** is the most cooling a Class B licence covers
- **1 year** is how long a registration lasts
