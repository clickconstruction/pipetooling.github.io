## Why it matters
In Texas the indoor unit is often in the attic, and so is the water heater. An attic in summer is far hotter than the day outside, and the floor between the framing is only drywall. The heat and the fall hurt more people than the machines do.

## Must know
- A summer attic is far hotter than the day outside. The body heats up faster than it can cool.
- Heat exhaustion comes first. The signs are heavy sweating, thirst, dizziness, tiredness and feeling sick.
- Heat stroke is the emergency. The signs are confusion, slurred speech, collapse, and skin that is hot and dry or soaked.
- For heat stroke, call 911 at once. Cool the person with ice or cold water until help comes.
- Attic work goes in short turns, with water, and with someone below who knows you are up there.
- The ceiling between the framing is drywall. It will not hold a boot, and the fall is to the floor below.
- The code gives the unit a walkway. It is solid floor at least **24 in** wide from the opening to the unit.
- {{figure:attic-strip|Step on the walkway or the framing, never between. Short turns, water, and someone below who knows you are up there.}}
- The walkway is no longer than **20 ft**. At the unit there is a level work space **30 in** by 30 in.
- The attic opening is at least 20 in by 30 in. It is also big enough for the largest unit to come out.
- There is a light with its switch at the opening, and an outlet near the unit.
- {{figure:attic-way|The opening, the walkway, the work space, the light and the outlet, as the code sizes them.}}

## Traps
- Feeling fine is not a reading. Heat stroke can start with confusion, and the person does not notice.
- Insulation hides the framing. A step on what looks solid can be a step through the ceiling.
- A missing walkway is not a reason to crawl the framing with a tank on your shoulder. It is a reason to stop.

## Numbers
- **24 in** is the least width of the walkway's solid floor
- **20 ft** is the longest the walkway may be
- **30 in by 30 in** is the level work space at the unit
