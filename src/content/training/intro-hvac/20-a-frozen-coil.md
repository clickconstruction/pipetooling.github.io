## Why it matters
An air conditioner can freeze itself solid on the hottest day of the year. The cause is almost always too little air or too little refrigerant. One of those is a filter anyone can change. The other is a leak for a carded technician.

## Must know
- The indoor coil runs just above freezing. Anything that makes it colder turns its water to ice.
- {{figure:frozen-coil-strip|Too little air leaves the coil too cold. Ice grows and blocks the air. Thermostat off, fan on, and let it melt.}}
- Too little air is the usual cause. A clogged filter, a blocked return or a dead blower all do it.
- Too little refrigerant is the other cause. Low pressure in the coil means a colder boil.
- The signs are weak air at the registers and a house that will not cool.
- Ice on the big wrapped line at the outdoor unit is another sign. That line should be cool and wet, never white.
- The ice blocks the air, so the coil gets colder still. A frozen coil never fixes itself while it runs.
- The first step is the thermostat set to off and the fan set to on. Room air melts the ice.
- A full thaw can take hours, and all that ice becomes water for the drain.
- Then the filter and the returns are checked. A coil that freezes again with good air is a call for a carded technician.

## Traps
- Ice is not a sign of strong cooling. A coil wearing ice is cooling almost nothing.
- Chipping the ice off bends the fins and can hole the tubes. Ice is melted, never chipped.
- Running a frozen system can send liquid refrigerant back to the compressor and ruin it.

## Numbers
- **2** causes, too little air or too little refrigerant
- **off** for the thermostat and **on** for the fan, to thaw it
- **hours** is how long a full thaw can take

## Check yourself
1. What is the usual cause of a frozen coil?
- [ ] Too much refrigerant
- [x] Too little air across the coil
- [ ] A hot day
- [ ] A big outdoor unit
> right: A clogged filter, a blocked return or a dead blower leaves the coil too cold.
> wrong: Low airflow is the usual cause. The coil gets too cold and its water turns to ice.

2. How is a frozen coil thawed?
- [ ] The ice is chipped off
- [ ] A torch is held to it
- [x] Thermostat off and fan on, so room air melts it
- [ ] The system is left running
> right: Room air blown across the coil melts the ice without hurting it.
> wrong: The thermostat goes to off and the fan to on. Ice is melted, never chipped.

3. Ice is on the big wrapped line at the outdoor unit. What does that tell you?
- [ ] The system is cooling well
- [x] The coil is freezing, from too little air or too little refrigerant
- [ ] The outdoor fan is too fast
- [ ] Nothing
> right: That line should be cool and wet. White with ice means the coil is freezing.
> wrong: Ice on the suction line is a sign of a frozen coil, not of good cooling.

4. The coil freezes again with a clean filter and clear returns. What now?
- [ ] Turn the thermostat lower
- [ ] Chip the ice and carry on
- [x] A carded technician checks for a leak
- [ ] Shut some registers
> right: With good air, the cause is the refrigerant, and that is the loop.
> wrong: Good air and still freezing points at low refrigerant. That is a carded technician's call.
