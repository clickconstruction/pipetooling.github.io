## Why it matters
A unit in an attic sits over somebody's ceiling. When its drain clogs, the water must go where a person will see it. Or the unit must stop. The code gives four ways, and every attic job uses one.

## Must know
- Where an overflow could damage the building, the code wants a second defence. A unit over a ceiling always needs one.
- The first way is an auxiliary pan, a second pan under the whole unit, with a drain of its own.
- That pan is at least **1-1/2 in** deep and at least **3 in** larger than the unit.
- Its drain ends at a conspicuous point, a place where someone will see it drip. Over a window is a common one.
- The second way is an overflow drain from the unit's own pan. It leaves the pan higher than the first drain.
- A float switch is a switch that a rising float trips. It shuts the unit off before the water spills.
- {{figure:pan-and-float|The unit's own drain is the first defence. The second pan and its drain, or a float switch, is the second.}}
- The third way is a second pan with no drain and a float switch in it.
- The fourth way is a float switch alone, set where a blocked drain will lift it.
- {{figure:four-defences|The four ways. A pan with a drain, an overflow drain, a pan with a float switch, or a float switch alone.}}
- The second drain is its own pipe to its own place. Tied into the first drain, one clog blocks both.
- A dry second pan is the normal state. Water in it means the first drain is blocked.

## Traps
- Water dripping over a window is not a bad gutter. It is the second drain saying the first one is clogged.
- A unit that will not start on a hot day may not be broken. Its float switch may have stopped it.
- A second pan is not a shelf. Tools and boxes left in it block the float and rust the pan.

## Numbers
- **1-1/2 in** is the least depth of the second pan
- **3 in** larger than the unit is as small as that pan may be
- **4** ways the code allows for the second defence

## Check yourself
1. Where does the second pan's drain end?
- [ ] In the first drain
- [ ] Inside the wall
- [x] Where someone will see it drip
- [ ] In the attic insulation
> right: A conspicuous point, so the drip is noticed and the first drain gets cleared.
> wrong: The second drain ends where a person will see it. A hidden drip warns nobody.

2. What does a float switch do when the water rises?
- [ ] Opens a bigger drain
- [ ] Starts a pump
- [x] Shuts the unit off before the water spills
- [ ] Sounds a horn at the street
> right: The rising float trips the switch, and the unit stops making water.
> wrong: A float switch stops the unit. With the unit off, the coil makes no more water.

3. Water is dripping from a pipe over a window. What does it mean?
- [ ] The gutter is full
- [ ] The system is working well
- [x] The first drain is blocked and the second one is carrying the water
- [ ] The refrigerant is leaking
> right: The second drain only runs when the first one cannot.
> wrong: That pipe is the second drain. It drips only when the first drain is blocked.

4. Why is the second drain never tied into the first?
- [ ] It would look untidy
- [x] One clog would then block both
- [ ] The water would run too fast
- [ ] The code wants more pipe used
> right: Two separate pipes mean one clog cannot take out both defences.
> wrong: Joined together, both drains fail at the same clog. Each one runs by itself to its own place.
