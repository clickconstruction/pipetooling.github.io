## Why it matters
Boiling is how the system picks heat up, and it does not take a flame. Refrigerant boils at temperatures that feel cold to you. Pressure decides how cold, and that one idea runs the whole loop.

## Must know
- A liquid soaks up a great deal of heat when it boils. The heat goes into the change, not into getting hotter.
- Water boils at **212°F** at sea level. Under less pressure it boils cooler. Under more it boils hotter.
- Refrigerant is the same, only far colder. At low pressure it boils at about **40°F** in a running indoor coil.
- House air at 75°F is hot to a liquid boiling at 40°F. The air's heat boils the refrigerant.
- So the evaporator is a coil full of boiling refrigerant. The air leaves it cooler and the refrigerant leaves as a vapor.
- The compressor squeezes that vapor. High pressure makes it hotter than the air outside.
- Now the outdoor air is the cooler thing. The heat flows out, and the vapor turns back into liquid.
- {{figure:boil-and-squeeze|Low pressure lets the refrigerant boil cold and soak up heat. High pressure makes it hot enough to give the heat away.}}
- Each refrigerant has one boiling temperature for each pressure. A gauge reads the pressure and a chart gives the temperature.
- Reading those gauges and that chart is a certified technician's work. Knowing why they matter is everyone's.

## Traps
- Boiling does not mean hot. Refrigerant boils at temperatures that would chill your hand.
- The compressor does not make heat to waste it. It raises the refrigerant above the outdoor temperature so the heat can leave.
- A pressure reading alone is not a verdict. It means something only beside the chart for that refrigerant.

## Numbers
- **212°F** water boils at sea level
- **about 40°F** refrigerant boils in a running indoor coil
- **1** boiling temperature for each pressure

## Check yourself
1. What happens to a liquid's heat when it boils?
- [ ] It makes the liquid hotter
- [x] It goes into the change from liquid to vapor
- [ ] It is lost
- [ ] It turns into pressure
> right: Boiling soaks up heat without a rise in temperature. That is how the coil picks heat up.
> wrong: A boiling liquid takes in heat and stays at its boiling temperature. The heat goes into the change.

2. What decides the temperature at which refrigerant boils?
- [ ] The size of the coil
- [ ] The speed of the fan
- [x] Its pressure
- [ ] The thermostat setting
> right: Low pressure, low boiling temperature. High pressure, high boiling temperature.
> wrong: Pressure sets the boiling temperature. That is why the loop has a low side and a high side.

3. Why does the compressor squeeze the vapor?
- [ ] To make it colder
- [ ] To turn it into water
- [x] To make it hotter than the outdoor air, so the heat can leave
- [ ] To slow it down
> right: Heat only flows from warm to cool. The squeeze makes the refrigerant the warm one outside.
> wrong: The squeeze raises the refrigerant above the outdoor temperature. Then the heat flows out by itself.

4. Whose work is reading the gauges against the chart?
- [ ] Anyone with a gauge
- [ ] The homeowner
- [x] A certified technician
- [ ] The inspector
> right: Connecting gauges opens the loop, and that takes the federal card.
> wrong: Gauges go on the sealed loop. That is a certified technician's work.
