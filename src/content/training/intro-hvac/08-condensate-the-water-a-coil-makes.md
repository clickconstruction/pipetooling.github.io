## Why it matters
A cooling coil makes water all summer, gallons of it on a damp day. That water has to leave by a pipe that slopes, stays clear and ends in the right place. Most water damage from an air conditioner starts here.

## Must know
- Condensate is the water that forms on the cold coil. It drips into the pan built into the unit.
- The primary drain is the pipe from that pan. It is at least **3/4 in** and never gets smaller on the way.
- The drain slopes down at least **1/8 in per ft** the whole way. A flat spot holds water and grows slime.
- {{figure:condensate-drain|Water drips from the coil into the pan. The drain leaves through a trap, slopes down all the way, and ends where the code allows.}}
- The drain is trapped where the maker says so. The trap lets water out while the blower pulls on the pan.
- The line has a capped tee near the unit. Through it a clog is cleared without cutting the pipe.
- The code says where the water may end. It never ends in a street or an alley, or where it makes a nuisance.
- A condensate drain is never tied straight into a plumbing drain, a waste pipe or a vent.
- It ends over a floor drain, a standpipe or a utility sink, or outdoors on the ground.
- An air gap is the open air between the end of the pipe and the drain under it. Sewer water cannot climb back across it.
- Cities add their own rules on where the water may end. The company follows the city the job is in.

## Traps
- A condensate line is not a small sewer line. Tied straight into a drain, it can bring sewer gas to the unit.
- A flat drain is not a slow drain. It is a clog that has not finished forming.
- No trap where the maker wants one holds the water in the pan. The blower pulls air up the pipe instead.

## Numbers
- **3/4 in** is the smallest a condensate drain may be
- **1/8 in per ft** is the least slope, all the way
- **never** straight into a plumbing drain, a waste pipe or a vent

## Check yourself
1. What is the smallest size a condensate drain may be?
- [ ] 1/4 in
- [ ] 1/2 in
- [x] 3/4 in
- [ ] 2 in
> right: The drain is at least 3/4 in and never gets smaller on the way.
> wrong: The code's least size is 3/4 in. A smaller line clogs with slime.

2. How much must the drain slope?
- [ ] It may run flat
- [x] At least 1/8 in per ft, the whole way
- [ ] At least 1 in per ft
- [ ] Only at the end
> right: An eighth of an inch a foot, with no flat spot anywhere.
> wrong: The least slope is 1/8 in per ft all the way. A flat spot holds water and grows slime.

3. Where may a condensate drain never be tied in?
- [ ] Over a floor drain
- [ ] Over a utility sink
- [x] Straight into a plumbing drain, a waste pipe or a vent
- [ ] Outdoors on the ground
> right: It ends in the open over a drain, never joined to one as pipe.
> wrong: The code bars a straight tie into a plumbing drain, a waste pipe or a vent. The drain ends in the open.

4. What is the capped tee near the unit for?
- [ ] Letting air into the house
- [x] Clearing a clog without cutting the pipe
- [ ] Adding refrigerant
- [ ] Holding the trap up
> right: The cap comes off, and the clog is cleared through the tee.
> wrong: The tee is the way into the line. The code wants a clog cleared without a cut.
