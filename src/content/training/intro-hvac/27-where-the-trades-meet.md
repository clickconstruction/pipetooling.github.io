## Why it matters
One company with three trades meets itself on every job. The plumber, the electrician and the HVAC crew share the same closet, the same attic and the same studs. Six places are where one trade's work changes another's.

## Must know
- The condensate drain is the first. HVAC makes the water and plumbing's rules carry it away.
- The drain keeps its slope and its trap, and it ends in the open over a drain. That is lesson 8.
- The gas line is the second. The plumber pipes it, with the shutoff, the sediment trap and one connector.
- The vent and the closet's air are the third. A furnace and a water heater share them.
- Change either appliance and the other one's vent and air are checked the same day.
- The power is the fourth. The disconnect, the breaker and the thermostat cable are tested dead before hands go in.
- {{figure:six-touch-points|Six places the trades meet. The drain, the gas, the vent and the air, the power, the holes, and the machines with water in them.}}
- The holes are the fifth. A line set, a duct, a cable and a pipe all run through the same studs and attic.
- Nothing is cut, crushed or moved to make room. The new work goes round, and a steel plate guards what sits close.
- The machines with water in them are the sixth, from lesson 18. Water in pipes is plumbing, and a sealed loop is a carded technician's.
- The rule under all six is a word before a cut. Tell the other trade before their work is touched.

## Traps
- Another trade's part in the way is not scrap. Ask before it moves.
- A job that touches one appliance in a closet touches the closet. The other appliance shares its vent and its air.
- The same company does not mean the same licence. Each trade's work stays with the people who hold its paper.

## Numbers
- **6** places the trades meet
- **1** rule under all of them, a word before a cut
- **the same day** is when the other appliance's vent and air are checked
