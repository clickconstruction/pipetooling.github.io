## Why it matters
A heat pump is an air conditioner that can run backwards. In summer it carries heat out. In winter it carries heat in. The same loop does both, so the outdoor unit runs on the coldest morning too.

## Must know
- A heat pump is the cooling loop with one more part, the reversing valve.
- The reversing valve swaps the two coils' jobs. It sends the hot refrigerant indoors instead of outdoors.
- In heating, the outdoor coil is the evaporator. It soaks up heat from the outdoor air.
- Cold air still holds heat. Refrigerant boiling far below freezing can pick heat up from a winter morning.
- The indoor coil becomes the condenser. It gives that heat to the house's air.
- {{figure:heat-pump-reverse|One valve turns the loop round. In summer the heat goes out. In winter the same loop brings heat in.}}
- In heating, frost grows on the outdoor coil. The unit runs a defrost, a few minutes in cooling, to melt it.
- Steam off the outdoor unit and a whoosh from the valve during a defrost are both normal.
- Heat strips are electric heaters in the air handler. They help on the coldest days and during a defrost.
- Heat strips use far more power than the heat pump does. The thermostat shows them as auxiliary or emergency heat.

## Traps
- A heat pump running in winter is not stuck in cooling. It is carrying heat inward.
- Steam from the outdoor unit in winter is not smoke. It is the frost boiling off in a defrost.
- Emergency heat is not a boost for a cold morning. It runs the heat strips alone and the power bill shows it.

## Numbers
- **1** valve turns the loop round
- **2** jobs for each coil, one in summer and one in winter
- **a few minutes** is how long a defrost runs

## Check yourself
1. What part makes a heat pump different from a plain air conditioner?
- [ ] A bigger compressor
- [ ] A second outdoor unit
- [x] The reversing valve
- [ ] A gas flame
> right: The reversing valve swaps the coils' jobs, so the loop can carry heat either way.
> wrong: A heat pump is the same loop with a reversing valve added.

2. In winter, where does a heat pump get its heat?
- [ ] From a flame
- [x] From the outdoor air
- [ ] From the ducts
- [ ] From the water heater
> right: Cold air still holds heat, and the outdoor coil soaks it up.
> wrong: A heat pump burns nothing. It picks heat up from the outdoor air and carries it in.

3. The outdoor unit steams on a cold morning. What is happening?
- [ ] It is on fire
- [ ] The refrigerant is leaking
- [x] It is in a defrost, melting the frost off its coil
- [ ] The heat strips have failed
> right: A defrost runs a few minutes to clear the frost. Steam is normal.
> wrong: Frost on the outdoor coil is melted by a short defrost. The steam is that frost leaving.

4. What are heat strips?
- [ ] Insulation on the ducts
- [x] Electric heaters in the air handler
- [ ] Fins on the outdoor coil
- [ ] Tape on the line set
> right: Heat strips are electric heat that helps on the coldest days and in a defrost.
> wrong: Heat strips are electric heaters in the air handler. They use far more power than the heat pump.
