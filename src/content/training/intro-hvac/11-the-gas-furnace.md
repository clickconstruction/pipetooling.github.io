## Why it matters
A gas furnace makes heat with a flame inside a metal box. The house's air never touches that flame. Know the wall between the two, and the order the parts start in, and a furnace stops being a mystery.

## Must know
- A furnace burns gas at its burners. The flame's hot gas runs through the heat exchanger.
- The heat exchanger is a metal wall between the flame's gas and the house's air. Heat crosses it. Gas does not.
- The blower pushes house air across the outside of the heat exchanger and on to the rooms.
- The flame's gas leaves by the flue, the passage that leads to the vent and out of the house.
- {{figure:furnace-cutaway|The flame's gas goes up the flue. The house air passes the other side of the metal wall. The two never mix.}}
- The parts start in a set order. The thermostat calls first.
- Next the inducer, a small fan, pulls the flame's gas through. A pressure switch proves that the inducer is pulling.
- Then the igniter glows, the gas valve opens and the burners light. A flame sensor proves that the flame is there.
- Last, the blower starts, once the heat exchanger is warm.
- {{figure:furnace-order|The thermostat calls. The inducer pulls and is proved. The burners light and the flame is proved. Then the blower starts.}}
- A standard furnace's vent runs hot and dry. The code calls that a Category I appliance.
- A condensing furnace takes so much heat that water forms in its vent. That is Category IV, vented in plastic pipe.
- {{figure:two-furnaces|A standard furnace vents hot and dry in metal. A condensing furnace vents cool and wet in plastic, and it needs a drain.}}

## Traps
- A furnace that starts and then stops is not broken by that alone. One of its proving switches is doing its job.
- A cracked heat exchanger is not a small repair. It lets the flame's gas into the house's air.
- A condensing furnace makes water in winter. Its drain matters as much as the air conditioner's.

## Numbers
- **1** metal wall between the flame's gas and the house's air
- **I** is the category of a standard furnace, vented hot and dry
- **IV** is the category of a condensing furnace, vented in plastic

## Check yourself
1. What keeps the flame's gas out of the house's air?
- [ ] The filter
- [ ] The blower
- [x] The heat exchanger, a metal wall between the two
- [ ] The thermostat
> right: Heat crosses the metal wall. The flame's gas stays on its own side.
> wrong: The heat exchanger is the wall between the flame's gas and the house's air.

2. What starts first after the thermostat calls for heat?
- [ ] The blower
- [x] The inducer
- [ ] The burners
- [ ] The gas valve
> right: The inducer pulls the flame's gas through before anything lights.
> wrong: The inducer starts first, and a pressure switch proves it is pulling. Then the burners light.

3. What does the flame sensor do?
- [ ] Lights the burners
- [ ] Measures the room
- [x] Proves that the flame is there
- [ ] Starts the blower
> right: If the sensor finds no flame, the gas valve shuts.
> wrong: The flame sensor proves the flame. Without a flame, the gas is shut off.

4. Which furnace makes water in its vent?
- [ ] A standard furnace, Category I
- [x] A condensing furnace, Category IV
- [ ] Every furnace
- [ ] None
> right: A condensing furnace takes so much heat out that water forms, so it vents in plastic and needs a drain.
> wrong: The condensing furnace, Category IV, makes water. The standard one vents hot and dry.
