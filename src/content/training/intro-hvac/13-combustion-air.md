## Why it matters
A flame eats air. A furnace and a water heater in a closet can use up the closet's air in minutes. Starved of air, a flame makes carbon monoxide, the poison gas of the lesson after next. The openings in that closet are not decoration.

## Must know
- Combustion air is the air a flame burns. Most gas appliances take it from the room they sit in.
- The code counts a room as big enough at **50 cubic feet** for each 1,000 Btu an hour of burner.
- A furnace and a water heater together can need a room far bigger than a closet.
- A closet gets its air through two openings to the rest of the house.
- One opening starts within **12 in** of the top of the closet. The other starts within 12 in of the bottom.
- Each opening has 1 sq in for each 1,000 Btu an hour, and never less than **100 sq in**.
- {{figure:closet-air|Two openings feed the closet, one high and one low. Block either one and the flames starve.}}
- Openings to the outdoors may be smaller. The code sets those sizes too.
- A direct vent appliance is sealed. It pipes its own air in from outdoors and uses none of the room's.
- Gas appliances stay out of bedrooms, bathrooms and storage closets, unless the code's exceptions are met.
- A flame short of air burns yellow and lazy and leaves soot. A good flame is blue and steady.

## Traps
- A louvred door on a furnace closet is not for looks. It is the closet's air, and a solid door starves the flames.
- Boxes stored against the low opening block it as surely as a board.
- A new exhaust fan or a tighter house can starve an old furnace that always had air before.

## Numbers
- **50 cubic feet** of room for each 1,000 Btu an hour
- **12 in** from the top and 12 in from the bottom are where the two openings start
- **100 sq in** is the least size of each opening

## Check yourself
1. How many openings feed a closet that holds a furnace and a water heater?
- [ ] None
- [ ] One, at the floor
- [x] Two, one high and one low
- [ ] Four
> right: One opening starts within 12 in of the top and one within 12 in of the bottom.
> wrong: A closet gets two openings, one high and one low, so air can move through it.

2. What is the least size of each opening to the rest of the house?
- [ ] 10 sq in
- [ ] 50 sq in
- [x] 100 sq in
- [ ] 1,000 sq in
> right: One square inch for each 1,000 Btu an hour, and never less than 100 sq in.
> wrong: Each opening is at least 100 sq in, and bigger for bigger burners.

3. What does a flame short of air look like?
- [ ] Blue and steady
- [x] Yellow and lazy, with soot
- [ ] Clear
- [ ] It looks the same
> right: Yellow, lazy and sooty is a starved flame. Blue and steady is a fed one.
> wrong: A starved flame burns yellow and lazy and leaves soot.

4. What is different about a direct vent appliance?
- [ ] It needs no vent
- [ ] It burns no gas
- [x] It is sealed and pipes its own air in from outdoors
- [ ] It needs a bigger closet
> right: A direct vent appliance uses none of the room's air.
> wrong: Direct vent means sealed. Its air comes in by its own pipe from outdoors.
