## Why it matters
This is the air conditioning call a plumbing crew gets most. The drain blocks, the water backs up, and something stops or something drips. The strip is what happens and what the crew does about it.

## Must know
- Slime grows in a condensate drain all summer. It collects in the trap and at any flat spot.
- {{figure:clogged-drain-strip|Slime blocks the drain. The pan fills and the float switch stops the unit. Clear the line, then prove it with water.}}
- When the drain blocks, the pan under the coil fills. The water has nowhere else to go.
- Then the second defence shows. The float switch stops the unit, or the second drain drips where it is seen.
- With no second defence, the pan spills. The first sign is a stain on the ceiling.
- The first step is the thermostat, set to off. A coil that is not cooling makes no more water.
- The clog is cleared through the capped tee, or pulled out from the far end with a wet vacuum.
- The pipe is never cut to clear it. The code wants the line built so a clog comes out without a cut.
- The proof is water. Pour some in the pan and watch it leave at the far end.
- The float drops when the pan empties, and the unit can run again.
- A drain that clogs every month has a flat spot or no trap. Then the pipe is the fix, not the vacuum.

## Traps
- A unit that will not start is not always a dead unit. Look in the pan before anyone opens a panel.
- Blowing the line out toward the unit can push the slime into the pan and the coil.
- A cleared drain is not a proven drain. It is proven when poured water comes out the far end.

## Numbers
- **first** set the thermostat to off
- **0** cuts in the pipe to clear a clog
- **a pour of water** is the proof the drain runs

## Check yourself
1. What is the first step when a condensate drain has backed up?
- [ ] Cut the drain pipe
- [x] Set the thermostat to off
- [ ] Add refrigerant
- [ ] Pull the float switch out
> right: A coil that is not cooling makes no more water. Then the clog is cleared.
> wrong: The thermostat goes to off first, so the coil stops making water.

2. Where is the clog cleared from?
- [ ] A cut in the pipe
- [ ] The outdoor unit
- [x] The capped tee, or the far end with a wet vacuum
- [ ] The thermostat
> right: The tee is the way in, and a wet vacuum at the far end pulls the slime out.
> wrong: The clog comes out through the capped tee or the far end. The pipe is never cut.

3. What proves the drain is running again?
- [ ] The unit starts
- [ ] The pan looks dry
- [x] Water poured in the pan comes out at the far end
- [ ] The float is up
> right: A pour of water that leaves at the far end is the only proof.
> wrong: Pour water in the pan and watch it leave. Nothing else proves the line is clear.

4. A drain clogs again every month. What is the likely cause?
- [ ] The weather
- [x] A flat spot in the pipe or no trap
- [ ] Too much refrigerant
- [ ] The thermostat
> right: Slime collects where water sits. The pipe needs its slope or its trap put right.
> wrong: A drain that keeps clogging has a flat spot or no trap. The pipe is the fix.
