## Why it matters
What a flame leaves behind has to get out of the house. On most water heaters and older furnaces nothing pushes it. It rises because it is hot. A vent that sags, leaks or is the wrong size sends it back into the room.

## Must know
- A flame leaves hot gas behind, mostly water vapor and carbon dioxide. It leaves the house by the vent.
- In a natural draft vent nothing pushes the gas. It rises because it is hotter than the air around it.
- The draft hood is the open collar on top of a water heater. Room air joins the flame's gas there.
- If the vent is blocked, the draft hood lets the gas spill into the room. It does not send it back into the burner.
- The vent connector is the pipe from the appliance to the vent. It slopes up at least **1/4 in per ft**.
- A vent connector has no dips and no sags. A low spot holds the gas back.
- {{figure:shared-vent|Each connector slopes up to the vent with no dip. The furnace and the water heater often share one vent.}}
- A furnace and a water heater often share one vent. The vent is sized for the two together.
- Take the furnace off a shared vent and the water heater is left alone. The vent is now too big for it.
- A vent too big stays cold. The gas cools, stalls and can spill at the draft hood.
- {{figure:left-alone-on-the-vent|With the furnace on it, the vent stays hot and draws. With the furnace gone, the water heater cannot warm it, and the gas spills.}}
- A condensing furnace vents in plastic pipe with a fan. That pipe slopes back so its water drains to the furnace.

## Traps
- A vent that rises a little and then dips is not sloped. One sag is enough to stall the gas.
- A new furnace is not only a furnace job. If it leaves the old vent, the water heater's vent has changed too.
- A vent pipe knocked loose in an attic is not a rattle. It is the flame's gas loose in the house.

## Numbers
- **1/4 in per ft** is the least a vent connector slopes up
- **0** dips or sags in a vent connector
- **2** appliances often share one vent

## Check yourself
1. What moves the gas up a natural draft vent?
- [ ] A fan
- [ ] The gas pressure
- [x] Its own heat, since hot gas rises
- [ ] The blower
> right: Nothing pushes it. The gas rises because it is hotter than the air around it.
> wrong: Natural draft has no fan. The hot gas rises by itself.

2. How much must a vent connector slope up?
- [ ] It may run level
- [x] At least 1/4 in per ft
- [ ] At least 2 in per ft
- [ ] It slopes down
> right: A quarter inch a foot, upward to the vent, with no dips or sags.
> wrong: The connector slopes up at least 1/4 in per ft, all the way to the vent.

3. The furnace is taken off a vent it shared with the water heater. What changes for the water heater?
- [ ] Nothing
- [ ] It burns less gas
- [x] It is alone on a vent too big for it, and the gas can stall and spill
- [ ] It needs no vent now
> right: The vent was sized for two. Alone, the water heater cannot keep it warm enough to draw.
> wrong: A vent sized for two is too big for one. The gas cools, stalls and can spill.

4. What does the draft hood do when the vent is blocked?
- [ ] Shuts the gas off
- [x] Lets the flame's gas spill into the room
- [ ] Opens a second vent
- [ ] Sounds an alarm
> right: The gas spills at the open collar. That is why a blocked vent is a danger to the room.
> wrong: The draft hood lets the gas escape into the room when the vent cannot take it.
