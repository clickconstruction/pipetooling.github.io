## Why it matters
Silica in concrete, brick, stone and mortar is harmless until it is cut, ground or drilled. The respirable dust causes silicosis years later, and there is no cure.

Asbestos fibers from pre-1980 materials cause mesothelioma, lung cancer and asbestosis **20 to 30 years** later. That damage cannot be reversed.

## Must know
- Crystalline silica is in sand, rock, concrete, brick, block, stone, grout, mortar and tile, not in paint or wood. Cutting, grinding, drilling, jackhammering, sandblasting, demolition, mixing grout and dry sweeping all make the dust.
- Respirable particles under **10 microns** lodge deep in the lungs and scar them. Silicosis can be chronic at **10 to 30 years**, accelerated at **5 to 10**, or acute within **2 years**. There is no cure, and smoking speeds it up.
- Action level: **25 µg/m³** as an 8-hour TWA. PEL: **50 µg/m³** as an 8-hour TWA. Below the action level under all foreseeable conditions, the standard does not apply.
- Wet methods and dust collection with HEPA vacuums come first. Never dry sweep or blow dust with compressed air. Respirators come only where the controls fall short.
- Employers may measure exposure, assume the action level, or follow **Table 1**, 18 common tasks with set controls and respirators. Following Table 1 is optional. Its respirator column splits at **4 hours** per shift and is stricter indoors.
- A written exposure control plan, reviewed **annually**, is required where exposure may reach the action level. A competent person runs it and takes your concerns. Respirator wearers of **30 or more days** a year get free medical exams, a baseline then every **3 years**.
- Asbestos is a fibrous mineral used through the **1970s** in fireproofing, insulation and roofing. It is harmless intact and dangerous once disturbed and airborne.
- ACM is material tested at more than **1%** asbestos. PACM is presumed but untested. In buildings from before **1980**, presume it in shingles, pipe insulation, fireproofing, vinyl flooring, popcorn ceilings and plaster.
- Asbestos PEL: **0.1 f/cc** as an 8-hour TWA. Excursion limit: **1.0 f/cc** over any **30 minutes**.
- Controls: certified abatement first, then negative-pressure enclosures, wet methods, local exhaust and HEPA filters that trap **99.97%** at **0.3 microns**. The competent person chooses.
- Wear disposable coveralls with head, hand and foot covers, a NIOSH-approved respirator, and eye protection when cutting or grinding. Remove coveralls inside out and bag them as asbestos waste. Waste is wetted, double-bagged in **6-mil** plastic, labeled, and sent with a licensed hauler to an approved landfill.
- Assess exposure the moment PACM is identified, and sample the air while exposure could exceed the PEL. Before anyone reoccupies an area, confirm licensing, lab reports, clearance air sampling and a third-party inspection.

## Traps
- The action level is at or above 25 µg/m³ as an 8-hour TWA. One reading above 25 during the shift is not it.
- Following Table 1 is optional. A disconnected vacuum hose or a clogged shroud recreates the hazard: stop and report.
- Working near intact asbestos is not an exposure. Cutting ceiling tiles, demolishing walls or tearing up vinyl flooring is.
- If unsure, presume asbestos and assess immediately, not at an annual review. Keep the PEL, 0.1 over 8 hours, apart from the excursion limit, 1.0 over 30 minutes.

## Numbers
- **25 µg/m³** silica action level
- **50 µg/m³** silica PEL
- **4 hours** where Table 1's respirator column splits
- **3 years** between silica medical exams
- **30 years** silica exposure records kept
- **0.1 f/cc** asbestos PEL over 8 hours
- **1.0 f/cc** asbestos excursion limit over 30 minutes
- **1%** asbestos makes a material ACM
- **1980** presume asbestos in buildings from before it
- **6-mil** bags for asbestos waste

## Check yourself
1. Which best describes the silica Action Level?
- [x] At or above 25 µg/m³ as an 8-hour TWA
- [ ] Any reading above 25 µg/m³ at any point in a shift
- [ ] At or above 50 µg/m³ as an 8-hour TWA
- [ ] Whenever exposure is possible
> right: An 8-hour average at or above 25 starts the standard. One spike does not.
> wrong: The action level is an 8-hour average, not a spike. 50 is the PEL, a different line.

2. Who should you contact with questions or concerns about silica exposure?
- [ ] The regional OSHA office
- [ ] A co-worker
- [x] The competent person
- [ ] An industrial hygienist
> right: The competent person runs the exposure control plan and takes your concerns.
> wrong: The exposure control plan names one person to take silica concerns. Start there, not with OSHA or a co-worker.

3. When should asbestos monitoring and exposure assessments be conducted?
- [ ] After a worker becomes ill
- [ ] Once a year
- [ ] Only once the PACM has actually been disturbed
- [x] The moment there is PACM at the site
> right: Presume it, assess it now. Not at an annual review and not after someone is sick.
> wrong: The assessment starts as soon as PACM is identified. Waiting for illness, a year or a disturbance is too late.

4. Stephen is remodeling a 1960s school. Which of these does NOT expose him to asbestos?
- [ ] Cutting into the ceiling tiles
- [x] Working near intact asbestos
- [ ] Knocking down the walls
- [ ] Ripping up the vinyl flooring
> right: Intact asbestos is not an exposure. Cutting, smashing and tearing it up is.
> wrong: Fibers only fly when the material is cut, knocked down or torn up. Standing near intact material is not an exposure.
