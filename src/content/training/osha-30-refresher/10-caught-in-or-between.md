## Why it matters
A small move of a machine or a trench wall crushes a person in an instant. These incidents are among the leading causes of construction deaths.

## Must know
- Caught-in or between means caught, crushed, squeezed or pinned between two or more objects. If it could crush or pin you, it is caught-in. If only the impact hurts, it is struck-by.
- Around equipment, get the operator's authorization first. Stay out of the marked swing radius. Never stand between a machine and a fixed object.
- Stack on stable, level ground. Lumber goes **16 ft** high by hand and **20 ft** by machine.
- Loose bricks stop at **7 ft** and taper above **4 ft**. Blocks taper above **6 ft**. Keep materials **6 ft** from any edge.
- {{figure:stacking-limits|How high each pile may go. Bricks and blocks taper as they rise.}}
- An unsafe stack: move away, warn others, notify your manager. Then stay nearby and keep people away.
- Moving parts pinch, crush, shear, wrap, nip and cut. A full enclosure beats a distance rule or gloves.
- Never run a machine with a missing, damaged or homemade guard. Remove a guard only after lockout.
- ROPS, the roll cage over the operator, is required on all heavy machinery on any ground.
- If it tips, stay inside, belted. Grip the wheel and lean away from the tip. Seat belts are enforced under the General Duty Clause.
- {{figure:tip-over|Belted in, hands on the wheel, leaning away from the fall. The cage does the rest. Never jump.}}
- A cubic yard of soil weighs **2,000 to 3,000 lb**, as much as a car. Protection goes in before anyone enters.
- {{figure:soil-cube-car|A yard of soil weighs as much as a car. A cave-in buries you under several.}}
- Warning signs of a cave-in: bubbling or cracking walls, cracks parallel to the trench, sagging or bulging, dry cracked soil.
- {{figure:cave-in-signs|Six ways a wall says it is about to go. On any one, everybody out.}}
- A reported hazard gets fixed right away. The drywall stack that trapped a worker had been reported the day before.

## Traps
- A dropped hammer is struck-by. A wall collapse, a cave-in or unguarded gears are caught-in or between.
- Cave-in precautions go in before workers enter, not before digging starts.
- When a tip-over starts, jumping out is wrong. Stay in the cage.
- A coworker refuses a seat belt. Tell your manager, not OSHA first.

## Numbers
- **16 ft** lumber by hand
- **20 ft** lumber by machine
- **7 ft** loose bricks
- **4 ft** bricks taper above
- **6 ft** blocks taper above
- **6 ft** materials from any edge
- **2,000 to 3,000 lb** a cubic yard of soil

## Check yourself
1. What is a caught-in or between hazard?
- [ ] A worker struck by a flying object
- [x] A worker caught between two objects
- [ ] Slips and falls
- [ ] A worker inhaling toxic fumes
> right: Caught, crushed, squeezed or pinned between two or more objects.
> wrong: Flying objects are struck-by, slips are falls and fumes are a health hazard. This one crushes or pins you.

2. How high can lumber be stacked if it is moved by hand?
- [ ] 7 feet
- [x] 16 feet
- [ ] 21 feet
- [ ] 30 feet
> right: By hand, that is the limit. A machine may stack to 20.
> wrong: 7 feet is the loose brick limit. Lumber by hand goes higher, and by machine higher still.

3. What is the best way to protect workers from an exposed pulley belt?
- [ ] Keep everyone 3 ft away
- [ ] Put a guardrail around it
- [x] Fully enclose the belt
- [ ] Hand out gloves
> right: A full enclosure is an engineering control. It works without anyone remembering a rule.
> wrong: A distance rule is forgotten, a guardrail gets moved and a glove gets pulled in. Close the hazard off.

4. When is it safe to enter a trench?
- [ ] When caution tape is around the opening
- [ ] When a coworker entered earlier and reported no problems
- [x] When a competent person has inspected it
- [ ] When it is more than 10 feet deep
> right: Inspected by the competent person, with the protection in place. Then you go in.
> wrong: Tape, depth and a coworker's luck prove nothing. Only the inspection and the protection do.
