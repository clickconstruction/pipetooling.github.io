## Why it matters
About **2.3 million** construction workers use scaffolds. That is **65%** of the industry. Proper protection would prevent about **4,500** injuries and **50** deaths a year.

## Must know
- A supported scaffold rests on a firm surface. A suspended scaffold hangs from above. Aerial lifts are the third kind.
- Every scaffold must hold its own weight plus **four times** the maximum intended load. Never overload one.
- A qualified person designs the scaffold and trains the people who work from it. A competent person supervises erecting, moving and dismantling.
- The competent person inspects the scaffold before each work shift. Never modify a scaffold yourself.
- Platforms are fully planked with scaffold-grade planks, no more than **1 in** from the uprights. Never paint a wood plank. Paint hides defects. Use mud sills under base plates on dirt or asphalt.
- Fall protection starts above **10 ft**. Guardrails go on every open side. Without them you wear a PFAS, the personal fall arrest system, tied only to an anchorage rated **5,000 lb**.
- Top rails sit **38 to 45 in** high, midrails halfway, toeboards at least **3.5 in**. Add screens or nets where materials stack higher than the toeboard.
- Stay **3 ft** from insulated power lines under **300 V**. Stay **10 ft** from uninsulated lines or anything over 300 V.
- Tie the scaffold to the structure once its height passes four times the narrowest base width. Ties go every **20 ft** up when the base is 3 ft wide or less. A wider base ties every **26 ft** up. Every scaffold ties every **30 ft** across.
- Climb down before a mobile scaffold moves. Riding is allowed only when the surface is within **3 degrees** of level. The height can be no more than **twice** the base width. Powered movement stays under **1 ft per second** and nobody rides beyond the wheels.
- Push a mobile scaffold near the base and lock the casters when it stops.
- Retrain when the site, the scaffold or the fall protection changes.

## Traps
- The quiz scores the inspection as before each use, not daily. The competent person does it before each work shift.
- Toeboards stop falling materials. Guardrails and a PFAS stop people.
- The course says OSHA planks. It means scaffold-grade wood planks.
- A buddy on the platform is not fall protection. Guardrails, a PFAS and training are.

## Numbers
- **4 times** the intended load, plus its own weight
- **10 ft** fall protection on a scaffold
- **38 to 45 in** the top rail
- **3.5 in** the toeboard
- **1 in** the widest gap at the uprights
- **5,000 lb** the anchorage
- **3 ft** from an insulated line under 300 V
- **10 ft** from any other power line
- **1 ft per second** the fastest a ridden scaffold moves

## Check yourself
1. Your crew is sent to work from a scaffold that is not safe. What should you do?
- [ ] Stay quiet
- [x] Talk to your manager about your concerns
- [ ] Go straight to OSHA
- [ ] Assume the builders know what they are doing
> right: You may refuse to work on it too. The employer hears it first, and OSHA if nothing changes.
> wrong: Speak up without fear of retaliation. Your employer hears it first, and OSHA only if nothing changes.

2. A worker removed the guardrails of a mobile scaffold and rocked it to move it. He fell 14 ft and died. Which of these would not have made it safer?
- [ ] Not modifying the scaffold
- [ ] Checking the wheels before climbing on
- [ ] Getting off before moving it
- [x] Using a ladder instead
> right: A ladder was no answer here. Three points of contact make taping and floating impractical.
> wrong: Never modify a scaffold, inspect it first, and get off before it moves. A ladder is no fix for that work.

3. Marco climbed a scaffold without fall protection to grab tools and fell to his death. Which of these would not have helped?
- [ ] Guardrails in place
- [ ] A personal fall arrest system
- [x] Having another person with him
- [ ] Training on proper scaffold use
> right: A buddy cannot catch a fall. Guardrails, a PFAS and training each would have helped.
> wrong: A second person on the platform stops nothing. A rail, a harness system or training would have.

4. Who does the employer not need to train on scaffold hazards?
- [ ] Workers erecting the scaffold
- [ ] Workers inspecting the scaffold
- [ ] Workers who work from the scaffold
- [x] Workers driving trucks
> right: Everyone who erects, inspects or works from a scaffold is trained. A driver who never climbs it is not.
> wrong: The training covers everyone who erects, inspects or works from the scaffold. Only people who never touch it are left out.
