## Why it matters
Poor handling causes sprains, fractures, amputations and crushing deaths. Back injuries are the most common musculoskeletal disorder. Most of these injuries happen when you have not prepared.

## Must know
- Manual handling's main risk is hurting your body. Overexertion is the primary manual hazard. Mechanical handling's main risk is losing control of the equipment.
- Back pain is the most common musculoskeletal disorder. Carpal tunnel, tendinitis, trigger finger, rotator cuff injuries and tennis elbow follow it.
- The safe lifting zone is **mid-thigh to mid-chest**. Stop and tell your manager at the first warning sign.
- Plan each task with a JHA, the job hazard analysis. Eliminate the lift first. Then engineering controls like forklifts, hoists and carts. Then training and weight markings. PPE like gloves and steel toes comes last.
- The lift has five steps. Plan the weight and the route. Squat with a straight back and grip close to the body. Move without twisting or rushing, then set down carefully.
- Over **50 lb** may need a second person or a mechanical aid. Count off a team lift together. Never roll a barrel or cylinder by hand.
- Only trained, certified people run cranes, forklifts and rigging. An untrained coworker does not move the forklift, not even 10 ft.
- Wire rope shows damage. Alloy chain hides it, and every link is a single point of failure. Synthetic slings are damaged by chemicals, UV and sharp edges.
- A missing tag means out of service. Rigging attachments are proof-tested at **125%** of rated load.
- At a conveyor, no loose clothing or jewelry. Never ride one or walk under it. Keep the guards on and lock out before clearing a jam.
- Block stacks over **6 ft** taper back half a block per tier. Interlock bagged materials. Cylinders stay no higher than **5 ft** unless racked. Hazardous materials go in leak-proof containers away from ignition sources.
- Clean as you work. Check the SDS, the safety data sheet, before disposing of a chemical. Use an enclosed chute when dropping material more than **20 ft**. Barricade and sign drop zones.

## Traps
- Safety training is an administrative control. Barriers, hoists and carts are engineering.
- In the granite-slab amputation case the fix was inspecting the lifting adapter and clamps. Not a bigger crane, not more PPE.
- Repetitive lifting injuries come from manual handling. Losing control comes from mechanical handling.
- Damage to a crane or rigging is reported at once, even when another crew caused it. An unreported boom strike killed a worker the next day.

## Numbers
- **50 lb** get help or a mechanical aid
- **125%** the rigging proof test
- **6 ft** block stacks taper above this
- **5 ft** the highest unracked cylinders
- **20 ft** an enclosed chute for drops past this
- **mid-thigh to mid-chest** the safe lifting zone

## Check yourself
1. According to the hierarchy of controls, what type of control is safety training?
- [ ] Engineering control
- [x] Administrative control
- [ ] PPE
- [ ] Substitution
> right: Training depends on people following it. That makes it administrative.
> wrong: A control that depends on people following it is administrative. Guards, hoists and barriers are engineering.

2. A crane moved two granite slabs clamped together. They slipped from the lifting adapter and struck a worker, who lost his lower leg. How could it have been prevented?
- [ ] Using a bigger crane
- [x] Inspecting the adapter and clamps
- [ ] Wearing more PPE
- [ ] Training the crew in first aid
> right: The slabs were within capacity. The clamps that held them were never checked.
> wrong: Capacity was not the problem and PPE stops no slab. The rigging that held the load needed inspecting.

3. Which of these is not a step that prevents injuries when handling materials?
- [ ] Notify your manager about a possible hazard
- [ ] Plan the job with a JHA
- [x] Lift from the waist
- [ ] Identify hazards and use controls to remove them
> right: Lift with the legs and a straight back. The other three are the real prevention steps.
> wrong: Planning, reporting and controls prevent injuries. Bending at the waist is how backs get hurt.

4. A rigging sling has no tag. What does that mean?
- [ ] It is rated for its full size
- [x] It is out of service
- [ ] It can lift up to 50 lb
- [ ] It was made before tags were required
> right: No tag, no rating, no lift. Tag it out and report it.
> wrong: The tag carries the rating. Without it nobody knows the capacity, so the sling is out of service.
