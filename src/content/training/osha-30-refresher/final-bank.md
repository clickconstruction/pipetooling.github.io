## Questions
1. Which mental health condition is triggered by a terrifying event?
- [ ] Depression
- [x] Post-traumatic stress disorder
- [ ] Acute stress disorder
- [ ] Substance use disorder
> right: PTSD is the long-term condition a terrifying event sets off.
> wrong: The long-term condition a terrifying event sets off is PTSD, the one with trauma in its name.

2. How should managers give feedback to workers?
- [ ] Criticize them at the huddle
- [ ] Wait until they make five mistakes
- [x] Provide timely and sincere feedback
- [ ] Only when they have a bad attitude
> right: Prompt, sincere and about the behavior, not the person.
> wrong: Good feedback comes soon after what you saw and means what it says.

3. Who ensures workers get arc flash training?
- [x] Employers
- [ ] Workers, paying for it themselves
- [ ] OSHA directly
- [ ] The manufacturer
> right: Training is the employer's duty. Workers take part and follow the procedures.
> wrong: The duty to train sits with the one who sends you to the work.

4. What should you do to prevent caught-in/between hazards when working with machinery?
- [ ] Wear loose clothing
- [x] Keep all safeguards in place
- [ ] Stand very close to the machine
- [ ] Ignore safety protocols
> right: Guards keep hands and clothing out of the moving parts.
> wrong: The guards are what stand between you and the moving parts. Leave them on.

5. What is a caught-in/between hazard?
- [ ] Being struck by a flying object
- [ ] Slipping and falling
- [x] A worker caught between two objects
- [ ] Inhaling toxic fumes
> right: Caught, crushed or squeezed between two or more objects.
> wrong: Flying objects are struck-by and fumes are a health hazard. This one is a squeeze.

6. What should you wear to protect yourself from smaller struck-by hazards like a dropped tool?
- [x] A hard hat
- [ ] A respirator
- [ ] A safety harness
- [ ] Ear protection
> right: A hard hat stops small objects. It does not stop a crane load.
> wrong: The PPE for something falling on your head sits on your head.

7. Which of the following presents a struck-by hazard?
- [ ] A scaffold without a midrail
- [x] Walking under a hoisted load
- [ ] Carrying a ladder near overhead power lines
- [ ] Working outside in extreme cold
> right: Loads shift and fall. Never stand or walk under one.
> wrong: A missing midrail is a fall hazard and a power line is electrocution. Look for what can drop.

8. What type of exposure involves being exposed to a hazard for a short time?
- [x] Acute exposure
- [ ] Chronic exposure
- [ ] Repeated exposure
- [ ] Long-term exposure
> right: Acute is short and sudden. Chronic is long and repeated.
> wrong: Short-time exposure has its own name. The other three all mean long or repeated.

9. What is an example of a biological hazard?
- [ ] Noise
- [ ] Solvents
- [x] Mold
- [ ] Extreme temperature
> right: Mold, poisonous plants and bloodborne pathogens are biological.
> wrong: Noise and temperature are physical and solvents are chemical. Living things are biological.

10. What is a common chemical hazard in construction?
- [ ] Vibrations
- [x] Solvents
- [ ] Noise
- [ ] Animals
> right: Solvents, paints and dusts are the chemical hazards you breathe or touch.
> wrong: Vibration and noise are physical, animals are biological. Pick the substance.

11. How can employers protect workers on extremely hot days?
- [ ] Have them wear layers
- [x] Acclimatize them gradually
- [ ] Work full shifts right away
- [ ] Skip breaks
> right: The body needs 1 to 2 weeks to get used to heat. Build the time up.
> wrong: Heat tolerance is built over 1 to 2 weeks, with breaks, not by pushing through.

12. What is an effective practice against loud worksite noise?
- [ ] Trade earplugs with a coworker
- [ ] Ignore short bursts of noise
- [x] Wear employer-provided hearing protection
- [ ] Shout over it
> right: Earplugs or earmuffs, provided free, every time it is loud.
> wrong: Short bursts and shared plugs still cost hearing. Use your own protection.

13. A coworker uses a table saw with no dust collection system. What should you do?
- [ ] Distance yourself
- [x] Inform your manager
- [ ] Join in
- [ ] Open a window
> right: Silica dust is a hazard for everyone nearby. The manager fixes it.
> wrong: Walking away or airing the room leaves the dust in the air. Report it.

14. Leigh is running late and puts on her PPE without inspecting it. Is that okay?
- [ ] Yes, as long as she does not do it twice
- [ ] Yes, if she checks it on her break
- [x] No, she checks her PPE before putting it on
- [ ] Yes, checking it is her boss's job
> right: The employer provides PPE. Checking it every day is on you.
> wrong: PPE is inspected before every use, by the one wearing it.

15. If stairways have four or more risers, what safety feature is required?
- [x] At least one handrail
- [ ] Handrails on both sides
- [ ] Painted risers
- [ ] None
> right: Four risers is the line for a handrail.
> wrong: Count the risers. From four up, the stairway gets a rail.

16. What is one of the most common hazards in excavations that can kill workers?
- [ ] Noise
- [x] Cave-ins
- [ ] Cold stress
- [ ] Dust
> right: About 40 workers a year die in trench collapses.
> wrong: Soil weighs 2,000 to 3,000 lb a cubic yard. Think about what falls in on you.

17. What should a worker do before using a ladder?
- [ ] Step on the top rung
- [x] Inspect it for damage and stability
- [ ] Set it on a sloped surface
- [ ] Remove its tags and labels
> right: Inspect before use. A defective ladder is tagged and taken out of service.
> wrong: Every tool is checked before use, and a ladder is a tool.

18. What is the weakest way to use a sling for lifting?
- [ ] Vertical
- [ ] Basket
- [x] Choker
- [ ] Horizontal
> right: A choker bends sharply at the choke point and loses capacity.
> wrong: The hitch that bends the sling sharply around the load is the weak one.

19. When should a crane be inspected?
- [ ] Only before its first use
- [ ] Only when it is new
- [x] Before use and after accidents
- [ ] Every 5 years
> right: Daily before use, after any accident, and annually by a qualified person.
> wrong: A crane is checked every day it works and after anything goes wrong.

20. Which hazard is most common in steel erection?
- [ ] Electric shock
- [x] Falls from heights
- [ ] Chemicals
- [ ] Vibration
> right: Steel work is high work. Falls lead every list.
> wrong: Think about where ironworkers stand. The top hazard is the drop.

21. What is the term for the sudden, forceful backward movement of a power tool or the material being cut?
- [ ] Pinching
- [x] Kickback
- [ ] Crossflow
- [ ] Reverb
> right: Kickback throws the saw or the stock back at you.
> wrong: The word for a saw jumping back at you is the one that says it.

22. What should be done if a tool's safety guard is not working properly?
- [ ] Remove the guard
- [ ] Keep using it carefully
- [x] Take the tool out of service
- [ ] Ignore it
> right: Tag it, remove it from service and report it.
> wrong: A bad guard is a defect. Defective tools come out of service, never used carefully.

23. Who uses hand signals to guide motor vehicle operators around hazards and through temporary lane closures?
- [ ] Supervisors
- [x] Flaggers
- [ ] Operators
- [ ] Carriers
> right: Trained flaggers direct traffic where work meets the road.
> wrong: The person with the sign and the flags has a name and training.

24. Where should diesel fuel be stored?
- [ ] In direct sunlight
- [x] In an approved container
- [ ] In the cab
- [ ] In high-traffic areas
> right: Approved containers, away from heat, traffic and the cab.
> wrong: Fuel goes in a container made for it, out of the sun and out of the way.

25. What is a common hazard in a permit-required confined space?
- [x] A hazardous atmosphere
- [ ] Ample oxygen
- [ ] Extreme weather
- [ ] No mechanical equipment
> right: Bad air is the hazard that makes the space permit-required.
> wrong: The air inside is what kills in a confined space. Test it before entry and keep testing.

26. What is the correct way to use a fire extinguisher?
- [ ] Aim at the top of the flames
- [x] Aim at the base of the fire
- [ ] Aim at the smoke
- [ ] Aim at the middle
> right: PASS: pull, aim at the base, squeeze, sweep, from 6 to 8 ft away.
> wrong: The fuel burns at the bottom. Aim where the fire feeds.

27. Which type of fire extinguisher should be used for electrical fires?
- [ ] Water
- [ ] Foam
- [x] Dry chemical
- [ ] Wet chemical
> right: Dry chemical does not conduct. Water and foam do.
> wrong: Anything wet carries current back to you. Pick the dry one.

28. What is required by a Hot Work Permit for welding?
- [ ] Noise baffling
- [x] A fire watch
- [ ] A water hose
- [ ] Radiation shielding
> right: The fire watch stays 30 minutes after the hot work ends.
> wrong: The permit calls for a trained person watching for fire, 30 minutes past the end.

29. How often should air monitoring be done during a permit-required confined space entry?
- [ ] Only before entry
- [ ] Once an hour
- [x] Continuously
- [ ] After the work
> right: The attendant monitors the air the whole time anyone is inside.
> wrong: Air changes while you work. One test at the start is not enough.

30. Which of the following does NFPA 70E require workers to do?
- [ ] Provide electrical safety program training
- [x] Follow their employer's electrical safety procedures
- [ ] Report near misses to OSHA
- [ ] Assess jobsite hazards
> right: Training, hazard assessment and the program are employer duties. Yours is to follow them.
> wrong: Under NFPA 70E the worker follows the program. Writing it and reporting to OSHA are not the worker's job.

31. What is ergonomics?
- [ ] Making tools fit the job
- [x] Making tools fit people
- [ ] Making people fit the job
- [ ] Making people fit the tools
> right: Fit the work to the worker, never the other way around.
> wrong: Ergonomics bends the tool and the task to the body, not the body to the tool.

32. What is an example of a PPE control for ergonomics?
- [ ] Safe work practices
- [ ] Frequent breaks
- [x] Anti-vibration gloves
- [ ] Anti-fatigue mats
> right: Gloves are worn, so they are PPE. Breaks and practices are administrative.
> wrong: PPE is something you wear. Mats, breaks and practices change the work, not your gear.

33. Supervisor Joe sees apprentice Sam grinding without a face shield. What could Joe say to Sam?
- [ ] "Why would you even consider not wearing a face shield?"
- [x] "I noticed you're not wearing a face shield."
- [ ] "Don't you know how dangerous what you're doing is?"
- [ ] "Stop! By not wearing a face shield, you're putting us all at risk."
> right: Neutral, specific and observable. Say what you saw.
> wrong: Name what you saw without judging. Questions that scold put the worker on the defensive.
