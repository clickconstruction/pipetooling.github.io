## Why it matters
Bad air in a sewer, crawl space or trench kills fast. The wrong respirator is no protection at all. An N95 does nothing against a gas or low oxygen.

## Must know
- There are four airborne hazards. Particulates like dust, fibers and fumes, gases and vapors, biological agents, and oxygen deficiency.
- Normal air is **20.9%** oxygen. Below **19.5%** is IDLH, immediately dangerous to life or health, and at **10%** or less people pass out or die. A monitor only warns, and ventilation is the engineering control.
- Air-purifying respirators clean the air you have. They are dust masks, half or full face masks with filters or cartridges, and PAPRs, the powered hoods.
- Atmosphere-supplying respirators bring clean air from outside, and IDLH air requires one. An airline unit gives the most protection but tethers you to a hose. An SCBA carries its own cylinder.
- The employer selects by **APF**, the assigned protection factor. Oxygen deficiency is the one hazard an APF cannot address.
- A NIOSH mask shows the maker, the model, the filter rating, NIOSH spelled right and the TC number. Only the lot number is optional. **N95** filters 95% and is not oil-resistant, **P99** filters 99% and is oil-proof.
- {{figure:niosh-label|Five things on a real mask. Only the lot number may be missing.}}
- Inspect the respirator first, then put it on and seal-check it before eyewear or a hard hat. A dust mask collapses on inhale and bulges on exhale.
- On a tight-fitting respirator, cover the exhalation valve and feel it bulge. A failed seal check means readjust and test again. Loose PAPR hoods need no seal check.
- Any odd smell, heat or hard breathing: get out, get cleaned up, get a replacement.
- To clean a reusable respirator, take it apart and wash it in mild detergent. Disinfect it **2 minutes** in 1 mL bleach per liter of water, then rinse, dry and test. Store it cool, dry, enclosed and in its natural shape, with cartridges stored separately.
- A written respiratory protection program is required wherever respiratory hazards exist. A licensed health professional clears you before use and annually.
- A fit test comes before first use and **annually**, even if the respirator was not used. Repeat it when the model changes or your face or weight changes. A qualitative test uses a banana smell or a sweet or bitter taste, and detecting it means a leak.

## Traps
- A seal check, every time you put it on, is not a fit test, the formal annual test.
- A failed seal check means readjust and retest. A damaged respirator means replace it.
- A crawl-space worker wearing an N95 collapsed in hydrogen cyanide. N95s stop particles only, and the employer had no respiratory program.
- A required NIOSH marking missing, or NIOSH spelled wrong, means a mask you do not use.

## Numbers
- **20.9%** oxygen in normal air
- **19.5%** oxygen, IDLH below it
- **10%** oxygen or less, people pass out or die
- **N95** filters 95% and is not oil-resistant
- **P99** filters 99% and is oil-proof
- **2 minutes** in bleach water to disinfect
- **annually** the fit test and the medical clearance

## Check yourself
1. What is the one airborne hazard that can't be addressed with an APF?
- [ ] Particulates
- [x] Oxygen deficiency
- [ ] Gases and vapors
- [ ] Biological agents
> right: Low oxygen needs a different air supply, not a better filter.
> wrong: An APF rates how well a respirator cleans the air. It cannot add oxygen that is not there.

2. What is the first step in donning a respirator?
- [ ] Tighten the straps
- [ ] Do a seal check
- [ ] Put on your eyewear and hard hat
- [x] Inspect it for damage and cleanliness
> right: Inspect first. A dirty or damaged respirator never goes on.
> wrong: The straps, the seal check and the hard hat all come after the inspection.

3. How often should respirator fit tests be repeated?
- [ ] Every time you put it on
- [ ] Only before first use
- [x] Annually
- [ ] Before and after each use
> right: Once a year, even if the respirator was never used.
> wrong: A seal check is every time you put it on. The formal fit test repeats once a year.

4. Which type of respirator provides clean air from an external source?
- [x] Atmosphere-supplying respirators
- [ ] Dust masks
- [ ] Air-purifying respirators
- [ ] Powered air-purifying respirators
> right: An airline unit or an SCBA brings air in from outside.
> wrong: Dust masks, APRs and PAPRs all clean the air you have. Only one kind brings air from outside.
