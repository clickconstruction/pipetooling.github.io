## Why it matters
Saying nothing tells the crew that unsafe behavior is acceptable. A quick, respectful word is often the difference between a near miss and a life-changing injury. Culture is what happens every day: reporting, housekeeping, leading by example and honest conversations.

## Must know
- Step in right away. Approach only when it is safe and make sure the worker sees you coming. Ask them to stop and calmly name the behavior. Missing PPE: get it before work resumes. Missing training: stop the operation until training is arranged.
- There are **four** stages of safety awareness. Stage one, unconsciously at risk, does not know the rule: educate them. Stage two, consciously at risk, knows the rule and skips it: explain the injury risk. Stage three, consciously safe, follows the rules: acknowledge it. Stage four, unconsciously safe, is safe by habit: reinforce it.
- Unsafe behavior has **four** causes. No understanding: train. No injury before: explain why the rule exists. Saving time: explain why the step matters, since a shortcut that worked gets repeated and shared. Willful intent: the employer talks with the worker to find the real concern. For every cause but a lack of understanding, positive reinforcement is the strongest tool.
- Feedback is specific and observable. Say "You left your goggles behind when you went to use the saw", not "You always forget your goggles". Keep it neutral: "I noticed you're not wearing proper PPE."
- Follow up in **two** steps. Verification asks whether it was corrected. Validation asks whether it stuck. If the behavior continues, involve the manager, HR and the safety department. The employer documents the behavior, the fix and the prevention plan.
- Supervisor Joe sees apprentice Sam grinding without a face shield. He talks to Sam first, trains and equips him, then asks Sam's trainer Dave about it. Being careful is not a substitute for being safe.
- Any employee can stop unsafe work at any time. A near miss is when someone almost got hurt or an incident almost happened. Report it right away and never assume someone else will. Punished for reporting? Go to the manager's boss, a union steward or OSHA, within **30 days** for a retaliation complaint.
- Cultural change is a shift in a company's values, practices and behaviors. In a **2021** survey about **47.7%** of women in the trades reported being held to a different standard. Diverse companies are **39%** more likely to outperform their peers.
- Lead by example. A project director who picked up trash himself got the cleanest site anyone had seen. When a storm moves in, notify management, stop work and shelter everyone. Behind schedule with the safety professional away is no reason to wait.
- Pre-job meetings cover the day's plan, hazards and controls. Toolbox talks share key points about one task, so vary the topic weekly and never just read a script. In a hard conversation be specific, stay nonjudgmental, avoid "always" and "never", ask open-ended questions and offer support.
- Listen to workers. A worker said his gloves got slippery, so the supervisor tested alternatives and updated the PPE policy. Worker comments are proactive indicators. For a worker at risk, speak up privately, offer help and in a crisis call or text **988**.
- Protect the community too. Spill prevention controls and spill kits for chemicals. Netting and brick guards where debris can fall. Trained flaggers where work enters the road. For noise, involve the crew in the fix and tell neighbors what will change before doing it.

## Traps
- Intervene with the worker directly first, not with their manager or OSHA. Never tell an apprentice to ignore the trainer. Talk to the trainer.
- Uncomfortable PPE means stop and find PPE that fits, never PPE off. A new, untrained worker running a lift is in stage one.
- The safety professional at lunch or on vacation is no reason to wait. Any employee can stop unsafe work.
- After a near miss, do not reset the breaker and move on. Report it, share the lesson and assume cables are live. Equipment upkeep and risk assessments are proactive indicators. Citations and equipment failures are reactive.

- [ ] Consciously at risk
- [ ] Consciously safe
- [x] Unconsciously safe
> right: Safe by habit is stage four. Keep them there with positive reinforcement.
> wrong: A worker who is safe without thinking has made it a habit. That is the fourth stage.

2. Supervisor Joe sees apprentice Sam grinding without a face shield. What should Joe do to intervene?
- [ ] Talk to Sam's manager
- [x] Talk to Sam
- [ ] Make a report to OSHA
- [ ] Document the observation in an inspection
> right: Go to the worker first, calmly and right away.
> wrong: The first move is with the worker at risk, not around them.

3. A weak facade in Philadelphia dropped a concrete slab on a pedestrian. What could have prevented it?
- [ ] Better housekeeping
- [x] Netting and brick guards
- [ ] More lighting
- [ ] Lower speed limits
> right: Netting and guards stop debris before it reaches the street.
> wrong: Housekeeping, lighting and speed limits do nothing about falling concrete.

4. Alex tells Marie that the crew's gloves get slippery after a while. How should Marie proceed?
- [ ] Calculate the cost of replacing the gloves
- [ ] Tell Alex to take better care of his gloves
- [ ] Do nothing
- [x] Thank Alex and investigate the gloves
> right: A worker's comment is a proactive indicator. Act on it before someone gets hurt.
> wrong: The worker just handed you a hazard report. Thank him and look into it.
