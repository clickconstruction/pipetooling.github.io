## Why it matters
Construction has one of the highest suicide rates of any trade. Stress, trauma and substance misuse drive the shortcuts that kill. Supervisors spot changes and refer, they do not diagnose.

## Must know
- Mental health is emotional, psychological and social well-being. It matters as much as physical health.
- Site stressors: physical demands, traumatic events, long hours, broken sleep, deadlines, heat and cold, unclear expectations and an unsupportive culture.
- Role overload, too many responsibilities, leads to burnout. Unclear expectations lead to confusion and anxiety.
- Physical signs: muscle tension, stomach aches, headaches, high blood pressure. Mental and emotional: depression, anxiety, poor sleep, substance misuse, worry, irritability, hopelessness.
- Trauma reactions are normal at first. Acute stress disorder comes within **1 month**, PTSD, post-traumatic stress disorder, persists past **4 weeks**, and both are treatable.
- Healthy coping is therapy, loved ones and exercise. Unhealthy is drugs, risk-taking, heavy drinking and binge eating.
- Say substance misuse, not abuse. The person has a problem, the person is not the problem.
- Alcohol is a depressant, opioids like fentanyl are habit-forming painkillers, and stimulants like cocaine speed the nervous system.
- Misuse shows as a pattern over time. Lateness, missed shifts, distraction, weight change, red eyes, isolation, irritability and money trouble.
- Never accuse, ask. Watch for mood changes, isolation and talk of being a burden, and ask privately and without judgment.
- Stay with someone in crisis and get help. Point to the EAP, the employee assistance program, or a professional.
- Call or text **988** in a crisis and **911** for immediate danger. Asking about suicide does not plant the idea.

## Traps
- ASD, acute stress disorder, versus PTSD is purely timing. Within a month is ASD, past four weeks is PTSD.
- Sleep disruption and burnout sort as mental signs. Acid reflux and fight-or-flight sort as physical.
- The right opener names the change you saw and asks whether everything is okay. "Everything happens for a reason" and "keep your head up" are wrong.

## Numbers
- **988** the crisis line, call or text
- **911** immediate danger
- **1 month** acute stress disorder is within this
- **4 weeks** PTSD persists beyond this
- **5** tips: everyone can help, know the signs, ask and listen, stay with them, resources exist

## Check yourself
1. A friend on the crew is tired, isolating and jokes about being dead. What should you say?
- [ ] "Everything happens for a reason."
- [x] "I noticed you mentioned how tired you've been lately. Is everything okay?"
- [ ] "You've been acting weird. Anything you want to tell me?"
- [ ] "Life can be tough sometimes. Keep your head up."
> right: Name the change you saw and ask. Asking does not put the idea in their head.
> wrong: Name a specific change and ask if they are okay. Platitudes minimize, and calling someone weird judges.

2. Which long-term mental health condition is triggered by a terrifying event?
- [ ] Depression
- [ ] Acute stress disorder
- [x] PTSD
- [ ] Substance use disorder
> right: PTSD persists past four weeks after the event. Both it and ASD are treatable.
> wrong: Acute stress disorder is the short-term one, within the first month. Past four weeks it is the post-traumatic kind.

3. A coworker's behavior has changed and you are worried about him. What is the right way to support him?
- [ ] Suggest he ask for a raise
- [ ] Tell him to tough it out
- [x] Share your concerns with a supervisor
- [ ] Ignore it
> right: Supervisors do not diagnose, but they are the first step to professional help and resources.
> wrong: Toughing it out and ignoring it help no one. A supervisor can refer him to the EAP or a professional.

4. Which of these does NOT help after a traumatic event?
- [ ] Talking with coworkers
- [ ] Asking about resources for ASD and PTSD
- [x] Trying not to talk about it
- [ ] Talking with a counselor or therapist
> right: Avoiding it does not make it go away, and can make the symptoms worse.
> wrong: Talking, asking about resources and seeing a counselor all help. Bottling it up makes symptoms worse.
