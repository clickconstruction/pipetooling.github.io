## Why it matters
Power-line contact, overloading, shock loading and poor communication kill ground workers as well as operators. At Willow Island in 1978, **51** workers died when concrete poured too fast and too wet gave way. Steel erection kills about **35** people a year, and falls cause **34%** of all construction deaths.

## Must know
- Never stand under a load. Stay out of the swing radius, the circle the crane's parts sweep through, and behind the barricade.
- {{figure:swing-radius|The circle the crane sweeps and the spots the operator cannot see. Stay outside, in sight.}}
- A crane is inspected before first use each day and after any accident or shock load. A qualified person inspects it **annually**. A hydraulic leak is reported and repaired before use, never sealed or wiped up.
- Overloading is the most common cause of tip-overs. Load weight comes from shipping papers, markings or cut sheets, never a guess. Only trained riggers rig, and the choker hitch is the weakest.
- A certified signal person is required whenever the operator cannot see the load or the landing. A stop signal from anyone on site stops the lift. A flagger is the one who directs traffic.
- {{figure:hand-signals|Three signals from the crane's signal person, and the flagger's two. A stop from anyone stops the lift.}}
- A snagged load: stop and assess, never add power. Mark overhead lines and keep the clearance. A boom that touches a line kills the worker on the tag line too.
- Wet concrete burns skin. Wear chemical-resistant gloves, long sleeves and pants, eye protection and waterproof boots sealed at the top. Wash splashes at once and flush eyes for **15 minutes**.
- Cap every exposed rebar end the moment it is exposed. Signs, tape and training do not stop an impalement.
- Forms and shores stay until the employer confirms the concrete can carry its own weight plus construction loads. A mixer drum is a confined space, so lock out and wear a respirator before entry.
- Nobody but the installers stands under precast being lifted or tilted. Brace panels until they are permanently connected.
- A limited access zone goes up while a masonry wall is built. It runs the length of the wall and is as wide as the wall height plus **4 ft**. Only the masons enter until the wall is supported. Masonry walls over **8 ft** are braced until permanent supports are in.
- Falls are steel erection's greatest risk. Decking holes over **2 in** are covered. Harnesses, lifelines and anchor points are fall protection. Nets and guardrails are fall prevention.
- Loads fall from unsecured slings, worn rigging, unbalanced loads and rigging released before the member is bolted. Frames collapse when braces or guys come off too early. Know the day's lift plan before you move through another trade's area.

## Traps
- A signal person is certified and uses standard signals. A spotter only prevents collisions and needs no certification.
- Harnesses, lifelines and anchor points are fall protection. Nets and guardrails are fall prevention. The quiz keeps them apart.
- The quiz wants chemical-resistant gloves specifically for concrete burns. A 10-ft wall needs a 14-ft limited access zone.
- If you see a problem on the site, report it and wait until it is fixed. Walking off is marked wrong.

## Numbers
- **annually** the crane's inspection by a qualified person
- **15 minutes** flush concrete out of the eyes
- **4 ft** the limited access zone adds this to the wall height
- **8 ft** masonry walls taller than this are braced
- **2 in** decking holes over this are covered
- **51** dead at Willow Island
- **34%** of construction deaths are falls

## Check yourself
1. A crane boom touched overhead trolley lines. The current ran down the tag line and killed the worker holding it. What could have prevented it?
- [ ] A more careful operator
- [ ] Not letting the worker hold the tag line
- [x] Marked lines and a safe-distance rule
- [ ] Grounding the crane
> right: Know where the lines are and keep the clearance. Care alone is not enough.
> wrong: Gloves, grounding and care do not beat high voltage. The crew has to know where the lines are and stay clear.

2. Which of the following options is best for avoiding incidents related to exposed rebar?
- [ ] Posting warning signs
- [ ] Marking the ends with colored tape
- [ ] Training workers to work around it
- [x] Capping every rebar end
> right: A cap is the only thing that stops an impalement. Fit it the moment the bar is exposed.
> wrong: Signs and tape only warn, and training does not remove the hazard. Only a cover on the bar stops it.

3. A new worker on the ground did not know a lift was near him. An unbalanced beam with no tag line swung, and the collapse crushed him. What safe practice could have prevented it?
- [ ] Wearing a harness
- [x] Knowing the day's plan
- [ ] Checking the bolts
- [ ] Reporting it to the manager
> right: He was under a load he never knew about. The plan for the day tells you where the lifts are.
> wrong: No harness stops a falling beam. The worker needed to know the day's lift plan before he stepped under it.

4. Which of the following could lead to a structural collapse during steel erection?
- [ ] Painting the steel before bolting
- [ ] Using two cranes
- [x] Removing braces or guys too early
- [ ] Having multiple trades on site at once
> right: Braces and guys hold the frame until the bolts and welds do. Pull them early and it comes down.
> wrong: Paint, a second crane and other trades do not drop a frame. Taking the temporary bracing off early does.
