## Why it matters
Work-related musculoskeletal disorders affect millions of workers. OSHA has no ergonomics standard. It enforces through the General Duty Clause instead.

## Must know
- Ergonomics fits tools, equipment and tasks to people, not people to the job.
- A musculoskeletal disorder is an injury or pain in muscles, nerves, joints or tendons.
- The **four** main hazards are heavy lifting, awkward postures, repetitive motion and vibrating tools.
- Carrying cement is lifting. Overhead work is posture. Hammering nails is repetition. A jackhammer is vibration.
- Injuries include herniated discs, tendinitis, carpal tunnel syndrome, rotator cuff tears and hand-arm vibration syndrome. Carpal tunnel syndrome is nerve compression that makes the hands numb and weak.
- Eliminate with prefabricated parts. Substitute power tools for repetitive hand work. Engineer with lifting equipment, anti-vibration tools and adjustable scaffolds.
- {{figure:controls-ladder|The same ladder for strain. Prefabricate, then power tools, then a hoist, then breaks, then gloves.}}
- Administrative controls are job rotation, breaks, training and weight labels. PPE is knee pads, anti-vibration gloves, supportive boots and wrist braces.
- PPE reduces strain but does not remove the risk.
- Set precision work about **4 in above** the elbow. Light work sits just below the elbow. Heavy work sits **4 to 6 in below** it.
- {{figure:lifting-zone|Precision above the elbow, light just below it, heavy 4 to 6 in down. Lift from the green zone.}}
- Take a break about **every hour** during repetitive or vibrating work. Stretch daily.
- Report aches, numbness and tingling early, and suggest fixes. The employer identifies risks, trains, provides handling equipment and encourages reporting.
- A roofer twisted while carrying a ladder alone and lost **3 weeks**. The fix was a helper and no twisting, not a shorter ladder.

## Traps
- The engineering control for heavy lifting is a mechanical lift or hoist, not back belts, a procedure or training.
- Hundreds of overhead screws call for an impact driver, not a drill or a hand screwdriver.
- Procedures and schedules are administrative controls. Changes to equipment or the workplace are engineering controls.

## Numbers
- **4** main ergonomic hazards
- **4 in above** the elbow for precision work
- **4 to 6 in below** the elbow for heavy work
- **every hour** a break from repetitive or vibrating work
- **3 weeks** the roofer lost to one twist

## Check yourself
1. What is ergonomics?
- [ ] Making tools fit the job
- [x] Making tools fit people
- [ ] Making people fit the job
- [ ] Making people fit the tools
> right: The tool, the task and the workplace are shaped to the worker.
> wrong: Ergonomics shapes the work around the worker, never the worker around the work.

2. Which of the following is an example of an engineering control for lifting heavy objects?
- [ ] A written procedure for the lift
- [ ] Training in lifting technique
- [ ] Back belts
- [x] A mechanical lift or hoist
> right: Equipment that takes the load off the body is an engineering control.
> wrong: Procedures and training are administrative. Belts are PPE. Equipment that carries the load is engineering.

3. How can PPE help in ergonomics?
- [ ] By eliminating the risk
- [ ] By increasing the workload
- [x] By reducing physical strain
- [ ] By avoiding safe practices
> right: Knee pads, anti-vibration gloves and braces cut the strain. The risk is still there.
> wrong: PPE eases the body's load. It never removes the hazard and never replaces safe practice.

4. You must install hundreds of screws overhead for a ceiling grid. Which tool is safest and most helpful?
- [x] An impact driver
- [ ] A manual screwdriver
- [ ] A power drill
- [ ] A hammer drill
> right: It protects the wrist from jerking and cuts upper-arm strain.
> wrong: A hand screwdriver is slow, a drill twists the wrist and a hammer drill is not for screws.
