## Why it matters
About **1,000** fires a year hit structures under construction. They cause about **5** deaths, **60** injuries and almost **$400 million** in damage. More than half start between midnight and 6 a.m.

A soldering or brazing torch is hot work. Hot work is any work that makes a source of ignition. In the Taylor Bridge fire, hot work went on with no fire watch. The fire burned **23,500** acres and **61** homes.

## Must know
- Fire needs heat, fuel, oxygen and a chain reaction. Remove any one and the fire goes out.
- Flammables like gasoline, sawdust and propane ignite easily. Combustibles like timber, cardboard and rubber need more heat.
- Fire classes: **A** ordinary combustibles, **B** flammable liquids and gases, **C** energized electrical equipment, **D** metals, **K** cooking oils.
- {{figure:fire-classes|Five classes and what puts each out. Never water on electricity.}}
- An electrical fire takes dry chemical or CO2, never water. A flammable liquid takes CO2, dry chemical or foam.
- Keep every ignition source at least **50 ft** from flammable liquids. Keep the containers closed and grounded. Keep fuel gas at least **20 ft** from oxygen, or behind a 5-ft noncombustible barrier rated for half an hour.
- Cylinders stay upright and secured with valve caps on. No oil or grease on oxygen fittings. Never store LP gas inside a building.
- Inspect hoses and torches each shift. Light a torch with a friction lighter, never a match. Read the SDS, the safety data sheet, before using a new gas.
- Cylinders are compressed gas and can explode if heated or roughly handled. Hoist them only in a cradle, sling board or pallet, never with choker slings or magnets.
- Move the hot work to a safe area. If you cannot, move the hazards. If you cannot, shield them. Keep flammables at least **35 ft** away.
- A hot work permit is issued for each shift and operation. It requires a trained fire watch, who stays **30 minutes** after the work ends and can stop the work.
- Extinguishers: one per **3,000 sq ft**, within **100 ft** of travel from any point. Within **50 ft** wherever more than **5 gallons** of flammable liquid or **5 lb** of flammable gas is in use. A gauge not in the green takes it out of service.
- Use PASS from **6 to 8 ft** away: Pull, Aim at the base, Squeeze, Sweep. If the fire does not shrink right away, get out.
- {{figure:pass-steps|Four moves from 6 to 8 ft away. If the fire does not shrink at once, get out.}}

## Traps
- An electrical fire takes dry chemical, never water, foam or wet chemical.
- A faulty extinguisher is replaced. Do not take it to the fire department or use it anyway.
- A burning flammable-liquid container can explode. Get away and call the fire department. Do not cool it, cover it or vent it.
- Hot work in an unventilated space ignited fumes and killed a welder. The fix is ventilation, not fire-resistant clothing.

## Numbers
- **20 ft** fuel gas from oxygen
- **5 ft** the barrier that replaces the 20 ft
- **30 minutes** the fire watch stays after
- **35 ft** flammables from hot work
- **50 ft** no ignition source near flammable liquids
- **3,000 sq ft** one extinguisher per
- **100 ft** travel to an extinguisher
- **6 to 8 ft** PASS distance

## Check yourself
1. Which type of fire extinguisher should be used for electrical fires?
- [ ] Water
- [ ] Foam
- [x] Dry chemical
- [ ] Wet chemical
> right: Class C is energized equipment. Dry chemical or CO2, never water.
> wrong: A live electrical fire is Class C. Water, foam and wet chemical are for other classes.

2. What is required by a hot work permit for welding?
- [ ] Noise baffling
- [ ] A water hose
- [ ] Radiation shielding
- [x] A fire watch
> right: A trained fire watch covers the area and stays 30 minutes after the work ends.
> wrong: The permit calls for a trained person who watches for fire and stays after the work ends.

3. What can cause gas cylinders to explode?
- [x] Rough handling
- [ ] Extreme cold
- [ ] Direct light
- [ ] Low pressure
> right: The gas is compressed. Keep cylinders upright, capped and secured, and hoist them in a cradle.
> wrong: Cold, light and low pressure do not burst a cylinder. Mishandling or heating it does.

4. What is the correct way to use a fire extinguisher?
- [ ] Aim at the top of the flames
- [ ] Aim at the smoke
- [x] Aim at the base of the fire
- [ ] Aim at the middle of the fire
> right: Pull, aim at the base, squeeze and sweep, from 6 to 8 ft away.
> wrong: PASS stands for pull, aim, squeeze and sweep. The aim goes where the fuel burns, not at the flames.
