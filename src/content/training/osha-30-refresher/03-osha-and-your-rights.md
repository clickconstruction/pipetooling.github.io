## Why it matters
OSHA is the legal backbone of everything else in the course. Who it covers, what your employer owes you, what an inspection looks like and what a citation means.

## Must know
- The OSH Act of **1970** created OSHA. It covers all employees, whatever their citizenship or the size of the company.
- Not covered: the self-employed, family on a family farm, and workers under mining, aviation, nuclear or military rules. Federal agencies are monitored but not fined.
- Construction standards are **29 CFR 1926**. Rule **1926.501** requires guardrails, nets or fall arrest at unprotected edges **6 ft** or higher.
- The General Duty Clause applies when no standard exists. The hazard is recognized, could cause serious harm, can be corrected, and the employer left it.
- **Six** hazard categories: safety, chemical, biological, physical, ergonomic and work organization. Safety hazards, unsafe conditions and unsafe acts, are the most common.
- An inspection follows a severe injury, imminent danger, a complaint, or random targeting of high-hazard work.
- The steps: opening conference, a walkaround with a worker representative, private interviews, document review, closing conference.
- A citation is issued within **6 months**. It is posted at the work area for **3 working days** or until corrected, whichever is longer.
- Fix the violation by the deadline with proof, or contest it. There are **seven** categories, from De Minimis with no fine to Willful and Repeated.
- Willful means the employer knew and did not comply. Serious means likely death or serious harm.
- A hazard concern goes to your supervisor first, then OSHA, never social media. Your complaint is a valid reason for an inspection.

## Traps
- Loud noise is physical, asbestos chemical, a frayed cord a safety hazard, blood and poison ivy biological.
- You have no right to refuse any task you dislike. You do have a duty never to tough it out.
- Company size does not matter. A carpenter at a 9-person company is covered.

## Numbers
- **1970** the OSH Act
- **29 CFR 1926** the construction standards
- **6 ft** fall protection at an unprotected edge
- **6** hazard categories
- **6 months** to issue a citation
- **3 working days** a citation stays posted, or until fixed
- **7** categories of violation

## Check yourself
1. Which is an example of a physical hazard?
- [ ] Toxic chemicals
- [x] Loud noise
- [ ] Poor lighting
- [ ] Disease-causing organisms
> right: Noise, temperature, radiation and vibration are physical. They harm by contact or environment.
> wrong: Chemicals are chemical, organisms are biological. Noise is the one that harms through contact or the environment.

2. What is a step OSHA officers may take during an inspection?
- [ ] They fix the issues
- [x] They interview workers
- [ ] They cite workers and managers
- [ ] They issue fines
> right: Private interviews are one of the five steps, after the opening conference and the walkaround.
> wrong: Officers inspect, talk and document. Fixing is the employer's job, and citations come to the employer afterward.

3. Connor has been cutting concrete all week and is covered in dust. How should he handle it?
- [ ] Tell coworkers to ignore it
- [ ] Post on social media
- [x] Talk to his supervisor
- [ ] File with OSHA right away
> right: The supervisor gets the first chance to fix it. OSHA comes if the employer refuses.
> wrong: Never ignore a hazard or air it online. The employer gets the first chance, OSHA the next.

4. Which of these is NOT a worker right?
- [ ] Speak up without retaliation
- [ ] Request an inspection and speak to the inspector
- [ ] File a complaint anonymously
- [x] Refuse any task your employer asks you to do
> right: Refusal is protected only for a real danger of death or serious injury. Not for any task you dislike.
> wrong: Speaking up, asking for an inspection and filing anonymously are all rights. A blanket refusal is not.
