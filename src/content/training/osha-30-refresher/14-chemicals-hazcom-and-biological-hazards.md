## Why it matters
Health hazards hide in everyday tasks. A chronic exposure feels harmless until the damage is done.

## Must know
- Four routes of entry: inhalation, absorption through skin or eyes, ingestion from dirty hands, and injection from sharps.
- **Acute** exposure is short with immediate effects. **Chronic** exposure builds over years with delayed effects.
- Chemical forms: fumes from heated metal, vapors from solvents, dust from grinding, mists from spraying, and liquids. Ventilation is the most effective control for solvent vapors.
- Asbestos and lead are hazardous when disturbed by scraping, grinding, demolition or renovation. Stop, do not disturb it, and report. A qualified person tests and a certified contractor abates. Abatement is the name for that removal.
- Under HazCom, the hazard communication standard, manufacturers evaluate hazards and write labels and SDSs, the safety data sheets. Employers keep containers labeled and an SDS for every chemical on site **24/7**. They train you to read labels and SDSs and keep a written program.
- An SDS has **16** sections. A label carries the product name, the supplier, pictograms, a signal word and hazard and precautionary statements. The content is standardized, the layout is not.
- The nine pictograms include the flame, health hazard, skull and crossbones, corrosion, and flame over circle for an oxidizer. The signal word **Danger** is more severe than **Warning**.
- {{figure:hazcom-pictograms|The nine pictograms. Learn them by sight here and you will know them on a label.}}
- Biological hazards: droppings and carcasses, insects, poison ivy, oak and sumac, mold, and bloodborne pathogens. Have a wasp nest removed rather than working around it.
- Poison ivy: leaves of three, let it be. The rash comes in **12 to 48 hours**. Never burn it, and rubbing alcohol within **30 minutes** helps.
- {{figure:poison-ivy|Three leaflets on one stem is poison ivy. Five is a different plant.}}
- Mold is riskiest for people with asthma, weak immune systems and older workers. Hepatitis B survives about **a week** on a surface.
- Universal precautions: treat all blood and body fluids as infectious. Cover your own cuts, wear gloves for any first aid, and put sharps in a sharps container.

## Traps
- Employers train you to find and read labels and SDSs, not on every single chemical.
- Grinding makes dust, welding makes fumes, painting makes vapors and mists.
- Mold on site means respirators for the abatement crew, not for everyone.
- "I have worked around it for years and feel fine" is the chronic-exposure trap. A biohazard symbol means stop and identify the hazard, not walk away.

## Numbers
- **16** sections in an SDS
- **24/7** the SDS available on site
- **12 to 48 hours** poison ivy rash
- **30 minutes** rubbing alcohol after poison ivy
- **a week** hepatitis B lives on a surface
- **Danger** the stronger signal word

## Check yourself
1. What type of exposure involves being exposed to a hazard for a short time?
- [ ] Chronic exposure
- [x] Acute exposure
- [ ] Repeated exposure
- [ ] Long-term exposure
> right: Short exposure, effects right away. Chronic is the one that builds over years.
> wrong: A short exposure with immediate effects is acute. Chronic, repeated and long-term all build over years.

2. What is the most effective safety measure against solvent vapors?
- [ ] Train the workers
- [ ] Wear respirators
- [x] Use ventilation systems
- [ ] Post warning signs
> right: Ventilation is an engineering control. It works without anyone having to remember.
> wrong: Training, respirators and signs depend on people. Moving the vapors out of the air is the engineering control.

3. Which work task might create dust that is a chemical hazard?
- [x] Grinding
- [ ] Welding
- [ ] Soldering
- [ ] Painting
> right: Grinding makes dust. Welding and soldering make fumes, painting makes vapors and mists.
> wrong: Welding and soldering make fumes and painting makes vapors and mists. Dust comes from grinding.

4. Which is an example of a biological hazard?
- [ ] Welding fumes
- [ ] Vibration
- [ ] Lead dust
- [x] Poisonous plants
> right: Plants, animals, insects, mold and blood are the biological hazards.
> wrong: Welding fumes and lead dust are chemical hazards and vibration is physical. Plants are biological.
