## Why it matters
Struck-by incidents cause **10 to 15%** of construction deaths. Vehicles and heavy machinery account for about **75%** of them.

## Must know
- Four types: flying, falling, swinging and rolling objects. Walking into a wall is not struck-by. A piece of it falling on you is.
- The swing radius is the full **360 degrees** out to the furthest moving point. It includes loads, cabs, tracks and buckets. Red rope on delineators marks it.
- Never stand under a crane load. Stay in the operator's sight. Guide loads with tag lines.
- Powder-actuated and pneumatic tools need certification. Everyone nearby wears a hard hat and safety glasses. An uncertified nail gun killed an apprentice **27 ft** away.
- Cleaning with compressed air: under **30 psi**, a chip guard, a face shield and hearing protection. Never aim it at anyone.
- A hard hat has limits. A 1-lb wrench injures from **30 ft** and a 2-lb hammer from **15 ft**. A 5-lb brick from **8 ft** and a 10-lb drill from **4 ft**. A 20-lb sledge from **2 ft**, a 50-lb block from **1 ft**.
- Working overhead, tether your tools and use toeboards at least **3.5 in** high. Keep everything **2 ft** back from a trench edge. Cover holes **2 in** or wider.
- Bricks are a falling hazard while lifted, placed, braced and drying. Brace walls **8 ft** or higher. Watch the forecast: a **70 mph** gust toppled five drying walls.
- Vehicle blind spots: directly behind, under a raised bucket, the far side of a side-set cab. Reversing needs a back-up alarm and a spotter. Inspect each shift.
- On foot, use marked paths and barriers. Make eye contact before approaching a vehicle.
- The 20-20-20 rule: every **20 minutes**, spend **20 seconds** looking **20 ft** around you.

## Traps
- Barriers are engineering. Prohibiting stacked work and swing-radius training are administrative. A vest or a hard hat is PPE.
- Radioing the crane operator cannot steady a falling load. Mark the radius and stay out of it.
- The cab and the unloading area are inside the swing radius. Waiting there to save time is not safe.
- A single brick is struck-by. A whole wall collapsing is caught-in or between.

## Numbers
- **360 degrees** the swing radius
- **30 psi** compressed air for cleaning
- **3.5 in** a toeboard
- **2 ft** nothing this close to a trench edge
- **2 in** cover a hole this wide
- **8 ft** brace walls this high
- **20-20-20** every 20 minutes, 20 seconds, 20 ft around
- **75%** struck-by deaths from vehicles and machines

## Check yourself
1. Which of the following presents a struck-by hazard?
- [ ] A scaffold without a midrail
- [x] Walking under a hoisted load
- [ ] Carrying a ladder near overhead power lines
- [ ] Working outside in extreme cold
> right: A load can shift or drop. Nothing you wear stops it.
> wrong: The missing midrail is a fall, the ladder is electrical and the cold is a health hazard. Think of what could hit you.

2. What type of control involves installing barriers around hazards?
- [ ] Administrative control
- [ ] PPE
- [x] Engineering control
- [ ] Substitution
> right: A barrier keeps you away from the hazard without anyone remembering a rule.
> wrong: A barrier is a physical change to the site. Rules and training are administrative, and gear is PPE.

3. What is the swing radius of heavy equipment?
- [ ] The safety path the equipment drives on
- [x] The circle where its parts move or rotate
- [ ] The area under the equipment where materials are lifted
- [ ] The circumference where blind spots are eliminated
> right: The full 360 degrees to the furthest moving point, loads and cab included.
> wrong: It is not a path or a lifting area. It is everywhere a moving part, load or cab can reach.

4. What should you wear to protect yourself from smaller struck-by hazards like a dropped tool?
- [ ] A respirator
- [x] A hard hat
- [ ] A safety harness
- [ ] Ear protection
> right: A hard hat stops small objects. It does not stop a crane load.
> wrong: A respirator, a harness and earplugs guard other hazards. Think of what covers your head.
