## Why it matters
Falls are the leading cause of construction deaths. Nearly **60** serious falls happen every day, and more than one worker a day dies. The course says **100%** of fatal falls are preventable.

## Must know
- A fall hazard is anything that can make you lose your balance or your support. Falls happen on the same level or to a lower level.
- A fall is fast. You take **0.3 s** to realize it, **0.33 s** to react and **1 s** to fall **16 ft**. Once it starts you cannot stop it.
- Report every fall, even one with no injury.
- Same-level falls come from slippery surfaces, cords, uneven ground and gaps over **2 in**.
- Lower-level falls come from scaffolds, roofs, ladders, lifts, unprotected edges and openings over **30 by 18 in**.
- Fall protection is required at **6 ft**, whatever the employer's size, the roof slope or the kind of job.
- A personal fall arrest system, a PFAS, stops a fall in progress. A restraint or a passive system like a guardrail prevents the fall.
- A hole cover holds **twice** the load, is secured, is marked HOLE or COVER and covers the whole opening. Cover any opening before you leave it. Guard skylights.
- Platforms above **10 ft** need guardrails on all sides. A 20-ft ladder's base sits **5 ft** out from the wall.
- The employer trains you before any work at height, again on any change, and every year.
- The site keeps a fall protection plan with a rescue plan. Never copy the plan from the last jobsite.
- Suspension trauma is the harm from hanging in a harness. It starts within **3 to 5 minutes** and can kill within **30 minutes**.

## Traps
- Prevent before you arrest. A PFAS is never the first choice when a guardrail or a restraint can do the job.
- Hot weather, repetitive motion, confined spaces and cordless tools are not fall hazards. Cords in a walkway are.
- Barrels and caution tape do not guard an opening. Build a guardrail or a cover.
- A coworker is hanging in a harness. Call for help and stay with them. Never detach the system.

## Numbers
- **6 ft** fall protection
- **10 ft** guardrails on a platform
- **2 in** a gap you can trip in
- **30 by 18 in** an opening you can fall through
- **twice** the load a hole cover holds
- **5 ft** a 20-ft ladder's base from the wall
- **3 to 5 min** suspension trauma begins
- **30 min** it can kill
- **100%** of fatal falls preventable

## Check yourself
1. Which is a fall hazard?
- [ ] Extremely hot weather
- [x] Generator cords in the walkway
- [ ] Repetitive motions
- [ ] Cordless power tools
> right: A cord across the walkway trips you. That is a same-level fall.
> wrong: Heat, repetition and cordless tools do not take your footing. A cord across the walkway does.

2. A 20-ft ladder is set against a wall. How far out does the base sit?
- [ ] 1 foot
- [ ] 4 feet
- [x] 5 feet
- [ ] 8 feet
> right: One foot out for every 4 feet of ladder. 20 divided by 4.
> wrong: The rule is 1 foot out for every 4 feet of ladder. Divide the length by 4.

3. A coworker falls off an edge and is hanging in their harness. What do you do?
- [ ] Wait for them to climb back up
- [x] Call for help and stay with them
- [ ] Detach them from the fall arrest system
- [ ] Ignore it
> right: Suspension trauma starts in minutes. The rescue starts the moment you call.
> wrong: Hanging in a harness can kill within 30 minutes. Get the rescue started and never unhook them.

4. Which is a best practice for a fall protection plan?
- [ ] Copy the plan from the last jobsite
- [ ] Review it only with new hires
- [x] Revise it as work practices change
- [ ] Deviate from it to meet deadlines
> right: The plan is written for this site and it stays alive. It changes when the work changes.
> wrong: A plan belongs to this site and this work. Nobody copies one, skips it or shortcuts it.
