## Why it matters
The safety and health program is the frame everything else plugs into. Whether the rules get followed depends on what supervisors do every day, and on the crew's unwritten rules.

## Must know
- OSHA compliance is the bare minimum. A program fits any size of company, is customized, and is reviewed **annually**.
- **Seven** elements. Management leadership, worker participation, hazard identification, hazard prevention and control, education and training, evaluation and improvement, multi-employer coordination.
- Participation includes contractors and temps. Workers set goals, report hazards, investigate incidents and track progress, with no barriers or retaliation.
- Injury costs are tangible, like lost income, medical bills and legal costs, or intangible, like stress and family impact.
- Senior leaders set the vision and resources, middle managers the procedures and training, supervisors run the field. Supervisors answer for failures.
- Lead by example. Wear the PPE, take no shortcuts, do the JHA first, act on every report and let anyone stop work.
- Active listening is engage, understand, confirm. Full attention, eye contact, no phone, clarifying questions, then repeat it back.
- Teaching demonstrates, coaching guides, feedback evaluates the behavior and not the person. Feedback is specific, immediate, frequent and sincere, with no praise-cancelling "but".
- Safety climate is workers' perceptions and shifts like the weather. Safety culture is the stated values shown in practice, and group norms often beat policy.
- Three approaches. Basic reacts after an incident, Compliant ticks boxes to avoid fines, Excellent makes safety a shared core value.
- A safety observation is not punishment. Note the situation, the exact behavior and the consequence, and give safe and unsafe behavior equal time.
- The system runs on Plan, Do, Check, Act, with annual reviews. "We've always done it this way" is the enemy.

## Traps
- Leaders who act only after an incident or citation rate Basic, not Compliant.
- Public recognition is not a listening technique, and it embarrasses some people.
- Safety as a core value belongs to everyone, not top management, HR or consultants. Promotions and bonuses are not program benefits.
- "Let it go today, mention it tomorrow" cannot stop today's accident. Fatigue near machinery is answered with machine guards, not shift rotation.

## Numbers
- **7** program elements
- **annually** the program review
- **3** approaches, Basic, Compliant and Excellent
- **4** steps, Plan Do Check Act
- **2** masons killed in the aerial-lift case, with no program

## Check yourself
1. Which is a tangible cost of an incident?
- [x] Loss of income
- [ ] Long-term health issues
- [ ] Impact on the family
- [ ] Physical and emotional stress
> right: Tangible costs are the ones the company can count, like income, medical and legal bills.
> wrong: Health, family and stress are real but hard to measure, so they count as intangible.

2. What is a key element of an effective safety and health program?
- [ ] Equipment is handed out only on request
- [x] Workers help set goals and report hazards
- [ ] Safety is the HR department's job only
- [ ] Training is given once a year only
> right: Worker participation runs through every element, from goals to investigations.
> wrong: A program lives on worker participation. Equipment on request, HR alone or yearly training is not enough.

3. How should managers give feedback to workers?
- [ ] Criticize them at the huddle
- [ ] Wait for five mistakes
- [x] Provide timely and sincere feedback
- [ ] Only for a bad attitude
> right: Feedback is immediate, specific, frequent and sincere, about the behavior and not the person.
> wrong: Feedback comes right away and sincerely, for safe and unsafe behavior alike, not saved up or aimed at attitude.

4. A messy laydown yard is being fixed with Plan, Do, Check, Act. Which step is the Check?
- [ ] Collect data and schedule the clean up
- [ ] Rearrange the yard so the forklift has room
- [x] Audit the new yard with the workers
- [ ] Formalize the new housekeeping procedure
> right: Check measures whether the change worked, with the people who use the yard.
> wrong: Collecting data is Plan, rearranging is Do, and writing the procedure is Act.
