## Why it matters
A cave-in is not the only thing that kills in a trench. Bad air, water and a slow rescue kill too. These are the field calls the course drills hardest.

## Must know
- A trench deeper than **4 ft** needs a ladder, stair or ramp within **25 ft** of every worker. One ladder in the middle of a 60-ft trench fails the rule.
- {{figure:trench-section|A way out within 25 ft of everyone, the box 18 in proud, spoil 2 ft back.}}
- Secure the ladder and extend it **3 ft** above the landing.
- {{figure:ladder-angle|Tied at the top and bottom, 3 ft past the landing, so you step off and not over.}}
- Under 4 ft a protective system is usually unnecessary. Water, unstable soil or loads at the edge change that.
- Warning signs: bulging, cracking, boiling, heaving, slumping and sliding. On any sign, or seeping water, stop work and reinforce the walls before anyone goes back in.
- {{figure:cave-in-signs|Six ways a wall says it is about to go. On any one, stop work and get out.}}
- Bad air collects at the bottom and can be invisible and odorless. Engine exhaust, contaminated soil, a cut gas or sewer line, chemicals, welding fumes and low oxygen all count.
- Test the air in any trench deeper than 4 ft where a hazardous atmosphere could exist. Use a calibrated **four-gas meter**: oxygen, flammable gas, carbon monoxide and hydrogen sulfide. Test before entry and re-test.
- The competent person's atmospheric permit lasts **one day**. It is cancelled when conditions turn unsafe. A gas smell means test, evacuate and re-enter only on a safe reading and a new permit.
- Carbon monoxide shows as dizziness and slurred speech. Get out and tell the crew.
- Remove water before entry. Water drowns, chills and erodes the walls, and it can hold poison gas. The competent person watches the pumping.
- Call **811** two to three business days before digging. It is a pre-dig request, not a response to a gas smell.
- The rescue plan is site-specific and the employer owns it. It names who is in charge and the rescue team. It lists ladders every 25 ft and trench boxes against a second collapse. It sets posted contacts, radios and code words.
- In a cave-in, call **911**, give first aid and wait for the responders. Never drive an injured worker yourself. The safest way out is a ladder.

## Traps
- A hazardous atmosphere is detected: the answer is stop the work and put controls in place, not just leave.
- Monitor oxygen, flammability and carbon monoxide. The work itself does not produce carbon dioxide.
- A gas smell means a test and an evacuation. Calling 811 is for before you dig.

## Numbers
- **4 ft** a way out and air testing
- **25 ft** the furthest a way out can be from any worker
- **3 ft** the ladder above the landing
- **1 day** the atmospheric permit
- **811** two to three business days before digging
- **911** the call in a cave-in
- **4 gases** oxygen, flammable gas, carbon monoxide and hydrogen sulfide

## Check yourself
1. What must be done if a hazardous atmosphere is detected at an excavation site?
- [ ] Continue the work with caution
- [ ] Leave the trench immediately
- [x] Stop and add safety measures
- [ ] Call for medical help
> right: Nobody works in bad air. Test, ventilate and re-permit before anyone goes back.
> wrong: Getting out alone is not the whole answer. The work stops and controls go in before anyone returns.

2. When is it safe for workers to re-enter an excavation after natural gas is detected?
- [ ] After waiting an hour
- [ ] When the meter stops alarming
- [x] Safe levels and a new permit
- [ ] When the fire department says so
> right: The competent person cancels the old permit at the evacuation. A new one comes with a safe reading.
> wrong: Time and a quiet meter prove nothing. The air has to test safe and the competent person has to sign again.

3. What is the safest method to exit an excavation in a cave-in?
- [x] Climbing out using ladders
- [ ] Waiting for rescuers
- [ ] Digging through the debris
- [ ] Crawling along the opposite wall
> right: That is why a ladder sits within 25 ft of every worker.
> wrong: Every other route adds risk. The way out is the ladder, which is why one is never more than 25 ft away.

4. A worker preparing a utility trench with water at the bottom got dizzy, passed out and died. What caused the death?
- [ ] A medical condition
- [ ] Drowning in the trench water
- [ ] A freak accident
- [x] Poison gas in the water
> right: Hydrogen sulfide rose from the water. Pump a trench dry before anyone goes in.
> wrong: The water was the source of the gas. Remove water before entry and test the air.
