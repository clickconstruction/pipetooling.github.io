## Why it matters
About **200** workers die each year in highway and street construction, and more than **5,000** are injured. Vehicles and equipment in the work zone cause over half of those deaths. Forklifts cause about **70** deaths and more than **15,000** injuries a year.

## Must know
- A motor vehicle moves people or goods on site under its own power. Inspect it at the start of each shift and fix defects before use.
- Report a malfunction or a broken sign to the employer. Never fix it, remove it or drive around it.
- Active features prevent crashes: brakes, lights, wipers, horn and back-up alarm. Passive features protect the people inside: seat belts, a cab shield, secured tools and fenders.
- Flaggers direct traffic with hand signals. Keep vehicle and pedestrian routes apart. Where there are no signs, yield and go slowly. An obstructed rear view needs a back-up alarm or a spotter.
- Red signs mean danger, the most severe hazard. Orange or yellow means warning. Yellow and black means caution. Blue is a safety notice.
- {{figure:sign-colours|Red is the worst. Orange or yellow could hurt you. Yellow and black says watch it. Blue is information.}}
- Training is free before anyone operates. A refresher and evaluation come **every 3 years**, and at once after unsafe operation or a near miss.
- A PIT, a powered industrial truck, carries, pushes, pulls, lifts or stacks material. Forklifts, pallet jacks and telehandlers are PITs, in **7** classes.
- The **load center** is the distance from the fork backrest to the load's center of gravity. The standard rating uses **24 in**. A load out at the fork tips can tip the truck.
- {{figure:forklift-load-center|The rating assumes the load's weight sits 24 in from the backrest. Further out, the truck tips forward.}}
- Keep the load low. An empty truck's center of gravity is higher than a loaded one's. Do not speed up after dropping a load.
- Inspect the forklift before each shift: lights, brakes, steering, horn, seat belt, tires and fuel or battery. Check the load and the pallet too. A failed inspection means report it and pull the key.
- If the load blocks your view, drive in reverse or use a spotter. Sound the horn at intersections. Carry people only on a purpose-built platform.
- Check LPG hoses with soapy water. Bubbles mean out of service and report it. Propane is about **-40°F**, so wear gloves. Only trained workers in PPE handle batteries.

## Traps
- A jammed seat belt means tell the manager and take the machine out of service. Level ground is no substitute.
- Raising a load does not fix a blind spot. It creates one.
- Leak checks use soapy water, not listening, smelling or touching.
- Mud on site is prevented with drainage, not tarps or plywood.

## Numbers
- **24 in** the standard load center
- **every 3 years** operator refresher and evaluation
- **7** classes of powered industrial truck
- **70** forklift deaths a year
- **200** road-work deaths a year
- **-40°F** propane, wear gloves

## Check yourself
1. What should be done to ensure motor vehicles are safe to operate?
- [x] Perform a pre-trip inspection
- [ ] Do maintenance after an accident
- [ ] Check the brakes at high speed
- [ ] Wait for a warning light
> right: Every vehicle is inspected at the start of each shift, before it moves.
> wrong: A vehicle is checked before each shift, not after something goes wrong.

2. Who uses hand signals to guide motor vehicle operators around hazards and through lane closures?
- [ ] Supervisors
- [ ] Operators
- [ ] Carriers
- [x] Flaggers
> right: Flaggers direct the traffic and the crews follow them.
> wrong: One role on site directs traffic by hand signal. It is not the supervisor or the driver.

3. How should hoses and connectors on a PIT be checked for leaks?
- [ ] Listen for a hiss
- [x] Soapy water, then look for bubbles
- [ ] Smell for gas
- [ ] Run your fingers along the hose
> right: Bubbles mean a leak. The truck comes out of service and you report it.
> wrong: Your ears, nose and fingers miss leaks. A liquid on the fitting shows them.

4. How should PIT batteries be handled?
- [ ] By vendors only
- [ ] By any operator who is authorized
- [x] By trained workers wearing PPE
- [ ] By supervisors only
> right: Training plus gloves, goggles, ventilation and an eyewash nearby.
> wrong: It is not about rank or who sells the battery. It is about training and protective gear.
