## Why it matters
Confined spaces kill more than **100** workers a year. The hazards are usually invisible. Every death in the course's confined-space cases involved no entry program or an untrained rescuer.

## Must know
- A confined space is big enough to enter and has limited ways in or out. It is not designed for continuous occupancy. Sewers, tanks, pipelines, manholes and culverts are examples. A trench is covered by the excavation rules instead.
- A permit-required confined space, a PRCS, adds at least one serious hazard. That can be a hazardous atmosphere, engulfment, a shape that narrows and traps, or another serious hazard.
- Entry happens as soon as any part of the body breaks the plane of the opening. Reaching in for a tool is an entry.
- The entrant does the work, reports symptoms and leaves on any order, symptom or alarm.
- The attendant stays outside with no other duties. They count entrants, stay in contact, monitor the air, order evacuation, keep others out and call rescue.
- The entry supervisor is a qualified person. They confirm acceptable conditions, sign and cancel the permit, and verify rescue is available.
- Oxygen from **19.5% to 23.5%** is acceptable. Normal air is about **21%**. Below 19.5% is oxygen-deficient and immediately dangerous. Flammable gas above **10% of the lower explosive limit** is unsafe.
- {{figure:oxygen-gauge|The safe band is 19.5 to 23.5 percent. Test oxygen first, before flammables and toxics.}}
- Test with a calibrated meter, oxygen first, then flammables, then toxics. Monitor continuously during entry and record readings on the permit.
- Rescue order: the entrant self-evacuates, then the attendant helps with a hoist or tripod, then the rescue service. Coworkers never go in. An unconscious entrant is pulled out by winch.
- {{figure:confined-space-roles|The entrant works, the attendant watches from outside, the supervisor signs. Rescue goes self, then tripod, then the service.}}
- On-site rescue teams practice at least every **12 months**. An off-site service like the fire department is arranged in advance and walks the site.
- Controls: empty the silo, isolate with lockout, blanking or capping, ventilate, then the permit, then PPE. An air-purifying respirator is useless in low oxygen, so use supplied air.
- The permit lists the space, purpose, date and duration, hazards, controls, names, test results, equipment and rescue. It is cancelled when the work is done and kept at least **1 year**.

## Traps
- The attendant, not the supervisor, talks with the entrants. The attendant takes on no other duty, like traffic control.
- In a non-permit space with carbon monoxide rising, evacuate and reevaluate. There is no permit to cancel.
- An unconscious entrant is retrieved by winch. Coworkers do not go in after them.
- The permit does not list training dates.

## Numbers
- **19.5% to 23.5%** acceptable oxygen
- **21%** normal oxygen
- **10% of the LEL** flammable gas is unsafe
- **1 year** keep the permit
- **12 months** rescue team practice
- **100** deaths a year
- **continuously** air monitoring during entry

## Check yourself
1. Who is responsible for determining if acceptable entry conditions are present in a PRCS?
- [ ] The entrant
- [ ] The attendant
- [x] The entry supervisor
- [ ] The fire marshal
> right: The entry supervisor confirms the conditions, signs the permit and verifies rescue is ready.
> wrong: The entrant works and the attendant watches. The one who signs the permit decides the conditions.

2. How often should air monitoring be done during a PRCS entry?
- [x] Continuously
- [ ] Only before entry
- [ ] Once an hour
- [ ] After the work
> right: The air can change while you are inside. The meter runs the whole time and the permit records it.
> wrong: A test before entry is not enough. The air is watched the whole time anyone is inside.

3. What should be done if a worker falls unconscious in a confined space?
- [ ] Send coworkers in after them
- [ ] Remove the lockouts
- [x] Pull them out with a winch
- [ ] Wait for emergency services to arrive
> right: The retrieval line and winch bring them out without a second person going in.
> wrong: Going in after them makes a second victim. The attendant uses the retrieval equipment from outside.

4. What should the attendant do if an unauthorized person tries to enter a PRCS?
- [ ] Ask whether they have been trained
- [ ] Avoid a confrontation with them
- [ ] Add their name to the entry permit
- [x] Block them and tell the entry supervisor
> right: Keeping unauthorized people out is one of the attendant's duties.
> wrong: The attendant keeps everyone not on the permit out and reports it. No questions, no additions.
