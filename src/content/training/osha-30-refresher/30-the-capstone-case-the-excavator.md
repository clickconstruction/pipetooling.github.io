## Why it matters
The course ends on one case, built on two NIOSH reports. Those are the government's write-ups of real deaths on the job. A new residential site starts with trenching for water and sewer lines. You, an excavator operator and a few others are on site. Four decisions decide whether everyone goes home.

## Must know
- The setup: a new residential site, trenching for water and sewer lines. You, an excavator operator and a few others are on site. The case walks **four** decisions.
- {{figure:excavator-site-map|The four decisions on one site. Each keeps a person out of the circle or off an unchecked machine.}}
- Decision **1**: an untrained new hire volunteers to spot for the excavator. Stop the work and escalate the need for a trained spotter. Any worker can stop unsafe work.
- Checking the weather fixes nothing. Swapping in a more experienced but still untrained worker fixes nothing either.
- Decision **2**: the employer names you competent person. Your daily inspection finds the excavator and its attachments uninspected and no manual on site. The soil is Type B, and the boom carries a quick coupler.
- A quick coupler is a hydraulic device at the end of the boom for fast bucket changes. Inspect the coupler and the excavator, and get both manuals to the site.
- Decision **3**: are the manufacturer's maintenance and inspection requirements vital to a complete inspection? Yes. Operators must run the manufacturer's connection test before using an attachment.
- Decision **4**: the boom will also lower pipe into the trench. That puts workers near the swing radius and under a raised load. There are no tag lines on the pipe.
- Do a JHA, the job hazard analysis. Put tag lines on the pipe and stand outside the swing radius. Never get in the trench, guide the pipe by hand or have the operator switch positions.
- What happens: the operator switches from hoisting back to the bucket. The bucket drops off the quick coupler and crashes to the ground. Standing outside the swing radius is what saves you.
- What really happened: nobody inspected the excavator or the coupler. No competent person was named, so nobody owned the trench, the inspections or the JHA. Nothing kept workers out of the swing radius or from under the load.
- Nobody was trained on the coupler and the manual was not on site. A worker standing under the live load was crushed and killed.
- The course's closing checklist. Do a JHA before work. Lead by example. Inspect often and keep records. Report hazards at once. Keep the site clean. Wear the right PPE. Involve workers, investigate every incident and near miss, and fix what you find. Follow every policy yourself. Every worker deserves to go home safe.

## Traps
- Checking the weather, or swapping in a more experienced but still untrained worker, fixes nothing. Stop the work and get a trained spotter.
- An uninspected coupler with no manual on site is a hazard, not a detail. Inspect it and get the manuals to the site.
- Never get in the trench, guide the pipe by hand or have the operator switch positions. Tag lines and distance are the answer.
- Under a raised load is never a place to stand. In the real case the worker was under the live load when the coupler failed.

## Numbers
- **4** decisions in the case
- **2** NIOSH reports behind it
- **1** competent person, named by the employer
- **daily** the competent person's trench inspection
- **0** tag lines on the pipe, the gap you close

## Check yourself
1. An untrained new hire volunteers to spot for the excavator. What should you do?
- [ ] Let the untrained new hire spot
- [ ] Check the weather first
- [x] Stop work and get a trained spotter
- [ ] Use a more experienced but untrained worker
> right: Any worker can stop unsafe work, and an untrained spotter must not work.
> wrong: Weather and experience are not training. Stop the work and get someone trained.

2. How can workers avoid getting caught in a trench collapse?
- [ ] Enter before the protection is in
- [x] Wait to enter until protections are in place and the trench is inspected
- [ ] Ignore the safety signs
- [ ] Work alone
> right: Protection first, then the competent person's inspection, then you. In the real case nobody owned either.
> wrong: Nobody enters an unprotected or uninspected trench. In the real case no competent person was named to inspect it.

3. What is the swing radius?
- [ ] The distance between two objects
- [x] The area a rotating object occupies
- [ ] The height of a stack
- [ ] The length of a crane
> right: In the case, standing outside the swing radius is what saves you.
> wrong: It is the circle around the machine where parts move or rotate. Stay out of it.

4. There are no tag lines on the pipe the boom will lower into the trench. What do you do?
- [x] Put tag lines on the pipe
- [ ] Get in the trench to receive it
- [ ] Guide the pipe by hand
- [ ] Ask the operator to switch positions
> right: Tag lines let you guide the pipe from outside the swing radius.
> wrong: Nobody goes in the trench or under the load. Guide it from a distance with a rope.
