## Why it matters
PPE is the last line of defense. It reduces the effect of a hazard and never removes it. It only works if it is the right type, fits, and is on before the exposure starts.

## Must know
- Hard hats meet **ANSI** standards. The gap between the shell and your head lets the suspension absorb an impact.
- **Type 1** protects the top of the head. **Type 2** protects the top and the sides.
- **Class C** gives no electrical protection. **Class G** is rated to **2,200 V** and **Class E** to **20,000 V**. Switch hats when the hazards change.
- Inspect a hard hat daily. Scratches, dents, cracks, paint, sunlight and stickers all weaken it. Replace the shell after any impact.
- Replace the shell after **2 years** of heavy use, or **5 years** after the date on the date wheel. Replace the suspension every **12 months**, every **6** with heavy use.
- Rubber or plastic gloves stop chemicals and electricity, fabric does not. Leather handles cuts and moderate heat and is flame-resistant. Metal mesh stops cuts and punctures only.
- Chemical ratings on gloves run **VG, G, F, P**, from very good to poor. Discard any damaged glove.
- Safety glasses stop front-facing particles. Goggles seal against splashes and vapors. A face shield goes over glasses or goggles, never alone.
- Prescription glasses are not safety glasses. Welding lenses are rated by shade number, **2 to 14**, against radiant light.
- Wear protective footwear wherever foot hazards exist, including slippery surfaces. Metatarsal guards protect the top of the foot.
- Work over water needs a PFD, a life jacket, for each worker. Ring buoys go every **200 ft** with **90 ft** of line, and a rescue lifeboat stays available. Inspect PFDs before and after every use.
- The employer assesses the hazards, picks the PPE, provides it free and trains you. You inspect it daily, maintain it, use it and ask when unsure.

## Traps
- Unloading bricks with no electrical hazard calls for Class C. Class G is the tempting wrong answer.
- Framing where wiring may be hidden is Class G. Roofing under power lines is Class E.
- Chop-sawing rebar needs safety glasses plus a face shield. Splashing epoxy needs goggles plus a face shield.
- Six months is the suspension interval under heavy use, never the shell interval. Chemicals call for rated rubber gloves, and leather is the flame-resistant choice.

## Numbers
- **2,200 V** Class G hard hat
- **20,000 V** Class E hard hat
- **2 years** the shell under heavy use
- **5 years** the shell from its manufacture date
- **12 months** the suspension
- **2 to 14** welding shade numbers
- **200 ft** between ring buoys
- **90 ft** of line on each buoy

## Check yourself
1. Leigh is running late and puts on her PPE without inspecting it. Is that okay?
- [ ] Yes, as long as she does not do it twice
- [ ] Yes, she can check it on her break
- [ ] Yes, checking PPE is her boss's job
- [x] No, she always checks PPE before putting it on
> right: The employer provides the PPE. You check it every day, before it goes on.
> wrong: PPE is checked daily by the worker who wears it, before it goes on.

2. Which glove material is flame-resistant?
- [ ] Fabric gloves
- [ ] Rubber gloves
- [x] Leather gloves
- [ ] Metal mesh gloves
> right: Leather handles cuts, moderate heat and flame.
> wrong: Leather resists flame and short heat. Rubber is for chemicals, mesh for cuts.

3. You are cutting wood on a jigsaw. Which eye protection do you need?
- [x] Safety glasses
- [ ] Safety goggles
- [ ] A face shield
- [ ] A welding mask
> right: A jigsaw throws sawdust only, a front-facing particle. Glasses are enough.
> wrong: Sawdust is a front-facing particle. Goggles are for splashes, a shield for flying metal, a mask for welding.

4. A hard hat shell looks perfect. How long after its manufacture date is it replaced anyway?
- [ ] 6 months
- [x] 5 years
- [ ] 12 months
- [ ] 2 years
> right: The date wheel under the brim sets the clock. Five years and the shell goes.
> wrong: The shell ages out five years from the date wheel. Two years is the heavy-use rule, 12 months the suspension.
