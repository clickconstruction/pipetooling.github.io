## Why it matters
Every job has the same shape. Plan it, inspect the gear, lock it out, wear the PPE, report what goes wrong. Nearly every death in the course came from rushing, a shortcut or a skipped step.

## Must know
- Do a JHA, the job hazard analysis, before any task. List the steps, the hazards in each and the controls.
- Do the JHA with the workers and judge the steps, not the people. Revise it on any change, like weather, a new chemical or a new site.
- Permits cover the highest-risk work. A hot work permit per shift, a confined-space permit kept **1 year**, a trench air permit for **one day**.
- Inspect before use. Tools and cords, harnesses, ladders, scaffolds each shift, cranes daily, trenches daily and after rain.
- Anything defective is tagged, pulled from service and reported. Never tape it, seal it or just use it carefully.
- Report every injury, unsafe condition and near miss at once, to the manager first. Investigations find facts, not fault, because blame stops reporting.
- Lockout and tagout covers every energy type. Your own lock, one key, your name on the tag, then test before you work.
- Only the person who put a lock on removes it. Switched-off or unplugged equipment can still shock or move.
- The employer picks and pays for PPE, you inspect it daily and wear it. Fall protection starts at **6 ft**, **10 ft** on scaffolds, with anchors rated **5,000 lb** per worker.
- Clean as you go, with materials **6 ft** from edges and **2 ft** from a trench edge. Cover holes over **2 in** and never dry sweep silica.
- Call **811** before any digging, the employer's job. You verify the marks and use hand tools within **3 ft** of a flag.
- Crisis line **988**, call or text, and **911** for immediate danger. Asking someone does not plant the idea.

## Traps
- Being behind schedule is never a reason. Nearly every fatality in the course involved rushing, a shortcut or a skipped step.
- "They told me it's locked out" is not enough. If you were not part of the lockout, the lock is not yours.
- If you cannot see the operator, the operator cannot see you. Make eye contact and get their okay before you approach, and stay out of the swing radius.
- A near miss is when someone almost got hurt. Report it like an injury, and never assume someone else will.

## Numbers
- **1 year** confined-space permits are kept
- **one day** a trench air permit is good for
- **6 ft** fall protection in construction
- **10 ft** fall protection on scaffolds
- **5,000 lb** the anchor, per worker
- **2 ft** materials back from a trench edge
- **2 in** cover any hole over this
- **811** before any digging
- **3 ft** hand tools only, near a flag
- **988** the crisis line, call or text

## Check yourself
1. What is required by a Hot Work Permit for welding?
- [ ] Noise baffling
- [ ] A water hose
- [x] A fire watch
- [ ] Radiation shielding
> right: The permit names a trained fire watch, who stays 30 minutes after the work ends.
> wrong: The permit is about fire. It calls for a trained person watching for one, not for noise or radiation gear.

2. What should a worker do before using a ladder?
- [x] Inspect it for damage and stability
- [ ] Step on the top rung
- [ ] Set it on a sloped or uneven surface
- [ ] Remove the tags and labels
> right: Inspect before use, every time. A defective ladder is tagged and pulled from service.
> wrong: Look it over for damage first. The top rung, a slope and missing labels are all hazards.

3. What should you wear to protect yourself from smaller struck-by hazards like a dropped tool?
- [ ] Respirator
- [ ] Safety harness
- [ ] Ear protection
- [x] Hard hat
> right: A hard hat stops small objects. It does not stop a crane load.
> wrong: A dropped tool comes from above and hits the head. Respirators, harnesses and earplugs guard other things.

4. Before digging, which of these is the worker's job and not the employer's?
- [ ] Call 811 to mark the lines
- [x] Verify where the utilities are marked
- [ ] Get maps of underground services
- [ ] Provide detection equipment
> right: The employer calls 811 and gets the maps. You check the marks and know where they are.
> wrong: Calling 811, the maps and the detectors are the employer's. You confirm the marks before you break ground.
