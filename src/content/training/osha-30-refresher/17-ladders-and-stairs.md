## Why it matters
About **160** workers die in ladder falls each year. A fall from a ladder is dangerous from any height. A portable ladder is the most hazardous way up, so ask first whether the job really needs one.

## Must know
- Stairways with **four or more risers** need a handrail on at least one side. Keep the rise and run the same on every step.
- Inspect stairs at least daily and keep them clear.
- Fixed ladders over **24 ft** need fall protection: a cage, a well or a ladder safety system.
- Keep **three points of contact**. Never carry tools in your hands while climbing.
- {{figure:three-points|Two hands and a foot, or two feet and a hand. The tool goes up on a belt or a rope.}}
- Fiberglass stays non-conductive when wet and is the choice near electricity. Aluminum conducts. Wood conducts once it is wet.
- Straight ladders stop at **30 ft** and step ladders at **20 ft**. A platform ladder carries one person. A double-cleated ladder lets two climb at once.
- Secure an extension ladder at the top and the bottom. Extend it **3 ft** above the landing at a **4:1** angle. Never stand on the top three rungs.
- {{figure:ladder-angle|One foot out for every four feet up, tied at both ends, 3 ft past the landing.}}
- Open a step ladder fully and lock the spreaders. Never stand on the top cap. Never use one to reach a roof.
- Inspect before each use. Take a defective ladder out of service. Never use a ladder as a scaffold or work platform.
- A job-made ladder has side rails **16 to 20 in** apart and rungs **12 in** on center. It runs no more than **24 ft**.
- Stay under the rated capacity. Never use a ladder whose labels are missing or damaged.
- The employer supplies safe ladders, training and a competent person. You inspect, use the ladder correctly and report defects.

## Traps
- Workers are responsible for using a ladder safely. The yearly inspection is the employer's job. The look before each use is yours.
- A wet wooden ladder conducts. Only fiberglass stays non-conductive when wet.
- In the wet-rung fall case the common hazard is the ladder's condition, not training or fatigue.

## Numbers
- **4 risers** a handrail on at least one side
- **24 ft** fall protection on a fixed ladder
- **3 ft** above the landing
- **4:1** the ladder angle
- **30 ft** the longest straight ladder
- **20 ft** the longest step ladder
- **3 points** of contact
- **12 in** between job-made rungs

## Check yourself
1. If stairways have four or more risers, what safety feature is required?
- [ ] Handrails on both sides of the stair
- [x] Handrails on at least one side
- [ ] Painted risers
- [ ] No feature is required
> right: One handrail is the minimum. Keep the rise and run the same too.
> wrong: Four risers is the line. Past it, one side of the stair gets a handrail.

2. What should a worker do before using a ladder?
- [ ] Step on the top rung
- [ ] Set it on a sloped or uneven surface
- [ ] Remove the tags and labels
- [x] Inspect it for damage and stability
> right: The look before each use is yours. A defective ladder comes out of service.
> wrong: Look the ladder over first. Damage or a wobble means it comes out of service.

3. Who is responsible for using a ladder safely?
- [ ] Employers
- [ ] Safety officers
- [x] Workers
- [ ] Supervisors
> right: The employer supplies safe ladders and training. Using one safely is on you.
> wrong: The employer provides and trains. The person on the rungs is the one who uses it safely.

4. What should you avoid when setting up a ladder?
- [ ] A stable, level surface
- [ ] Stabilizers
- [ ] Securing the ladder
- [x] Slippery surfaces
> right: A slick footing lets the base kick out. Set it on firm, level ground.
> wrong: Stable ground, stabilizers and tie-offs all help. A slick footing is the one to avoid.
