## Why it matters
Moving water has weight. A valve that shuts fast stops it dead, and the pipe takes the hit. The bang is the sound, and the loosened joints are the cost.

## Must know
- Moving water stopped dead by a fast valve hits the pipe. That bang is water hammer.
- {{figure:water-hammer-strip|A valve shutting fast stops moving water dead. The pipe takes the hit. An arrestor's air cushion takes it instead.}}
- Fast valves start it. A washing machine, a dishwasher and an ice maker shut with a solenoid in a blink.
- The spike can be many times the line pressure. Over time it loosens joints and breaks fittings.
- A water hammer arrestor is a sealed chamber with a cushion of air behind a piston. The water hits the cushion instead of the pipe.
- The arrestor goes close to the fast valve, on the hot and the cold. The washer's go at its hose bibs.
- Old houses used an air chamber, a capped stub of pipe above the valve. It fills with water in months and stops working.
- A loose pipe bangs even without a spike. Strap the pipe so it cannot move.
- High house pressure makes hammer worse. Check the PRV before you add arrestors.

## Traps
- A bang when the washer stops is hammer. A bang when the heater runs is the tank, a different fix.
- An air chamber is not an arrestor. It waterlogs and the bang comes back.
- Strapping stops the noise of a loose pipe, not the spike. The arrestor stops the spike.

## Numbers
- **a fast valve** starts it, a washer or a dishwasher
- **an arrestor** near that valve
- **an air chamber** waterlogs in months
- **the PRV** first when the whole house bangs

## Check yourself
1. What is water hammer?
- [ ] Air in the pipes
- [x] Moving water stopped dead by a fast valve, hitting the pipe
- [ ] The water heater boiling
- [ ] A loose faucet
> right: The water's weight has to stop somewhere. The pipe is where.
> wrong: Hammer is the hit of moving water stopped dead. A fast valve starts it.

2. Which appliance most often causes it?
- [ ] The water heater
- [x] A washing machine, with its solenoid valves
- [ ] The toilet
- [ ] The dishwasher's drain
> right: The washer's valves shut in a blink, many times a load.
> wrong: The washing machine's fast solenoid valves are the usual cause.

3. What does an arrestor do?
- [ ] Slows the valve
- [x] Gives the water an air cushion to hit instead of the pipe
- [ ] Lowers the house pressure
- [ ] Straps the pipe
> right: The piston and the air behind it take the spike.
> wrong: An arrestor is a cushion of air for the water to hit.

4. Why do old air chambers stop working?
- [ ] They rust
- [x] They fill with water in months
- [ ] They are too small
- [ ] The code banned them
> right: Water soaks up the air, and a chamber full of water is just more pipe.
> wrong: An air chamber waterlogs. Once it is full of water it cushions nothing.
