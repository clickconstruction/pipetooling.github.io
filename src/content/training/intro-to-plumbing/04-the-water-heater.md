## Why it matters
The water heater is the one supply fixture that can hurt someone. It holds hot water under pressure. Know how water moves through it and what the safety valve is for.

## Must know
- Cold water enters at the top and drops down a dip tube to the bottom of the tank.
- {{figure:water-heater-cutaway|Cold goes in at the bottom through the dip tube. Hot leaves at the top. The T&P valve is the safety.}}
- The burner or element heats the bottom. Hot water rises and leaves from the top of the tank.
- Set the thermostat to **120°F**. Hotter scalds, and the tank wastes fuel.
- The T&P valve is the temperature and pressure relief valve. It opens at **150 psi** or **210°F**.
- The T&P valve's drain line runs down and ends near the floor. Never cap it or plug it.
- A common tank holds **40 to 50 gal**. A family of four uses most of it in a morning.
- A house with a PRV or a check valve, a one-way valve, needs an expansion tank. Heated water grows and needs somewhere to go.
- The drain valve at the bottom lets you flush out sediment. Sediment makes the tank rumble and work harder.

## Traps
- A dripping T&P valve is doing its job. Find out why the pressure or the heat is high before you replace it.
- A capped T&P line turns the tank into a bomb. The valve must be free to blow.
- The dip tube is why cold goes in at the top and still ends up at the bottom.

## Numbers
- **120°F** the set point
- **150 psi or 210°F** opens the T&P valve
- **40 to 50 gal** a common tank
- **an expansion tank** when there is a PRV or a check valve

## Check yourself
1. Where does hot water leave the tank?
- [ ] At the bottom
- [x] At the top
- [ ] Through the T&P valve
- [ ] Through the drain valve
> right: Hot water rises. The hot outlet is at the top.
> wrong: Hot rises to the top of the tank, and that is where the hot line leaves.

2. What is the T&P valve for?
- [ ] Setting the temperature
- [x] Opening when the heat or the pressure gets too high
- [ ] Draining sediment
- [ ] Letting cold water in
> right: It is the safety. It opens at 150 psi or 210°F.
> wrong: The T&P valve is a relief valve. It opens to let off dangerous heat or pressure.

3. Why does a house with a PRV need an expansion tank?
- [ ] The PRV leaks
- [ ] The heater is too small
- [x] Heated water grows and the closed system gives it nowhere to go
- [ ] The code says so for every house
> right: The PRV closes the system. The expansion tank gives the grown water room.
> wrong: Heated water takes more room. With a PRV it cannot push back to the street, so a tank takes it.

4. What should you do with a T&P valve's drain line?
- [ ] Cap it so it cannot drip
- [ ] Run it uphill to the attic
- [x] Run it down and leave the end open near the floor
- [ ] Remove it
> right: The valve has to blow freely. The line goes down, open, near the floor.
> wrong: Never cap it. The line runs down and ends open near the floor so the valve can blow.
