## Why it matters
All the water in a house enters through one pipe. One valve stops all of it. When a pipe bursts, the person who knows where that valve is saves the floor.

## Must know
- The street main runs under the road. A service line tees off it to each house.
- {{figure:meter-and-main-shutoff|The city's valve is at the meter. Yours is where the pipe enters the house.}}
- The meter sits in a box at the curb. It counts every gallon that goes in.
- The city's valve is beside the meter. It is the city's to turn, and some need a special key.
- Your main shutoff is where the service line enters the house. It is often on a riser near the front wall or in the garage.
- The main shutoff stops every faucet, every toilet and the water heater at once.
- Find the main shutoff before you need it. On a new job, find it first.
- A common home service line is **3/4 in**. Bigger houses run **1 in**.
- Turn a main shutoff slowly. A fast turn slams the water and can hurt the pipe.

## Traps
- The valve at the meter is the city's. Turning it without leave can bring a fine or a broken valve.
- A valve nobody has turned in years may not shut all the way. Test it with the water off at a faucet.
- The main shutoff does not stop water already in the pipes. Open a low faucet to drain them.

## Numbers
- **2** valves, the city's at the meter and yours at the house
- **3/4 in** the common service line
- **first** find the shutoff before you need it

## Check yourself
1. Where is a house's main shutoff usually found?
- [ ] At the water heater
- [x] Where the service line enters the house
- [ ] On the roof
- [ ] Under the kitchen sink
> right: The valve sits on the pipe as it comes in, often near the front wall or in the garage.
> wrong: The main shutoff is on the service line where it enters the house, not at a fixture.

2. Whose valve is the one beside the meter?
- [x] The city's
- [ ] The homeowner's
- [ ] The plumber's
- [ ] Nobody's, it is never turned
> right: The city owns the meter and the valve beside it.
> wrong: The valve at the meter belongs to the city. The one at the house is the owner's.

3. What is the first thing to do on a new job?
- [ ] Turn on the water heater
- [ ] Open every faucet
- [x] Find the main shutoff
- [ ] Read the meter
> right: Find it before you need it. A burst pipe gives you no time to search.
> wrong: Before anything else, find the main shutoff. You cannot search for it with water on the floor.

4. Why turn a main shutoff slowly?
- [ ] It is easier on your hand
- [x] A fast turn slams the water and can hurt the pipe
- [ ] The meter will miscount
- [ ] It does not matter
> right: Moving water stopped dead hits the pipe hard. Slow is safe.
> wrong: Water in motion has weight. Stop it slowly or the pipe takes the hit.
