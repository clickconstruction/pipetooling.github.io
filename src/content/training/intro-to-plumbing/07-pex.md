## Why it matters
PEX is the plastic pipe in most new houses. It bends around corners, so it needs fewer fittings. Its joints are made with a tool, not a flame.

## Must know
- PEX is a flexible plastic pipe for hot and cold water. It comes in **three kinds**, A, B and C.
- PEX bends, so a run can turn a corner with no fitting. Fewer fittings means fewer places to leak.
- A bend must not be too tight. Keep the bend to **6 to 8 times** the pipe's width or wider.
- {{figure:pex-bend|A bend of 6 to 8 pipe widths needs no fitting. A kink is damage and is cut out.}}
- A crimp joint pushes the pipe over a barbed fitting and squeezes a ring down over it.
- {{figure:pex-joints|A crimp ring squeezes PEX onto a barbed fitting. An expansion joint stretches it over the fitting and lets it shrink back.}}
- An expansion joint stretches the pipe and its ring with a tool. The pipe slides over a bigger fitting and shrinks back to grip it.
- PEX is rated for **100 psi at 180°F**. It is not for the T&P drain line or anything hotter.
- Keep PEX out of **sunlight**. Ultraviolet light breaks it down, even through a window.
- PEX is red for hot and blue for cold by habit. The colour is a label, not a different pipe.

## Traps
- A kinked PEX line is a damaged line. Cut the kink out, never bend it back.
- The crimp ring goes a set distance from the end of the pipe. A ring off the barb leaks.
- Red PEX is not rated hotter than blue. The colour only says which line it is.

## Numbers
- **A, B, C** the three kinds
- **100 psi at 180°F** the rating
- **no sunlight** keep it covered
- **6 to 8** pipe widths the tightest bend

## Check yourself
1. Why does PEX need fewer fittings than copper?
- [x] It bends around corners
- [ ] It is bigger
- [ ] It is glued
- [ ] It is thicker
> right: A bend takes the corner. No elbow, no joint.
> wrong: PEX bends, so a run turns a corner without a fitting.

2. What does an expansion joint do?
- [ ] Squeezes a ring down over the pipe
- [ ] Glues the pipe into a socket
- [x] Stretches the pipe over a bigger fitting and lets it shrink back
- [ ] Melts the pipe to the fitting
> right: The pipe shrinks back onto the fitting and grips it.
> wrong: Expansion stretches the pipe, then lets it shrink onto the fitting. The crimp is the one with the ring.

3. What does sunlight do to PEX?
- [ ] Nothing
- [ ] Makes it stronger
- [x] Breaks it down
- [ ] Changes its colour only
> right: Ultraviolet light weakens PEX. Keep it covered.
> wrong: Sunlight breaks PEX down. It must stay out of the light, even behind glass.

4. What should you do with a kinked PEX line?
- [ ] Bend it back straight
- [ ] Heat it with a torch
- [x] Cut the kink out and join the ends
- [ ] Leave it, the water will fix it
> right: A kink is damage. It is cut out, never straightened.
> wrong: A kink has already damaged the pipe. Cut it out and join the ends with a fitting.
