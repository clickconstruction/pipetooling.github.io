## Why it matters
Every trap has a vent, and the vents leave through the roof. A vent can be its own pipe, a pipe two fixtures share, or a drain doing a vent's job. Knowing the three lets you read which pipe in a wall is air.

## Must know
- Every trap has a vent. The vents join each other above the fixtures and leave through the roof.
- {{figure:vent-types|Every trap has a vent. The vents join and leave through the roof.}}
- An individual vent serves one trap. A common vent serves two fixtures that drain into one pipe.
- A wet vent is a drain that also vents another fixture. A bathroom group often shares one.
- {{figure:wet-vent|The lav drains into the pipe that vents the toilet. One pipe does two jobs.}}
- A vent rises at least 6 in above the fixture's rim before it turns level.
- A vent ends at least **6 in** above the roof. That clears the snow and the water on it.
- A vent end stays **10 ft** from a window or door, or ends **3 ft** above it. That keeps the gas from coming back inside.
- The smallest vent is **1-1/4 in**.

## Traps
- The top of a drain stack above the last fixture is called the stack vent. A pipe that carries only air is a vent stack.
- A vent that turns level below the fixture's rim can fill with water. Vents rise first.
- A wet vent does two jobs, so it is sized for both. It is a size bigger than the drain alone would be.

## Numbers
- **6 in** above the roof
- **10 ft** from a window, or 3 ft above it
- **1-1/4 in** the smallest vent

## Check yourself
1. What is a common vent?
- [ ] A vent through the roof
- [x] One vent serving two fixtures that drain into one pipe
- [ ] A vent for one trap
- [ ] A drain that also vents
> right: Two fixtures, one drain, one vent.
> wrong: A common vent is shared by two fixtures that drain into one pipe. One trap alone has an individual vent.

2. How far above the roof does a vent end?
- [ ] Flush with the roof
- [x] At least 6 in
- [ ] At least 3 ft
- [ ] At least 10 ft
> right: Six inches clears snow and water on the roof.
> wrong: The vent ends at least 6 in above the roof.

3. What is a wet vent?
- [ ] A vent with water in it by mistake
- [x] A drain that also vents another fixture
- [ ] A vent in a bathroom
- [ ] The vent for a toilet
> right: One pipe drains one fixture and vents the next.
> wrong: A wet vent is a drain doing a vent's job for another fixture.

4. Why does a vent end 10 ft from a window or 3 ft above it?
- [x] So sewer gas does not come back inside
- [ ] So rain does not get in
- [ ] So the vent is easy to find
- [ ] It does not matter
> right: The vent carries gas out. The rule keeps it from drifting back in a window.
> wrong: The distance keeps the vent's gas out of the window.
