## Why it matters
A plumber visits a new house three times, and an inspector follows each visit. Knowing which visit you are on tells you what is being built and what can still be changed.

## Must know
- A plumber visits a new house three times. Rough-in, top-out and trim, with an inspection after each.
- {{figure:three-visits|Rough-in puts the pipes under the slab and in the walls. Top-out brings them to the fixture. Trim sets the fixtures.}}
- Rough-in is the first visit. The drain and vent pipe goes under the slab, before the concrete.
- The under-slab rough is inspected before the concrete is poured. Under a slab, nothing is fixed cheaply.
- Top-out is the second visit. The drains and vents go up through the roof and the supplies reach each fixture's stub.
- At top-out the walls are open and the test is on. This is what the house lives with for fifty years.
- Trim is the last visit. The fixtures, the faucets, the toilets and the water heater are set after the drywall and the paint.
- A stub-out is a capped pipe end waiting for its fixture. Hot on the left, cold on the right, the drain below.
- Rough-in dimensions decide where a fixture can go. A toilet's drain sits **12 in** from the finished wall.
- {{figure:toilet-rough-in|Measure 12 in to the finished wall. Measured to the stud, the toilet sits half an inch too close.}}

## Traps
- The slab inspection is the one you cannot redo. Under the concrete, a mistake is a jackhammer.
- Trim is not where plumbing is fixed. By then the walls are closed.
- A rough-in measured to the stud, not the finished wall, sets the toilet half an inch too close. Measure to the drywall.

## Numbers
- **3** visits
- **3** inspections
- **12 in** a toilet's drain from the finished wall
- **before** the slab the under-slab rough is inspected
