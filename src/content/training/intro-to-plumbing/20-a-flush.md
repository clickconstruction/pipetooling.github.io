## Why it matters
A flush is both systems working in about four seconds. The supply fills, the drain and the vent empty. If you can follow one flush, you can follow any fixture in the house.

## Must know
- A flush is the tank dumping, the bowl emptying, the vent breathing and the supply refilling, in that order.
- {{figure:flush-strip|The tank dumps into the bowl. The bowl's own trap siphons. The vent lets air follow. The fill valve refills the tank.}}
- Press the handle and the flapper lifts. The tank's water drops into the bowl through the rim and the jet.
- {{figure:toilet-tank|Inside the tank. The flapper lifts off the flush valve. The float shuts the fill valve when the water is back.}}
- The bowl's own trap fills past its top and starts a siphon. The siphon pulls the bowl's water down the drain in one pull.
- When air reaches the trap the siphon breaks with a gulp. The bowl refills to its seal from the tank's refill tube.
- The fill valve opens as the tank empties and closes when the float rises. The supply does the refilling.
- A new toilet uses **1.6 gal** a flush. A WaterSense toilet uses **1.28 gal**. Old ones used 3.5 or more.
- In the trade the toilet is the closet, so its drain is the closet drain. It is at least **3 in**.
- The vent lets air follow the slug of water down the stack.
- A flapper that leaks lets the tank run. The fill valve cycles and the water bill climbs.

## Traps
- The tank does not push the water out. The bowl's siphon pulls it.
- A weak flush is often a clogged rim or jet, not low pressure. The supply only refills the tank.
- A running toilet is a leaking flapper or a float set too high, so water spills down the overflow tube.

## Numbers
- **1.6 gal** a flush, 1.28 for WaterSense
- **3 in** the smallest closet drain
- **its own trap** cast in the bowl
- **the fill valve** refills from the supply

## Check yourself
1. What empties the bowl?
- [ ] The tank pushing the water out
- [x] The siphon in the bowl's own trap
- [ ] The vent
- [ ] The fill valve
> right: The tank's water starts a siphon in the bowl's trap, and the siphon does the pulling.
> wrong: The bowl empties by siphon. The tank only starts it.

2. What refills the tank after a flush?
- [ ] The bowl
- [ ] The vent
- [x] The fill valve, from the supply
- [ ] The flapper
> right: The float drops, the fill valve opens, and the supply fills the tank.
> wrong: The fill valve refills the tank from the supply line.

3. How much water does a WaterSense toilet use a flush?
- [ ] 1.6 gal
- [x] 1.28 gal
- [ ] 3.5 gal
- [ ] 5 gal
> right: 1.28 gal is the WaterSense mark. A plain new toilet uses 1.6.
> wrong: WaterSense is 1.28 gal a flush.

4. What makes a toilet run between flushes?
- [ ] Low pressure
- [x] A leaking flapper or a float set too high
- [ ] A blocked vent
- [ ] A dry trap
> right: Water leaves the tank past the flapper or over the overflow tube, so the fill valve keeps cycling.
> wrong: A running toilet is losing tank water past the flapper or over the overflow tube.
