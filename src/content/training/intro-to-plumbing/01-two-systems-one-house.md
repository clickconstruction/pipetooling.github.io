## Why it matters
Every plumbing job is one of two systems. Water comes in under pressure. Water leaves by gravity. Once you can tell which one you are looking at, the rest of the course makes sense.

## Must know
- A house has two plumbing systems. The supply brings clean water in. The drain carries used water out.
- {{figure:two-systems|Water comes in under pressure. Water leaves by gravity. The two never touch.}}
- The supply is under pressure. The street pushes water through small pipes to every faucet, even upstairs.
- The drain has no pump. Used water runs downhill through big pipes at a set slope.
- The fixture is where the two meet. A sink, a toilet or a tub takes clean water in and sends used water out.
- The two systems never touch. Clean water stops at an air gap above the fixture, and used water starts below it.
- A trap under every fixture holds a plug of water. That plug keeps sewer gas out of the house.
- A vent lets air into the drain so water can leave without pulling the trap dry.
- Supply pipe is small and strong. Drain pipe is big and sloped. You can tell them apart at a glance.

## Traps
- A drain is not a smaller supply pipe. The two carry water in opposite directions by opposite means.
- The faucet is not where the systems join. The water falls through air before it lands in the basin.
- A drain never needs pressure. If someone says a drain is pressurised, something is wrong.

## Numbers
- **2** systems in every house
- **0** places the two connect
- **the fixture** is where they meet

## Check yourself
1. What moves the water in the supply system?
- [x] Pressure from the street or a pump
- [ ] Gravity
- [ ] The vent
- [ ] The trap
> right: The supply is pushed. That push is pressure, and it reaches every faucet.
> wrong: Gravity moves the drain, not the supply. The supply is pushed by pressure.

2. What moves used water out of the house?
- [ ] Pressure from the street
- [x] Gravity, down a sloped pipe
- [ ] A fan in the vent
- [ ] The water heater
> right: The drain has no pump. Water runs downhill at a set slope.
> wrong: The drain is not pushed. Used water runs downhill by gravity through a sloped pipe.

3. Where do the supply and the drain meet?
- [ ] Inside the water heater
- [ ] At the meter
- [x] At the fixture, across an air gap
- [ ] At the trap
> right: Clean water falls through air into the fixture. Used water starts below it.
> wrong: They meet at the fixture, and only across an air gap. They never join as pipes.

4. What does the trap under a fixture hold?
- [ ] Pressure for the faucet
- [ ] A filter for grit
- [x] A plug of water that blocks sewer gas
- [ ] Hot water
> right: The water sitting in the bend is a seal. Sewer gas stops at it.
> wrong: The trap holds water, not a filter. That water is the seal against sewer gas.
