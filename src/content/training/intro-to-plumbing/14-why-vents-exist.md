## Why it matters
Water leaving a drain pulls air behind it. If no air can get in, it pulls the trap's seal out instead. The vent is how the drain breathes, and it is the part a first-timer leaves out.

## Must know
- Water leaving a trap pulls air behind it. With no air to follow, it pulls the seal out of the trap. That is a siphon.
- {{figure:siphon-and-vent|Without a vent the rushing water pulls the seal out behind it. With a vent, air follows the water and the seal stays.}}
- A vent is a pipe that lets air into the drain behind the water. The water leaves and the seal stays.
- Every trap has a vent. The vent joins the drain within a set distance of the trap, along the trap arm.
- The trap arm for a **1-1/2 in** trap is at most **6 ft**. For a **2 in** trap it is **8 ft**.
- The trap arm drops no more than its own width over its length. A steeper arm siphons the trap.
- {{figure:trap-arm-drop|The arm drops one pipe width at most before the vent. Past 6 ft or 8 ft it siphons.}}
- Back pressure is the other danger. Water dropping down a stack pushes air ahead of it, and without a vent that air blows the seal out.
- A gurgle at a drain is the sound of air pulled through a seal. The vent is missing or blocked.
- The vent also carries sewer gas up and out above the roof, where it cannot come inside.

## Traps
- A vent carries air, never water. Water in a vent means a fitting faces the wrong way.
- The longest trap arm is set by the pipe size, not by what fits. Over the table, the trap siphons.
- An S-trap has no vent and siphons itself with every use. The code bans it.

## Numbers
- **1** vent for every trap
- **6 ft** the 1-1/2 in trap arm
- **8 ft** the 2 in trap arm
- **its own width** the most an arm may drop

## Check yourself
1. What does a siphon do to a trap?
- [ ] Fills it
- [x] Pulls the seal out of it
- [ ] Cleans it
- [ ] Nothing
> right: The leaving water drags the seal along behind it.
> wrong: A siphon pulls the water out of the bend. The seal is gone.

2. What does a vent let into the drain?
- [ ] Water
- [x] Air
- [ ] Light
- [ ] Pressure from the street
> right: Air follows the water, so the trap's water stays put.
> wrong: A vent lets air in behind the leaving water.

3. How long may a 1-1/2 in trap arm be?
- [ ] 2 ft
- [x] 6 ft
- [ ] 12 ft
- [ ] Any length
> right: Six feet for a 1-1/2 in trap. A 2 in trap gets 8 ft.
> wrong: The 1-1/2 in trap arm stops at 6 ft. Longer, and the trap siphons.

4. What does a gurgle at a drain mean?
- [ ] The water is too hot
- [ ] The slope is right
- [x] Air is being pulled through the seal, so the vent is missing or blocked
- [ ] The trap is too deep
> right: The gurgle is air crossing the water. The drain is not breathing through its vent.
> wrong: A gurgle is air pulled through the seal. Look for a missing or blocked vent.
