## Why it matters
Three pipes carry the drains. Two are plastics that look alike and must never share a glue. One is iron that outlives the house. Knowing which is which keeps a joint from failing a year later.

## Must know
- Three pipes carry drains. PVC is white, ABS is black, cast iron is grey and heavy.
- A hub is the bell end of a drain fitting that the pipe sits in. Plastic pipe glues into a hub.
- {{figure:dwv-materials|PVC and ABS are glued, each with its own cement. Cast iron is clamped with a no-hub band.}}
- PVC is glued with primer and PVC cement. The purple primer shows the inspector the joint was primed.
- ABS is glued with ABS cement alone. It takes no primer.
- PVC and ABS never share a cement. Joining them takes a transition cement where the code allows it, or a listed coupling.
- Cast iron has no hub. It is joined with a no-hub coupling, a rubber sleeve inside a steel band, tightened to **60 in-lb**.
- Cast iron is quiet. A plastic stack lets you hear every flush through the wall. Cast iron does not.
- Cast iron lasts **50 to 100 years**. Old houses still run on it, and it is cut with a snap cutter.
- Plastic drain pipe grows with heat. A long run needs room to move, or it bows and pulls its joints.

## Traps
- Black and white are two plastics, not two colours of one. The wrong cement makes a joint that looks fine and leaks.
- A no-hub band is torqued, not just tightened. Too tight splits the sleeve. Too loose slips.
- Cast iron is heavy. It hangs every 5 ft. Plastic hangs every 4 ft.

## Numbers
- **white** PVC, black ABS
- **never** the same cement on both
- **60 in-lb** on a no-hub band
- **50 to 100 years** for cast iron

## Check yourself
1. Which drain pipe is black?
- [ ] PVC
- [x] ABS
- [ ] Cast iron
- [ ] Copper
> right: ABS is the black plastic. PVC is white.
> wrong: The black drain pipe is ABS.

2. How is cast iron joined?
- [ ] Glued with primer and cement
- [ ] Soldered
- [x] A no-hub coupling, a rubber sleeve in a steel band
- [ ] Threaded
> right: The sleeve seals, the band holds it, and a torque wrench sets it.
> wrong: Cast iron takes a no-hub coupling, torqued to 60 in-lb.

3. Can you join PVC to ABS with PVC cement?
- [ ] Yes, it is all plastic
- [x] No, it takes a transition cement or a listed coupling
- [ ] Yes, with primer first
- [ ] Only on vents
> right: The two plastics need their own cement, or a transition made for both.
> wrong: PVC cement does not weld ABS. Use a transition cement or a coupling.

4. Why do some houses still choose cast iron?
- [ ] It is cheaper
- [ ] It is lighter
- [x] It is quiet and lasts 50 to 100 years
- [ ] It is easier to cut
> right: You cannot hear the flush, and it outlives the plastic.
> wrong: Cast iron costs more and weighs more. It is chosen for quiet and a long life.
