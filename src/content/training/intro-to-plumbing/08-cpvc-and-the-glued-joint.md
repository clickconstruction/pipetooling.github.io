## Why it matters
CPVC is the cream-coloured plastic pipe that takes hot water. It is glued, and a glued joint done right is one piece of plastic. Done wrong, it leaks weeks later.

## Must know
- CPVC carries hot and cold water. Plain PVC does not take hot water and is for drains and cold lines only.
- A glued joint is a solvent weld. The cement melts both faces and they set as one piece.
- {{figure:solvent-joint|Primer softens both faces. Cement melts them into one piece.}}
- Primer goes on first, on the pipe's outside and the socket's inside. It softens the plastic.
- Cement goes on both faces. Push the pipe in with a quarter turn and hold it **30 seconds**.
- CPVC takes its own cement. PVC cement does not weld CPVC.
- A joint sets in minutes and cures in **hours**. Wait before you put pressure on it.
- CPVC is rated for **100 psi at 180°F**. It gets brittle with age and with some chemicals.
- Cut CPVC square and clean the burr off. A ragged end scrapes the cement out of the socket.

## Traps
- A joint pushed in without the turn leaves a dry line in the cement. That line is where it weeps.
- Pressure on a joint that has not cured pushes it apart. The cure takes hours, not minutes.
- Old CPVC breaks when you grab it. Support it before you cut near it.

## Numbers
- **100 psi at 180°F** the rating
- **its own cement** and never PVC's
- **30 seconds** hold the joint
- **hours** before pressure

## Check yourself
1. What does the primer do?
- [x] Softens the faces so the cement can weld them
- [ ] Colours the joint purple so the inspector sees it
- [ ] Glues the joint by itself
- [ ] Cleans the water
> right: Primer softens the plastic. The cement then melts the faces together.
> wrong: Primer prepares the faces by softening them. The cement does the welding.

2. How long do you hold a glued joint after pushing it in?
- [ ] 2 seconds
- [x] 30 seconds
- [ ] 10 minutes
- [ ] You do not hold it
> right: Thirty seconds, or the pipe backs out of the socket.
> wrong: Hold it 30 seconds. The tapered socket pushes the pipe back out before the cement sets.

3. Can PVC cement weld a CPVC joint?
- [ ] Yes, they are the same plastic
- [x] No, CPVC needs its own cement
- [ ] Yes, if you use more
- [ ] Only on cold lines
> right: Each plastic has its own cement. The wrong one never welds.
> wrong: CPVC takes CPVC cement. PVC cement on CPVC makes a joint that fails.

4. When can a new glued joint take pressure?
- [ ] At once
- [ ] After 30 seconds
- [x] After it cures, which takes hours
- [ ] After a day in the sun
> right: Set is minutes. Cure is hours. Pressure waits for the cure.
> wrong: A joint sets fast but cures slowly. Pressure before the cure pushes it apart.
