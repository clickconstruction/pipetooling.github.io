## Why it matters
Every fixture needs two lines, hot and cold. How those lines get around the house decides how the water feels at the tap. It also decides how many fittings hide in the walls.

## Must know
- Cold water feeds every fixture and the water heater. Hot water leaves the heater on its own line.
- Hot is on the left and cold is on the right at every fixture. That is the rule everywhere.
- Trunk and branch runs one big line through the house with small lines teeing off to each fixture.
- {{figure:trunk-vs-manifold|Trunk and branch is one big line with small lines off it. A manifold runs one line to each fixture.}}
- The trunk is **3/4 in**. The branches are **1/2 in**.
- A manifold is a block with a port for every fixture. One **1/2 in** line runs from the manifold to each fixture.
- A manifold means fewer fittings in the walls and a shutoff for each fixture in one place.
- A trunk and branch system uses less pipe. A manifold uses more pipe and fewer joints.
- On a trunk, running two fixtures at once can drop the pressure at both. On a manifold, each line is its own.

## Traps
- Hot on the left is not a suggestion. A swapped faucet scalds someone who expects cold.
- A long hot line means a long wait at the tap. The water in the pipe has gone cold.
- More pipe is not more joints. A manifold runs more pipe with almost no joints in the walls.

## Numbers
- **hot** on the left, cold on the right
- **3/4 in** the trunk
- **1/2 in** a branch or a manifold line
- **one line** per fixture from a manifold

## Check yourself
1. Which side is hot at a faucet?
- [x] The left
- [ ] The right
- [ ] Either, it depends on the house
- [ ] The middle
> right: Hot left, cold right, everywhere.
> wrong: Hot is always on the left. Cold is always on the right.

2. What is a manifold?
- [ ] A big trunk line
- [x] A block with one line out to each fixture
- [ ] A kind of water heater
- [ ] A valve at the meter
> right: Each fixture gets its own line from the block.
> wrong: A manifold is the block that sends one line to each fixture.

3. What size is the trunk in a trunk and branch system?
- [ ] 1/2 in
- [x] 3/4 in
- [ ] 1 in
- [ ] 2 in
> right: The trunk is 3/4 in. The branches off it are 1/2 in.
> wrong: The trunk is the big one, 3/4 in. The branches are 1/2 in.

4. Why does a manifold leave fewer fittings in the walls?
- [x] Each line runs whole from the block to its fixture
- [ ] The lines are bigger
- [ ] It uses less pipe
- [ ] The fittings are inside the manifold
> right: One line, no tees, no joints hidden in the wall.
> wrong: Each manifold line runs unbroken to its fixture, so the joints are at the ends, not in the walls.
