## Why it matters
A trap keeps sewer gas out only while it holds water. Water that nobody replaces goes away. The smell in a spare bathroom is usually this, and the fix is a cup of water.

## Must know
- A trap keeps its seal only while water stays in the bend. Water nobody replaces evaporates.
- {{figure:dry-trap-strip|A trap left unused dries out. Sewer gas comes through the empty bend.}}
- A floor drain nobody uses can dry in **a month** in a warm room.
- A guest bath, a basement floor drain, a laundry standpipe or a shop sink are the usual ones.
- The sign is a smell with no leak. The gas comes up through the empty bend.
- **One cup** of water refills the seal. A spoon of mineral oil on top slows the evaporation.
- A trap primer is a small valve that drips water into a trap each time a nearby fixture runs.
- A trap can also be siphoned dry or blown dry by back pressure. Then the vent is the fix, not a cup of water.
- Wind over a roof vent can rock a seal out over time. A deeper seal, up to 4 in, holds longer.

## Traps
- A smell with no leak is a dry trap, not a bad plumber. Pour water and wait.
- A trap that dries every week was siphoned, not evaporated. Look at the vent.
- Bleach down a dry trap does nothing for the gas. Water is the seal.

## Numbers
- **a month** of no use can dry a floor drain
- **1 cup** refills it
- **a trap primer** keeps it full
- **mineral oil** slows the evaporation

## Check yourself
1. What happens to a trap that is never used?
- [ ] It clogs
- [x] It dries out and lets sewer gas through
- [ ] It fills with grease
- [ ] Nothing
> right: The water evaporates and the bend is open to the sewer.
> wrong: An unused trap dries out. With no water there is no seal.

2. What refills a dry trap?
- [ ] Bleach
- [x] A cup of water
- [ ] A new trap
- [ ] Opening the cleanout
> right: Water is the seal. A cup fills the bend again.
> wrong: Pour a cup of water in. The water is the seal.

3. What is a trap primer?
- [ ] A chemical that cleans the trap
- [x] A valve that drips water into a trap when a fixture nearby runs
- [ ] The first trap on a line
- [ ] A cap on the vent
> right: Each time the nearby fixture runs, the primer sends a little water to the trap.
> wrong: A trap primer is a small valve that keeps a seldom-used trap full.

4. A trap dries out every week. What is the likely cause?
- [ ] The room is warm
- [ ] The water is hard
- [x] It is being siphoned, so look at the vent
- [ ] The trap is too deep
> right: Evaporation takes a month. A week means the water is pulled out.
> wrong: A trap that empties that fast is siphoned or blown out. The vent is the fix.
