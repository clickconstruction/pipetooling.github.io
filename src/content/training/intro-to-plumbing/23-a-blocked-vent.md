## Why it matters
A vent blocked at the roof stops the drain breathing. The drain gulps air through the nearest trap instead. The signs look like a clog, and a cable finds nothing.

## Must know
- When the vent is blocked, water leaving the drain pulls its air through the nearest trap instead.
- {{figure:blocked-vent-strip|A blocked vent makes the drain gulp. The trap next to it loses its seal.}}
- Leaves, a bird's nest, a ball or ice close a vent. So does a vent that was never run.
- The gurgle at a sink when the toilet flushes is the toilet pulling air through the sink's trap.
- Slow drains all over the house with no clog is a vent problem. The water has no air behind it.
- A smell after the gurgle means the seal went with the air.
- The fix is at the roof. Clear the vent from the top with a hose or a cable.
- In cold country a vent frosts shut from the steam inside it. The pipe through the roof is at least **3 in** there.
- An air admittance valve, an AAV, is a one-way air valve under a sink. It goes where no pipe can reach the roof.
- An AAV opens on suction to let air in and closes by itself. It sits at least **4 in** above the trap arm, where a hand can reach it.
- An AAV that sticks shut acts like a blocked vent for its one fixture.

## Traps
- A gurgle is not a clog. Cabling the drain finds nothing because the trouble is above it.
- The slow drain is everywhere at once. A clog is in one place.
- Do not cap a vent to stop a smell. The smell comes from the trap the capped vent empties.
- An AAV is not a vent through the roof. It lets air in and never lets gas out.

## Numbers
- **a gurgle** is air through a seal
- **the roof** is where to look first
- **leaves, a nest, ice** are the usual blocks
- **3 in** through the roof in frost country

## Check yourself
1. The sink gurgles when the toilet flushes. What is happening?
- [ ] The sink is clogged
- [x] The toilet is pulling air through the sink's trap because the vent is blocked
- [ ] The water heater is cycling
- [ ] The pressure is too high
> right: With no air from the vent, the flush pulls it through the nearest seal.
> wrong: A gurgle is air through a seal. The vent is blocked, not the sink.

2. Where is a blocked vent cleared?
- [ ] At the cleanout
- [x] At the roof, from the top
- [ ] At the trap
- [ ] At the meter
> right: The block is at the top, so the hose or the cable goes in there.
> wrong: A vent is cleared from the roof. The block is at its end.

3. Every drain in the house is slow and no clog is found. What is likely?
- [ ] Low pressure
- [x] A blocked vent
- [ ] A dry trap
- [ ] A bad PRV
> right: Slow everywhere at once is the air, not a clog in one place.
> wrong: A clog slows one place. Slow everywhere is the vent.

4. What closes a vent in cold country?
- [ ] Snow on the roof
- [x] Frost from the steam inside it
- [ ] Ice in the trap
- [ ] The cold makes the pipe shrink shut
> right: Warm wet air rising in the vent freezes at the cold end and closes it.
> wrong: The vent's own steam frosts shut at the top. A bigger pipe through the roof resists it.
