## Why it matters
You do not shut off the whole house to fix one faucet. Small valves at each fixture stop one line. Outside, a hose bib needs a part that stops the hose pulling dirty water back in.

## Must know
- An angle stop sits under every fixture, one on the hot line and one on the cold.
- {{figure:fixture-valves|An angle stop under every fixture. A vacuum breaker on every hose bib.}}
- Shut the angle stop and you can change the faucet with the rest of the house running.
- A toilet has one angle stop. It only takes cold water.
- A hose bib is the faucet on the outside wall. Its spout is threaded for a hose.
- A vacuum breaker screws onto the bib's spout. It stops water in the hose flowing back into the house.
- Without a breaker, a pressure drop can pull a bucket or a pool back up the hose into the house.
- A frost-proof hose bib has a long stem. Its valve seat sits **8 to 12 in** inside the warm wall.
- Take the hose off a frost-proof bib in winter. A hose left on holds water in the stem and it freezes.

## Traps
- An angle stop that has not turned in years may not shut. Be ready to shut the main.
- A vacuum breaker is not a cap. If it is missing, the bib is open to backflow.
- A frost-proof bib with a hose left on splits inside the wall. The leak shows in spring.

## Numbers
- **1** angle stop per line
- **every** hose bib has a vacuum breaker
- **8 to 12 in** the frost-proof bib reaches into the wall

## Check yourself
1. How many angle stops does a sink have?
- [ ] One
- [x] Two, one hot and one cold
- [ ] Three
- [ ] None
> right: One stop on each supply line under the sink.
> wrong: A sink takes hot and cold, so it has two angle stops.

2. What does a vacuum breaker on a hose bib do?
- [ ] Raises the pressure in the hose
- [ ] Keeps the bib from freezing
- [x] Stops hose water flowing back into the house
- [ ] Filters the water
> right: It is a backflow stop. The bucket cannot come back up the hose.
> wrong: The breaker stops backflow. Water in the hose cannot be pulled into the drinking lines.

3. Why does a toilet have only one angle stop?
- [x] It takes cold water only
- [ ] It is too small for two
- [ ] The code limits it
- [ ] It has no supply line
> right: No hot line, so no hot stop.
> wrong: A toilet only uses cold water, so it only needs one stop.

4. Why take the hose off a frost-proof bib in winter?
- [ ] The hose will freeze solid
- [x] A hose left on traps water in the stem, which freezes and splits it
- [ ] The bib stops working with a hose on
- [ ] It does not matter
> right: The long stem only stays safe when it drains. A hose stops it draining.
> wrong: A hose holds water in the stem. The stem freezes inside the wall and the split shows in spring.
