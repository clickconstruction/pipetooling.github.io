## Why it matters
Four mistakes look fine from across the room and fail the house a year later. Each one is a rule from this course, drawn beside the right way.

## Must know
- Four mistakes a first-timer makes, each drawn beside the right way.
- {{figure:first-timer-mistakes|An S-trap, a tee on its back, a flat run, a trap arm too long. Each beside the right way.}}
- An S-trap drops straight down after the bend with no vent. It siphons itself every time. The right way is a P-trap with a vented arm.
- A sanitary tee on its back takes a branch into a horizontal run. The branch's water stalls. The right way is a wye or a combo.
- {{figure:san-tee-vs-wye|A sanitary tee drops a branch into a stack. A wye joins a branch to a horizontal run.}}
- A flat run has no slope. Water sits and solids settle. The right way is **1/4 in per ft**.
- A trap arm too long, past the table, siphons the trap. The right way is **6 ft** for 1-1/2 in and 8 ft for 2 in, with the vent inside it.
- Each of the four passes water on the first day. Each fails the inspection, and then the house.
- Three more to watch. A vent run flat below the rim fills with water. A fitting glued facing upstream. A cleanout nobody can reach.

## Traps
- It drains fine is not a test. An S-trap drains fine and then smells.
- A tee on its back passes water today. The clog comes in a year.
- The arm table is by pipe size, not by what fits between the studs.

## Numbers
- **an S-trap** siphons itself
- **a tee on its back** fights the flow
- **1/4 in per ft** or it is flat
- **6 ft** the 1-1/2 in arm

## Check yourself
1. What does an S-trap do?
- [ ] Holds a deeper seal
- [x] Siphons itself with every use
- [ ] Saves a vent
- [ ] Drains faster
> right: With no vent and a straight drop, the leaving water pulls the seal out every time.
> wrong: An S-trap has no vent. It siphons its own seal out, which is why the code bans it.

2. What replaces a sanitary tee on its back?
- [ ] A supply tee
- [x] A wye or a combo
- [ ] A coupling
- [ ] A short sweep
> right: The branch joins a horizontal run through a wye's long merge.
> wrong: A horizontal run takes a wye or a combo. A tee on its back stalls the branch.

3. A drain was run level to clear the joists. What happens?
- [ ] Nothing, level is fine for short runs
- [x] Water sits, solids settle and it clogs
- [ ] It drains faster
- [ ] The vent fills with water
> right: With no slope the solids stay where they land.
> wrong: A flat run holds water and settles solids. It clogs.

4. A 1-1/2 in trap arm runs 9 ft to its vent. What is wrong?
- [ ] Nothing
- [ ] It is too short
- [x] It is over 6 ft, so the trap siphons
- [ ] The vent is too big
> right: The table says 6 ft for 1-1/2 in. Past it the trap loses its seal.
> wrong: A 1-1/2 in arm stops at 6 ft. Nine feet siphons the trap.
