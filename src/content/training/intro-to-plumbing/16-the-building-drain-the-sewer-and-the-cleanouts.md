## Why it matters
Every drain in the house ends in one pipe under it. That pipe becomes the sewer outside the wall. When the whole house backs up, this is the pipe, and the cleanout is the door into it.

## Must know
- Every stack drops to the building drain, the big pipe under the slab or in the crawl space.
- {{figure:house-to-sewer|The building drain runs under the house and becomes the sewer outside the wall. A cleanout where it leaves.}}
- The building drain is usually **4 in**. It holds the same slope as every drain.
- The building drain becomes the building sewer **30 in** outside the foundation wall. From there it runs to the city sewer or a septic tank.
- A septic tank is a buried box, often **1,000 gal**. Solids settle in it and the liquid runs out to a drain field.
- {{figure:septic-tank|Solids settle in the tank behind its baffles. The liquid runs on to the drain field.}}
- A cleanout is a capped opening a plumber opens to run a cable into the pipe.
- A cleanout goes at the base of each stack and where the drain leaves the house. Every turn of more than **45°** gets one too.
- A straight run gets a cleanout every **100 ft**.
- A cleanout cap faces the way the cable needs to go, with room to work in front of it.

## Traps
- The cleanout at the house is the first door into a sewer clog. Pulling a toilet is the last.
- A septic tank is not a sewer. Grease, wipes and chemicals that a city plant handles ruin a tank's field.
- The building drain carries everything. A belly in it backs up the lowest fixtures first.

## Numbers
- **4 in** the common building drain
- **30 in** outside the wall the sewer begins
- **100 ft** between cleanouts
- **45°** a turn over this needs a cleanout
- **1,000 gal** a common septic tank
