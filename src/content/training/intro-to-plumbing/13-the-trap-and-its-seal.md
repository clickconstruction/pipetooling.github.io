## Why it matters
Every drain in the house is open to the sewer. The only thing between the sewer gas and the room is a bend full of water. Know what that bend does and what empties it.

## Must know
- Every fixture drains through a trap, a bend in the pipe that stays full of water.
- {{figure:p-trap-seal|The water sitting in the bend is the seal. Sewer gas stops at it.}}
- The water in the bend is the seal. Sewer gas cannot pass through water, so it stops there.
- The seal is **2 to 4 in** deep. It is measured from the top of the dip to the top of the outlet.
- A sink trap is **1-1/2 in**. A lavatory trap is **1-1/4 in**. A toilet's trap is cast into the bowl.
- One trap per fixture. Two traps in a row hold air between them, and the drain gurgles and slows.
- The pipe leaving the trap is the trap arm. It runs to the vent and on to the drain.
- A plug at the bottom of the bend lets a plumber open the trap without cutting it.
- A trap keeps its seal only if the water it loses is put back. The vent, in the next lesson, makes sure of that.

## Traps
- A deeper seal is not a better trap. Over 4 in the trap holds solids and clogs.
- A toilet has a trap even though you cannot see one. It is the curve inside the bowl.
- A dry trap looks fine from above. The smell says the seal is gone.

## Numbers
- **2 to 4 in** of seal
- **1** trap per fixture, never two
- **1-1/4 in** a lav trap
- **1-1/2 in** a sink trap

## Check yourself
1. What stops sewer gas at a fixture?
- [ ] A filter in the drain
- [x] The water held in the trap's bend
- [ ] The vent
- [ ] The angle stop
> right: Gas cannot pass through water. The bend holds that water.
> wrong: The trap's water is the seal. Nothing else at the fixture stops gas.

2. How deep is a trap seal?
- [ ] 1/2 in
- [x] 2 to 4 in
- [ ] 6 to 8 in
- [ ] 12 in
> right: Two to four inches of water, measured from the dip to the outlet.
> wrong: The seal is 2 to 4 in. Shallower dries out, deeper holds solids.

3. Where is a toilet's trap?

- [ ] In the tank
- [x] Cast into the bowl
- [ ] A toilet has no trap
> right: The curve inside the bowl is the trap. The water you see is its seal.
> wrong: The toilet's trap is the curve cast into the bowl itself.
