## Why it matters
Waste has no pump. It runs downhill, and the code says how steep. Too flat and the water sits. Too steep and it leaves the solids behind. Most drain trouble starts with the slope.

## Must know
- Drain pipe runs downhill. The fall is called the slope, and the code sets it.
- {{figure:slope-rule|A quarter inch of fall for every foot of pipe. Flatter leaves water. Steeper leaves solids.}}
- The slope is **1/4 in per ft** for pipe 2-1/2 in and smaller. Pipe from 3 in up may run at **1/8 in per ft**.
- Too flat and water sits in the pipe. Solids settle and the line clogs.
- Too steep and the water outruns the solids. They are left behind on the pipe wall.
- Water moving about **2 ft per second** carries solids along. The code's slope gives that speed.
- A level shows the slope. On a 2 ft level, 1/4 in per ft is a 1/2 in gap at one end.
- Every horizontal drain holds its slope the whole way. A belly, a low spot, is a clog waiting.
- Hangers hold the slope. A sagging pipe makes a belly even when it was set right.

## Traps
- Steeper is not better. A drain pitched like a slide leaves the solids behind.
- A pipe can lose its slope after the inspection. A hanger that lets go makes a belly.
- A vent carries air, not water, so it has no fall to keep. It only slopes back toward the drain.

## Numbers
- **1/4 in per ft** for pipe up to 2-1/2 in
- **1/8 in per ft** allowed from 3 in
- **2 ft per second** carries solids
- **a belly** is a clog waiting

## Check yourself
1. What is the slope for a 2 in drain?
- [ ] 1/8 in per ft
- [x] 1/4 in per ft
- [ ] 1 in per ft
- [ ] Level
> right: Pipe up to 2-1/2 in runs at 1/4 in per ft.
> wrong: Small drains run at 1/4 in per ft. The 1/8 in slope is for 3 in and up.

2. What happens when a drain is too steep?
- [ ] Nothing, faster is better
- [x] The water outruns the solids and leaves them behind
- [ ] The pipe bursts
- [ ] The vent fills with water
> right: Water alone goes down the slide. The solids stay on the wall.
> wrong: Too steep separates the water from the solids. The solids are left behind.

3. What is a belly in a drain?
- [ ] A fitting that turns the pipe
- [ ] The widest part of the trap
- [x] A low spot where water sits
- [ ] The cleanout cap
> right: Water sits in the low spot, solids settle, and the line clogs.
> wrong: A belly is a sag in the run. Water sits in it and it clogs.

4. What holds a pipe's slope between the fittings?
- [x] The hangers
- [ ] The glue
- [ ] The water pressure
- [ ] The vent
> right: Hangers carry the pipe at its slope. One that lets go makes a belly.
> wrong: The hangers hold the slope. A sagging pipe loses it.
