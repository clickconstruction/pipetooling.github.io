## Why it matters
Drain fittings look like supply fittings with a curve. The curve is the whole point. It keeps solids moving, and it only works when the fitting faces the right way.

## Must know
- Drain fittings sweep. Supply fittings turn hard. The sweep keeps solids moving with the water.
- {{figure:san-tee-vs-wye|A sanitary tee drops a branch into a vertical stack. A wye lets a branch join a horizontal run at an angle.}}
- A sanitary tee has a branch that curves down into the run. It is for a branch dropping into a vertical stack.
- A sanitary tee never lies on its back on a horizontal drain. The branch's water hits the far wall and stalls.
- A wye brings a branch in at **45°**. It is for joining a horizontal run, where the water needs a long merge.
- A wye also takes a cleanout. The cable goes in at 45° and follows the flow.
- A fitting is set so its sweep points the way the water goes. Backwards, it fights the flow and catches solids.
- A double sanitary tee serves two fixtures back to back on one stack.
- A sanitary tee turned over, sweep up, is how a vent joins a stack. Air goes up, so the sweep goes up.

## Traps
- A tee on a drain means a sanitary tee, never the supply tee. The square branch stalls solids.
- A wye on a vertical stack is fine. A sanitary tee on a horizontal run is not. The direction decides.
- A sweep pointing against the flow is the mistake an inspector sees from the door.

## Numbers
- **sanitary tee** on a vertical drain only
- **wye** on a horizontal run
- **45°** the wye's branch
- **never** a tee on its back

- [x] To keep the solids moving with the water

> wrong: The sweep keeps solids moving. A square corner catches them.

4. What is wrong with a sanitary tee on its back?

> right: Water dropping into a horizontal pipe needs the long merge of a wye.
