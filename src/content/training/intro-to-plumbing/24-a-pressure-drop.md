## Why it matters
Pressure is shared. Two fixtures on one small line split the push, and both run weak. Most weak-water calls are one of five things, and a gauge sorts them in a minute.

## Must know
- Two fixtures on one small line share its push. Both run weaker than either alone.
- {{figure:pressure-drop-strip|Two fixtures on one small line share the push. A half-shut valve or a pinched pipe does the same.}}
- A half-shut valve acts like a small pipe. A gate valve stuck half open starves the house.
- Height costs pressure. The second floor reads about **4 psi** lower than the first.
- A failing PRV reads low, or high, or swings. A gauge on a hose bib tells which.
- Old galvanized steel pipe closes up with rust inside. The house gets weaker year by year.
- A clogged aerator or a kinked supply line is a drop at one fixture only. A drop everywhere is upstream.
- A shower goes cold when a toilet flushes because the cold line lost push to the toilet. A pressure balance valve in the shower fixes it.
- The street can drop too. A broken main or a hydrant in use pulls the whole block down.

## Traps
- Weak at one faucet is that faucet. Weak everywhere is the valve, the PRV, the pipe or the street.
- A stuck gate valve looks open. The handle turns and nothing moves inside.
- Hot weak and cold strong is the heater's side, a valve or sediment, not the street.

## Numbers
- **4 psi** lost for every 10 ft of height
- **a half-shut valve** acts like a small pipe
- **a PRV** that fails reads low or high
- **a gauge** on a hose bib tells which

## Check yourself
1. The shower is weak but the kitchen is fine. Where is the trouble?
- [x] At the shower, its head or its supply line
- [ ] At the PRV
- [ ] At the street
- [ ] At the water heater
> right: One weak fixture is that fixture. Everywhere weak is upstream.
> wrong: Weak at one place is that place. The PRV and the street would weaken the whole house.

2. What does a gate valve stuck half open do to the house?
- [ ] Nothing, half is enough
- [x] Starves it, like a small pipe in the line
- [ ] Raises the pressure
- [ ] Makes the water hot
> right: The half-open gate is a choke point for every fixture after it.
> wrong: A half-shut valve acts like a small pipe and starves everything past it.

3. Why does the shower go cold when a toilet flushes?
- [ ] The heater turned off
- [x] The cold line lost push to the toilet, so the mix went hot
- [ ] The vent is blocked
- [ ] The PRV opened
> right: Less cold reaches the shower, so the mix swings hot and then cold.
> wrong: The toilet takes the cold line's push. A pressure balance valve in the shower holds the mix.

4. What tells you whether a PRV has failed?
- [ ] The sound of the pipes
- [ ] The water bill
- [x] A gauge reading on a hose bib
- [ ] The age of the house
> right: The gauge reads the house side. Low, high or swinging says the PRV.
> wrong: Read a gauge on a hose bib. Feel and sound only hint.
