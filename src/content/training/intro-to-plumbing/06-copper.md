## Why it matters
Copper has carried water in houses for a hundred years. It is joined with heat and solder. Knowing how a joint works tells you why some leak and most never do.

## Must know
- Copper pipe comes in three wall thicknesses. Type **M** is thin, **L** is medium and **K** is thick.
- {{figure:copper-types|Type M prints red, L blue and K green. The thin one is M, the thick one is K.}}
- Houses use type M or L. Underground service lines use type K.
- The common sizes in a house are **1/2 in** and **3/4 in**.
- A copper joint is soldered. The pipe sits inside the fitting's cup with a hair of a gap around it.
- {{figure:copper-sweat-joint|The heat pulls the solder into the gap between the pipe and the fitting.}}
- The torch heats the fitting, not the solder. The solder touches the mouth of the cup and the heat draws it in.
- Both faces are cleaned bright and brushed with flux first. Dirt or water in the joint means a leak.
- Solder for drinking water has been **lead-free** since **1986**.
- Copper can also be joined with press fittings, which crimp on with a tool and need no heat.

## Traps
- Melting solder onto a cold joint makes a blob, not a seal. Heat the fitting until the solder flows on its own.
- A drop of water in the pipe keeps the joint from reaching heat. Drain the line first.
- Type K is the thick one, not the thin one. The letters do not run thin to thick in order.

## Numbers
- **M, L, K** thin to thick
- **1/2 and 3/4 in** the common sizes
- **lead-free** solder since 1986

## Check yourself
1. Which copper type has the thickest wall?
- [ ] Type M
- [ ] Type L
- [x] Type K
- [ ] They are all the same
> right: K is the thick one, used underground.
> wrong: Type K is the thickest. M is thin, L is in between.

2. What does the torch heat when soldering?
- [ ] The solder
- [x] The fitting
- [ ] The flux
- [ ] The water inside
> right: The hot fitting draws the solder into the gap.
> wrong: Heat the fitting. The solder melts when it touches the hot joint, not the flame.

3. Why must the pipe be dry before soldering?
- [ ] Water rusts copper
- [x] Water keeps the joint from reaching soldering heat
- [ ] Water washes the flux away
- [ ] It does not matter
> right: Water boils off the heat. The joint never gets hot enough to flow.
> wrong: A drop of water steals the heat, so the solder never flows. Drain the line first.

4. Since when has solder for drinking water been lead-free?
- [ ] 1950
- [ ] 1970
- [x] 1986
- [ ] 2010
> right: 1986 is the year lead solder left drinking water lines.
> wrong: Lead-free solder has been the rule for drinking water since 1986.
