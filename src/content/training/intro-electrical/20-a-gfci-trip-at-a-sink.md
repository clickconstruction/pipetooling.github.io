## Why it matters
A GFCI at a sink trips when something near the water leaks a little current. Often that something is the plumber's own tool or a damp cord. The strip is what happens and what the plumber does.

## Must know
- A wet hand, a damp tool or a worn cord leaks a little current. It goes to the ground or the water.
- {{figure:gfci-trip-strip|A damp cord leaks current. The GFCI sees it leave and trips. Dry the cord. Press Reset once. If it will not hold, call.}}
- The GFCI sees the current leave and trips. 5 mA was enough, and nobody felt a thing.
- Every outlet past the GFCI on its circuit is dead too. A dead outlet across the room can be this GFCI.
- The first step is the cause. Dry the cord, unplug the tool, move the water.
- Then Reset, once. The button clicks and the outlet comes back.
- If the GFCI will not hold, the leak is still there. The GFCI is working and the circuit is a call.
- A GFCI breaker does the same job from the panel. Its reset is the breaker's handle, off then on.
- A tool that trips a GFCI every time has a leak inside it. The tool leaves the job, not the GFCI.

## Traps
- Reset is not the first step. The cause comes first, or it trips again.
- A GFCI that keeps tripping is not a bad GFCI. It is a leak that keeps leaking.
- A dead outlet is not a dead circuit. It can sit past a GFCI that tripped.

## Numbers
- **Reset** once, after the cause
- **5 mA** was enough
- **the outlets past it** are dead too

## Check yourself
1. What is the first step after a GFCI trips at a sink?
- [ ] Press Reset
- [x] Find and fix the cause, dry the cord or unplug the tool
- [ ] Reset the breaker
- [ ] Replace the GFCI
> right: The cause first. Reset after.
> wrong: Reset without the cause fixed trips again. Dry it, unplug it, then Reset once.

2. A GFCI will not hold after Reset. What does that mean?
- [ ] The GFCI is worn out
- [x] The leak is still there
- [ ] The breaker is off
- [ ] The outlet needs a ground
> right: It is holding the trip because current is still leaking.
> wrong: A GFCI that will not hold is working. The leak is still on the circuit.

3. An outlet across the room is dead. What might it be?
- [ ] The meter was pulled
- [x] It is past the GFCI that tripped, on the same circuit
- [ ] The neutral is too long
- [ ] The light switch is off
> right: One GFCI protects a string of outlets, and the whole string goes dead.
> wrong: Outlets past a tripped GFCI are dead too. Look for the one with the buttons.

4. A tool trips the GFCI every time it is plugged in. What is done?
- [ ] The GFCI is replaced
- [ ] The tool is plugged in somewhere else
- [x] The tool leaves the job
- [ ] The ground pin is removed
> right: The tool is leaking inside. It leaves service.
> wrong: A tool that trips every GFCI has a leak in it. It leaves the job, not the outlet.
