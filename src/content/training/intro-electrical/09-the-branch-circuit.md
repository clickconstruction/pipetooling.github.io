## Why it matters
Everything past the panel is a branch circuit. One breaker, one run of wire and the outlets on it. Knowing what shares a breaker tells you what else goes dead with it.

## Must know
- A branch circuit is one breaker, one wire run and everything on it.
- The breaker's number is the most amps the wire may carry. Past it, the breaker trips.
- A general circuit is 15 A or 20 A. It feeds lights and outlets in a few rooms.
- Some loads get a circuit of their own. The dishwasher, the disposal, the water heater and the dryer.
- The code counts watts, not outlets. A circuit is sized for the load it will see.
- Outlets on one circuit share it. A heater on one outlet leaves less for the tool on the next.
- The kitchen has at least two 20 A circuits for the counters. The bathroom has its own 20 A circuit.
- A circuit off at the breaker is dead to the last outlet on it. Something still live is on another breaker.
- The panel's directory names each breaker. It is often wrong, and a tester is the truth.

## Traps
- An outlet dead is not the circuit dead. The outlet may be downstream of a tripped GFCI.
- Two outlets side by side can be two circuits. Each one is tested on its own.
- The directory is a guess until it is checked. Trust the tester, not the label.

## Numbers
- **15 A or 20 A** a general circuit
- **one breaker** per branch
- **2** 20 A circuits for the kitchen counters, at least
- **the code counts watts** not outlets

## Check yourself
1. What is a branch circuit?
- [ ] The wires from the pole to the panel
- [x] One breaker, one wire run and the outlets on it
- [ ] The two bus bars
- [ ] The ground path
> right: Everything past one breaker is its branch circuit.
> wrong: A branch circuit is one breaker and all it feeds. The service wires are before the panel.

2. What does a 20 A breaker limit?
- [x] The amps the wire behind it may carry
- [ ] The number of outlets on it
- [ ] The volts on the circuit
- [ ] How long a tool can run
> right: The breaker is there for the wire.
> wrong: The breaker limits amps for the wire. Outlets and run time are not what it counts.

3. Which of these usually gets a circuit of its own?
- [ ] A bedroom lamp
- [ ] A hallway light
- [x] The dishwasher
- [ ] A phone charger
> right: The dishwasher is a fixed appliance and takes its own circuit.
> wrong: Lamps and chargers share general circuits. The dishwasher gets its own.

4. The panel directory says a breaker feeds the garage. What does that prove?
- [ ] The garage is dead when it is off
- [ ] The garage is live when it is on
- [x] Nothing until a tester says so
- [ ] The garage has a GFCI
> right: Directories are often wrong. The tester is the truth.
> wrong: A label is a guess. Only a tester says what is dead.
