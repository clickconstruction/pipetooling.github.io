## Questions
1. Which two wires make the loop that does the work?
- [x] The hot and the neutral
- [ ] The hot and the ground
- [ ] The neutral and the ground
- [ ] The two grounds
> right: Out on the hot, back on the neutral.
> wrong: The loop is the hot out and the neutral back. The ground waits for a fault.

2. What does the word live mean for a wire?
- [ ] Current is flowing in it right now
- [x] It is ready to push current the moment a path appears
- [ ] It is the neutral
- [ ] It is warm
> right: A live wire may carry nothing until you become the path.
> wrong: Live is about the push, not the flow. A live wire with no current is still dangerous.

3. Which part of the service belongs to the utility?
- [ ] The branch circuits
- [ ] The main panel
- [x] The drop and the meter
- [ ] The water heater's breaker
> right: Up to and including the meter is theirs.
> wrong: The wires from the pole and the meter are the utility's. The panel is the house's.

4. With the main breaker off, what in the panel is still live?
- [ ] Nothing
- [x] The lugs above the main
- [ ] The ground bar
- [ ] Every branch breaker
> right: The service wires land above the main and stay live.
> wrong: The main kills what is below it. The lugs above it are live until the utility pulls the meter.

5. What feeds a subpanel?
- [ ] The meter directly
- [x] One breaker in the main panel
- [ ] The ground rod
- [ ] Its own service drop
> right: One breaker in the main panel, and that breaker is its off.
> wrong: A subpanel hangs off one breaker in the main panel.

6. Which word is like gallons per minute?
- [ ] Volts
- [x] Amps
- [ ] Watts
- [ ] Gauge
> right: Amps is the flow.
> wrong: Amps is the flow. Volts is the push and watts is the work.

7. How many hots does a 240 V circuit use?
- [ ] One
- [x] Two
- [ ] Three
- [ ] None, it uses the neutral twice
> right: Two hots, 120 V each to the neutral, 240 V between them.
> wrong: A 240 V circuit runs between the two hots.

8. What colour is the neutral in the wall?
- [ ] Black
- [ ] Red
- [x] White
- [ ] Green
> right: White is the neutral.
> wrong: The neutral is white. Black and red are hots, and green or bare is the ground.

9. In a subpanel, do the neutral and the ground join?
- [ ] Yes, in every panel
- [x] No, only in the main panel
- [ ] Only if the subpanel is outside
- [ ] Only on 240 V circuits
> right: One bond, at the service, in the main panel.
> wrong: They join once, in the main panel. A subpanel keeps them apart.

10. What is bonding?
- [ ] Gluing a joint
- [x] Tying metal parts together so they are all on the ground
- [ ] Running a wire to the rod
- [ ] Taping a splice
> right: Bonding ties the metal together. Grounding takes it to the earth.
> wrong: Bonding is metal tied to metal, all on the ground path. The rod is grounding.

11. What is the rod outside the house for?
- [ ] Holding the drop up
- [x] Giving the ground a path into the earth
- [ ] Carrying the neutral
- [ ] Lightning only
> right: The rod is an electrode. The ground ends in the earth there.
> wrong: The rod takes the ground into the earth. It is not for the neutral or the drop.

12. How much metal pipe in the earth makes the water pipe an electrode?
- [ ] 1 ft
- [ ] 5 ft
- [x] 10 ft or more
- [ ] 100 ft
> right: Ten feet of metal in the earth, and the pipe counts as an electrode.
> wrong: The pipe is an electrode once 10 ft or more of it runs in the earth.

13. Where do you look for a jumper before a cut in a metal water line?
- [ ] At the water heater only
- [x] Around the meter, a filter, a softener and any gap
- [ ] On the gas line
- [ ] Under the kitchen sink
> right: Anywhere a section can come out, the jumper goes round it.
> wrong: The jumper goes round the meter and anything else that can come out of the line.

14. Who moves a jumper that is in the way?
- [ ] The plumber
- [ ] The homeowner
- [x] The electrician
- [ ] The inspector
> right: A plumber never removes one. The electrician moves it.
> wrong: The jumper is the electrician's. The plumber leaves it and asks.

15. How many 20 A circuits serve the kitchen counters, at least?
- [ ] One
- [x] Two
- [ ] Four
- [ ] None, the counters share the lights
> right: Two small-appliance circuits at least.
> wrong: The counters get at least two 20 A circuits of their own.

16. What does the code count when it sizes a circuit?
- [x] Watts
- [ ] Outlets
- [ ] Rooms
- [ ] Switches
> right: The load in watts, not the number of outlets.
> wrong: The code counts watts. Outlets are not the measure.

17. Which breaker goes with 14 AWG wire?
- [x] 15 A
- [ ] 20 A
- [ ] 30 A
- [ ] 50 A
> right: 14 AWG and 15 A are a pair.
> wrong: 14 AWG takes a 15 A breaker. 12 AWG takes 20 A and 10 AWG takes 30 A.

18. What is a short?
- [ ] Too many tools on one circuit
- [x] The hot touching the neutral or the ground
- [ ] A wire that is too small
- [ ] A breaker that is too big
> right: Hot to neutral or hot to ground, and the current spikes.
> wrong: A short is the hot touching the neutral or the ground. Too many tools is an overload.

19. How fast does an overload trip a breaker?
- [ ] At once
- [x] In minutes
- [ ] In days
- [ ] Never
> right: The breaker warms and opens in minutes. A short trips it at once.
> wrong: An overload takes minutes. A short is the one that trips at once.

20. A breaker trips again right after one reset. What does that mean?
- [ ] The breaker is worn out
- [ ] Reset it again until it holds
- [x] Something on the circuit is wrong, and it is a call
- [ ] The house needs a bigger breaker
> right: One reset. A second trip means a fault that is not yours to find.
> wrong: A breaker that trips again is doing its job. It is a call, not a second reset.

21. What does a warm breaker cover tell you?
- [ ] Nothing, breakers run warm
- [x] Something is wrong, say so and do not reset it
- [ ] The house is using 240 V
- [ ] The panel needs a bigger main
> right: Heat at a breaker is a sign. Say so and leave it.
> wrong: A warm breaker or cover is a warning. It is not reset.
