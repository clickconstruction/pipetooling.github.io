## Why it matters
The metal water pipe is part of the ground. Plumbers cut it, pull meters out of it and replace sections with plastic. One wire keeps the ground whole while they do, and it is the one wire a plumber must never remove.

## Must know
- The metal water pipe is part of the ground path. A fault can run along it to the earth.
- {{figure:bonding-jumper|The ground runs through the metal water pipe. A jumper carries it around the meter. The jumper stays, whatever comes out of the pipe.}}
- A jumper is a wire with a clamp at each end. It carries the ground around a gap in the pipe.
- The jumper goes around the water meter, a filter, a softener and anything else that can come out.
- With the jumper on, the meter can come out and the ground path stays whole.
- With the jumper off, the gap is open. A fault in the house has no way to the earth through the pipe.
- A plastic section in a metal run is a gap for good. The ground stops at the plastic.
- A plumber never removes a jumper. If it is in the way, the electrician moves it.
- A metal to plastic change gets a word to the electrician first. They may need a new jumper or a rod.
- The jumper is small and dull and easy to miss. Look for it before any cut in a metal water line.

## Traps
- A jumper is not clutter. Clamps and a bare wire around the meter are the ground going round the gap.
- A plastic fitting that holds the meter opens the gap with no cut. So does a plastic filter. The jumper still has to be there.
- The gap is not safe just because the water is off. The ground has nothing to do with the water.

## Numbers
- **a jumper** around the meter, a filter and any break
- **never** remove a jumper
- **tell the electrician** before a metal to plastic change

## Check yourself
1. What does the jumper around the water meter do?
- [ ] Stops the meter from leaking
- [x] Carries the ground around the meter
- [ ] Bonds the meter to the gas line
- [ ] Holds the meter in place
> right: It keeps the ground path whole with the meter out.
> wrong: The jumper is the ground going round a gap. It has nothing to do with water.

2. A jumper is in the way of your cut. What do you do?
- [ ] Take it off and put it back after
- [ ] Cut it, it is only a wire
- [x] Leave it, and ask the electrician to move it
- [ ] Hang it on the next pipe
> right: A plumber never removes a jumper. The electrician moves it.
> wrong: The jumper stays. If it must move, the electrician moves it.

3. You replace a metal section with plastic. What happens to the ground path?
- [ ] Nothing, water still flows
- [x] It stops at the plastic
- [ ] It moves to the gas line
- [ ] It gets stronger
> right: Plastic does not carry current. The path ends there.
> wrong: Plastic is a gap in the ground path. The electrician needs to know before the change.

4. Who gets a word before a metal to plastic change in a water line?
- [ ] The inspector only
- [ ] Nobody, it is plumbing
- [x] The electrician
- [ ] The utility
> right: The change opens the ground path, and the electrician decides what replaces it.
> wrong: A metal to plastic change is an electrical question too. The electrician hears first.
