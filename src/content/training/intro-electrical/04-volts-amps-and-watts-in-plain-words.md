## Why it matters
Three words come up on every label and every breaker. Volts, amps and watts. Each one matches something you already know from water.

## Must know
- Volts is the push. It is like pressure in a pipe.
- Amps is the flow. It is like gallons per minute.
- Watts is the work. Push times flow is work, like a shower that needs both pressure and flow.
- {{figure:volts-amps-watts|Volts is the push. Amps is the flow. Watts is the work they do together.}}
- Volts times amps equals watts. A 120 V circuit at 15 A can do 1,800 W of work.
- A wall outlet pushes at 120 V. A big appliance pushes at 240 V.
- A breaker's number is amps. It is the most flow the wire behind it may carry.
- A tool's label says watts or amps. Either one tells you how hard it pulls on the circuit.
- The same work at twice the volts takes half the amps. That is why big loads run on 240 V.
- Volts hurt. Amps kill. A tiny flow through the chest is enough, and the OSHA refresher gives the numbers.

## Traps
- Low volts is not no danger. A car battery at 12 V can weld a wrench to its posts.
- A breaker's amps is not the tool's amps. The breaker limits the wire, and the tool pulls what it pulls.
- Watts is not a speed. It is how much work is being done right now.

## Numbers
- **volts × amps = watts** the one rule
- **120 V** the push on a wall outlet, 240 V on a big appliance
- **1,800 W** the work 120 V at 15 A can do

## Check yourself
1. Which word is like pressure in a pipe?
- [x] Volts
- [ ] Amps
- [ ] Watts
- [ ] Gallons
> right: Volts is the push.
> wrong: Volts is the push, like pressure. Amps is the flow and watts is the work.

2. A 120 V circuit carries 15 A. How many watts is that?
- [ ] 135 W
- [ ] 8 W
- [x] 1,800 W
- [ ] 15,000 W
> right: 120 times 15 is 1,800.
> wrong: Volts times amps is watts. 120 times 15 is 1,800 W.

3. Why do big loads run on 240 V instead of 120 V?
- [ ] 240 V is safer
- [x] The same work takes half the amps
- [ ] 240 V wire is cheaper
- [ ] The code says every appliance must
> right: Twice the push means half the flow for the same work, so the wire stays smaller.
> wrong: Doubling the volts halves the amps for the same watts. That is the reason.

4. What does the number on a breaker tell you?
- [ ] The volts on the circuit
- [x] The most amps the wire behind it may carry
- [ ] How many outlets are on it
- [ ] The watts of the biggest tool
> right: The breaker's number is amps, and it protects the wire.
> wrong: The breaker's number is amps. It is the limit for the wire, not a count of outlets.
