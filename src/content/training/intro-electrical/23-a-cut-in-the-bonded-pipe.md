## Why it matters
Lesson 8 was the jumper at rest. This is the day it goes wrong. A plumber cuts out a section of metal water pipe. The ground path now runs through the two pipe ends and whoever holds them.

## Must know
- The metal water pipe is part of the ground path. Current from a fault anywhere upstream can be running along it.
- {{figure:cut-pipe-strip|The ground runs through the pipe. The jumper is on, and the cut is safe. The jumper is off, and the person holding both ends is the path.}}
- With a jumper across the section, the ground goes round the gap. The cut opens nothing.
- With no jumper, the cut opens the ground path. The two pipe ends are the two sides of a gap.
- A fault upstream now has no way to the earth. Or it finds one, through the person holding both ends.
- The current need not be a fault. A neutral that shares the pipe can put current on it every day.
- A plastic section in a metal run is the same gap, for good. The ground stops where the plastic starts.
- A temporary jumper across a cut is the electrician's call, not the plumber's. The plumber asks before the cut.
- A metal to plastic change gets a word to the electrician before the job. A new electrode or jumper may need to go in.
- The pipe is never the only ground. The rod is there too. But the pipe is the one a plumber opens.

## Traps
- The water off is not the ground off. The ground has nothing to do with the water.
- A short section is not a safe cut. The gap is a gap whatever its length.
- A plastic repair coupling is a gap too. It is the same as a plastic section.

## Numbers
- **the jumper** before the cut
- **both ends** of the gap are the path without it
- **plastic** in a metal run is the same gap, for good
- **tell the electrician** before a metal to plastic change
- **a temporary jumper** is the electrician's call

## Check yourself
1. What happens to the ground path when a metal water pipe is cut with no jumper?
- [ ] Nothing, the rod takes over
- [x] It opens, and the two pipe ends are the gap
- [ ] It moves to the gas line
- [ ] It gets shorter
> right: The gap is in the path, and the person across it is the way round.
> wrong: A cut with no jumper opens the ground path. Whoever holds both ends is the path.

2. Who decides on a temporary jumper across a cut?
- [ ] The plumber
- [ ] The homeowner
- [x] The electrician
- [ ] The inspector
> right: The plumber asks before the cut. The electrician decides.
> wrong: A jumper is the electrician's. The plumber asks before cutting.

3. Does turning the water off make the cut safe?
- [ ] Yes, no water means no current
- [x] No, the ground has nothing to do with the water
- [ ] Only if the meter is pulled
- [ ] Yes, after an hour
> right: Current on the pipe does not care about the water in it.
> wrong: The water off changes nothing for the ground. The jumper is what makes the cut safe.

4. A plastic repair coupling goes into a metal line. What is it to the ground path?
- [ ] A better ground
- [x] A gap, the same as a plastic section
- [ ] Nothing, it is short
- [ ] A new electrode
> right: Plastic carries no current, long or short.
> wrong: A plastic coupling is a gap in the ground path. The electrician hears before it goes in.
