## Why it matters
A tool on a cord on a wet floor is the path waiting for a person. The numbers that hurt are small, and a breaker never sees them. The GFCI is the only thing between the two.

## Must know
- A wet floor, a wet hand and a cord with a nick are the three parts of a shock. One person makes the loop.
- {{figure:wet-floor-strip|A cord on a wet floor. The tool's frame goes live. The GFCI trips before the person is the path. Without one, the person is.}}
- Through a wet hand, across the chest and out the feet is the path that kills.
- {{figure:current-scale|The GFCI trips at the first tick. The breaker sits so far up that everything that hurts you happens below it.}}
- The numbers are the OSHA refresher's. 1 mA is a tingle. 5 mA is a slight shock.
- About 30 mA locks your muscles so you cannot let go. 50 to 150 mA stops breathing. 200 mA can stop the heart.
- A breaker trips at 15,000 mA. It protects the wiring, not you.
- A GFCI trips at 5 mA in 1/40 s. That is the first tick on the scale, and the only protection that is for a person.
- Every cord on a job runs through a GFCI. A cord with no GFCI on a wet floor is a cord nobody uses.
- A tool with a frayed cord leaves the job. Tape is not a fix.

## Traps
- A wet floor is not safe because the tool is double insulated. The cord's nick is the hazard, not the tool.
- The breaker is not between you and a shock. 15 A is thousands of times what stops a heart.
- A tingle is not nothing. It is 1 mA already finding a path through you.

## Numbers
- **1 mA** a tingle
- **5 mA** a slight shock, and the GFCI trips
- **30 mA** you cannot let go
- **50 to 150 mA** breathing stops
- **200 mA** can stop the heart
- **15,000 mA** the breaker trips
- **a GFCI** on every cord on a job
