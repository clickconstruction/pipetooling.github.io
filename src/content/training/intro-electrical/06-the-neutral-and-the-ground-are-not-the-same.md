## Why it matters
Two wires go back to the panel and both end up at the earth. One works all day and the other waits. Mixing them up is the mistake behind a lot of shocks.

## Must know
- The neutral carries current every time something runs. It is the way back for the loop.
- The ground carries current only when something has gone wrong. On a good day it carries nothing.
- {{figure:neutral-vs-ground|The neutral carries the current back every day. The ground carries it only when something has gone wrong. They join once, in the main panel.}}
- The neutral is white. The ground is bare copper or green.
- The neutral and the ground meet in one place, the main panel. Nowhere else.
- In a subpanel they stay apart. The neutral bar sits on plastic feet and the ground bar bolts to the box.
- The ground is there for a fault. A hot wire that touches a metal case puts current on the ground wire.
- That fault current runs back to the panel and trips the breaker. Without a ground, the case stays live and waits for a hand.
- A neutral can bite. It carries current, and a broken neutral can sit at 120 V.
- Metal boxes, metal pipes and the frames of appliances are tied to the ground. That is bonding.

## Traps
- White is not safe. The neutral carries the same current as the hot, every day.
- The ground wire is not a place for a neutral to land. Joined anywhere but the main panel, the ground carries current all day.
- A ground wire with current on it means a fault somewhere. It is a call, not a plumbing problem.

## Numbers
- **the neutral** carries current all day
- **the ground** carries it only in a fault
- **1** place they join, the main panel

## Check yourself
1. Which wire carries current every time something runs?
- [ ] The ground
- [x] The neutral
- [ ] The clamp
- [ ] The cover plate
> right: The neutral is the way back for every loop.
> wrong: The neutral carries the current back every day. The ground only carries it in a fault.

2. Where do the neutral and the ground join?
- [ ] In every outlet box
- [ ] In the subpanel
- [x] In the main panel, and nowhere else
- [ ] At the water heater
> right: One bond at the service, in the main panel.
> wrong: They join once, in the main panel. Everywhere else they stay apart.

3. A hot wire touches the metal case of a washer. Where does the current go?
- [ ] Nowhere, the case is not a wire
- [x] Down the ground wire to the panel, and the breaker trips
- [ ] Into the water supply
- [ ] Into the neutral only
> right: The ground gives the fault a path back, and the breaker sees the surge and trips.
> wrong: The ground wire is for this. The fault runs back on it and trips the breaker.

4. Can a white wire shock you?
- [x] Yes, it carries current and a broken neutral can be at 120 V
- [ ] No, white means neutral and neutral is safe
- [ ] Only in a subpanel
- [ ] Only if it is wet
> right: White is the neutral and it carries the loop's current. Treat it as live.
> wrong: The neutral carries current every day. A broken one can sit at full voltage.
