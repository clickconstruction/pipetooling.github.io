## Why it matters
Two numbers run the whole house. The small loads get 120 V and the big ones get 240 V. The difference is which two wires you are between.

## Must know
- The two hots coming into the house are each 120 V to the neutral.
- One hot and the neutral make a 120 V circuit. That is every wall outlet and light.
- The two hots together make 240 V. That is the water heater, the dryer, the range and the AC.
- {{figure:one-twenty-two-forty|One hot and the neutral is 120 V. The two hots together is 240 V.}}
- A 240 V circuit uses a two-pole breaker, two breakers joined under one handle. It trips both hots at once.
- A 240 V load often has no neutral. Both wires are hot, and the current goes out on one and back on the other.
- A range or a dryer uses both hots and the neutral too. That is a 120/240 V circuit.
- Big loads take 240 V so the amps stay smaller and the wire stays smaller.

## Traps
- A red wire on a 240 V circuit is a hot, not a marker. It is as live as the black one.
- A two-pole breaker off still leaves the lugs above the main live. The rule from lesson 3 holds.
- On a 240 V circuit the second wire is not a neutral. Testing one wire dead proves nothing about the other.

## Numbers
- **120 V** one hot to the neutral
- **240 V** hot to hot
- **a two-pole breaker** for every 240 V circuit

## Check yourself
1. What makes 240 V in a house?
- [ ] One hot and the neutral
- [x] The two hots together
- [ ] The hot and the ground
- [ ] The neutral and the ground
> right: Each hot is 120 V to the neutral, and hot to hot is 240 V.
> wrong: One hot to the neutral is 120 V. The two hots together are 240 V.

2. Which of these runs on 240 V?
- [ ] A bathroom light
- [ ] A phone charger
- [x] An electric water heater
- [ ] A doorbell
> right: The water heater is a big load, and big loads take 240 V.
> wrong: Lights, chargers and doorbells are 120 V or less. The water heater is 240 V.

3. What kind of breaker does a 240 V circuit use?
- [ ] Two single breakers side by side with no tie
- [x] A two-pole breaker with one handle
- [ ] A light switch
- [ ] No breaker, the main covers it
> right: One handle trips both hots together.
> wrong: A 240 V circuit uses one two-pole breaker, so both hots trip at once.

4. On a 240 V water heater circuit, one wire tests dead. What does that tell you about the other?
- [ ] It is dead too
- [ ] It is the neutral
- [x] Nothing, it gets its own test
- [ ] It is the ground
> right: Both wires are hots. Each one is tested on its own.
> wrong: The second wire is a hot, not a neutral. It gets its own test.
