## Why it matters
The ground wire has to end somewhere. It ends in the earth, through a rod and through the metal water pipe. That second one is where plumbing comes in.

## Must know
- The ground wire from the panel runs to the earth. The thing it lands on is called the grounding electrode.
- One electrode is a rod driven into the ground outside. It is 8 ft long and often there are two.
- {{figure:ground-path|The ground runs from the panel to a rod in the earth and to the metal water pipe. A fault goes there and not through you.}}
- Another electrode is the metal water pipe from the street, if it runs 10 ft or more in the earth.
- The ground wire clamps onto the metal water pipe within the first 5 ft of pipe inside the wall.
- A metal water pipe inside the house is bonded too. The pipe is tied to the ground whether it is the electrode or not.
- A fault goes to the earth through these paths. That is a path with no person in it.
- A jumper carries the ground around the water meter, so pulling the meter does not break the path. The next lesson is the jumper.
- A plastic water service has no metal to the street. The rod does the job and the inside pipe is still bonded.
- The clamp on the pipe is the electrician's. It is not a hanger and not a place to hang a strap.

## Traps
- A clamp on your pipe is not in your way. It is the ground, and it stays.
- A rod with no wire on it is a ground that goes nowhere. Say so to the electrician.
- Bonded is not grounded. Bonding ties metal together. Grounding takes it to the earth.

## Numbers
- **1** rod, often 2
- **8 ft** long
- **10 ft** of metal pipe in the earth makes the water pipe an electrode
- **5 ft** inside the wall is where the clamp goes
- **a jumper** around the meter

## Check yourself
1. Where does the ground wire from the panel end?
- [ ] At the neutral bar
- [x] In the earth, at a rod and the metal water pipe
- [ ] At the meter base
- [ ] At the water heater
> right: The earth is the end of the ground path.
> wrong: The ground ends in the earth, through a rod and the metal water pipe.

2. How long is a ground rod?
- [ ] 2 ft
- [ ] 4 ft
- [x] 8 ft
- [ ] 20 ft
> right: Eight feet into the earth.
> wrong: A ground rod is 8 ft long.

3. Where does the ground wire clamp onto the metal water pipe?
- [x] Within the first 5 ft of pipe inside the wall
- [ ] At the water heater
- [ ] At the farthest fixture
- [ ] Outside at the curb
> right: Close to where the pipe comes in, so nothing in the house can break the path before it.
> wrong: The clamp goes within 5 ft of where the pipe enters the house.

4. You find a clamp and a bare wire on the water pipe you need to work on. What is it?
- [ ] A hanger
- [ ] A leftover from the old meter
- [x] The ground, and it stays
- [ ] A water line for the ice maker
> right: The clamp is the electrician's ground. Work around it.
> wrong: A clamp with a bare or green wire is the ground. It is not yours to move.
