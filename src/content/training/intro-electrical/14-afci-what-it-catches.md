## Why it matters
Fires start in walls where nobody sees them. An arc is a tiny spark that jumps a gap in a loose or damaged wire. It burns below any breaker's trip point. The AFCI listens for it.

## Must know
- An arc is current jumping a gap through the air. A loose screw, a cracked wire, a nail through a cable.
- {{figure:afci-listens|An AFCI listens for the arc of a loose wire or a nail through a cable. It trips before the fire. It is not a GFCI.}}
- An arc is hot enough to light wood and paper. It can burn for a long time below a breaker's trip point.
- An AFCI listens for the pattern of an arc on the wire and trips when it hears one.
- It is not a GFCI. A GFCI catches current leaving the circuit. An AFCI catches current jumping inside it.
- It usually lives in the breaker at the panel. Its handle looks like a GFCI breaker's, with a Test button.
- It is required in bedrooms and most living areas. The code cycle sets the list, and the lessons give the company's rule.
- A nail or a screw through a cable is what it is for. A pipe strap screwed into a cable is one.
- An AFCI that trips with one tool running may be hearing that tool's motor. Say so, and the electrician decides.

## Traps
- An AFCI trip is not a GFCI trip. Nothing leaked, something arced.
- A cable behind drywall is not safe from a screw. Lesson 28 is where cables sit in the stud.
- An AFCI breaker is not a bigger breaker. It trips at the same amps and listens as well.

## Numbers
- **arcs** not shocks
- **bedrooms and living areas** where it is required
- **the breaker** is where it usually lives
- **a nail through a cable** is what it is for

## Check yourself
1. What does an AFCI catch?
- [ ] A shock through a person
- [x] An arc inside the wiring
- [ ] Too many amps
- [ ] A short to the ground
> right: It listens for the pattern of an arc and trips before the fire.
> wrong: An AFCI catches arcs. Shocks are the GFCI's and amps are the breaker's.

2. Where does an AFCI usually live?
- [ ] In every outlet
- [x] In the breaker at the panel
- [ ] At the meter
- [ ] In the light switch
> right: Most AFCIs are breakers with a Test button.
> wrong: An AFCI is usually a breaker in the panel.

3. Which is a job for an AFCI and not a GFCI?
- [ ] A wet cord on the floor
- [x] A nail through a cable in the wall
- [ ] A tool with a frayed cord in a puddle
- [ ] A hair dryer in a sink
> right: A nail through a cable arcs. The AFCI hears it.
> wrong: Water and people are the GFCI's. An arc in a cable is the AFCI's.

4. A pipe strap's screw goes into a cable behind the drywall. What can follow?
- [ ] Nothing, the cable is insulated
- [x] An arc in the wall, and the AFCI trips
- [ ] The water pressure drops
- [ ] The GFCI trips
> right: A screw in a cable is the arc the AFCI is for.
> wrong: A screw through a cable can arc and start a fire. The AFCI is the one that catches it.
