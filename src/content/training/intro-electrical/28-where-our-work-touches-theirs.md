## Why it matters
Five places on a plumbing job meet a circuit. Each one has one rule. Knowing the five is most of staying safe around the other trade.

## Must know
- The water heater. Its circuit is off at the breaker and tested dead before a cover comes off, under the company's rule.
- {{figure:five-touch-points|Five places a plumbing job meets a circuit. The water heater, the sink, the pumps, the jumper, and the holes you drill. The cable runs through the middle of the stud, and the hole goes where it is not.}}
- Under the sink. The disposal's outlet and the dishwasher's box are live with the switch off.
- The pumps. The sump and the well start by themselves. The plug is pulled or the breaker is off before a hand goes in.
- The jumper. The bonding jumper on the metal water pipe stays. A metal to plastic change gets a word to the electrician.
- The holes. A cable in a stud sits set back from the stud's face, and a plate covers it where it cannot.
- A cable sits at least 1-1/4 in back from the face of the stud. A screw or a nail shorter than that misses it.
- {{figure:stud-setback|A cable sits 1-1/4 in back from the stud's face, so a normal screw misses it. Closer than that, a steel plate covers it.}}
- Where a cable is closer than that, a steel nail plate covers it. The plate is the sign a cable is behind.
- A drill into a cable is a short, a fire or a shock. A long screw into a cable is an arc behind the drywall.
- Before a hole in a wall, a floor or a joist, the question is what runs through it. A tester and a look are cheap.

## Traps
- A wall that looks empty is not empty. Cables run where the holes are easiest, and so do pipes.
- A nail plate is not a hanger plate. It says a cable is right behind it.
- A strap screw is as bad as a nail. Lesson 14's arc is a strap screw through a cable.

## Numbers
- **5** places our work touches theirs
- **1-1/4 in** from the stud's face is where cables sit
- **a nail plate** where a cable sits closer

## Check yourself

> right: Set back so a normal screw or nail misses it.
> wrong: A cable sits at least 1-1/4 in back from the face. Closer than that takes a nail plate.

2. What does a steel nail plate on a stud tell you?
- [ ] The stud is load bearing
- [x] A cable or a pipe runs right behind it
- [ ] The wall is fire rated
- [ ] A hanger goes there
> right: The plate protects what is too close to the face.
> wrong: A nail plate covers a cable or a pipe that sits too close to the stud's face.

3. Which of the five places starts by itself?
- [ ] The water heater
- [ ] The jumper
- [x] The pumps
- [ ] The disposal
> right: The sump and the well start on a float or a pressure switch.
> wrong: The pumps start with nobody there. The plug is pulled before a hand goes in.

4. What happens when a long screw goes through a cable behind the drywall?
- [ ] Nothing, the cable is insulated
- [x] An arc, a short or a shock
- [ ] The water pressure drops
- [ ] The GFCI resets
> right: A screw through a cable is the arc of lesson 14, or a short, or a shock.
> wrong: A screw in a cable can arc, short or shock. The setback and the plate are there to stop it.
