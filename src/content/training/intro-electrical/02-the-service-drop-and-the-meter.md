## Why it matters
Power reaches the house before anyone in the house can switch it off. The wires from the pole and the meter on the wall are the utility's, the power company's. They are always live. Know where that live zone ends.

## Must know
- Power arrives on three wires from the pole or from underground. Two are hots and one is the neutral.
- {{figure:service-and-meter|Three wires come from the pole to the meter. Everything on the street side of the meter stays live.}}
- The service drop is the overhead run from the pole to the house. Underground it is called a service lateral.
- The wires land at the weatherhead, a hood on the roof or the wall, and run down to the meter.
- The meter counts the power used. It sits in a base on the outside wall, and it is the utility's.
- Everything on the street side of the meter is the utility's. Nothing in the house turns it off.
- The main breaker is after the meter. With the main off, the meter and the wires to it are still live.
- Only the utility kills the power at the meter. They pull the meter or cut the drop.
- A meter base with no cover or a drop hanging low is a call to the utility, not a job.
- Water near the meter base is an electrical emergency. The water shuts off somewhere else.

## Traps
- The main breaker off is not the house dead. The service wires above it are live.
- A ladder or a length of pipe raised near the drop can reach a live wire. Look up before you lift.
- The meter is not a switch. Pulling it is the utility's job and nobody else's.

## Numbers
- **3** wires come in, two hots and a neutral
- **the utility's** up to the meter
- **never off** even with the main breaker off

## Check yourself
1. Which part of the house's electrical system is never off?
- [ ] The kitchen outlets
- [x] The wires from the pole to the meter
- [ ] The water heater's circuit
- [ ] The lights
> right: The street side of the meter is the utility's and stays live.
> wrong: Outlets and lights go off at their breakers. The wires up to the meter stay live whatever you do.

2. How many wires come into a house from the pole?
- [ ] 1
- [ ] 2
- [x] 3
- [ ] 4
> right: Two hots and a neutral.
> wrong: Three wires come in. Two hots and one neutral.

3. Who can kill the power at the meter?
- [ ] The homeowner
- [ ] The plumber
- [ ] The electrician
- [x] The utility
> right: The meter and the drop are the utility's. Only they pull or cut it.
> wrong: Nobody in the trades touches the meter. The utility pulls it or cuts the drop.

4. A leak drips onto the meter base outside. What do you do?
- [ ] Open the base and dry it
- [ ] Throw the main breaker and keep going
- [x] Step back, shut the water off somewhere else, and call the utility
- [ ] Pull the meter
> right: The base is live and the main does not kill it. Water off somewhere else, then the call.
> wrong: Nothing in the house kills the meter base. Water off somewhere else, then the utility.
