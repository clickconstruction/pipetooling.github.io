## Why it matters
A receptacle is a wall outlet, the one electrical part a plumber plugs into all day. Its three holes are the three wires. Knowing which is which tells you what a tester is reading and why a plug only fits one way.

## Must know
- The three holes in a receptacle are the three wires. Each one lands on the back of the device.
- {{figure:receptacle-faces|The short slot is hot. The long slot is neutral. The round hole is ground. A 240 V face takes a different plug.}}
- The short slot is the hot. The long slot is the neutral. The round hole is the ground.
- A plug fits one way because the blades match the slots. The wide blade is the neutral.
- A 240 V receptacle has a different face. A 120 V plug cannot fit it, on purpose.
- A dryer's face and a range's face are different again. Each one says its amps by its shape.
- A receptacle with no round hole is an old two-wire outlet. It has no ground.
- A GFCI receptacle carries two buttons, Test and Reset. It trips when current leaks out of the loop, and lesson 13 explains it.
- A receptacle is live on its back and its screws whenever its circuit is on. The cover plate is what keeps fingers out.
- A plug-in tester shows which wires are right and which are swapped. Three lights, and the card on it says what they mean.

## Traps
- A plug that sits loose in an outlet is a worn outlet, not a bad plug. It warms up, and it is a call.
- An adapter that drops the ground pin drops the ground. The tool's case is no longer protected.
- A three-prong plug forced into a two-hole outlet has no ground. The case of the tool is waiting for a fault.

## Numbers
- **short** slot hot
- **long** slot neutral
- **round** hole ground

## Check yourself
1. Which slot of a receptacle is the hot?
- [x] The short one
- [ ] The long one
- [ ] The round hole
- [ ] Either slot
> right: Short is hot, long is neutral, round is ground.
> wrong: The short slot is the hot. The long slot is the neutral.

2. Why can a 120 V plug not fit a 240 V receptacle?
- [ ] The plug is too old
- [x] The faces are different on purpose
- [ ] 240 V receptacles have no slots
- [ ] It can, with an adapter
> right: The shape keeps a 120 V tool off a 240 V circuit.
> wrong: The faces differ so the wrong plug cannot go in. No adapter should make it fit.

3. What does the round hole carry?
- [ ] The hot
- [ ] The neutral
- [x] The ground
- [ ] Nothing, it is a guide for the plug
> right: The round hole is the ground pin's home.
> wrong: The round hole is the ground. It carries current only in a fault.

4. A plug sits loose and warm in an outlet. What is that?
- [ ] A bad plug
- [x] A worn outlet, and a call
- [ ] Normal for a big tool
- [ ] A tripped GFCI
> right: A loose, warm outlet is worn out and heating. It is the electrician's.
> wrong: The outlet is worn. Heat at an outlet is a sign, and it is a call.
