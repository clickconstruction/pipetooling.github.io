## Why it matters
Water and a live panel make a path. A leak above a panel is an electrical emergency before it is a plumbing one. The plumber's part is to stop the water somewhere else and not touch the panel.

## Must know
- Water in a panel or on a live box makes a path. It can carry current to the box, the floor and a person.
- {{figure:water-on-panel-strip|A leak drips onto the panel. The panel is live. Shut the water off at the main, not the power at the panel. Call.}}
- A wet panel is not touched. Not the cover, not the main breaker, not the door.
- The water is shut off somewhere else. The main shutoff for the house, or the valve above the leak.
- The power is killed by the utility at the meter if it has to be. Nobody in the house throws a wet main.
- A water heater sits in a pan with a drain for this reason. The pan catches the leak before it reaches anything live.
- A panel under a bathroom, a kitchen or a water heater is a leak waiting. The code keeps panels out of bathrooms for it.
- Water on the floor near a panel is the same rule. Nobody steps into it to reach the panel.
- After the water is off, the electrician reads the panel before anyone touches it. Water inside takes days to dry.

## Traps
- The main breaker is not the water shutoff. One is wet and live, the other is a valve.
- A dry cover is not a dry panel. The water runs down inside where the bus bars are.
- The water off is not the end. The panel is still live until the electrician says otherwise.

## Numbers
- **do not touch** the panel
- **the main shutoff** for the water, not the main breaker
- **the utility** can kill the power at the meter
- **the water heater pan** is why it has a drain

## Check yourself
1. A leak drips onto the panel. What is the first move?
- [ ] Throw the main breaker
- [ ] Dry the panel with a towel
- [x] Shut the water off somewhere else and step back
- [ ] Open the panel to see the damage
> right: The water stops at the main shutoff or the valve above. The panel is not touched.
> wrong: Nobody touches a wet panel. The water is shut off elsewhere, then the call.

2. Who kills the power to a wet panel if it has to be killed?
- [ ] The plumber at the main breaker
- [ ] The homeowner
- [x] The utility, at the meter
- [ ] The water heater's thermostat
> right: The street side is the utility's, and they can pull the meter.
> wrong: A wet main is not thrown by anyone in the house. The utility kills it at the meter.

3. Why does a water heater sit in a pan with a drain?
- [ ] To keep the floor clean
- [x] To catch a leak before it reaches anything live or anything below
- [ ] To hold the expansion tank
- [ ] The code needs a drain for the T&P valve
> right: The pan catches the water and the drain takes it away.
> wrong: The pan is for a leak. It keeps the water off what is below and around the heater.

4. The water is off and the cover looks dry. Can the panel be used?
- [ ] Yes, if the cover is dry
- [ ] Yes, after an hour
- [x] Not until the electrician has read it
- [ ] Yes, with gloves
> right: Water runs inside to the bus bars. The electrician reads it first.
> wrong: A dry cover says nothing about the inside. The electrician clears a wet panel.
