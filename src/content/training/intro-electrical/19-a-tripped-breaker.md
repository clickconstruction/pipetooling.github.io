## Why it matters
A breaker trips for a reason. Resetting it once is a plumber's call. Resetting it twice is hiding a fault. This strip is the whole rule in four frames.

## Must know
- A breaker trips because too much current went through it. A short, an overload, or a fault to the ground.
- {{figure:tripped-breaker-strip|The handle sits in the middle. Push it off, then on. If it trips again, stop and call.}}
- A tripped handle sits in the middle, between on and off. Some breakers show a red or orange flag.
- A reset is the handle pushed all the way off, then back to on. From the middle it will not go straight to on.
- One reset. If the breaker holds, something pulled too much for a moment, and the work goes on with care.
- If it trips again, the fault is still there. A second reset only heats whatever is wrong.
- A breaker that trips the moment it is reset is a short. A breaker that trips after a while is an overload or a bad connection.
- A warm breaker, a smell, or a buzz means the breaker itself is the problem. It is not reset at all.
- The reset is done with the panel's cover on, with one hand, standing to the side.
- A GFCI or an AFCI breaker has a Test button too. Its reset is the same handle, off then on.

## Traps
- A breaker in the middle is not on. It looks on from across the room and it is not.
- A second trip is not bad luck. It is the breaker saying the fault is still on the circuit.
- A breaker that will not reset is not stuck. It is holding the trip because the fault is live.

## Numbers
- **once** is how many times a plumber resets a breaker
- **the middle** position means tripped
- **a second trip** means call

## Check yourself
1. Where does a tripped breaker's handle sit?
- [ ] On
- [ ] Off
- [x] In the middle
- [ ] Pulled out of the panel
> right: Between on and off, often with a coloured flag.
> wrong: A tripped breaker sits in the middle. It has to go to off before it goes to on.

2. How many times does a plumber reset a breaker?
- [x] Once
- [ ] Twice
- [ ] Until it holds
- [ ] Never
> right: One reset. A second trip means a call.
> wrong: One reset is a plumber's call. After that the fault is the electrician's.

3. The breaker trips the instant it is reset. What is that?
- [ ] An overload
- [x] A short
- [ ] A tripped GFCI
- [ ] A bad bulb
> right: A short spikes the current and the breaker opens at once.
> wrong: A trip at the instant of reset is a short on the circuit.

4. The breaker is warm and buzzing. What do you do?
- [ ] Reset it with a glove on
- [ ] Reset it twice
- [x] Leave it, and call
- [ ] Tap it with a tool
> right: A warm or buzzing breaker is the problem itself. Nobody resets it.
> wrong: Heat or a buzz at a breaker is a fault in the breaker. It is a call, not a reset.
