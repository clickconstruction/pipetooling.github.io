## Why it matters
Under the kitchen sink are two electrical loads in a wet cabinet, and both get touched on a plumbing call. One runs on a switch and one runs on its own. Both boxes are live.

## Must know
- The disposal is a motor under the sink. It runs on a wall switch or an air switch on the counter.
- {{figure:under-the-sink|The disposal runs on a switched outlet. The dishwasher has its own circuit. Both boxes under the sink stay live with the switch off.}}
- The disposal plugs into an outlet under the sink that the switch controls, or it is wired direct.
- The dishwasher runs on its own circuit. It is often a 20 A circuit for the dishwasher alone.
- Both boxes under the sink are live whenever their breakers are on. The switch off does not change that.
- The disposal has a reset button on its bottom. It pops when the motor jams and overheats.
- A jammed disposal is freed with the wrench in the hole underneath, with the switch off and the plug pulled.
- A dishwasher's wiring box is behind its kick plate at the front. It is live with the door open or shut.
- Water pooled under a sink can reach the outlet and the boxes. That is a GFCI's job, and a call if there is none.
- An air switch runs a puff of air to a switch at the disposal. It is air at the counter and 120 V at the unit.

## Traps
- The disposal's switch off does not kill the outlet. The breaker does, and it is tested after.
- The dishwasher is not on the disposal's circuit. Two breakers, two tests.
- A wet cabinet is not safe because the switch is off. The boxes are live.

## Numbers
- **20 A** a kitchen appliance circuit
- **the wall switch** runs the disposal
- **its own circuit** for the dishwasher
- **live boxes** under the sink
- **a reset button** on the disposal's bottom

## Check yourself
1. What runs the disposal?
- [x] A wall switch or an air switch
- [ ] The dishwasher's timer
- [ ] The float in the sink
- [ ] The breaker only
> right: A switch on the wall or an air button on the counter.
> wrong: The disposal is switched. A wall switch or an air switch starts it.

2. With the disposal's switch off, is the outlet under the sink dead?
- [ ] Yes, the switch kills it
- [x] No, only the breaker kills it
- [ ] Yes, if the water is off
- [ ] Only the top half is dead
> right: The switch breaks the hot to the disposal. The box is still live.
> wrong: The box under the sink stays live. The breaker is the off, and it is tested after.

3. Where is the dishwasher's wiring box?
- [ ] Under the sink with the disposal
- [ ] At the panel
- [x] Behind the kick plate at the front
- [ ] Inside the door
> right: Low at the front, behind the kick plate.
> wrong: The dishwasher's box is behind its kick plate. It is live with the door open or shut.

4. Water pools under the sink beside the outlet. What protects a person?
- [ ] The switch
- [x] A GFCI, and nothing if there is none
- [ ] The cabinet door
- [ ] The disposal's reset button
> right: A GFCI trips on the leak. Without one, the water and the person are the path.
> wrong: Only a GFCI catches current going through water to a person. No GFCI is a call.
