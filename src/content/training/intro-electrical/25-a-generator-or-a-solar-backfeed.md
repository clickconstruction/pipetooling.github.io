## Why it matters
A house can make its own power. A generator or a solar array, the panels on the roof, can push current into wires the utility turned off. It can push out to the street. A wire the plumber thinks is dead can be live from the other end.

## Must know
- A generator or a solar array is a second source of power in the house.
- {{figure:backfeed-strip|The utility is off. The generator or the solar array pushes power the other way. The wire that should be dead is live from the other end.}}
- A transfer switch keeps the house's source inside the house. It disconnects the utility before it connects the generator.
- A backfeed is power going out through the meter to the street. It can kill a lineman, the utility's worker up the pole, on a line they turned off.
- Solar makes power whenever the sun is up. An array that is still connected can feed the house with the main off.
- A battery system does the same in the dark. It can hold a circuit live with the utility off.
- Never assume dead on a house with a generator, solar or a battery. The tester is the only truth.
- A generator cord plugged into a wall outlet is a backfeed waiting. That is a call, not a plumbing job.
- The solar disconnect is outside near the meter. It is the utility's and the electrician's to throw, not the plumber's.
- A house with solar has a sticker at the meter and the panel. Reading it is the first step on that house.

## Traps
- The main off is not the house dead on a solar house. The array can feed the panel from its own side.
- A quiet generator is not an off generator. An automatic one starts when the utility drops.
- A dead wire at noon is not a dead wire at one. The sun or the generator can bring it back.

## Numbers
- **a transfer switch** keeps it inside
- **a backfeed** goes out to the lineman
- **never assume dead** on a house with either

## Check yourself
1. What does a transfer switch do?
- [ ] Turns the solar on
- [x] Disconnects the utility before it connects the generator
- [ ] Trips the main breaker
- [ ] Charges the battery
> right: One source at a time, and nothing goes out to the street.
> wrong: A transfer switch keeps the generator's power inside by disconnecting the utility first.

2. What is a backfeed?
- [ ] A breaker wired backwards
- [x] Power going out through the meter to the street
- [ ] A loose ground wire
- [ ] A reverse-flow valve
> right: It goes out to a line the utility turned off, where a lineman may be working.
> wrong: A backfeed is the house's power pushing out to the street.

3. The main breaker is off on a house with solar. Is the panel dead?
- [ ] Yes, the main kills everything below it
- [x] Not for sure, the array can feed it from its own side
- [ ] Yes, at night
- [ ] Yes, if the meter is pulled
> right: A connected array feeds the panel while the sun is up. The tester is the truth.
> wrong: On a solar house the main off is not the panel dead. Never assume dead.

4. What tells you a house has solar before you open anything?
- [ ] The water heater
- [x] The sticker at the meter and the panel
- [ ] The roof is dark
- [ ] The thermostat
> right: The sticker is required, and it is the first thing to read.
> wrong: A house with solar carries a sticker at the meter and the panel. Read it first.
