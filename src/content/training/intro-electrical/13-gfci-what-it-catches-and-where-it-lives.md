## Why it matters
The breaker never sees a shock. The GFCI does. It is the one device that watches for current going through a person. It lives where water and a person can meet.

## Must know
- A GFCI compares the current going out on the hot with the current coming back on the neutral.
- {{figure:gfci-compare|The GFCI counts current out and current back. A difference means some went through you. It trips before the breaker knows.}}
- A difference between the two means some current went somewhere else. Through a person, through water, through a pipe.
- It trips at 5 mA of difference, in 1/40 of a second. That is before a shock can lock a muscle.
- It needs no ground to work. It only needs the two currents to differ.
- It lives where water and a person meet. Bathrooms, kitchens, garages, outdoors, basements, laundry, and within 6 ft of a sink.
- A GFCI receptacle protects the outlets wired downstream of it. One GFCI covers a string.
- A GFCI breaker protects the whole circuit from the panel.
- Press Test monthly. The Reset button pops out and the outlet goes dead, and Reset brings it back.
- A GFCI that will not reset is seeing a leak that is still there. It is not broken, it is working.

## Traps
- A GFCI is not a breaker. It catches a leak of 5 mA. The breaker needs thousands.
- A dead outlet in a bathroom is often a tripped GFCI somewhere else. Look for the one with the buttons.
- A GFCI cannot catch a shock from hot to neutral through a person. Both currents still match.

## Numbers
- **5 mA** the difference that trips it
- **1/40 s** to trip
- **no ground** needed
- **6 ft** of a sink
- **bathrooms, kitchens, garages, outdoors, basements, laundry** where it lives
- **monthly** press Test

## Check yourself
1. What does a GFCI compare?
- [ ] Volts and amps
- [x] Current out on the hot and current back on the neutral
- [ ] The hot and the ground
- [ ] The breaker's amps and the tool's amps
> right: Out and back must match. A difference means a leak.
> wrong: A GFCI counts current out and current back. The difference is what trips it.

2. How much difference trips a GFCI?
- [ ] 15 A
- [ ] 1 A
- [x] 5 mA
- [ ] 200 mA
> right: Five thousandths of an amp.
> wrong: A GFCI trips at 5 mA. That is far below what hurts.

3. Does a GFCI need a ground wire to work?
- [ ] Yes, always
- [x] No, it only needs the two currents to differ
- [ ] Only outdoors
- [ ] Only on 240 V
> right: It compares out and back. No ground is part of that.
> wrong: A GFCI works with no ground. It watches the hot and the neutral only.

4. A GFCI will not reset. What does that mean?
- [ ] It is broken
- [x] The leak is still there
- [ ] The breaker is off
- [ ] It needs a new Test button
> right: It holds the trip while current is still leaking. That is its job.
> wrong: A GFCI that will not hold is still seeing a leak. Find the leak, not a new GFCI.
