The Lien desk's Do now draws a Reminders strip above the rows. The strip has four steps, and every sent notice stands on one of them. This guide shows what each step means, what a pressed step opens, and where the schedule of days lives.

## Press a step

Press a step on the Reminders strip. A brief opens under the strip in the step's own words. The brief says what the step is and why a notice is on it. The brief names the usual move and whose press it is. The brief also names the other moves you may take instead. Every press stays yours. The brief ends with what closes the step by itself, like a payment.

The Notices, Affidavits and Retainage strips open the same kind of brief.

The list below the brief sorts itself. The rows on that step come first under **On this step**. The rest follow under **On other steps**. No row fades and every button still works. Press {{button:outline|Every step}} to put the list back. A second press on the same step does the same.

## Our schedule after a notice

The brief opens with a strip of days. Day 0 is the day the packet went. Day 7 is when a courtesy notice may go. Day 10 is when the attorney’s letter is due. Day 14 is when the attorney’s letter is overdue. Each notice on the step shows its own day on the strip. Past the strip come the last two points. Seven days before the affidavit's day, you file instead of reminding. Then comes the affidavit's day, which the law sets for each job.

The same strip sits in {{button:outline|§ Rules}} under **Our schedule after a notice**. There it says whose rule each day is. Day 7 and the week between courtesy notices are the owner's choice. Days 10 and 14 are counsel's rule. The seven days before the affidavit are our margin. That margin keeps a reminder from naming a lien day the law would not allow.

## The four steps

:::cards

:::card 1 Reminder due

The packet went and nothing came back. From day 7 the desk offers {{button:blue|Send a courtesy notice}}. One press sends an email and a text in plain words. The office presses it. A notice before day 7 waits here and is not counted. The step reads *· 2 before day 7* beside its count.

:::

:::card 2 Reminder sent

A courtesy notice went. The desk waits a week for an answer. After a week with nothing, another may go. From day 10 the row leads with {{button:blue|Send the attorney’s letter}} instead.

:::

:::card 3 Attorney’s letter

The attorney’s letter is counsel's certified second letter. The desk offers it from day 10 and calls it overdue from day 14. A notice stands here when the attorney’s letter is due and the courtesy is held. A notice also stands here while an attorney’s letter is drafting, awaiting the leader, in the run or sent. The leader presses {{button:blue|Send the attorney’s letter}}.

:::

:::card 4 Closed

The bill is paid. Or the GC authorized direct pay in writing. Or the owner called, or they answered, or the affidavit is filed. Or no reminder may go at all. Nothing is left to press. The notice reads under Sent.

:::

:::

## Why two rows at the same day get different buttons

Two notices can both be at day 21 and both overdue for the attorney’s letter. One row offers {{button:blue|Send a courtesy notice}} and the other offers {{button:blue|Send the attorney’s letter}}. The difference is whether a courtesy notice may go.

:::fold When the courtesy is held

A reminder may never name a lien day the law would not allow. So the desk holds the courtesy in four cases. The property is a homestead. The job has no approved work month yet. The filing window closed. The affidavit is due within 7 days, so you file it instead. Counsel's letter still goes, so the row offers the attorney’s letter. The card on the row's dots names the reason.

:::

## See the names behind a count

Press a step on the Reminders strip. The notices on that step with no row on the list read under the strip. Each notice has {{button:outline|Open}}, which opens the notice under Sent. The step's rule reads there too, in one line. Read [send a courtesy notice before a lien](/help/send-a-courtesy-notice-before-a-lien) for the courtesy itself.
