The Lien desk's Do now draws a Reminders strip above the rows. The strip has four steps, and every sent notice stands on one of them. This guide shows what each step means, what a pressed step opens, and where the schedule of days lives.

## Press a step

Press a step on the Reminders strip. A brief opens under the strip in the step's own words. The brief says what the step is and why a notice is on it. The brief names the usual move and whose press it is. The brief also names the other moves you may take instead. Every press stays yours. The brief ends with what closes the step by itself, like a payment.

The Notices, Affidavits and Retainage strips open the same kind of brief. The brief has six parts. In plain words says what the step is. Why a notice is here says the rule. What to do now names the press and what it does. The part called If that is not right lists the other presses and what each means. Then come what ends the step on its own, and what happens if nobody acts.

The list below the brief sorts itself. The rows on that step come first under **On this step**. The rest follow under **On other steps**. No row fades and every button still works. Press {{button:outline|Every step}} to put the list back. A second press on the same step does the same.

## Our schedule after a notice

The brief draws one notice's timeline down the card. Each row is one thing that happened or may happen, in date order. The row shows the date, what it is in plain words, what kind of thing it is, and its status. A red square is a legal requirement, like the day the notice was mailed and the lien's last day. A dashed bar is a window we chose, like the courtesy reminder or the attorney's letter. A green square is a payment, with the amount and what is still owed. Today is a band across the card. The band says you are here, and names the one press to make.

Each row's right side says what happens next. The courtesy window says how many days are left to send it. The attorney's letter says how many days past its window it is. The lien's day says how many days are left and what is lost if it passes. Press **Show another** to draw a different notice on the step.

The same days sit in {{button:outline|§ Rules}} under **Our schedule after a notice**. There it says whose rule each day is. Day 7 is the owner's choice. Days 10 and 14 are counsel's rule. The seven days before the affidavit are our margin.

Under it, **The lien month** names the office's rhythm across each month. Reminders go on days 1 to 5. Notices go on days 6 to 9. Liens are filed on days 10 to 13. The 14th and 15th catch what slipped. Nothing is scheduled from the 16th. The days are the owner's choice.

## The four steps

:::cards

:::card 1 Reminder due

The packet went and nothing came back. From day 7 the desk offers {{button:blue|Send a courtesy notice}}. One press sends an email and a text in plain words. The office presses it. A notice before day 7 waits here and is not counted. The step reads *· 2 before day 7* beside its count.

:::

:::card 2 Reminder sent

The one courtesy notice went. The desk waits a week for an answer. After a week with nothing, the row leads with {{button:blue|Send the attorney’s letter}}. The desk never asks for a second courtesy. You may still send one by hand from the footer when a channel bounced. A notice also stands here while they pay it down.

:::

:::card 3 Attorney’s letter

The attorney’s letter is counsel's certified second letter. The desk offers it from day 10 and calls it overdue from day 14. A notice stands here when the attorney’s letter is due and the courtesy is held. A notice also stands here while an attorney’s letter is drafting, awaiting the leader, in the run or sent. The leader presses {{button:blue|Send the attorney’s letter}}.

:::

:::card 4 Closed

The bill is paid. Or the GC authorized direct pay in writing. Or the owner called, or they answered, or the affidavit is filed. Or no reminder may go at all. Nothing is left to press. The notice reads under Sent, or under To draft when a new month is due.

:::

:::

## When they pay part of it

A payment after the notice pauses the reminders. The notice stands on step 2 as *paying it down*. The brief changes with it. The usual move becomes {{button:blue|Record what they promised}}. The timeline shows the payment as a green row with what is still owed. The payment row says when the desk asks again. The attorney's letter row reads *waits* while they pay. Their promise is a row of its own with the day they gave. The affidavit's week and the lien's day still come. A promise moves no legal day. Only the money does.

## Why two rows at the same day get different buttons

Two notices can both be at day 21 and both overdue for the attorney’s letter. One row offers {{button:blue|Send a courtesy notice}} and the other offers {{button:blue|Send the attorney’s letter}}. The difference is whether a courtesy notice may go.

:::fold When the courtesy is held

A reminder may never name a lien day the law would not allow. So the desk holds the courtesy in three cases. The property is a homestead. The job has no approved work month yet. The filing window closed. Counsel's letter still goes, so the row offers the attorney’s letter. The card on the row's dots names the reason. Seven days before the lien's day is the affidavit's week. Then the desk recommends the affidavit, and the courtesy button stays on the footer.

:::

## See the names behind a count

Press a step on the Reminders strip. The notices on that step with no row on the list read under the strip. Each notice has {{button:outline|Open}}, which opens the notice on its own pile. That is Sent, or To draft when a new month is due. The step's rule reads there too, in one line. Read [send a courtesy notice before a lien](/help/send-a-courtesy-notice-before-a-lien) for the courtesy itself.
