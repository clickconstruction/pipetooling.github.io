The Materials tab on Jobs is the office side of the cages and boxes on a big site. It reads the same ledger the crew writes on the phone. You see what came in, what is on site, what was used and what is missing, job by job.

## Open the desk

Open **Jobs** and press **Materials**. The desk lists every job with a container and sorts the ones losing money to the top. Press a job to open its page.

Under the money, a job that came up short this week reads how many lines were short. The words turn amber and add *with no price* when a short part has no price. The money leaves that part out, so the figure is then the least that walked away.

A job whose boxes did not load says so in its row, with the reason. The row's figures are left blank, never drawn as zero. Press **Read again** on that row. A site's screen says the same at its top when one of its jobs did not load.

The same desk opens from the Materials page. Open **Materials** and press **On site**, the tab under the word Track. A badge beside Track counts the boxes due a count and names what walked away this week. On a narrow window Track may show as its name alone. Press **Track** and its tabs open.

The Pipeline's tools menu has a **Materials desk** row with the count of jobs that lost something this week. A job's window has a **Materials** tab with the same facts and **Open the materials page**. A bid's Procure step has the same door once the bid is a job. The line under its four steps reads **Materials on site**.

The Needs you card on the Dashboard carries two lines for the office. One names the money of material that walked away this week across every job. The other counts the boxes not counted in 14 days. Press **Open the desk** on a line to see the jobs. When one job holds every row, the press reads **Open the job** and opens that job's page.

## See a whole site on one screen

A site is a development. A site's boxes can sit under more than one job. The desk groups those jobs under one row with the site's name. Press the site's name to open the whole site.

The Development page has the same door. Press **Open the site’s materials** in the head of Jobs & bids.

The top row of figures reads the site at a glance.

- **Last recorded** says when someone last pressed anything on this site. The figure turns amber after seven quiet days.
- **Boxes** and **On hand** count what is there.
- **Due a count** counts the boxes past 14 days. A smaller line says how many more fall due this week.
- **Can't find** and **Short at a count** count the lines to chase.
- **Missing this week** is the money that walked away.
- **This week** is what came in and what was used.
- **Worth** is the money of the material that has a price. A smaller line says how many lines have one.

The counts are only as fresh as the last press. Read **Last recorded** before you trust a total.

Press **Due a count**, **Can't find** or **Short at a count** to narrow the list to those rows. Press it again to see every row.

Under the figures, **By part** lists one row per part across the site's jobs. The row shows the total and the boxes that hold it. Press a row to see each box with its place, its job and its last count. Press a box's stencil to open that job's page.

Two names are one row when their words are the same. *2" 90 PVC* and *2 inch 90 pvc* are one row, and the row says **2 spellings**. Each name keeps its own count in its box. A name with a different size or a missing word stays its own row. A fitting with two sizes keeps the order you typed. *4" to 2" reducer* and *2" to 4" reducer* are two rows. The app never guesses.

Some names are nearly the same. One may have one more word, or a small spelling slip. Under such a row the screen asks **Same part?** and shows the other name. Press {{button:blue|Same part}} to make them one row with one total. Press **Different** to keep them apart. The screen remembers either answer for this site and does not ask again.

The chip **Same part?** beside By place lists only the rows with a question. The app asks only when the sizes match. A 2 inch part and a 3 inch part are never offered as one.

To take a join back, press the row to open it. **Names joined here** lists each pair with who joined it. Press **Undo** beside a pair. A join never changes a count or a box.

**Worth** is never the whole site unless every line has a price. Read the smaller line with it, such as *9 of 118 lines have a price*. Press **Worth** to list the rows with no price, most pieces first.

A name typed on site has no price until it is tied to the parts book. Press a row to open it. Press **This is a parts-book part** and type words from the part's name. Press the part in the list. The row now reads the book's name and price, and **Worth** grows. **Undo** under **Names joined here** takes the tie back.

To price many rows, press **Worth** and then {{button:blue|Price 88 rows one at a time}}. The number on the button is how many rows have no price. A window opens on the row with the most pieces.

The window shows parts from the parts book whose names hold every word of the row's name. Each one is marked **Offer** and shows its price and the day it was checked. An offer is a guess. Read the name before you press it. A name with no material can fit a PVC part and a copper part.

Press an offer to say the row is that part. The number keys **1**, **2** and **3** press the offers too. The row takes that part's name and price, and the window moves to the next row. Press **Other part…** to search the parts book with other words. Press **Skip** or the **S** key to leave a row for later.

Each parts-book offer has a second press on its right, **Just price**. Use it when the price is right and the part is not the same thing. The row takes that price for one piece. The row keeps the name the crew wrote, and the pieces are not counted as that part.

An offer marked **a bill** is what a supply house charged for a line that fits. Press it to use that price for one piece. The offer names the house, the day and the unit the bill sold it by. A bill may sell pipe by the foot, so read the unit first.

When nothing fits, type a price for one piece in the box and press {{button:blue|Save $3.12 for one}}. The button shows the price you typed.

Some rows are not a part to price, such as scrap, tools or a mixed box. Press **Not a part** or the **N** key. The row leaves the count, and **Worth** says how many lines were left out.

The foot of the window names your last answer. Press **Undo** there to take it back. Press **Done for now** to close the window. The rows you priced have left the list.

To change an answer later, press the row on the list to open it. **Priced here** lists each answer with who gave it. Press **Undo** beside a price. Press **Bring back** beside a row that was left out.

A price you accept here belongs to this site. Every device reads it, and so does the job's **Missing** table. A price older than 90 days stays and turns amber, as a parts-book price does.

A press on **Worth** shows three chips over the list. **No price** lists the rows that still need one. **An old price** lists the rows on a price older than 90 days. **Left out** lists the rows set aside as not a part. Each chip shows its count.

To refresh old prices, press **An old price** and then {{button:blue|Check 9 old prices one at a time}}. The window shows the price each row reads now. The window offers a bill only when the bill is newer than that price. Press the bill, or type today's price for one piece. For a parts-book part, **Open the Parts Book** leads to where the book's own price is checked.

When **No price** is empty the list says *Nothing waiting on a price on this site*. Press **Show every row** to see the whole site again.

Type in **Find a part or a box on this site** to narrow the rows. A line above them adds up what is shown. The line reads like *206 pieces in the 2 rows shown*.

Press **By place** to read the boxes by where they stand. A place name that two jobs share is one section.

Nothing on this screen changes a count. A count and a correction stay on the job's page and the phone.

Press **Print the material location report** at the top to print the site. The [report](/help/print-the-material-location-report) opens on the site's Development page. The report's **By part** chip prints this same list.

## See what one person did on a site

Open a site and press **By person**. The list turns into every press on the site, newest first, a day at a time.

Pick a name under **Person** to read one person's presses alone. A line beside the name says when that person last pressed.

Each row says when, what, the part, how many, where and the note. A count reads what the app expected against what was found, like *40 → 12*. Press the box's name to open that box.

Four figures sit over the list. They count the presses, the pieces taken or used, the boxes pressed on and the presses worth a look.

A press worth a look wears an amber chip. A first count of a line wears none. The first part added to a line wears none. A count that found what the app expected wears none.

- **count changed the number** is a count that changed a number the app already had.
- **added by hand** is a part added to a line that already had a number, with no invoice or PO.
- **no note** is a take or a use that does not say where the material went.

Press **Worth a look only** to read those rows alone. Pick a kind under **Kind of press** to read one kind, like *Taken*.

A box moved from one place to another is in the list too. It reads *Moved a box*.

**By person** is for the office. Nothing on it changes a count.

## Read the row

The job's number sits at the top of the page, before its name. Press the number to open the job window.

Under the job's name one row reads the material's path left to right. It reads {{chip:blue|1,250 came in}} → {{chip:green|1,105 on site}} → {{chip:gray|120 used}} → {{chip:yellow|25 missing}}. Each figure is in its own colour. An amber badge counts what waits for a hand. A red badge is the money that walked away this week. Press a figure to open its section.

The page reads the boxes again by itself. That happens when you come back to it, and every few minutes while it stays open. A crew's press shows without a reload.

A very large job can pass 5,000 rows in its ledger. Then History and the boxes show the newest 5,000 rows. The totals come from the database, and a line under the job's name says so.

## On site

The boxes sit grouped by their place on the site. **All**, **Due a count** and **Can't find** narrow them. **List**, **By part** and **Map** at the row's end switch the view. List is the boxes. By part is one row per part on the job with its total and its boxes. Map is the site map with the boxes' pins. On a job that is on a site, By part has a line with **Open the whole site**.

Press {{button:outline|Add a container}} to add a cage or box with its stencil and its place. On a line, **Correct…** sets the count the crew should have found. It writes a count row in your name.

Beside **Edit** on a box, press {{button:outline|Where is it?}}. The box's window opens on its **Where is it?** tab. The tab says the place, the note, and who pinned the box and on what day. Under the words is the site from above with the box's pin. The tab is the same sheet the crew has on the phone. [Find a box on the site](/help/keep-track-of-the-material-in-the-cages-and-boxes-on-site#find-a-box-on-the-site) says what each press does.

Beside a place's name above its boxes, press **＋ Paint the way here**. The painting window opens on a new map named for the place. Draw the way from the gate to the place and save it. The crew then opens that way from any box in the place. A place with a way reads **Open the way** and **Take the way off**. Taking the way off keeps the map.

On the **Where is it?** tab, press **Send someone to it** to make a task for the box. The New task window opens with the words, the box's pin as the task's place and the painted way. Pick the person and send it.

## Move everything on site to another job

A crew can start a count under the wrong job. The boxes then sit on a job that is not theirs. This press moves them to the right job in one go.

Open the wrong job's page and look under its boxes. Press **Move everything on site to another job…**. Only a dev, a master technician, an assistant or a controller can press it. For everyone else the press is grey and says why.

Pick the job the material belongs on. The jobs on the same site are listed first. Type a number or a name in **Find another job** to reach any other job. Then press {{button:blue|Read the plan}}.

The plan says what moves, table by table. It names a place that becomes one with a place of the same name. It names a box whose stencil the other job already has, and the new name that box takes. It reads each job's boxes and parts before and after. Nothing has moved yet.

The button names the move with its counts, such as {{button:blue|Move 91 boxes and 284 presses to JP1091}}. Press it and the boxes move. Every box keeps its code, its label and its history. Only the job it sits under changes. A note lands on both jobs.

The wrong job's page then reads empty. The right job's page reads the boxes. The box photos' files follow to that job's Drive folder by themselves. A strip above the boxes shows them on their way.

A refusal is said with its reason, and nothing moves. A phone that still has the wrong job open should open the right one. A press it sends lands on the box's job now.

On the Materials desk, a site that holds boxes under two jobs has the same press on its row. There you pick both jobs from that site's jobs.

## Missing

The table holds one row per part. It reads what was bought and what was added on site. Then what was used, returned, taken or damaged, and what the crew could not find. Then what is still on site and what walked away. Below it, **Reported this week** lists each short count and each Can't find with the person and the day.

The money uses the parts book's price. Beside each figure a small chip reads {{chip:gray|parts book · checked Oct 3}}, the day someone last checked that price. The chip turns amber when the check is older than 90 days. Then the price typed off the invoice that landed the part stands in, and the chip reads {{chip:gray|invoice price}}. A part typed in by name on site has no page in the book, so it keeps the invoice price. A part with no price at all shows its count alone.

## Land a delivery

Open **Deliveries**. Press **Add the invoice to the job’s Drive folder** and drop the invoice there. That keeps the invoice with the job. The app does not read the file yet, so its lines land from pasted text.

A job with no folder yet shows {{button:blue|Make the job’s Drive folders}}. Press it once. The app makes the job's folder under Jobs with Customer Pictures, Plans and Team only inside. The office sees it, and a box photo from the phone files there by itself. On the desk, **Make folders for 12 jobs** does the same for every live job without one. A job whose link is not a folder is held with the reason. A job with a folder but no pictures or plans link has the same press on its window's Documents tab. It fills in what is missing.

To land the lines, press **Paste the text instead** and paste the invoice's lines. Press {{button:blue|Read the lines}} and look them over. The Part box of a line offers the names the job already has. Pick one when the invoice words the same part another way. Press {{button:blue|Add 12 lines to Unsorted as a delivery}}. The parts wait in Unsorted for the crew to put away.

The button counts the lines with a count and a name, and sends those. A line with no count or no name stays on the screen for you to finish. One sentence says what happened: the lines landed, or they are kept on this device until the signal is back.

A line the app could not read is listed above the table, as you pasted it. Press **Add it as a line** and type its count, or leave it out. *2 1/2" x 10 PVC pipe* is one of these. The app cannot tell whether the 2 is a count or part of the size.

Some invoices print one figure on a line. The app does not guess whether that is the price of each or the line's total. Press **The price of each** or **The line’s total** once, and every such line follows. Until you say, those lines land with no price.

An invoice that already landed on this job is named above the button. Press the button and a window asks before the lines land a second time.

A line the app cannot save stays on the table with its reason under it. The lines that landed leave the table. Fix the line or leave it out, then press the button again. It sends only the lines still on the table, so no line lands twice.

## Print a label

Press **Label** on a box to print that box alone, or **Print labels** for every box on the job. A job's row on the Materials page has **Labels** too. A paper label carries the stencil, the job, the place, a small code and ruled lines to write on. A tape label carries the stencil and the code alone. The crew's camera opens the box from the code.

On the Materials page, press **Print labels** at the right end of the tabs. A window lists every job that holds a cage or a box. The band on top shows what this computer prints on, with one label drawn at its printed size. Press **Change to paper** or **Change to Brother tape** to switch.

Each job is a card. It says how many boxes and cages the job has, their names, and how many stand in each place. An amber chip counts the boxes whose label has not printed yet. A green chip says every box is labelled. A blue chip counts the boxes that already wear a sticker. A line says how much tape the job takes, or how many pages.

Press a job's name to open its labels. The buttons under a card open a part of them. **The 6 not printed yet** ticks only the boxes that still wait for a label. **All 91** ticks every box. When a run stopped earlier today, the card says so, and **The rest, 51 boxes** picks it up. Nothing prints until you press the print button in the next window.

The app marks a box as labelled when its label prints. On paper, the mark is made when the print window closes. If the labels did not come out, press **Take the mark off 12 boxes** under the print button. For a box that already wears a label, tick it and press **Mark 1 box as labelled**. A box that wears a sticker counts as labelled. The app began keeping the mark on November 17, 2026. A box made before that day is not counted until its label prints.

## Print future stickers

A future sticker is a label printed before its box. Printing a sticker reserves its place. The sticker gets its own link, such as prts.co/b/4F2A-9C18. The sticker also gets the box's name to read, such as G78. The link and the name are held for a box that is filled in later. No sticker prints without a link of its own. Stick one on each cage or box on site. The crew then scans it on the phone and says which box it is on. Read [stick a sticker on a box](/help/keep-track-of-the-material-in-the-cages-and-boxes-on-site#stick-a-sticker-on-a-box) for that part.

On a job's page, press **Print future stickers** beside **Print labels**. On the Materials page, press **Print future stickers** at the right end of the tabs and pick the job. Until November 2026 the press read *Print blank stickers*. New boxes made from the stickers go on that job. The office, a primary and a superintendent can print a run. For everyone else the press is grey and says why.

On the Materials page the window lists each job as a card. A card says how many boxes the job holds and how many runs were printed for it. An amber line counts the stickers reserved and not on a box yet. The line names them and says who reserved them and when. It also says what printed, such as 40 printed, 18 not printed yet. Under those words, press **Print the 58 reserved stickers, G93 to G150** to print them. The press prints the same stickers with the same links. Nothing new is reserved. A run that printed in part has a second press before it. Press **Print the 18 not printed yet, G133 to G150** to print only the stickers that never came out. A job with more than one run has one press for each run. **Print future stickers** on the card is the other press, and it reserves new stickers. **Open the job's boxes** goes to the job's page, where you put them on their boxes. A card also says the name a new run opens on. The band on top shows one sticker with that name at its printed size.

The window opens on **Names**. The field holds the site's next free name, such as G78. A name an earlier run reserved is not free, even with no box yet. With **200** picked, the run is G78 to G277. Type a range to set the end yourself, such as `G78-300`. The line under the field says what the app read. **How many** then shows the count of the range. A range can step a letter too. `A1-D50` is four rows of fifty. `G78B-D` is three cages at one spot. If the app cannot read the range, the line says why and the press is grey. Clear the field to print stickers with no name. Such a sticker prints its code in larger type, and it works on any site.

Some of the names may be boxes on the site already. The window counts them and says their stickers go on those boxes. Some of the names may be on stickers from an earlier run that are on no box yet. Printing them again makes two stickers for one name. The press turns grey and a line says which names. Press **Print them anyway** if you mean to print them again.

Pick how many with **30**, **60**, **100** or **200**, or type a number up to 500. A box with more than one side needs more than one sticker. Set **Copies of each** with the minus and plus presses. Every copy of a sticker opens the same box.

Stickers print on Brother tape. The window connects to the P-touch as the Labels window does. Open **What the stickers will look like** at the foot of the window to see the strips before you print. The fold draws the first six strips with the run's own names. The link on each strip reads XXXX-XXXX. The X's stand in for the sticker's own link, which is given when the run prints. A line over the strips says so. Press **Show all 200** to draw the rest smaller. A run with no names draws one strip, since the others differ only by their codes. The fold also draws the test strip. Press **Print 1 test strip** first. The test strip reads TEST and is not a sticker. Then press {{button:blue|Print 200 future stickers, G78 to G277}}. With copies the press names the sets, such as {{button:blue|Print 200 sets of future stickers, G78 to G277}}. The app reserves the stickers and the strips print at once. The window then says the stickers are reserved. Each strip carries the QR code, the name in large type and the short code. A name of five to seven characters prints a little smaller. A sticker with no name prints its code in larger type.

Press **Stop printing** to end a run early. One more strip prints, so the printer can cut the tape. If the run stops part way, by your press or by the printer, the window says how many stickers printed. The press becomes **Print the rest, G163 to G300**. A line says the stickers left are still reserved. The app reserves a run at the press, so a run is reserved even if no strip came out. The app marks a sticker as printed when the last of its copies comes out. A sticker part way through its copies is not marked. You can close the window and print the stickers left on another day. On the job's reserved line, press **Print the 138 not printed yet, G163 to G300**. Through the print window the app cannot see the tape. There it marks the stickers when the print window closes. If they did not come out, press **Take the mark off 58 stickers** under the print button. A test strip is never marked. The app began keeping the mark on November 17, 2026. An older run says nothing of what printed until one of its stickers prints. Out of tape? Put in a new cassette and close the cover. The window checks the printer by itself, and the press comes back. A run on the page can be printed in part. Pick its first sticker in **From** and its last in **to**. Connect the printer again and press it. The stickers already printed are not printed twice.

Under the job's boxes, a fold lists every run of stickers. Each run shows its names, who reserved it and when. The run also shows how many stickers are on boxes. The rest are reserved and not on a box yet. A run says what printed of those, such as 2 printed, 3 not printed yet. Above the boxes, a line counts the reserved stickers. When one run holds them, the line names them, such as G92 to G200. The line also says what printed, such as None printed yet. Then it says who reserved that run and when. Press **These are on boxes** beside the line to put named stickers on their boxes with no scan. Press **Runs** to open the fold. Under the line, press **Print the 109 reserved stickers, G92 to G200** to print them. The window opens on that run and reserves nothing new. When some of them printed, **Print the 18 not printed yet, G183 to G200** sits before it. Stickers already on boxes are left out. To print those too, pick the run's first sticker in **From** and its last in **to**. The window also has **Copies of each**, which opens on the count the run was made with. Press plus or minus to print more or fewer of each sticker, up to six. The change is for this print only. For a role that cannot print a run, the press is grey and says why. The window says the same count before a new run, so nobody prints 200 more over 96 reserved. The same press sits under that count. A new run opens on the name after the last reserved one. A sticker printed with the box's own name adds nothing beside the box's name. A sticker printed with another name reads *sticker reads G78* there. Press **Reprint 200 stickers** to print the whole run again. A reprint prints the same codes, so nothing can collide. Press **Print the rest, G205 to G300** to print only the stickers not yet on a box. In the window, **Export the stickers as a CSV** writes the run for a label editor.

A roll of named stickers needs no scan to go on its boxes. In the **Runs** fold, press **These are on boxes** on the run. Type the names that were stuck on, such as `G78-150`. Pick how the boxes sit and their place. The window says how many boxes will be made. A name that is a box on the site already takes its sticker. A name with no reserved sticker is left alone. Press {{button:blue|Put 73 stickers on their boxes}}. The window then says what happened. Scan a box later to say what is inside.

## Print a manifest

A manifest is the paper that hangs on a box and says what is in it. Press **Print the manifest** on a job's page. The window opens on every box. Press a stencil to leave that box out. **Every box** picks them all again and **None** clears them. The press names its count, such as {{button:blue|Print 2 manifests}}.

Each box prints on its own Letter page in large plain type. The page carries the stencil, the job and the site. It carries the same code the label has, so the crew's camera opens the box from the manifest. The page shows the day the manifest was printed. The page also shows the day something was last recorded in the box, and by whom. Under the dates it lists what is in the box with each number. Ruled lines at the foot take what is added on site by hand. Each line has room for the item, the quantity, who added it and the day.

Laminate the page and hang it on the box in a document holder. The manifest is a plain print and files no copy. When someone writes a line on it, press the same thing in the app so the count stays true.

For every cage and box across a development, print the [material location report](/help/print-the-material-location-report) from the Development page.

## Retire every box on a job

When a job is done and its cages have gone back to the yard, retire them all at once. Under the job's boxes press {{button:outline|Retire 2 empty boxes}}. The press counts the empty boxes. A box with parts in it stays, and the line beside the press names it. Move its parts or mark them used, then press again.

A window lists what goes and what stays, with the reason for each. The button in the window carries the same count. After the press, a toast says how many boxes were retired. It carries **Undo** for one minute, which brings every one of them back. A retired box keeps its stickers and its history.

## Bring back a retired box

A job's container list ends with a fold named Retired. Open it to see every cage or box retired on the job, with the day it left. Press **Bring back G4** and the box is live again. Its QR label works as before, and nothing in its history changes.

The press is withheld when a live box on the job already carries that stencil. The fold says which one, so no job carries two G4s. Retire the live one first, or give it another stencil.

## Find a box from anywhere

Open the search box at the top of the app and type a stencil, a part or a place. **Boxes on site** lists the box with its job. Type a site's name and the list has a row for the whole site. Press that row to open the site's materials. Press a box's row to open the job's materials page. **Open on the phone screen** opens the box itself.

## Find a part on any job

Open **Materials** and press **On site**. Type a part, its maker or a stencil in **Find a part or a box on any job**. Each row names the box, its job and how many are on hand. Press **Open the job** to go to that job's page.

A label the camera cannot read has a code printed under it. Press **Scan a label** at the right end of the tabs and type the eight characters. Press {{button:blue|Open the box}} and the box opens on the phone screen.
