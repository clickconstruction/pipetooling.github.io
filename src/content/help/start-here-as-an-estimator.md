Your world is Bids: from the board through takeoff and pricing to a submitted proposal. You don't manage crews or company money.

## Your day

1. **Bid Board** — every bid by stage, with due dates. See [bid due date and time](?g=bid-due-date-time).

:::example A bid's life on the board
{{chip:gray|Unsent}} → {{chip:yellow|Pending}} → {{chip:green|Won}} or {{chip:red|Lost}} — and Won work moves to {{chip:blue|Started}} once the office runs it
:::
2. **Takeoff** — count fixtures and tie-ins off the plans.
3. **Pricing and Labor** — the books turn those counts into materials, hours, and a price. Versions let you price the same project more than one way; Labor lists the fixtures counted on the version you're on, and shows a short loading card while a bid opens — never an empty list for a bid that has fixtures.
4. **Cover Letter and Submission** — build the proposal and send it. If you're bidding one project to several GCs, see [bid one project to multiple GCs](?g=bid-one-project-to-multiple-gcs).

## Your other tabs

- **Materials** — the price book and assemblies behind your numbers; [find assemblies that include a part](?g=find-assemblies-that-include-a-part).
- **Map** — where your bids are, colored by outcome.
- **Prospects** — leads coming in.
- **Calendar**, **Checklist**, **Job Parts Tally**.

## When a bid wins

Accepted work becomes a job for the office to run — you'll see [estimate accepted notifications](?g=estimate-accepted-notifications). Jobs, dispatch, and billing are theirs; if the job needs a price change, that comes back to you as a change order.

## Service-type limits

If your account is restricted to certain service types, pickers only show matching work. That's deliberate — ask the office to widen it if something you need is missing.
