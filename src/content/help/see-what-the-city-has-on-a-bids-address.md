A city keeps a record of every permit on an address. When a bid's address is in a city we read, the bid's window shows what the city has on it. It also shows the other jobs you priced for the same GC.

## See this site

Open a bid from the **Bid Board** and press **Edit**. Look under **Location**, below the address and the distance.

The block is named **This site**. Its first line says what the city has on the address. It says whether a permit was applied for or issued, and how long ago. It also says whether a general plumbing permit is on the address.

Three tiles can sit under that line. They show the value the applicant declared, the area on the permit, and your bid. The last tile says your bid as a share of the declared value.

A list follows with every permit we can see on the address. A {{chip:green|Looks like this job}} chip marks a permit whose project name shares a word with your bid. It is a hint. A shared address can carry another tenant's permit, so read the names.

Each row says the name the city lists on the permit. That name is the contact the city has. It is not always the builder.

The last line says where the records come from and the day they start.

## Say who the city lists on a permit

A permit lists one contact. It is often a person, and it is not always the builder.

Under the permit that looks like your job, the block asks once. It reads **Do you know who that is?** Pick one of three answers.

- {{button:blue|Works for the GC}} shows the other permits that name is on. Then press **Show all under the GC**, or **This permit only**. One person can file for more than one builder, so look first.
- **Someone else…** asks who it is. Pick the owner, an architect or engineer, a name that files for others, or another GC.
- **Not sure** hides the question from you alone. The rest of the team still sees it.

Sometimes the name begins the GC's own name. A branch or a longer legal name reads this way. The block then asks **Same company?** instead. Pick **Same company**, **Not them…** or **Not sure**. They work as the three answers above do.

Every answer keeps your name and the day. Press **Undo** to take one back. The builder's sheet takes one back too. See [Know the builders](/help/research-permits-for-work-to-bid#know-the-builders).

## When there is nothing to show

The block never says a site has no permits. It says one of three things.

- **No permit seen** means the city's records hold nothing on that address since the day they start.
- **No permit records** means the bid is in a town whose permits we do not read. Today we read San Antonio, Austin, New Braunfels, San Marcos and Seguin.
- **Cannot be looked up** means the address is not a street address. Add the street number and the city.

## See the jobs you priced for a GC

Look under **People**, below the GC's card. The block is named **What we can see about** the GC.

It lists the bids you priced for that GC on street addresses in a city we read. Each one shows what the city has on its address. A {{chip:red|Plumbing permit · another name}} chip means a general plumbing permit lists someone other than the building permit's contact.

One line counts the sites. It says how many have a permit, out of how many we can see. A chip says how many of your bids with that GC the count covers.

Another line names the towns where the GC's other bids are. No permit records are read there.

On a phone the jobs fold behind **See the jobs**.

Below the jobs sits **Other permits under names tied to them**. It lists the GC's own name and each name someone tied to the GC. Each row says whose word it is and how many permits the city lists that name on. Press **See them** to read the list. Press **Take back** to undo a tie.

## Link a customer to its names on permits

Our name for a customer is often not the name a city lists. You can link the two.

Press **Find their names on permits** under the names tied to the GC. The same press sits on the customer's page, in the **Names on permits** card.

A sheet opens with names that may be them. Each one says why it is offered. The strongest reason is a permit on an address you bid to them. The others are about the name. One name may begin the other. They may be one letter apart. They may share a word.

Press **Compare** to see ours beside the city's. What the two share is marked green.

Then answer the name once.

- {{button:blue|Same company}} ties the name to the customer. Its permits show under them from then on. For a person the press reads **Works for them**.
- **Not them** keeps your No. The name is not offered for that customer again.

Nothing fits? Type in the search box. It reaches every name on permits.

The app never ties a name by itself. It only joins a name that is exactly the same.

## See permit news on the Bid Board

Open the **Bid Board**. A strip named **Permit news on your bids** sits above the sections when there is news.

Each line is one bid that is not yet won or lost. It says what is new on the bid's address. Press {{button:outline|Open the bid}} to open that bid.

Three things count as news.

- A permit application was filed in the last 30 days.
- A building permit was issued in the last 30 days.
- A general plumbing permit lists a name other than the building permit's contact. This one comes first. It stays until you mark the bid won or lost.

The strip marks nothing for you. Read the line, then decide.

A bid with a permit on its address also carries a chip on its row. It reads {{chip:yellow|Applied Sep 11}} or {{chip:green|Issued Nov 3}}. A {{chip:red|Plumbing permit · another name}} chip matches the first kind of news.

## Who sees it

The office and estimators see the two blocks, the strip and the chips. They are the same people who see **Prospects → Permits**.
