A city keeps a record of every permit on an address. When a bid's address is in a city we read, the bid's window shows what the city has on it. It also shows the other jobs you priced for the same GC.

## See this site

Open a bid from the **Bid Board** and press **Edit**. Look under **Location**, below the address and the distance.

The block is named **This site**. Its first line says what the city has on the address. It says whether a permit was applied for or issued, and how long ago. It also says whether a general plumbing permit is on the address.

Three tiles can sit under that line. They show the value the applicant declared, the area on the permit, and your bid. The last tile says your bid as a share of the declared value.

A list follows with the permits we can see on the address. When your bid's address names a suite, a permit that names a different suite is left out. A line under the list says how many were left out.

A {{chip:green|Looks like this job}} chip marks a permit whose project name shares a word with your bid. It is a hint. A shared address can carry another tenant's permit, so read the names.

Each row says the name the city lists on the permit. That name is the contact the city has. It is not always the builder.

A {{chip:green|Ours}} chip marks a plumbing permit that lists one of our own names. The first line then says the permit lists us, with the name and the day. You say which names are ours [in Settings](#say-which-names-are-us).

The last line says where the records come from and the day they start.

## Say who the city lists on a permit

A permit lists one contact. It is often a person, and it is not always the builder.

Under the permit that looks like your job, the block asks once. It reads **Do you know who that is?** Pick one of three answers.

- {{button:blue|Works for the GC}} shows the other permits that name is on. Then press **Show all under the GC**, or **This permit only**. One person can file for more than one builder, so look first.
- **Someone else…** asks who it is. Pick the owner, an architect or engineer, a name that files for others, or another GC.
- **Not sure** hides the question from you alone. The rest of the team still sees it.

Sometimes the name begins the GC's own name. A branch or a longer legal name reads this way. The block then asks **Same company?** instead. Pick **Same company**, **Not them…** or **Not sure**. These answers work as the three above do.

Every answer keeps your name and the day. Press **Undo** to take one back. The builder's sheet takes one back too. See [Know the builders](/help/research-permits-for-work-to-bid#know-the-builders).

## When there is nothing to show

The block never says a site has no permits. It says one of three things.

- **No permit seen** means the city's records hold nothing on that address since the day they start.
- **No permit records** means the bid is in a town whose permits we do not read. Today we read San Antonio, Austin, New Braunfels, San Marcos and Seguin.
- **Cannot be looked up** means the address is not a street address. Add the street number and the city.

## See the jobs you priced for a GC

Look under **People**, below the GC's card. The block is named **What we can see about** the GC.

It lists the bids you priced for that GC on street addresses in a city we read. Each one shows what the city has on its address. A {{chip:red|Plumbing permit · another name}} chip means a general plumbing permit lists someone other than the building permit's contact. A {{chip:green|Ours}} chip means that permit lists one of our own names.

One line counts the sites. It says how many have a permit, out of how many we can see. The same line counts the sites where a general plumbing permit lists us. A chip says how many of your bids with that GC the count covers.

Another line names the towns where the GC's other bids are. No permit records are read there.

On a phone the jobs in a bid's window fold behind **See the jobs**.

Below the jobs sits **Other permits under names tied to them**. It lists the GC's own name and each name someone tied to the GC. Each row says whose word it is and how many permits the city lists that name on. Press **See them** to read the list. Press **Take back** to undo a tie.

## See the same block on the customer's page

Go to **Customers** and open the customer. Press the **Permits** tab.

The tab holds the same block as the bid's window. The block lists the jobs you priced for them, then the names tied to them. On a phone the first six jobs show here, with a press for the rest.

Two customer rows sometimes carry one name. The tab reads them together and says so under the block.

The **Profile** tab keeps a **Names on permits** line where the card used to be. Press **Open Permits** on that line to reach the tab.

The office and estimators see the tab.

## Link a customer to its names on permits

Our name for a customer is often not the name a city lists. You can link the two.

Press **Find their names on permits** under the names tied to the GC. The same press sits on the customer's page, on the **Permits** tab.

A sheet opens with names that may be them. Each one says why it is offered. The strongest reason is a permit on an address you bid to them. The others are about the name. One name may begin the other. They may be one letter apart. They may share a word.

Press **Compare** to see ours beside the city's. What the two share is marked green.

Then answer the name once.

- {{button:blue|Same company}} ties the name to the customer. Its permits show under them from then on. For a person the press reads **Works for them**.
- **Not them** keeps your No. The name is not offered for that customer again.

Nothing fits? Type in the search box. It reaches every name on permits.

The app never ties a name by itself. It only joins a name that is exactly the same.

## Answer permit news on Followup

Open **Bids**. A red number beside **Followup** counts the permit news on your bids that nobody has answered. Press the number. It opens **Permit news**, the last lens on Followup.

A bid is yours when you estimate it or manage it. A bid someone marked for you is yours too.

Each card is one bid that is not yet won or lost. Read it from the top.

- A tag says what kind of news it is.
- The bid's number and name come next. Press the number to open the bid.
- One large line says what the city shows.
- The next line gives the work in the city's own words and the permit's number.
- The last line says why the permit looks like your bid. A tick is a match the records show. A question mark is a thing the records do not say.

Four things count as news.

- A general plumbing permit lists a name other than the building permit's contact. This card comes first.
- A general plumbing permit that lists us was applied for or issued in the last 30 days.
- A building permit was issued in the last 30 days.
- A permit application was filed in the last 30 days.

Each card asks one thing and takes one press.

A plumbing permit under another name on a sent bid asks **Did we lose this bid?** It has three answers.

- {{button:outline|We lost it…}} opens the board's own window in the card. For a bid with one GC, type what they said. Then press the reason. A bid sent to more than one GC lists its GCs. Press the state beside a GC's name, then press **lost**. Nothing is saved until you pick.
- {{button:outline|Still waiting}} keeps the permit on the bid. Nothing about the bid changes.
- {{button:outline|Not our job}} takes the permit off the bid for everyone. Press it when the permit is another job on the same address.

Every other card takes {{button:outline|Got it}} or {{button:outline|Not our job}}. A building permit on a sent bid also has **Open the Call queue ›**.

An answered card folds to one line. Press **Undo** on that line to take the answer back.

Press **Answered on your bids** to see older answers. Each row says who answered and when. Press **Take back** or **Put back** to undo one.

Press **Everyone's** to read the news on other people's bids. Your answer on their bid counts for them too. The red number counts your own bids only.

Three rules keep a card off a bid it is not true of.

- The permit names a different suite than your bid. It is another tenant's, so it gives no card and no chip.
- The plumbing permit was there before the bid was started. It gives no card. For six months before the bid it leaves a grey chip, {{chip:gray|Plumbing permit here since Jul 11}}. An older one stays in **This site** only.
- The bid is not a plumbing bid. A plumbing permit says nothing about who has the electrical work.

No card marks a bid for you. Read it, then decide.

On the **Bid Board**, a bid with a permit on its address carries a chip on its row. It reads {{chip:yellow|Applied Sep 11}} or {{chip:green|Issued Nov 3}}. A red chip matches the first kind of news. It names the name the city lists and the permit's day, like {{chip:red|Permit lists Copperline Plumbing · Aug 27}}. A {{chip:green|Plumbing permit · ours}} chip means the plumbing permit lists one of our own names. A chip stays on the row after its card is answered.

A chip with an arrow is a press. It opens **Permit news** on that bid's card. The same chip sits beside the bid on a builder's card in the **Call queue** and on **Waiting to hear**. A chip with no arrow has no card. It only says what the city has.

Our own permit never marks a bid won. Mark the bid yourself when you know.

## Say which names are us

A city lists one name on a plumbing permit. The app cannot know that name is us until someone says so. The owner or a dev keeps the list.

Open [Settings → Company](/settings?tab=settings-company#settings-company-our-permit-names). The card is named **Our names on plumbing permits**.

1. Type a name as the city lists it. Use the company name on a permit, or a master plumber's own name when he files under it.
2. Press {{button:blue|Add the name}}.

Capitals and punctuation make no difference. LLC and Inc make no difference either.

The company name on paper bills counts already. The card says so, and you do not add it.

Press {{button:outline|Take back}} beside a name to take it off the list. A permit under that name then reads as another name again.

The name must be the same as the one on the permit. A name that is only close does not count. Read a permit's row under **This site** to see how the city writes it.

## Who sees it

The office and estimators see the two blocks, Permit news and the chips. They are the same people who see **Prospects → Permits**. Only the owner and a dev see the card in Settings.
