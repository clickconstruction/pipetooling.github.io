Every city publishes the permits it issues. A building permit says who is about to build what and where. The Permits tab under Prospects pulls those permits onto one ledger. You read each one and say what we do about it.

## Open the ledger

Open **Prospects** and press the **Permits** tab. Estimators see the tab too, with or without the Prospects flag.

The ledger shows one city at a time. San Antonio is first. Pick the window with **Filed in the last 30 days**. The **Declared value** pick keeps small permits off the list. It starts at $25k and up, or not declared. Tick **Include houses** to see single family permits too.

The line under the filters says when the feed was last pulled and what is new since you last looked. The same line counts the rows on each lens.

:::example The Pulled line

Pulled 6:10 AM · 14 new since you looked · 9 wait on a look · 3 approached · 2 to look into · 22 not for us · 31 already have a plumber

:::

## Read a row

Each row is one permit. The columns say when it was filed, where, what work, who pulled it, and whether a plumber already has it.

The **Plumber** column is the signal. **none yet** means no plumbing permit sits on that number or address. A plumber's name means the job is taken. **on file, no plumber named** means a plumbing permit sits there under the applicant's own name. That job is taken too.

Press **map** beside an address to see the site in Google Maps. Press the address to open the building.

Press the round **G** beside a name to search Google for it. The search opens in a new tab.

## Read the word under a name

Under who pulled a permit sits one word from our own bid history with that name. **warm** means we have won with them. **cold** means three or more priced bids and nothing won. **new name** means we never bid to them. Hover the word for the reason. The word is a hint. You decide.

## See the rows on a map

Press **Map** beside **Ledger**. The same rows draw as pins, coloured by their lens. A number is a cluster. Click it to open it. The office is ringed at 25 and 50 miles.

Click a pin to read the permit beside the map. Press {{button:blue|Decide…}} there the same as on the ledger.

## See everything about the building

Press an address. A sheet named **The building** opens with four parts.

:::cards

:::card The permit, as the city gave it

The type, the number, the work, the day and the declared value. A press under them shows every field the city sent.

:::

:::card The property, at the county

The owner of record, their mailing address, the land and building values, the year built and the lot.

:::

:::card Every permit on this address

Each permit the feed holds there, newest first. One line says whether a plumbing permit is among them.

:::

:::card More, one press away

**Open the city's permit search** opens the city's own record. **Open the county clerk's records** opens the deeds.

:::

:::

Press **Google the address** to search Google for it. Press the name beside **Pulled by** to open the card on who pulled it.

## Find out who pulled it

Press the name under **Who pulled it**. A card opens with six parts.

:::cards

:::card The company, on Google

The phone, the website, the office address and what kind of business it is. When Google is only guessing, the card says so.

:::

:::card The property, at the county

The owner of record, their mailing address and the appraised value, from Bexar County's own parcel map. Open at the appraisal district shows the full record.

:::

:::card Our history with the name

The same word as on the ledger, with the count behind it.

:::

:::card The city's own record

Press **Copy** for the permit number, then **Open the city's permit search** and paste it. The city lists the applicant, the contractor and the licensed plumber there.

:::

:::card Their other permits

The other permits the same name pulled in the last year, newest first.

:::

:::card The state's business record

Press **Copy** for the company's name, then **Open the state's search** and paste it. The state says whether the company is in good standing.

:::

:::

Press **Google the company** to search Google for the name. A person is searched with their company.

Press **Approach them with these facts** to make the lead with the phone and the website already on it. Each name and each address is looked up once a month. **Look again** asks fresh.

## Decide on a row

Press {{button:blue|Decide…}} on a row. One sheet opens with four verdicts.

:::cards

:::card Approach them

Call who pulled it. The press makes a GC lead on the GCs deck with the permit on it. A lead of the same name is reused.

:::

:::card Look into it

Worth a closer read. The row waits on the list and comes back on the day you pick.

:::

:::card Not for us

Wrong trade, wrong size or wrong part of town. The row leaves the list with your reason.

:::

:::card Already taken

A plumber has it. The row leaves the list.

:::

:::

Every verdict needs a reason. The sheet refuses an empty one. Your name and the day go on the row, so the next estimator sees who looked and why.

## Find the lead you made

After **Approach them**, the row reads **Approached** with a link to **the GC lead**. The lead sits on the GCs deck with **A permit** as its source. Work it there the way you work any lead. See [find jobs to build as the GC](/help/find-jobs-to-build-as-the-gc).

## Pull the feed yourself

The feed is read every morning before the office opens. Press **Pull now** to read it again. The city posts its permits a day or two after it issues them.

## See who wins what

Under **Showing**, pick a lens to see the rows on it. **Everything** shows every permit in the window. **Already have a plumber** shows the jobs other plumbers hold. A strip above the rows counts the plumbers named in the window, most first. Over a season it tells you who wins what in each part of town.
