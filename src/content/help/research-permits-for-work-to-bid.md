Every city publishes the permits it issues. A building permit says who is about to build what and where. The Permits tab under Prospects pulls those permits in. You skim each one and pick the few worth investigating. Then you say what we do about them.

## Pick a city

The city box sits at the top, beside the value pick. The box lists the cities the app reads. They are San Antonio, Austin, New Braunfels, San Marcos and Seguin. The app remembers the city you pick.

On a phone, Skim keeps the city on the strip, beside the magnifier. Tap the city to pick another.

## See how fresh a city is

Each city in the box shows its newest permit day. Open the box to read all five.

:::example The city box

San Antonio · newest Nov 13

Austin · newest Nov 16

New Braunfels · quiet since Nov 3

:::

A city can stop publishing while our pull still runs every morning. Its row then reads **quiet since** and the day it stopped. The box turns amber while that city is on screen. A caught-up skim in that city says the same. It does not promise permits tomorrow.

A quiet city's list is only as new as that day. Press an address, then press **Open the city's permit search** to look for newer permits.

A city reads quiet after seven days with nothing newer. San Antonio publishes a week at a time, so it gets ten days.

A pull of ours that stopped never makes a city read quiet. The line under the strip says what stopped.

On a phone the chip names the day only for a quiet city.

Austin posts a permit once the city issues it. San Antonio also posts applications, so its permits show earlier.

Every city's Property line reads its own county for the owner of record. Austin reads Travis County. San Marcos reads Hays County. New Braunfels reads Comal County. Seguin reads Guadalupe County.

Almost every Austin permit names its lot at Travis County. The app reads that lot first. The owner is then the owner of the lot the permit is on.

In San Antonio a shopping centre can be several lots under one address. The app then takes the lot under the permit's point. A loop keeps the same house number on its east and west stretches. The app passes by a lot on the opposite stretch.

A new building often has no parcel under its street address yet. When the permit names no lot the county holds, the app reads the parcel at the permit's point. The card says so.

A city reaches over its county line in places. The app then reads the county the lot is in. The card names that county.

Part of Austin is in Williamson County. A permit there reads Williamson County for its owner.

Austin sends many permits with no map point. The app then takes the point from the lot's parcel at Travis County. Over the county line it takes it at Williamson County. A permit whose parcel the county does not hold yet shows *No point on this permit*.

The cities do not all publish the same facts.

| City | Who pulled it | Declared value | Shows applications |
|---|---|---|---|
| San Antonio | Yes | Yes | Yes |
| Austin | Yes | Yes | No |
| New Braunfels | Yes | No | Yes |
| San Marcos | No | No | Yes |
| Seguin | No | No | Yes |

San Marcos and Seguin name no one on a permit. Open the building there and press **Open this permit at the city** when the press shows. The city's own page names the contractor.

## Skim the permits

Open **Prospects** and press the **Permits** tab. It opens on **Skim**. Estimators see the tab too, with or without the Prospects flag.

Skim shows one permit at a time. The best ones come first. A green line on the card says why it sits where it does.

Two pictures sit at the top of the card. One is a street view of the address, with the month it was taken. The other is a map with the miles from the office. Press either one to open it in Google.

Press {{button:green|Investigate}} if the permit is worth a closer look. Press **Pass** if it is not. On a desk the right arrow investigates and the left arrow passes. On a phone you can drag the card. Right investigates and left passes.

Nothing is typed. After a pass a dark bar offers four reasons. Tap one if you like. Press **Undo** in the bar to take your answer back. Your answer is saved a few seconds later.

Press the name beside **Who** to see who pulled it. Press the line beside **Property** to see the building. Both have **Investigate** and **Pass** at the foot.

The count says how many are left. A permit stays in the deck until someone answers it. The Dashboard's **Needs you** card says when permits wait. It counts every city and names each one that has some. The Leader is asked first. The rest of the team is asked once a permit has waited two days. When none are left the card says so. Press **Dig in** to open the ones marked to investigate.

## Work the ones you are investigating

A strip under the filters counts where every permit is. **Skim** waits on an answer. **Investigating** waits on a closer look. **Pursuing** has a lead. **Closed** is done. The number on **Skim** is how many cards are left. It drops as you answer.

After you press Investigate the dark bar asks who it is for. Tap a name to hand it over. You can also pick a name beside **Hand to** on a row under Investigating.

Press **Investigating** and press a permit's name. It opens with what was done on it. Press **Pursue…** to go after it. Say what it is for and who follows up. A general contractor's lead goes on the Customers deck. An owner's or an architect's lead goes on Jobs to GC. Press **Not pursuing…** to drop it. Tap one reason.

Press **Put back** on a row under Investigating to send it to the skim again.

## Find a permit on any list

Press the magnifier at the start of the strip. Type what you remember. A street, a permit number, a project, a builder or a city all work. Two words narrow it, like *austin lamar*.

The search looks on every list in every city. Each result says where the permit sits now and the last thing done to it. Press its name to open it.

A passed permit has **Investigate** and **Put back** on its row. Press **San Antonio only** to look in one city. The box names the city on screen.

The other cities are read one after another. The count says which city is still being read.

## Find a closed permit

Press **Closed**. Three chips sort the list. **Passed** holds every pass. **Has a plumber** holds the ones a plumber took. **Not pursuing** holds the ones dropped after a look.

Type a street, a builder or a permit number in the search. Press **Put back** to send a permit to the skim again.

Press **On a map** to see the same permits as pins. Every permit on the chip is there, however old. Press a pin to read its permit beside the map. Press **List** to go back.

Anyone can press **Investigate** on a passed row. The press moves the permit to **Investigating** under your name. The press works on your own pass too.

## See who moved a permit

Every answer is kept with a name and a day. A card that was answered before shows a **History** row. The row lists the last two moves, newest first.

Open the permit to read all of it. The sheet lists every move under **What we did**. A line reads like *Sam passed, Nov 9: too far*.

## Take a second look at what someone else passed

A grey count sits beside **Skim** when someone else has passed permits. The count reads like *12 passed*. Press the count. The deck deals those permits one card at a time, best first. The number is how many wait on you.

The amber line on the card says who passed and when. The line gives their reason when they tapped one.

Press **Investigate** to move the permit to **Investigating** under your name. Press **Pass** to agree with the first pass. Undo works the same as on the skim.

Each permit gets one second look. After two people pass it, it leaves the deck for everyone. Your own passes never show here.

When the skim is caught up, it offers this deck too.

## Know the builders

Press **Builders**. The list shows every name on a commercial permit we can see. While a city's records reach less than a year, a line over the list says the day they start. **Read back to** shows beside it only while San Antonio's records start after January 2025. The press reads about three more months. Older permits count for a builder. Older permits never land on the skim. Each row says how many permits the name is on and how many have no plumber yet. Each row also shows our bids with them and the plumber they use. Our bids are the ones made in the last two years. A practice bid does not count. **not named on the permits** means their jobs have plumbing permits, all under the builder's own name. San Antonio's permits never name a plumber. So the list leaves the plumber out there. **Our bids** counts a customer with the same name, and a customer someone tied to the name.

Press **3 or more permits and never bid** to see who to get to know.

Press **Looks like a customer** to see names that may be one of our customers. Each row says why. Press **Same company** or **Not them**. Nothing is tied until you press.

The Dashboard's **Needs you** card says how many names wait on that answer. The line adds up the number on the chip in every city. Press **Answer them** there to open this list. That line goes away once every name is answered.

Press a builder's name. Pick **Good** or **Avoid** and say why in a line. The mark shows on every skim card of that builder. A Good builder's permits come first. An Avoid builder's permits come last.

Pick **Files for others** under what kind of company for a name that pulls permits for other people. Its cards then lead with the county's owner.

The builder's sheet also says which of our customers the name is. Look under **Our customer under this name**. Each customer shows whose word tied the name and the day. Press **Take back** to undo one.

Sometimes a customer's name begins the name on the permit. The sheet then asks **Same company?** Press **Same company**, **Not them** or **Not sure**. Nothing is tied until you press.

Is the right customer not shown? Press **Tie to a customer** and search our customers. Then press **Same company** beside the right one. For a person the press reads **Works for them**.

## Call five builders this week

Press **Market** on the strip, beside **Builders**. Market says what the permits mean for us. The list on top is **Call this week**. Each name is a builder who keeps starting commercial jobs here. A job is counted once. An application and its permit are one job. So are the buildings of one project filed on one day.

Each row says why the name is on the list.

- {{chip:green|Picking up}} means two or more jobs more in the last 90 days than in the 90 before.
- {{chip:green|New and busy}} means three or more jobs in the last 90 days and none before.
- {{chip:blue|Never bid}} means we priced no job for them in the last two years.
- {{chip:yellow|2 jobs open now}} means two jobs applied for with no plumbing permit yet.
- {{chip:red|We bid 4, won 0}} means we priced four of their jobs in two years and won none. The table under the list counts the same bids.

Five names show, so one person can call them all. A name with a building word comes first, such as Construction, Contracting or Builders. The other companies follow. Then a job open now, then a name picking up or new, then a name never bid. No number is a score.

{{chip:gray|May file for others}} marks a name that reads like a permit service or a design firm. {{chip:gray|Could be a person}} marks a name with no company in it. Those names wait behind the builders. Say what a name is on its builder's sheet to settle it.

Press an address after **Open now** to open the building. Press **Open builder** to open the builder's sheet.

Press {{button:blue|Pursue}} to go after a builder. Say what for, who follows up and what they are. The lead goes on a calling deck, as a pursue on the skim does. Press **Not for us** to pass on the name. Say why in one press, or leave it unsaid.

Either answer takes the name off the list for everyone. The next name takes its place. A pursued name may come back after 90 days. A passed name may come back after a year. Either comes back only once it applies for a new job. Press **Answered** under the list to see the answered names. Press **Put back** to undo one.

The chips over the list filter it. Each chip shows its count. A chip is left out in a city whose records cannot fill it.

**Ask for** on a row says who to ask for at the company. Read [Check who to ask for](#check-who-to-ask-for) before you call.

The list waits when the app cannot read the permits. The red line says why, and no name shows. Press **Retry** to read them again. When only the answers fail to read, the list still shows. A name already answered may then be on the list, and the line under the reason says so. When only the lookups fail to read, the list still shows. No row then says who to ask for.

## Check who to ask for

**Ask for** sits on each row of **Call this week**. The block holds what was looked up about the company. Up to three facts show, one on a line.

- **What it is** says the kind of company. The five kinds are builder, owner, design firm, permit service and trade.
- **Office** is the office phone.
- **Ask for** is one person and their title.

Each fact names the page it came from and the day it was read. Press the page to open it. Nobody at the company was called or written to.

A fact in grey was looked up and not checked. {{chip:gray|Looked up, not checked}} sits beside **Ask for** until someone checks a fact. Treat a grey fact as a lead, since a lookup can be wrong.

Press **Checked** when a fact holds. You check the phone by calling it. You check the person by asking for them. The fact turns bold, like the facts from the permits. Your name and the day go on the line.

Press **Wrong** when a fact does not hold. The fact leaves the row for everyone, and the line says who marked it. Press **Put back** to undo a slip. Press **Not right** on a checked fact that has gone stale.

A looked-up kind also tags the name. The tag is an outline until someone checks the kind. A checked builder moves up with the names that have a building word. A checked design firm, permit service or trade waits behind the other companies.

No lookup takes a name off the list. Press **Not for us** to do that. The kind you say on the builder's sheet always wins over a looked-up kind.

*Could not tell from a public page* means no page said what the company is. Nothing is guessed. Press **Google the builder** to search for what is missing.

A name with no lookup says *Nobody looked this name up yet.* A lookup is run once for a set of names. A name that reads as one person is never looked up.

## See whether the companies we bid build here

The table under the call list lists every company we priced a job for in two years. Each row shows our bids, our wins and the permits its names pulled. The permits are commercial ones from the last twelve months, in every city the app reads. A practice bid does not count. A customer named for a bid site does not count either.

The last column says what the row reads as.

- {{chip:green|Builds, and we win}} means they pull permits and we have won with them. Keep them close.
- {{chip:red|Builds, we never win}} means three or more bids and no win. Ask them why.
- {{chip:yellow|Builds, two tries}} means one or two bids so far. One or two bids is too early to say.
- {{chip:gray|No permit on record}} means no permit carries their exact name. The company may still build.

Three chips filter the table. **Builds here** shows the companies with a permit. **No permit on record** shows the rest. **Tie a name** shows the ones where a name on the permits begins like theirs.

A company may file under a person or under another name. Press **Tie a name** on its row to find that name. The sheet suggests names and says why. Nothing is tied until you press. A tied name's permits then count for the company.

The small words under a company say where its permits are. Press one to open that builder's sheet.

Four numbers sit at the top of **Market**. The first two count the builders to call this week and their jobs open now. The third says how many of the companies we bid pulled a permit. The fourth is the middle wait on the clock.

## Read the clock

The clock sits under the table on **Market**. The clock's bar shows how long you have once the city shows a job. The dark mark is the middle wait. The band is the middle half of the jobs.

In San Antonio the clock runs from an application to its permit. The wait is weeks long. Call while the application waits.

In Austin the clock runs from the building permit to the plumber's permit. The wait is short there. The job is taken by the time the city shows it. Read Austin to learn who builds.

A city with too few dated permits draws no clock.

## See who holds the work

**Who holds the work** sits beside the clock on **Market**. The box lists the plumbers named on the city's permits, by jobs.

The line under the bars says how tied the builders are to one plumber. A builder is judged on three or more jobs with a named plumber. One plumber on three jobs in four means the builder uses one. One plumber on half means the builder leans to one. Fewer means the builder shops around.

The builders that shop around are the open doors. The names of those builders sit under the line. Press one to open the builder.

San Antonio's permits never name a plumber. The box is not drawn there.

## See how much is being built

The last boxes on **Market** count the city's commercial permits. The bars show the permits in each whole month. This month is still filling, so the bars leave it out.

The second box counts the permits by kind. An application is its own kind. The line under the bars says how big the jobs are. A city whose values repeat one figure draws no size.

## Read the count

Six numbers sit under the lists. The first says how many permits arrived this month. The next three say how many were skimmed, marked to investigate and pursued. The last two say how many became a bid and a win. The lines under them say who skimmed and why permits were passed.

## Open the ledger

Press **Ledger** on the strip to see the permits as a list.

The ledger shows one city at a time. San Antonio is first. Pick the window with **Filed in the last 30 days**. The **Declared value** pick keeps small permits off the list. It starts at $25k and up, or not declared. Tick **Include houses** to see single family permits too.

The line under the filters says when the feed was last pulled and what is new since you last looked. The same line counts the rows on each lens.

:::example The Pulled line

Pulled 6:10 AM · 14 new since you looked · 9 to skim · 2 investigating · 3 pursuing · 22 passed · 31 have a plumber

:::

## Read a row

Each row is one permit. The columns say when it was filed, where, what work, who pulled it, and whether a plumber already has it.

The **Plumber** column is the signal. **none yet** means no plumbing permit is tied to that job. A plumber's name means the job is taken. **on file, no plumber named** means a plumbing permit sits there under the applicant's own name. That job is taken too.

A plumbing permit is tied to a job by its permit number or by its street address. San Antonio is the exception. There a job's plumbing permit carries the job's own number and the applicant's name. So in San Antonio a permit at the address counts only when the same applicant filed it. Another tenant's permit in the same centre never closes a job there.

A permit that does not count is still shown. Under **none yet** a line reads **at this address, not counted**. It names who the permit lists and the day. The skim card says the same under **Who**, with the work the permit names. Read it and judge. If it is this job's plumber, press **Decide…** on the row and pick **Has a plumber**.

Press **map** beside an address to see the site in Google Maps. Press the address to open the building.

Press the round **G** beside a name to search Google for it. The search opens in a new tab.

## Read the word under a name

Under who pulled a permit sits one word from our own bid history with that name. **warm** means we have won with them. **cold** means three or more priced bids and nothing won. **new name** means we never bid to them. Hover the word for the reason. The word is a hint. You decide.

## See the rows on a map

Press **Map** beside **Ledger**. The same rows draw as pins, coloured by their lens. A number is a cluster. Click it to open it. The office is ringed at 25 and 50 miles.

Click a pin to read the permit beside the map. Press {{button:blue|Decide…}} there the same as on the ledger.

The map has **Satellite**, **Streets** and **Time**. Click a pin, then press **Time** to see that lot on earlier days. [See a place on a map on an earlier day](/help/see-a-place-on-an-earlier-day) shows how.

## See everything about the building

Press an address. A sheet named **The building** opens with four parts.

:::cards

:::card The permit, as the city gave it

The type, the number, the work, the day and the declared value. A press under them shows every field the city sent.

:::

:::card The property, at the county

The owner of record, their mailing address, the land and building values and the lot. Bexar County also gives the year built and the built area. Travis County gives the year built.

A big lot holds many addresses. When the county files the lot under another address, the sheet says what the county calls it.

:::

:::card Every permit on this address

Each permit the feed holds there, newest first. One line says whether a plumbing permit is among them.

:::

:::card More, one press away

**Open the city's permit search** opens the city's own record. **Open the county clerk's records** opens the deeds.

:::

:::

Press **Google the address** to search Google for it. Press the name beside **Pulled by** to open the card on who pulled it.

## Find out who pulled it

Press the name under **Who pulled it**. A card opens with six parts.

:::cards

:::card The company, on Google

The phone, the website, the office address and what kind of business it is. When Google is only guessing, the card says so.

:::

:::card The property, at the county

The owner of record, their mailing address and the appraised value, from the county's own parcel map. Open at the appraisal district shows the full record.

:::

:::card Our history with the name

The same word as on the ledger, with the count behind it.

:::

:::card The city's own record

Press **Copy** for the permit number, then **Open the city's permit search** and paste it. The city lists the applicant, the contractor and the licensed plumber there.

:::

:::card Their other permits

The other permits we can see under the same name, newest first. The line over them says how far back the records go.

:::

:::card The state's business record

Press **Copy** for the company's name, then **Open the state's search** and paste it. The state says whether the company is in good standing.

:::

:::

Press **Google the company** to search Google for the name. A person is searched with their company.

Press **Pursue with these facts** to make the lead with the phone and the website already on it. Each name and each address is looked up once a month. An address with no parcel is looked up again after a week. **Look again** asks fresh.

## Decide on a row

Press {{button:blue|Decide…}} on a row. One sheet opens with four verdicts.

:::cards

:::card Pursue

Go after it. Call who pulled it. The press makes a lead with the permit on it. Say who they are in **They are**. A general contractor goes on the Customers deck. An owner or an architect goes on Jobs to GC. A lead of the same name is reused.

:::

:::card Investigate

Worth a closer look. The row waits under Investigating and comes back on the day you pick.

:::

:::card Pass

Wrong trade, wrong size or wrong part of town. The row leaves the list with your reason.

:::

:::card Has a plumber

A plumber has it. The row leaves the list.

:::

:::

Every verdict needs a reason. The sheet refuses an empty one. Your name and the day go on the row, so the next estimator sees who looked and why.

## Find the lead you made

After **Pursue**, the row reads **Pursuing** with a link to **the lead**. The link opens the deck the lead sits on. A general contractor sits on the Customers deck, with the permit in its notes. An owner or an architect sits on Jobs to GC, with the permit as a job. Each lead has **A permit** as its source. Work a lead there the way you work any lead. See [find jobs to build as the GC](/help/find-jobs-to-build-as-the-gc).

## Pull the feed yourself

The feed is read every morning before the office opens. Press **Pull now** to read it again. The city posts its permits a day or two after it issues them.

## See who wins what

Under **Showing**, pick a lens to see the rows on it. **Everything** shows every permit in the window. **Has a plumber** shows the jobs other plumbers hold. A strip above the rows counts the plumbers named in the window, most first. Over a season it tells you who wins what in each part of town.
