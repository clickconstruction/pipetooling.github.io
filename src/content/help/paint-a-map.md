A painted map shows what a street address cannot say. Walk the way in to a job, or draw it on the view from above. Mark the gate, the meter or where to park.

Anyone who can open a job can paint a map for it.

## Start a map

The Dashboard has a {{button:outline|Paint a map}} press near the top. You do not have to be clocked in to see it.

- In Job Mode the press is one of the squares under the header.
- On the office's phone the press is a link under the clock.
- Everywhere else the press is a square after **Job Report**.

Press {{button:outline|Paint a map}}. The **Paint a map** sheet opens.

The app sets the job when it knows it. Clocked in, that is the job you are clocked onto. Otherwise it is the stop you are at today, or the next one.

The company's office job is never the job the app sets. A day at the office is not a site to paint a way in for.

With no clock and no stop today, the sheet opens on your next job. That is the first job on your schedule in the next six days. A way in is most use the day before the crew arrives.

With no job coming up, the sheet opens on your last job. That is the newest job you clocked onto or had on your schedule in the last two weeks.

A line under **For** says which job the app picked, and its day.

The sheet names the job under **For**. Press **Change the job** if the app picked the wrong one.

### Pick another job

Every painted map is on a job. **Change the job** opens **Which job is it for?**. That window also opens first when the app has no next job and no last job for you.

- **Search every job** is the box at the top. Type a number, a name or an address.
- **Today** lists the jobs on your schedule today.
- **Coming up** lists up to four jobs on your schedule in the next two weeks, the soonest first.
- **Your last jobs** lists up to six jobs you were on, the newest first.

A way in is most use the day before the crew arrives. So a job under **Coming up** says its day, such as *tomorrow*.

A plain day beside a job under **Your last jobs** is the last day you clocked onto it. The word *scheduled* over a day means the job was on your schedule that day. The word *changed* over a day means you changed the job in the app that day. The words *on the team* mean the office put you on the job.

Work at a desk? The jobs you changed fill the list. Taking a payment, moving a stage and editing a job each count.

The company's office job is in none of these lists. The search still finds it.

Press a job. The **Paint a map** sheet opens on it.

Standing on the site? Press **Near me** beside the search. Your phone asks to share where you are. Say yes.

**Near you** then lists the closest jobs first, with how far each is. Your phone is read once, on that press.

The list holds only jobs whose address the app has placed on a map. Search for any job it leaves out.

A job you are not on shows grey. You cannot paint a map for it. Ask the office to put you on the job.

### From a job

A painted map also sits wherever the app shows an address.

- On a job's window, look under the address at the top.
- On your own job card in Job Mode, look above the customer's lines.
- On a development's page, look under its address.

Press {{button:outline|＋ Paint a map}}. The same sheet opens, on that job.

### The sheet

1. Check the job under **For**.
2. Type what the map is, or press a name such as {{chip:gray|The way in}}.
3. Type a line for whoever opens it, if a line would help.
4. Pick how to start.

- {{button:red|Walk or drive it}} paints the line as you move.
- {{button:blue|Draw it by hand}} lets you press the line out on the map.
- {{button:outline|Start with a place}} begins with a dot.

The map opens on the job's address. Nothing is saved yet.

A job in a development has two choices under **For**. Press the job's name to paint for that job alone. Press the development's name to paint for every lot.

A map started on a development's page is for every lot. It shows on each job in the development.

### From the search

Press the search button at the top of the app and type **paint**. Press **Paint a map**.

The sheet opens on your next job or your last, as it does from the Dashboard. With a job on your schedule today, **Which job is it for?** opens first.

### Picking a job with no signal

The list of jobs cannot be read with no signal. The app says so and tries again by itself.

Press **Paint now and pick the job later** to paint anyway. Only you and the office can open a map on no job.

Open that map later and press **Put it on a job…** to give it one.

### A map on the wrong job

Open the map and press **Move it to another job…**. Press the job it belongs on.

The press shows for whoever painted the map, and for the office.

## Walk or drive the line

Press {{button:red|Walk or drive it}}. Your phone asks to share where you are. Say yes.

A red bar at the top says **Painting**, with a clock. Your phone is read only while that bar is red.

Walk the way in with the phone in your hand. The line grows behind you on the map. The window shows the length so far.

Under the length, one line says how your phone's location is doing. The line is there the whole time the bar is red.

- *Finding you…* shows before the first reading. The first reading takes a few seconds in the open.
- *Precise Location is on.* shows with a green tick, and how sure your phone is, such as *Sure within 20 ft.*
- An amber line means the signal is weak. See [When the phone is not sure](#when-the-phone-is-not-sure).
- *Precise Location looks off.* means your phone gives only the area you are in. See [When Precise Location looks off](#when-precise-location-looks-off).

**Keep the screen on.** The app holds the screen on for you. A phone's browser cannot read where you are once the screen locks. A phone in a pocket draws nothing.

- Press {{button:outline|Pause}} to stop the reading at once. The bar turns grey.
- Press {{button:red|Keep painting}} to go on. The next stretch is a new line on the same map.
- Press {{button:outline|＋ Place}} to mark what is here. The painting goes on behind it.
- Press {{button:blue|Done}} when you reach the end.

Painting pauses by itself after 5 minutes standing still. It ends by itself after an hour.

### When the screen went off

A call, another app or the lock button all pause the painting. The window then says the line has a gap.

- {{button:red|Keep painting from here}} starts a new line where you stand now.
- {{button:outline|Join the gap with a straight line}} carries the old line on to where you stand.

### When the phone is not sure

Walls and trees bend the signal. The line turns amber and says *Precise Location is on, but the signal is weak here.* It also says how sure your phone is, such as *Your phone is only sure within 60 ft.*

That stretch is drawn as faint dots and is left out of the length. Fix it by hand when you look the line over.

A phone that is unsure by more than about 330 feet reads *The signal is weak here.*

The switch is not the trouble either way. Step into the open and the line turns green again.

### When Precise Location looks off

**Precise Location** is a switch in your phone's settings. With it off, your phone gives only the area you are in. That area can be miles across, so no line can be painted from it.

The app is never told whether the switch is on. The app reads how sure your phone is. A phone that stays unsure by a mile or more for 4 seconds reads *Precise Location looks off.*

The window then shows your phone's two numbers side by side. *Your phone now* is how sure it is. *With Precise Location on* is how sure a phone is with the switch on.

The map pulls back to a dashed circle. You are somewhere in that circle. No dot is drawn, since your phone does not know the spot.

Under the two numbers are the steps to the switch, for your phone alone. In Safari on an iPhone they are these.

1. Open the **Settings** app.
2. Tap **Privacy & Security**, then **Location Services**.
3. Tap **Safari Websites**.
4. Turn on **Precise Location**.
5. Come back to the app.

Chrome on an iPhone has its own row. Tap **Chrome** at step 3.

On an Android phone the switch is named **Use precise location**.

1. Press and hold the browser's icon, then tap **App info**.
2. Tap **Permissions**, then **Location**.
3. Turn on **Use precise location**.
4. Come back to the app.

Leaving for the settings pauses the painting, as any other app does. Press {{button:red|Keep painting from here}} when you are back. The line says *Finding you…*, then *Precise Location is on.* The map comes back in to where you stand.

Is the switch on already? Then your phone may only be hearing a cell tower. Step into the open and wait a moment.

You can press {{button:blue|Draw it by hand instead}} at any time. The map then goes back to the job.

## Look it over, then save

Press {{button:blue|Done}}. **Look it over** shows every line of the walk on the map. Nothing has left your phone yet.

- **Fix this line by hand** opens the line's corners. Drag a corner onto the right spot, or press one and remove it. Then press {{button:blue|Use this line}}.
- **Leave this line out** drops one line.
- {{button:outline|＋ Place}} marks a spot, such as the gate. A place is saved when you add it.
- {{button:outline|Keep painting}} goes back to walking.
- {{button:outline|Throw it away}} drops the whole walk. A place you added stays on the map.

Press {{button:blue|Save the map}} to keep it.

A saved line is only a shape. It keeps no time along it. It cannot say how fast you went or where you stopped.

:::example The way in, walked
**Walk or drive it** → walk from the gate to the slab → {{button:outline|Gate}} at the gate → **Done** → **Save the map**.
:::

### With no signal

Your phone finds itself without a signal, so the line paints the same. The picture under it may be blank.

If the save does not go through, the walk stays on your phone. The app says so. Press {{button:blue|Save the map}} again when you have signal.

### A walk you did not save

Close the map before saving and the app asks what to do with the walk.

- **Close, and keep the walk on this phone** keeps it for a day, paused. The app offers it back with **Look it over** and **Throw it away**.
- **Close, and throw the walk away** drops it at once. Nothing was sent anywhere, so nothing is left behind.
- **Stay** goes back to the map.

## Draw the line by hand

Drawing by hand needs no location. It is the better way to show a drive from the office, and the better way to get a length.

Press the map where the line starts. Press again at each turn. Each press is a corner.

The window shows the length as you go, such as *about 830 ft*.

- Press {{button:outline|Undo}} to take back your last move.
- Press a corner and then {{button:outline|Remove this corner}} to take it out.
- Drag a corner to move it.
- Drag the small dot in the middle of a leg to make a corner there.

Press {{button:blue|Save the map}} when the line is right. The map and its line are saved together.

:::example A way in, in six presses
Press at the truck gate → press at each bend of the drive → press at the slab → **Save the map**.
:::

## Use the magnifying glass

A fingertip covers fifty feet of ground or more. On a phone, a magnifying glass helps you place a corner exactly.

Press the map and hold still. A round glass opens above your finger. Slide your finger and watch the cross in the glass. Lift your finger to set the corner.

The glass opens when you drag a corner too.

A corner dragged close to the end of another line jumps onto it. The ring of the glass turns green. Pull away and it lets go.

The glass is as sharp as the picture. One dot of the picture is a foot or two of ground. A corner can be placed that closely and no closer.

A mouse is already that exact, so a desk gets no glass. Roll the wheel to zoom in.

## Add a place

A place is a named dot on the map. It says what is there, such as the gate, the meter or where to park.

1. Open the map and press {{button:outline|＋ Place}}. A blue dot appears in the middle of the map.
2. Drag the blue dot onto the spot. On a phone the magnifying glass opens as you drag.
3. Press what it is, such as {{button:outline|Gate}}. Type a name of your own if you want one.
4. Type what whoever comes next should know under **Describe it**.
5. Press **＋ Add a photo** to show what the spot looks like. On a phone the camera opens.
6. Press {{button:blue|Add this place}}.

The place gets a number. The same number is on its dot and beside its name.

On a walk, {{button:outline|＋ Place}} is under the red bar. It is on **Look it over** too.

### A mark for each kind

Each press under **What is here?** is a kind of place. The presses are big, two to a row. A thumb in a glove can land on one.

A kind gives the dot its own mark, such as a gate or a valve. The place's number moves to the corner of the mark.

There are eight kinds. The kinds are {{button:outline|Gate}}, {{button:outline|Park here}}, {{button:outline|Door}}, {{button:outline|Meter}}, {{button:outline|Shutoff}}, {{button:outline|Cleanout}}, {{button:outline|Buried line}} and {{button:outline|Hazard}}.

{{button:outline|Hazard}} is drawn in red. Use it for an open trench or a live wire.

A press names a place that has no name yet. A press never changes a name you typed.

Press a kind a second time to take it off. The dot goes back to its plain number.

Type a name and press no kind, and the dot keeps its plain number.

### Say what is buried

Press {{button:outline|Buried line}}. The sheet asks **What is buried?**.

Press {{button:outline|Water}}, {{button:outline|Sewer}}, {{button:outline|Gas}}, {{button:outline|Electric}}, {{button:outline|Irrigation}} or {{button:outline|Cable}}.

The place is named for it, such as *Buried sewer line*.

The place's mark takes the color a locator paints on the ground.

- Water is blue.
- Sewer is green.
- Gas is yellow.
- Electric is red.
- Irrigation is purple.
- Cable is orange.

Press the same answer again to take it off. The place stays a buried line.

### Something not on the list

Press {{button:outline|＋ Something else}}. That press runs the full width of the sheet.

The box under it asks **What is it?**, and the keyboard opens. Type the name, such as *Septic tank lid*.

The dot keeps its plain number.

Under **What is buried?** the same press asks what is buried. The place stays a buried line.

### Describe it

**Describe it** takes as much as you need. Say what it is, how deep it is and which way it runs.

The box grows as you type.

A list shows the start of a long description. Press **Read it all** to open the rest. Press **Show less** to fold it again.

A printed page and a shared link show the whole description. A picture and a text show the start of it.

Standing on the spot? Press {{button:outline|Put the dot where I am}}. The dot moves to you when your phone is sure enough. Your phone is read once, on that press, and is not followed.

A line under the press says how that reading did.

- *Precise Location is on.* means the dot is where you stand.
- An amber line means the signal is weak. The dot is near you. Drag it onto the spot.
- *Precise Location looks off.* means the dot was not moved. Your phone gave only an area, and its middle can be miles away.

When the dot was not moved, drag it onto the spot by hand. Or turn the switch on and press again. [When Precise Location looks off](#when-precise-location-looks-off) has the steps.

Sometimes your phone gives no reading at all. An amber box under the press then says why, and the dot was not moved. Drag the dot onto the spot, or press again.

Never type a gate code or a lock code on a place. Anyone the map is shared with would read it.

:::example What is where, in three places
＋ Place → {{button:outline|Gate}} → photo → **Add this place**. Then the same for {{button:outline|Meter}} and {{button:outline|Shutoff}}.
:::

To change a place you set, press **Change this place** beside it. Drag its dot, change its words or take another photo. Then press {{button:blue|Save the place}}. **Remove this place** takes it off the map for everyone.

Press a photo in the list to see it large.

### More than one photo

A place can hold more photos after its first. Show the trench open, then show it filled.

1. Press **Change this place** beside the place.
2. Press {{button:outline|＋ Another photo}}. On a phone the camera opens.
3. Press {{button:blue|Save the place}}.

The extra photos sit under the place's words in the list. Press one to see it large.

Press **Remove** under a photo to take it off. A place holds twelve more photos.

A shared link shows a place's first photo only.

### Walk the line of a buried place

A buried line has a length. Walk it before the trench is filled, and the place keeps that length.

1. Open **Places on this job** and find the buried place.
2. Press {{button:outline|＋ Walk this line}}. Or press {{button:outline|＋ Draw this line}} to press it out on the map.
3. Walk the line and press **Done**. Then press **Save the map**.
4. Close the map. **Places on this job** opens again on the place.

The line is on a painted map of its own, named for the place.

The place's row now says the length, such as *About 60 ft, give or take 10 ft*. Press **Open the line** to see it.

The line is drawn in the color of what is buried. A sewer line is green.

You see the two presses on a place you set. The office sees them on every buried place.

## Send one place to someone

Each place has a press of its own for this. You do not share the whole map.

On a phone, press **Text this place…** beside the place. Messages opens with the words already written. You pick who gets it.

The text says what the place is and the word you left on it. It ends with a map link to the exact spot.

Whoever gets it presses the link. A map app opens with a dot on the spot. They need no sign-in and give no name.

They see that one spot. They do not see the painted map or its other places.

At a desk the press reads **Copy this place**. It copies the same words. Paste them into a text or an email.

Anyone who can see a place can send it. To send the whole map, press {{button:outline|Share}}.

## Send more than the spot

**Text this place…** sends the spot. It does not send the photo. A place with a photo says so under the press.

To send more, press **Send more…** beside it. A short sheet opens. It shows only the ways that fit this place.

- **This place with its photo.** Your phone's share sheet opens with the photo and the words. Pick Messages. Then pick who gets it.
- **All 3 places on this job.** Messages opens with every place in one text. Each place has its own map link. The press names your own count.
- **The whole map.** The Share sheet opens. From there you send a link to the painted map.

The photo's way shows on a phone, for a place that has a photo.

The way for every place shows when there are two places or more. At a desk it copies them.

**The whole map** shows if you painted the map or work in the office.

Nobody who gets a photo or the places needs a sign-in. A link to the whole map asks for their name first.

Some places have no **Send more…**. That place has no photo, stands alone, and is on a map you cannot share.

## Keep a place on the job

Some spots matter on every map of a job. The gate is one. The meter is another. Keep those on the job itself.

1. Open the job. Under its address, press {{button:outline|＋ Place on this job}}.
2. Drag the blue dot onto the spot.
3. Press what it is, such as {{button:outline|Gate}}.
4. Press {{button:blue|Add this place}}.

You do not paint a map first. The job now has a line named **Places on this job**. Press it to see them all.

A place on the job wears a letter. A place on one map wears a number.

Every painted map of the job shows the job's places. They have a dashed ring and sit under **On this job**. To change one, press **Open Places on this job**.

A link or a copy of a painted map does not carry the job's places. To send them, open **Places on this job** and press {{button:outline|Share}}.

Anyone can change or remove a place they set. Only the office can throw the whole list away.

### Places an earlier job kept at this address

The next job at a house is a different job. The cleanout is where it was.

Press the line or a row to open that list. A new place you set goes on your own job.

Only a job's list of places follows the address. A painted map of the way in stays on its own job.

A job with no customer address on file shares nothing. Ask the office to pick the customer's address on the job.

Adding a photo to the job anyway? Each new photo in **Job photos** has {{button:outline|＋ Keep this spot on the job}}. A photo on **Leave Report** has {{button:outline|Keep this spot}}. Either press opens the same sheet with that photo in it.

## Read a length

A length is always *about*. It comes with a give or take, such as *About 830 ft, give or take 10 ft*.

A line drawn by hand is good to a few feet at each end. A walked line is rougher. A phone under open sky is good to about 16 feet, and worse beside buildings and trees.

So a walked 50 feet can read 35 or 65. Either line is fine for how far a driver goes. Neither is a number to price from.

For a number you will price, measure with a wheel or a tape. Then type it onto the line.

1. Open the map.
2. Under the line, press **Type a length measured with a wheel or a tape**.
3. Type the feet. Press {{chip:blue|A wheel}} or {{chip:gray|A tape}}.
4. Press {{button:blue|Save the length}}.

The line then reads *612 ft, measured with a wheel*.

## Open a map

A job with a painted map shows a line under its address. The line has the map's name, its length and how many places it holds. Press it to open the map.

The line shows on every tab of the job's window. It shows on your job card in Job Mode too.

You can open a map from anywhere. Press the search button at the top and type the map's name or the job's name.

### In the job's Documents

The job's **Documents** tab has a section named **Painted maps**. It lists the job's maps and its development's maps.

Each row says the map's length, its places, who painted it and when. Press a map's name to open it. Press {{button:outline|Paint a map}} to start a new one for the job.

Press {{button:outline|Share}} on a row to get the map's link without opening the map.

A map there is the map as it is now. It is not a stored copy. A page you printed is kept apart, under **Sent from this job** on the same tab.

### Every map in one list

Open [Documents](/documents?tab=painted-maps) and press the **Painted maps** tab. It lists every painted map you can open, newest first.

Each row says where the map is, what is on it and who painted it. A map on no job says **On no job yet**. Press a map's name to open it.

A long list has a box to find a map. Type part of its name, its job or its words.

Press {{button:outline|Share}} on a row to get that map's link.

### On other maps

The **Map** page has a chip named {{chip:gray|Painted maps}}. Press it to draw every painted map where it is.

Hold the pointer over a line to read its map's name and length. Press the line to open the map.

A job's painted maps also show on its own site map, under the boxes. Open the job's **Materials** tab and press **Map**.

Press **On my way** on your job card. The sheet lists the maps painted for the job you picked. Press one to see the way in before you drive.

A development's page lists its own maps first. Each lot's maps come after, with the lot's name.

Press {{button:outline|Directions to the start}} to open the phone's maps app at the start of the first line. A maps app can route to a gate on a road. It cannot follow a painted line.

Press {{button:outline|From above}} or {{button:outline|Streets}} to change the picture under the line.

### On a task

A task can carry a painted map. The person who gets the task sees the line on the task and opens the map from there.

- **With a place**: the map is the way to that place. [Add the way in](/help/give-someone-a-task#add-the-way-in) shows how.
- **By itself**: for work that is a line, like a trench. [Add a map](/help/give-someone-a-task#add-a-map) shows how.

Whoever gets the task can open the map, even on a job they are not on the crew of.

## Change a map

Press {{button:blue|Draw a line}} to add another line to the map. Press {{button:outline|Walk or drive a line}} to walk one. Press {{button:outline|＋ Place}} to add a place. Anyone who can open the job can add one.

Under a line you made:

- **Move its corners** opens a line drawn by hand to drag its corners again.
- **Remove this line** takes it off the map for everyone.

Under the lines, **Rename this map** and **Throw this map away** show for whoever painted the map.

The office can change any line and any map.

## Share a map

A painted map can go to someone outside the company. A customer, a delivery driver or another trade opens it with no sign-in.

### Right after you save

The map says **Saved** in a green band as soon as a save lands. The band says where the map is and who can open it.

On a phone, {{button:blue|Text it from my phone…}} sits under the band. Press it to text the map without opening **Share**.

Messages opens the moment you press. The band tells you if the link in a text will not open. Do not send that text.

The press shows for whoever painted the map, and for the office.

### From the Share sheet

Open the map and press {{button:outline|Share}}. The press shows for whoever painted the map, and for the office.

:::steps

1. Press {{button:blue|Text it from my phone…}}. Your phone's Messages opens with the words and the link written.
2. Pick who gets it, and send it.

The first press makes the link. You do not make one first.

Messages opens the moment you press. On a weak signal the sheet may say the link is ready first. Then press {{button:blue|Open Messages}}.

The sheet tells you if the link in a text will not open. Do not send that text. Press the blue press again.

At a desk there is no **Text it from my phone…**. {{button:blue|Copy the link}} leads there.

### Other ways to send it

Four more presses sit under the blue one. Any of the four makes the link if none is out.

- Press **Show a code to scan** when the person is standing next to you. A phone's camera opens the map from the code.
- Press **Copy the link** and paste it into a text or an email.
- Press **Send it another way…** for mail, a group chat or a phone nearby. Your phone's own share sheet opens.
- Press **Make a picture** for someone who will not open a link.

Anyone who has the link can open the map. Send it only to people who should have it.

### Text it to the job's customer

The office can text the link from the company's number. Press **Text the customer…** on the sheet.

The customer's text box opens with the map's name and the link in it. Change the words if you like.

Press **Send text…** and look it over before it goes. Nothing is sent until you do.

The text lands in the customer's thread on the job. Their answer comes back to Messages.

On a map with no job the press is grey. The grey press says the map is on no job, since there is no customer to text.

### They give their name first

The page asks **Your name** before it shows the map. It says what we keep before they type.

We keep the name, the phone or computer they opened it on, and the time. A name is whatever they type. We cannot check it.

The page remembers the name on that phone. The same person is not asked again.

A teammate who is signed in types nothing. The page uses the name on their account.

### What they see

They see the map's name, its lines, and its places with their words and photos. They can press **Directions to the start**.

A place with a kind shows its mark to them too. A gate looks like a gate on their map.

They do not see who painted it or when. They see nothing else about the job.

The sheet says this too. Press **What they see** at the foot of the sheet.

Never put a gate code on a painted map. A link shows everything on the map to whoever holds it.

### Where they are

The page has a press named **Show where I am**. It puts a blue dot on the map for the reader.

The line under the press says we will see where they are while it is on. We keep the first place and the latest one. We never keep a trail.

A reader who does not press it shares no place. The map still works for them.

The page also says how sure the reader's phone is.

- A sure phone gets a green tick, with words such as *Your phone is sure within 15 ft.*
- A weak signal reads *The signal is weak here.* The dot is still drawn.
- A phone that gives only an area gets no dot. The page reads *Your phone is only giving the area you are in.*

When a phone gives only an area, the page lists the steps to turn on **Precise Location**. The page names the switch nowhere else.

The map pulls back to a dashed circle when that area is near the map. The reader is somewhere in the circle.

The company is then sent the area and how wide it is. The page says so under the steps.

### See who opened it

Open **Share** on the map again. Under the presses, **Who opened it** lists each reader.

A row says the name they gave, their phone or computer, when they opened it and how many times. A teammate's row says **signed in**.

A reader who pressed **Show where I am** has one more line. Press **See that place** to open it in a map app.

A reader whose phone gave only an area reads *Showed only the area they were in*, with how wide it was. An area has no place to open.

Only the office and whoever painted the map see this list.

### End a link

A link ends by itself after 90 days. The sheet says the day.

Press **End this link now** to end it sooner. Anyone who opens it after that reads that the map is no longer shared.

The sheet says how many times a link was opened.

A link can be sent again only from the device that made it. On another device, your first press makes a new link for that device.

## Hand over a copy

Some people will not open a link. Some places need paper. The Share sheet makes a copy for them.

A copy cannot be taken back. It goes out of date when the map changes. Send the link when you can.

A copy makes the link if none is out. So every copy carries a way back to the map as it is today.

### A picture

Press **Make a picture**. The sheet draws the map, its places and the day as one image.

Then press {{button:blue|Share the picture}} to send it from your phone. On a computer the press reads {{button:blue|Save the picture}}.

A picture shows in a text thread without a press. The word Start marks where the first line begins.

A place with a kind keeps its mark in the picture and on the printed page.

Press **Every way to share** to go back to the other presses.

### A page to print

Press **Print or a file**, then **Print, or save as a PDF**. One page opens with the map, the places and their photos.

Pick your printer, or pick Save as PDF in the print window.

The page carries a code to scan. The code opens the map as it is today.

The app keeps a copy of every page you print. The office finds it on the job's **Documents** tab.

### A file for another map

An engineer or a surveyor may keep a map of their own. Press **Print or a file** to find two presses that make a file for it.

- **A .kml file** opens in Google Earth and Google My Maps.
- **A .gpx file** opens in a hiking app or a handheld.

A file holds the lines and the places with their words. It holds no photos.

A file draws no mark. A place's kind is written in its name, such as *Hazard: Open trench*.

## Read a map on Google Maps

Open a saved map to read it. The map under the lines is Google Maps.

**From above** is Google's photograph, with the street names on it. **Streets** is Google's road map.

Press {{button:blue|Draw a line}} or {{button:outline|＋ Place}} and the picture under the map changes. You draw on the view from above the app has always used.

The map then stays on that picture until you close it. Your new line stays on the photograph you drew it over.

The two photographs were taken on different days. A line can sit a few feet to one side on the other one.

**Time** changes the picture too, since the older photographs are not Google's. Press **Time** again to go back to Google Maps.

A picture and a printed page are drawn on the view from above, never on Google Maps.

When Google Maps cannot load, the map opens on the view from above. Nothing on the map is lost.

The page a link opens is not on Google Maps yet.

## When the picture is old

The view from above can be a year or two old. A new development may show as a bare field.

Walk or drive the way once, and the line shows what the picture does not.

## See the ground on an earlier day

Open a saved map. Press **Time** beside **From above** and **Streets**. A row of dates opens under the map.

Press a date to see the ground on that day, with the lines over it. Press **Today** to see today's picture again.

**Time** is for reading a map. The press goes away while you draw a line or place a dot. [See a place on a map on an earlier day](/help/see-a-place-on-an-earlier-day) has the rest.
