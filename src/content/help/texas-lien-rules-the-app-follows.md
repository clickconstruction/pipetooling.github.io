Every lien deadline and every line in a demand letter comes from a rule in Texas law. This page lists each rule the app follows. Each rule is named by the question it answers. Under the question comes a one-line answer, then the rule, then what you do in the app.

## How to use this page

Each rule has four parts. The first line is the short answer. **What it says** is the rule. **What you do** is where you act on it in the app. **Cite** is the law it comes from.

Press {{button:outline|§ Rules}} on the Lien desk or in the Lien window. The rules open in a window over the desk, at the rule for what is on screen. The other rules are folded to their question and their short answer. Press a question to unfold it. The list on the left jumps to a rule. The find box at the top narrows the rules to what you type. Enter walks the matches and Esc closes the window. When the desk has a job picked, a strip at the top shows that job's two dates.

A rule counsel has not read yet says so in an amber line. The app follows it today on the owner's reading.

New to liens? Start with [understand how liens work and which lien tool to use](/help/understand-how-liens-work-and-which-lien-tool-to-use).

This page was checked on 2026-09-14 against the statute text at statutes.capitol.texas.gov. The case cites were checked on FindLaw and Justia. This page is the one copy of the rules. When a rule changes, it changes here.

## The clock

### When each date falls

Count from the month the work was done, never from the bill date. Each unpaid month has its own clock.

**What it says.** Every lien deadline counts from the month the labor or materials were furnished. The bill date does not matter. Each unpaid month has its own clock. The clock reads the approved clock sessions. A sub's notice is due by the 15th of the third month after the work. The lien is due by the 15th of the fourth month after the last month worked. On a house both dates are a month earlier.

:::example Work done in 2026, on a job with a GC

| Work done in | Notice due, commercial | Notice due, a house | Lien due, commercial | Lien due, a house |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| June | Sep 15 | Aug 17 | Oct 15 | Sep 15 |
| July | Oct 15 | Sep 15 | Nov 16 | Oct 15 |
| August | Nov 16 | Oct 15 | Dec 15 | Nov 16 |

Aug 15 and Nov 15 fall on a weekend, so they move to Monday. In the § Rules window this table shows the months around today.

:::

**What you do.** Nothing by hand. The Lien desk, the Payment forecast and the notice's *months covered* line all count from the work month for you. [Open the Lien desk](/jobs?tab=stages&liendesk=1).

**Cite.** *Prop. Code § 53.056(a-1), § 53.052*

### A house moves every date up a month

A house, a duplex, a triplex, a fourplex or a condo its owner lives in is residential. Every deadline is a month earlier.

**What it says.** Some properties are residential. They are a house, a duplex, a triplex, a fourplex and a condo unit its owner lives in. On a residential property every deadline moves a month earlier.

**What you do.** Set the kind on the property record. The notice date and the filing date shift a month earlier on their own. When the kind is not set, the app shows the commercial dates and says so. [Open the Lien desk](/jobs?tab=stages&liendesk=1) and press a red *Fix the property* gate to set it.

**Cite.** *§ 53.001(10), § 53.052(b), § 53.056(a-1)*

### A 15th on a weekend moves to Monday

A deadline on a Saturday or a Sunday moves to the next business day. The app moves weekends only.

**What it says.** A deadline on a Saturday, a Sunday or a legal holiday moves to the next business day.

**What you do.** Nothing. The app moves weekends for you. When the 15th is a holiday, the app still shows the 15th. That is the earlier, safe date.

**Cite.** *§ 53.003*

## The notice

### Does this job need a monthly notice?

Only when there is a GC. No GC means we contracted with the owner, and the affidavit stands alone.

**What it says.** A GC on the job means we are a subcontractor. GC means the general contractor. Then every unpaid month needs a notice before any lien. The notice goes to the owner and to the GC. No GC means we contracted with the owner. Then no monthly notice is needed. The affidavit stands alone.

**What you do.** Name the GC on the job. The Lien desk's Notices list then shows the job. A job with no GC is never on that list. The notice tab shows a *Role* line that says which one the job is. [Open the Lien desk](/jobs?tab=stages&liendesk=1&kind=notice).

**Cite.** *§ 53.056, § 53.052*

### What the notice is and where it goes

A form the statute sets. It goes to the owner of record and to the GC, by certified mail, with the invoice behind it.

**What it says.** The notice is a form the statute sets. It goes to the owner of record and to the original contractor. The owner of record is the owner the county's records name. It is due by the 15th of the third month after the work month. On a residential property it is due by the 15th of the second month. It goes by certified mail or another service that can be traced. It may include the invoice.

**What you do.** Draft it on the Lien desk under Notices. The desk picks the months and the owner of record, prints the form, and encloses the invoice behind it. [Open the Notices list](/jobs?tab=stages&liendesk=1&kind=notice).

**Cite.** *§ 53.056(a-1) to (a-3), § 53.003*

### What the notice does for the owner

The owner may hold back what we are owed from the GC. Money the owner pays out after the notice is the owner's risk.

**What it says.** The owner may withhold what we are owed from the GC. This is called fund trapping. The owner is liable for money paid out after the notice.

**What you do.** Nothing extra. Counsel's cover letter goes on the owner's copy of every notice the desk sends. You can untick the letter on a draft. When a supply house is still owed, the letter ends with a paragraph about it. It names the house, its money and its notice day. It says the owner may withhold. It says what paying the GC anyway risks. The demand letter points the owner to the notice. It does not point to a demand.

**Cite.** *§ 53.081, § 53.082, § 53.084*

### The owner demand is gone

The old demand for payment to the owner was repealed. The notice is the paper the owner gets.

**What it says.** The old *§ 53.083* demand for payment to the owner was repealed. The repeal covers contracts made on or after 2022-01-01.

**What you do.** Nothing. The app has no owner demand. The notice is the paper the owner gets.

**Cite.** *HB 2237 (87th Leg.) § 36(8)*

## The lien

### What the affidavit needs before it can be filed

Four things. The owner of record, the county and the legal description, a recorded notice on a sub job, and no homestead.

**What it says.** The affidavit is the lien itself. It is sworn. It is filed with the County Clerk of the property's county. It is due by the 15th of the fourth month after the last month worked. On a residential property it is due by the 15th of the third month. A copy is served on the owner and the GC within 5 days of filing.

The affidavit needs four things.

- The owner of record.
- The county and the legal description.
- A recorded notice, on a sub job.
- A property that is not a homestead.

**What you do.** Open the job's Lien window on the Mechanic's lien tab. It checks the four facts first. Press {{button:outline|Record filing}} to record the filing. A red Needs you card watches the serve-by date. Press {{button:outline|Record service}} to record the service. [Open the Affidavits list](/jobs?tab=stages&liendesk=1&kind=affidavit).

**Cite.** *§ 53.052, § 53.054, § 53.055*

### How soon the lien can be filed

As soon as the work is done and the steps are taken. The 15th dates are the last day to file, not the first.

**What it says.** The statute sets only the last day for each paper. Nothing in it makes you wait for that day. A sub's affidavit may be filed as soon as its notice is mailed. A sub still on the job may file for months already finished. Counsel confirmed the first on 2026-09-22 for a GC who had stopped answering.

**Counsel has not read this.** Filing while still on the job, for months already finished, is the app's reading. Counsel has not confirmed it.

**What you do.** On a sub job, send the month's notice first. Then file the affidavit as soon as the four facts are in hand. On a direct job there is no notice to wait for. File as soon as the four facts are in hand. The Lien desk's timeline prints each paper's first day as *open since*. [Open the Affidavits list](/jobs?tab=stages&liendesk=1&kind=affidavit).

**Cite.** *§ 53.052, § 53.056*

### A homestead needs one more statement

A lien on a homestead is invalid without a statement the statute gives. The app prints it under the notice on every residential property.

**What it says.** A homestead is the home the owner lives in and claims as their homestead. A lien on a homestead is invalid without one statement. The *§ 53.056* notice must include that statement or have it attached. The statement is in *§ 53.254(g)*. It names two conditions.

- The owner fails to withhold enough to cover the claim after notice.
- The owner fails to reserve 10 percent during construction and for 30 days after the contractor's work is complete.

The statement closes with a paragraph on what compliance protects.

**What you do.** Nothing extra. The app prints the statement in the statute's own words. It prints it under the form on every residential or homestead-flagged property. It never prints it on a commercial property. The owner's copy and the GC's copy both carry it. The *§ 53.056(a-2)* form itself carries no warning block under the current Code. That was read on 2026-09-22. The app prints the form as prescribed.

**Cite.** *§ 53.254*

### A late notice can still carry the affidavit

While the affidavit window is open, a notice sent after its own 15th still counts. The app has followed this reading since 2026-10-06.

**Counsel has not read this.** Counsel's memo of 2026-09-22 read a closed month as information only. The owner chose this reading on 2026-10-06, knowing both.

**What it says.** A month's notice is due by its 15th. The affidavit is due a month later. The owner reads the statute to let a notice sent in that last month still support the affidavit.

**What you do.** Nothing extra. A missed month stays on the Lien desk while the affidavit window is open. Its notice goes out in the next run. [Open the Notices list](/jobs?tab=stages&liendesk=1&kind=notice).

**Cite.** *§ 53.056, § 53.052*

### When a release or a waiver is owed

Once paid, the customer is owed a release. It is conditional while a bill is unpaid and unconditional once the bill is paid.

**What it says.** Once paid, the customer is owed a release. It is conditional while a bill is unpaid. It is unconditional once the bill is paid. A recorded affidavit gets a release of record. A waiver we ask a sub to sign follows one of the four statutory forms. An unconditional waiver may not be required before payment.

**What you do.** Four places handle them.

- The Release of Lien window.
- **Bill Customer → Lien releases**.
- The Release of record tab in the Lien window.
- **Subs → Pay → Lien waiver**.

[How to give a customer a lien release](/help/give-a-customer-a-lien-release).

**Cite.** *§ 53.281, § 53.284, § 53.286*

## The demand letter

### Attorney's fees need a demand first

The customer must be asked to pay, and the bill must then stay unpaid 30 days. A demand letter is that ask.

**What it says.** Attorney's fees can be claimed on a claim for services, labor, materials, a sworn account or a contract. A sworn account is a suit on an itemized bill that someone at Click swears is true and unpaid. The claim must first be presented. Presented means the customer was asked to pay. Then it must stay unpaid for 30 days. There is no set form. A demand letter is presentment.

**What you do.** Send the demand letter from the Lien window. The letter carries a sentence and a date for the fee clock.

**Cite.** *CPRC § 38.001(b), § 38.002*. The case is *Jones v. Kelley, 614 S.W.2d 95 (Tex. 1981)*.

### Demand exactly what is owed

Demanding more than is due forfeits the fee claim. So does refusing an offer to pay the true amount.

**What it says.** Two mistakes forfeit the fee claim. One is demanding more than is due. The other is refusing tender of the true amount. Tender is an offer to pay.

**What you do.** Nothing by hand. The statement of account is read from the bill. It is never typed.

**Cite.** *Findlay v. Cave, 611 S.W.2d 57 (Tex. 1981)*

### A suit on an account needs an itemized record

A sworn account lists each item with its name, its date and its charge, and allows every payment and credit.

**What it says.** A suit on an account needs a systematic, itemized record. The record gives the name, the date and the charge of each item. It allows all payments and credits. A suit on that record is a sworn account.

**What you do.** Nothing by hand. Each invoice gets a statement of account. The invoice is enclosed as Exhibit A. An exhibit is a document sent behind the letter as proof, lettered A, B and so on.

**Cite.** *Tex. R. Civ. P. 185*. The case is *Panditi v. Apostle, 180 S.W.3d 924 (Tex. App.—Dallas 2006)*.

### When certified mail is required

Only the chapter 53 notices and the theft of service presumption need certified mail. A demand letter may go by email.

**What it says.** Certified mail is required for two things only. They are the chapter 53 notices and the *§ 31.04* presumption. For presentment it is optional. It is still good evidence.

**What you do.** Certified mail is the default channel everywhere. Email is the second channel. An email is recorded as an email.

**Cite.** *Prop. Code § 53.003*

### What a justice court can hear

A justice court hears claims up to $20,000, not counting interest.

**What it says.** A justice court hears claims up to $20,000. Interest is not counted in that amount.

**What you do.** Nothing by hand. The demand letter's court line uses it.

**Cite.** *Gov't Code § 27.031(a)(1)*

## Interest

### Interest at 1.5 percent a month on an unpaid bill

Every job to improve real property, homes included. The bill is due 35 days after it is received, and interest runs from the day after.

**What it says.** This rule applies to every contract to improve real property. Homeowner jobs are included. There is no residential carve-out. A written payment request is due by the 35th day after it is received. From the day after, the unpaid amount bears 1.5 percent a month. Fees are at the court's discretion. Work may not be suspended on a home for one to four families.

**What you do.** Tick the interest line in the demand letter. It reads *§ 28.004* and runs from the 36th day after the bill went out.

**Cite.** *Prop. Code § 28.001, § 28.002(a), § 28.003, § 28.004, § 28.005, § 28.009(e)*

### Interest at 6 percent a year when no bill was sent

With no rate agreed and no bill sent, the legal rate is 6 percent a year. It runs from the 30th day after the amount is due.

**What it says.** This rate applies when no rate was agreed and no written request was sent. It is 6 percent a year. It runs from the 30th day after the amount is due.

**What you do.** Nothing extra. The interest line falls back to this rate when the bill was never sent.

**Cite.** *Fin. Code § 302.002*

## Limits on a collection letter

### What a letter to a homeowner may not say

A homeowner's debt is a consumer debt. The letter may threaten a suit or a lien. It may not threaten a criminal charge or say fees will be added.

**What it says.** Texas has a debt collection act. It binds the original creditor on a consumer debt. A homeowner's debt is a consumer debt. A GC's debt is not. The act forbids five things.

- Threatening a criminal charge over a payment dispute.
- Threatening an action the law prohibits.
- Adding a charge that no agreement or statute authorizes.
- Misrepresenting the amount.
- Saying that fees will be added.

Threats of a civil suit or a lien are allowed.

**What you do.** Nothing by hand. The lien line is offered only while a lien can still be filed. Every charge names its statute. The letter says it will *seek* fees. It never says they *will be added*. The *§ 31.04* line is off.

**Cite.** *Fin. Code § 392.001, § 392.301(a)(2), (6), (8), (b)(2), § 392.303(a)(2), § 392.304(a)(8), (12), (13)*

### Theft of service stays off the letter

The law can presume an intent not to pay after a certified demand goes unpaid 10 days. The app keeps that line off until the attorney package.

**What it says.** The law can presume intent to avoid payment. That presumption needs a written demand. The demand goes by certified or registered mail with a return receipt. A commercial delivery service also counts. It goes to the address on the service agreement. The bill must stay unpaid 10 days after receipt. This is the source of the app's default of 10 business days.

**What you do.** Leave the *§ 31.04* line off. It ships off and stays off until the attorney package.

**Cite.** *Penal Code § 31.04(a)(4), (b)(2), (c)*
