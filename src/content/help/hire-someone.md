A new person used to be four separate jobs on four screens: the invite on Manage accounts, the roster row on People, the wage on People pay config, the workday on Your account. **Hire** is one form that does them in order and shows you each result.

## Open it

People → Users → {{button:blue|+ Hire}}. Anyone who can add to the roster can open it; sending the invite (which makes their login) is a dev's step, so a non-dev's hire is a roster row and a pay row, and a dev sends the invite from their desk later.

## Fill it in

- **Name** — the name they will be called everywhere. Pay is keyed by this name, so a second person with the exact same name is refused; use a distinguishing form.
- **Kind** — Helper, Subcontractor, Assistant, Leader and so on. It decides the login's role.
- **Email** and **Send the invite** — with an invite, the login is created and the emailed link lets them set a password; **Start in training mode** keeps every write blocked until you switch it off. Without an email, the person is a roster row only.
- **Starts** — the employment start date, written on the roster row.
- **Pay** — the hourly wage, always; tick **Salaried** for a flat 8-hour weekday day priced at that wage, and pick the workday's start time. A pay row with no wage cannot be saved: that is exactly how a test account once put 40 phantom hours a week into every payroll total.
- **Paperwork packet** — optional; the documents appear as unsent and you send each one from Contracts.

## What happens, in order

:::example The steps
1. Invite — the login and the roster row, linked from birth · 2. Pay — the pay row · 3. Workday — the salaried template, and today's sessions on the clock strip · 4. Paperwork — the packet assigned
:::

Each step is its own write. One that fails stops the run with the reason on its line and a {{button:outline|Retry}} that picks up there without redoing the earlier steps. When every step is green, {{button:blue|Open their desk}} takes you to the person — the office rate, vehicle hand-off and housing live there.
