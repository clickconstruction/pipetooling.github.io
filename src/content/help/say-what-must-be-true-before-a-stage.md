Before we start is a short list of what must be true before the crew goes to a stage. You write it on the job, and you tick each thing as you learn it is true.

Each thing on the list is a bullet. A bullet waits on them or on us. Them is the customer or their builder. Us is our own work, like material that has to land.

The list blocks nothing. A stage can still be scheduled, and the crew can still clock in.

## Find the list

1. Open the job.
2. Press the **Documents** tab.
3. Find **Before we start**. Each stage of the job is one line there.
4. Press a stage to open it.

On Jobs, the Subs tab has a door too. Press **⋯** on a stage's row and pick **Before we start…**.

The office sees the list on every job. An estimator, an account man and a superintendent see it on their own jobs.

## Start a list

A stage with no list offers a starter.

1. Press {{button:blue|Use the four}} to start from four common bullets.
2. Or press {{button:outline|Write my own}} and type the first bullet.

Change the starter to fit the job. Take off what does not apply.

For Rough In, Top Out and Trim Set the company can keep its own starter. Then the press reads *Use the company's* and its count.

## Add a bullet

1. Type the bullet in **Add a bullet, in plain words**.
2. Pick {{chip:blue|from them}} or {{chip:gray|ours}}.
3. Press {{button:outline|Add}}.

Write a bullet as a plain fact that is true or not. *Power is on for our tools.* is a good one.

## Tick what they told you

1. Find the bullet under **Still open**.
2. Press its box.

One press records their word, today. The bullet drops to **Has a yes**, which is dulled so the open ones stand out.

The line under a bullet with a yes says who said it, how it reached us and the day. Each of the three is underlined. Press one to change it right there.

Press the box again to take a yes back.

## Say how we know

A yes always says how we know it. There are three ways.

{{chip:gray|their word}} {{chip:gray|their photo}} {{chip:gray|we saw it}}

1. Press **⋯** on the bullet's row.
2. Pick one under **How do we know?**

## Ask for a photo

Some things are too costly to take on their word.

1. Press **⋯** on the bullet's row.
2. Press {{chip:gray|Ask for a photo}}.

Now their word alone leaves the bullet open. Its line reads *Still needs a photo.*

Press {{chip:gray|They sent a photo}} and pick the picture they sent. Or press {{chip:gray|We saw it}} when one of ours has seen it on site.

## Tick a bullet of ours

A bullet of ours is not a box you tick. It shows where it stands and one press beside it.

Press **Mark it done** when our part is ready. Press **Mark it not done** to undo that.

## Read the four lines

An open stage starts with four lines. Each line holds one fact.

| Line | What it says |
|---|---|
| We start | Our first day and our last day. |
| They say ready | The day they gave. Each new day is kept beside the old ones. |
| Notice we need | How many days of notice we need before our first day. |
| We decide by | The day we must decide. It is our first day less the notice. |

An underlined value is one you can change. Press it, type, and press {{button:blue|Keep}}. Press **Cancel** to leave it as it was.

A bold value is worked out by the app. It changes by itself when the others change.

Only the office and an estimator can change our days. Everyone else reads them with a small *office only* tag.

## Set the notice

The notice starts on a default. A far site needs ten days and a near one needs two.

1. Press **Set this site's own** beside the notice.
2. Type the number of days.
3. Press {{button:blue|Keep}}.

The number holds for every stage on that site. A job on no site keeps its own number.

To go back, press the number and then press **Back to the default**.

## When their day is after ours

Type the day they say they will be ready. The line then says how many days that is after our start.

The line also adds up how far they have moved their day since they first gave one.

Press {{button:blue|Move our start}} to move our first day to three days after theirs. Our last day moves with it.

## Read the ready bar

Each stage has a row of small boxes and a percent. One box is one bullet.

A green box has a yes. An empty box is still open. Every bullet counts the same, so five of six reads 83%.

The bar reads 100% only when every bullet has a yes.

## See every stage on a site

The site's Development page has a look named **Schedule**. It shows one card for each stage ahead on the site.

1. Open the site's Development page.
2. Press **Schedule** at the end of the strip of looks.

A banner on top says how many answers they owe us. It also says how many days they have moved on the site.

Each card has a coloured dot and one sentence. The sentence says where the stage stands.

| Dot | What it means |
|---|---|
| Red | Their day is after ours, or the day to decide or the last check has come. |
| Amber | We are waiting on something, and the day to decide is near. |
| Green | Nothing is open. |
| Grey | The stage has no list yet, or its day to decide is far off. |

The cards with trouble come first. Red is on top, then amber, green and grey.

Rest on a card's ready bar to see what it still takes to start. On a phone, press the bar.

Press the card's one press to open the stage. You see the same four lines and the same lists as on the job.

## Ask them about what is open

An open stage with something open on them has an **Ask** press at the bottom.

1. Press the **Ask** press. It names who you are asking and how many things.
2. Read the message. Change any word.
3. Press {{button:blue|Text it}} or {{button:outline|Email it}}.
4. Their box in Messages opens under the ask, filled with your words.
5. Send it from that box.

Nothing goes out when you press **Text it**. The message leaves only when you send it from the box.

Read who the box is addressed to before you send. It is the customer on the job.

Did you ask another way? Press {{chip:gray|I texted it}}, {{chip:gray|I emailed it}} or {{chip:gray|I called}}. The ask is kept and nothing is sent.

Every ask is kept with its day. An open bullet then reads when you asked and how.

On the Schedule look, the banner has one **Ask** press for the whole site. It covers every stage whose day to decide is near.

The office's Dashboard says when a customer owes us answers. Press **Open the list** on that line to go to the site.

## Go or hold

The decision to send the crew is a press, and your name goes on it. The office and the job's superintendent can press it.

1. Open the stage.
2. Press {{button:outline|Go or hold}} at the bottom.
3. Read what is still open.
4. Press {{button:green|Go}} to send the crew for our first day.

With something still open, the press reads **Go, on their promise**. The stage comes back to you the day before the crew leaves.

To hold the stage instead:

1. Pick the day our start moves to.
2. Read the note for the customer.
3. Press **Hold until** and the day.

A hold moves our days to the day you picked. Our last day moves with our first.

A hold tells nobody by itself. After the hold, the note is ready to send. Press {{button:blue|Text it}} or {{button:outline|Email it}}, then send it from their box in Messages.

Press **Take the Go back** to undo a Go. Press **Take the hold back** to undo a hold. A hold you take back does not move our days again.

Nothing is blocked. A crew can still be scheduled with no Go.

## Add a bullet to the company's list

The office can keep a good bullet for every job.

1. Press **⋯** on the bullet's row.
2. Press the chip that adds it to the company's list.

The chip shows on a Rough In, a Top Out and a Trim Set stage. The next stage of that kind is offered the company's list as its starter.
