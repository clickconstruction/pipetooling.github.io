The margin brush on Bids → Pricing prices rows at your target margin right where you read them. No calculator, and no scrolling back to a toolbar.

Margin is profit divided by revenue. You use the **margin brush** for this.

## Pick up the brush

You press {{button:outline|🖌 Margin ›}} on the totals strip, left of {{button:blue|Solve ›}}. A purple ring unfolds with the margin box and your **recent margins** as chips. The loaded one is lit solid purple. You type a percent or tap a chip. That is the margin the brush carries.

## Sweep the rows

- **Press and sweep across rows.** Every row the brush crosses is priced at that margin the instant it is touched. The price is cost divided by one minus the margin, rounded to whole dollars. The Revenue / Profit / Margin totals move as you go. One motion, many rows.
- Rows you have held with 📌, fixed-price rows, and rows with no Takeoffs cost are never painted. There is nothing to price from. You set those by hand.
- You let go and the sweep saves in one batch. {{button:outline|↩ Undo sweep}} takes the whole stroke back.
- **‹** or Esc puts the brush down.

Your three most recent margins follow you from bid to bid on that device, most recent first. The margin you just swept with becomes the top chip.

:::example Walking a bid
34 costed rows. Load 50%, sweep down the big fixtures; load 40%, sweep the fittings — every row lands on its number as you pass it, and the Margin column confirms each one instantly.
:::

Every price the brush writes is a normal saved price. You edit any of them by hand afterward. Or you use the row's **Reset** to fall back to the price book. Margin here means profit ÷ revenue. That is the same definition as the Margin column and the ⓘ line breakdown.
