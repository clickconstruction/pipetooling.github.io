Your email footer and your business card are drawn from your own facts and photo. Change your photo or your title, and both redraw.

Open Settings, then **Your account**. Find the card **Your card and footer**, under your signature.

## Add your photo

:::steps

Press {{button:outline|Add a photo}}. A sheet opens.

Press {{button:outline|Take a photo}} or {{button:outline|Choose a photo}}. Head and shoulders in good light works best.

Drag the picture so your face sits in the ring. Slide **Zoom** to bring it closer.

Press {{button:blue|Save the photo}}.

:::

The photo is kept whole. You can open the sheet again and move it at any time. The office can see your photo. Nobody else can until you send a footer.

## Check your words

**Name on the card** is your full name, as a customer should read it. Your account name stands in when you leave it empty.

**Title** is the same title your email signature uses. Leave it empty and the line is left off.

Press {{button:blue|Save the card}} after a change.

Your email and your mobile come from My Profile. Change them there. The office line is the same for everyone.

## Pick a look

A look is your footer and your card in other colours. The layout stays the same.

The row **Look** shows each look the owner offers, drawn with your own name and photo. Press one. Your footer and your card both change, and the pick is saved.

The row is hidden when the owner offers one look. The owner switches looks on and off under Settings, on **People & teams**, on the card **Card and footer looks**.

## Pick a card size

A card size changes the back of your business card. The front and your email footer stay as they are.

The row **Card size** sits under **Business card**. The row shows each size the owner offers, drawn with your own name and photo.

:::cards

:::card Standard

The card as the designer set it.

:::

:::card Larger photo

Your photo grows by a third. The words grow a little.

:::

:::card Larger text

The words grow by a quarter. Your photo grows a little.

:::

:::

Press one. The back of your card changes, and the pick is saved. Any size works in any look.

Pick **Larger text** when the print on your card is hard to read. Pick **Larger photo** when customers should know your face.

The row is hidden when the owner offers one size. The owner switches sizes on and off on the same card as the looks.

## Put the footer in your mail

Press {{button:blue|Download the picture}}. The picture is the footer as designed.

:::steps

Open Gmail on a computer.

Open Settings, then See all settings, then Signature.

Press the Insert image button and choose Upload. Pick the picture.

Press Save Changes at the bottom of the page.

:::

Gmail on a phone uses the signature you set on a computer. Apple Mail and Outlook take a pasted picture. Press {{button:outline|Copy the picture}} for those.

## Print your card

Press {{button:blue|Download the print file}}. A print shop takes the file as it is. The front and the back are each on a page, with bleed and crop marks.

Press {{button:outline|Download a sheet of ten}} for card stock in the office printer. An office printer drifts a little between the two sides. Use the sheet for a few cards in a hurry, not for a box.

## Make one for a teammate

The office can make a card and a footer for anyone. Open People, then **Users**.

Open a person's row menu and press **Card and footer…**. The same card opens with their facts. Add their photo, set the name on their card and their title, and take their footer and card.

Press {{button:outline|Cards and footers}} on the same page for the whole team. Everyone with a photo starts ticked. Press {{button:blue|Make 3 cards}} for one print file with a front and a back for each person. Press {{button:outline|Download 3 footers}} for one picture each.

A person with no photo is held, and the row for that person says so. Press {{button:outline|Add a photo ▸}} on their row to open their card. Everyone else is still made.

## When a press is grey

The presses stay grey until you have a photo. The card says so under them. Add a photo and they light up.
