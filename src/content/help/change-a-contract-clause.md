A contract we send a trade partner is made of clauses. Anyone in the office can change one. The owner, a controller or a dev approves the change before it goes out.

## Open the clauses

1. Open [Contracts and clauses](/settings?tab=settings-clauses) in Settings.
2. Find the paper's card. It shows the paper's version and its clauses.

The clauses for everyone come first, under **Everyone**. A clause for one trade sits on that trade's exhibit. Each clause shows who it applies to and its own version.

## Change a clause

1. Press {{button:outline|Edit}} on the clause.
2. Change the words under **English**.
3. Write them under **Spanish** too, when you can.
4. Under **Applies to**, pick **Everyone** or the trades it covers.
5. Say why under **Why**.
6. Press {{button:blue|Send for approval}}.

The change waits under the clause until the owner, a controller or a dev approves it. It shows on their Needs you card.

Each of them gets a push on their phone and an email with the change. The push opens the paper's card. The message after you save says who it reached.

A clause for a trade covers only the trades you pick. A clause for Electrical is not on the low voltage exhibit unless you pick both.

:::example A change waiting on approval
**Rosa proposed a change** Oct 12, 10:00 AM. The insurer asked for it.
Insurance: general liability of $2,000,000 per occurrence.
{{chip:yellow|Waiting on your approval}}
:::

## Add or retire a clause

To add a clause, press {{button:outline|+ a clause}} on the paper's card. It goes after the last clause.

To take a clause off the next paper, press {{button:outline|Retire}} on it. A retired clause is never deleted. Signed copies keep it.

## Approve or decline a change

This part is for the owner, a controller or a dev.

1. Press {{button:outline|Review}} on the **Contracts** line of your Needs you card. It opens the paper's card.
2. Read the change under the clause.
3. Press {{button:blue|Approve}} to send it out. The paper's version goes up by one.
4. To say no, press {{button:outline|Decline}} and say why. The person who made it reads your why on the clause's history.

Either way, the person who made the change gets an email that says what you decided. A declined change's email carries your why.

Approve is refused when the clause changed after the change was made. Decline it and ask for it again.

Your own changes go out as soon as you save them. They show on the clause's history too. See [work the Needs you list on the dashboard](/help/needs-you-card) for the card itself.

## See a paper as a company gets it

1. Press {{button:outline|Preview per trade}} on the paper's card.
2. Pick the company under **As it would go to**.
3. Pick **English** or **Spanish**.

The preview shows the clauses for everyone. Then it shows the exhibit for that company's trades, then the version.

## Build a paper from settings

Some papers change with the job. A settings list says what can change. Only a controller or a dev sets one.

1. Press {{button:outline|Settings list}} on the paper's card.
2. Type a setting's name under **A new setting** and press **Add a setting**.
3. Add its choices, the loosest first. Use the arrows to move one.
4. Type what the summary says for each choice.
5. Tick the box on a choice that leaves the topic out of the paper.
6. Press {{button:blue|Save the settings list}}.

The window lists what is missing while the list cannot be saved. No clause wording goes in a settings list.

A blank is a word a clause holds in braces. Add one under **Blanks a clause may hold**. A choice can fill it.

A level is one pick for every setting. Add one under **Levels**.

## Tie a clause to a choice

On a paper with a settings list, a clause's window has one more line.

1. Press {{button:outline|Edit}} on the clause.
2. Under **Only when**, pick the setting.
3. Pick the choice that puts the clause in the paper.
4. Under **For the other choices**, say where the clause sits.
5. Publish it or send it for approval.

- **No other wording** leaves the clause out for every other choice.
- **Another wording of clause 2** makes it one of that place's wordings.
- **The first wording of a new place** starts a place that needs a wording for each choice.

The wordings of one place share a number on the card. They read 2A and 2B.

The card says {{chip:gray|Every choice has its wording}} when nothing is missing. A gap reads there in words, and it stops the paper.

## Mark a revision as read by an attorney

This part is for a controller or a dev.

1. Press **Mark revision 4 as read by an attorney** on the paper's card.
2. Type the attorney's name.
3. Pick the day it was read.
4. Press the blue button.

The mark belongs to that revision. Any later change makes the paper unread again.

## Preview a paper built from settings

1. Press {{button:outline|Preview with settings}} on the paper's card.
2. Press a level, or pick a choice for each setting.
3. Type a value for a blank that no choice fills.

The paper changes as you pick. A line above it says what stops the paper, in words.

Press **Summary** to read the same picks in plain words. The first line names the level. The summary is not the contract.

Press {{button:outline|Print the sample}} to print the paper for a made-up company. SAMPLE lies across every page. A paper that cannot go has no sample.

A paper built from settings goes to nobody yet. It will go from a job, with the job's settings.

## What a signed copy keeps

The company reads the clauses on its sign page, in English or Spanish. The version shows under them. If a clause changes while the page is open, the page asks them to reload before they sign.

A signed copy keeps the words and the version it was signed with. A later change never reaches it. Nor does a retire. The signed record shows the clauses as they were signed. The next paper sent carries the new words.

## Build a paper from clauses

1. Under **Build a paper from clauses**, pick a document from the Contract library.
2. Press {{button:blue|Write its first clause}}.

Its first clause goes through approval like any other. Fillable forms such as the W-9 are not offered.
