The Lien desk opens on its To do view. The Board sits across the top of that view. The Board is the whole lien month as one picture. Four tiles sit above two columns of bins. Every job is a small chip in the bin of its stage. The list of rows sits under the Board. This guide says what each part means and what a press does.

## The four tiles

The first tile counts what is due by the next day. It reads like *21 by Monday*, then how many are to file and how many are to send. A red line under it says how many will not make that day as they stand. A paper will not make its day when it has more stages left than working days. Press the red line and the list shows only those papers.

The second tile counts everything waiting on the leader, by name. Its small line names the two parts, like *1 to approve · 7 to sign*. **To approve** is a paper the office sent for approval. **To sign** is an approved notice with no signature on it yet. Those notices stand in their own bin, *Approved · to sign*. Press the tile and the list shows only those rows.

The third tile counts the approved notices that can print, and the printed ones still to mail. Its small line says how many are to print and how many are to mail. A notice that waits on a signature is on the second tile, not this one. Press the tile and the run opens. The same press stands in the bin under it. Read [a press beside the bin name](#a-press-beside-the-bin-name).

The fourth tile is the money still unpaid on the desk. Each job counts once.

## The two columns

The left column is the monthly notices. Its bins run down in order. They are **Needs the owner**, **To send for Master approval**, **Awaiting Master approval**, *Approved · to sign*, *Approved · to print*, *Approved · to mail* and **Sent**. An approved notice with no signature on it stands in *Approved · to sign*. This happens when the leader said to send it and has not signed yet. Once it is signed it stands in *Approved · to print* until its packet prints. Then it stands in *Approved · to mail* until its mail numbers are typed in. The Monthly notices list uses the same two names. A thin grey arrow joins each bin to the next.

The right column is the lien affidavits. Its first two bins are **Needs the property facts** and **Waiting on its notice**. Then come **To send for Master approval**, **Awaiting Master approval** and **Ready to file**. Its last two are *Filed · copies to mail* and *Served*. A filed lien owes a copy of its affidavit to the owner within five days. It stands in *Filed · copies to mail* until the copies are mailed and the day is recorded. Then it stands in *Served*. Its chip there is coloured by the day the copies must be mailed by. That bin has a press of its own, like **Mail 12 liens’ copies ›**. The press opens one window that prints the copies and takes their mail numbers.

**Needs the property facts** holds an affidavit that still needs a fact. The fact is on the property record or on the GC's customer record. **Waiting on its notice** holds an affidavit that needs nothing but its notice. A job with a GC needs a notice mailed and on record before its affidavit can go. An affidavit that needs both a fact and a notice stays in **Needs the property facts**.

One arrow crosses from Sent on the left to Waiting on its notice on the right. The notice on record is the affidavit's gate. When the notice is mailed and recorded, the affidavit moves down to **To send for Master approval**. On a phone the columns stand one over the other and that arrow is not drawn.

A bin with a blue left edge has something to do now. Its press is beside its name. Each bin's count sits at its right. A bin with no paper is one quiet line with a zero.

## What a chip's colour means

A chip is a job's number. Its colour says how soon its day is.

- Red means the day is within a day, or already past.
- Amber means the day is this week.
- Purple means the paper waits on the leader.
- Grey means the day is later.

A chip with a red mark after its number will not make its day as it stands. It has more stages to pass than working days left.

A job that needs its owner stands in both columns. Point at either chip and both light up. One press on the owner clears both.

An affidavit in **Waiting on its notice** lights with its notice chip the same way. The notice chip shows where the notice stands.

## Point at a chip

A chip is only a number. Point at it and a card opens beside it. Nothing is pressed yet.

The card says which job it is. It gives the job's name, its address and its customer. It names the GC when the GC is not the customer.

Then it says where the paper stands. It names the column and the bin, like *Monthly notice* and **To send for Master approval**. It gives the one thing to know, the day and how long is left. It says when the paper will not make its day, and what the job still owes. A job with a paper in the other column says where that one stands too.

A paper that will not make its day also lists what is still to do. Each line is one stage, in order, like *Send it for approval*. The stage that waits on the job's leader names him. The line over the list says how many working days are left.

The last line says where a press will take you. Move the pointer away and the card goes. The Tab key reaches a chip and opens its card the same way. A phone has no pointer, so it shows no card.

## Press a chip

Every chip is a press to the right place for its stage. Point at a chip to read where it goes.

- A chip in **Needs the owner** or **Needs the property facts** opens the property record at the missing line.
- A chip in **Waiting on its notice** opens the job's notice. When the notice is approved, the chip opens the run on that envelope.
- A chip in **To send for Master approval** or **Awaiting Master approval** opens that job's paper on **By paper**.
- A chip in *Approved · to sign* opens the notice, where it is signed.
- A chip in *Approved · to print* or *Approved · to mail* opens the run on that envelope.
- A chip in **Ready to file** opens the affidavit.
- A chip in *Filed · copies to mail* or *Served* opens the job's Lien window.
- A chip in a closed bin opens the window that asks what to do. See [A window that has closed](#a-window-that-has-closed).

A paper opened from a chip is a step of its own in the browser. Press Back and the desk returns to To do with the Board in view. The property record and the run open over the Board. Close them and the Board is where it was.

The same press sits on the job number of every To do row. It also sits on the number of every notice and affidavit row on **By paper**. Every row on **By date** has it too. A number is a zoom everywhere on the desk. On a To do row the job's name still opens the job itself.

## A press beside the bin name

Some bins carry a press on the same line as the bin's name. The press acts on the papers in that bin. Nothing stands between the tiles and the bins. Only the lien office sees these presses.

On *Approved · to sign* the office's press reads like {{button:outline|Remind Malachi of 7 papers ›}}. It tells the leader they wait. The desk asks first. The leader's own press there reads like {{button:blue|Sign 7 notices ›}}. It opens the approved notices, and he signs each on its own pane.

On *Approved · to print* the press reads like {{button:blue|Print and mail 8 notices}}. The count is the bin's own count. Press it and the run opens on every approved notice, for every GC.

On *Approved · to mail* the press reads like {{button:outline|Enter mail numbers · 6}}. It opens the same run, where you type each envelope's number. Once every approved notice has printed, this press turns blue.

On **Ready to file** the press reads like {{button:blue|Print 10 for notarization}}. It prints the stack right there. You stay on **To do**. A line under the bin's name says what it leaves out, like *2 held on a property fact*. Press **See them ›** to read why. A second line says what prints in front of the stack, like *Prints with the help sheet and the notice records*. Press **Change ›** to tick those pages on the affidavits list.

On the affidavits' **To send for Master approval** the press reads like {{button:outline|Send 5 for approval ›}}. It opens the affidavits list at that pile. One press there sends every paper someone has read.

On *Filed · copies to mail* the press reads like {{button:outline|Mail 10 liens' copies ›}}. It opens the window that prints each lien's copies.

A press with nothing ready is grey. The line under it still says what waits. A bin with no paper and nothing ready has no press. On a narrow window the press drops to its own line, under the bin's name.

Scroll down and the two prints stand beside the find box, in fewer words. The notices' press is on the left, as its column is. On a phone the bins are folded, so the two prints stand side by side above them.

## Press a bin

Press a bin's name and the list under the Board shows only that bin's rows. A line under the bins says how many it is showing. Press {{button:outline|Show every row}} to see every row again. Pressing the same bin a second time does the same. A pressed bin also brings the list up to you, on a phone and on a computer.

The Board never sends, files or moves anything. Every press on a row below still does what it did.

## Scroll down to the list

The Board fills most of the screen. Scroll down and the Board moves away as the list comes up. The list then has the whole screen.

The find box stays at the top while you read the list. Beside it sit the Board's numbers, like *21 by tomorrow*. The waiting number and the run number are presses. They go where their tiles go. The red count of what will not make its day is a press too.

When a bin is pressed, that line says what the list is showing instead. It reads like *Showing 4 rows in To send for Master approval*. {{button:outline|Show every row}} sits beside it.

Press {{button:outline|↑ The Board}} to go back to the top.

On a very tall screen the Board and the list fit together. There the Board stays put and the list scrolls under it.

## The list follows the Board

On a computer the list under the Board is the Board written out. It has one group for each bin, under the bin's own name. The groups run in the Board's order. The notices come first, from **Needs the owner** down. The affidavits follow.

Each heading says how many papers the bin holds and what their jobs still owe. Inside a bin the papers that will not make their day come first. These are the chips with the red mark. Start there. A paper that needs a fact needs it today to go out with the rest.

Papers that share one act are one row. Twelve affidavits ready to file read as one row, *12 affidavits approved and ready to file*. Its press is {{button:blue|Print 10 for notarization ›}}. It opens the affidavits list at **Ready to file**. The button of the same name there prints the batch. Press **The 12 jobs** on the row to see each job, its notice and what it owes. Affidavits not yet sent for approval fold the same way, under {{button:blue|Send 3 for approval ›}}.

A paper the office waits on sits in its **Awaiting Master approval** bin. The office cannot approve these papers. It can remind the leader. Press {{button:outline|Remind Malachi of 3 papers}} on that heading. When only a retainage notice waits, the press is on the **Other lien papers** heading. The desk asks first. Then the leader gets the message again, on the phone or by email. A text with the link goes too, from the company number. Anyone in the office can send it, whoever sent the paper. A notice is one message each. A retainage notice is one message too. The affidavits share one message. An approved notice the leader has yet to sign is in the same press. The notices to sign share one message too. On a day with nothing to approve, the press is on the Board, beside the line that counts them. Nothing on the papers changes. The desk then says how many papers the leader was reminded of. A reminder that did not go is named with its reason, so you can tell the leader another way. The desk also says whether the text went. When no text went, it says why, like no mobile number on the leader's account. **Send texts** must be on for the text to go. Read [turn on texts from the company number](/help/turn-on-texts-from-the-company-number). After a send the button says when, like *Reminded at 2:14 PM*.

The leader's own rows stay on top, under **Only you can sign** and **Only you can approve**. **Reminders** keep their own heading. A paper with no bin, like a retainage notice, sits under **Other lien papers**.

Everything due later than a week waits under **Later**. One line says which bins hold how many. Press **Show the 8** to read the rows. A find or a pressed bin opens them for you.

On a phone the list is drawn by day. The papers at risk lead their day. The waiting rows follow the days that need you.

## What a row says

On a computer each row is two lines. The first line is the paper, the job and what the job still owes. The row's press sits at the right.

The second line says the one thing to know. Under **Later** it starts with the paper's bin, in the Board's own words, like **Ready to file**. Under a bin's own heading the row does not say the bin again. A notice says its months. A paper waiting on approval says when it was sent and how long it has waited. A paper that waits on a fact says the fact, and its press answers it. {{button:blue|Answer the homestead question}} opens the property record on that question.

An affidavit row also says where its notice stands. It reads like *Notice sent Oct 14*, *No notice needed* or *No notice on record*. A notice that is started and not mailed says so, like *Notice approved · not mailed yet*.

A row in **Waiting on its notice** says where the notice stands in a sentence. Its press goes to the notice's next step. For an approved notice that step is the run.

Point at a bin's name on a row to read where that paper stands. It opens the card the four dots used to open. A row with nothing else to say keeps the name for that. A row with no bin, like a retainage notice, keeps its dots.

A heading carries the day when every row under it shares that day. Then no row repeats it. A row with another day says its own.

## Held and retainage

**Held** stands on a column's heading line, at its right. It shows only while a job is in it. A dashed border marks it. Press the name and the list shows only those rows.

Retainage is one line under the notices. It counts the retainage notices by stage, like *1 to send*. It also counts jobs where retainage looks held and none is recorded, like *2 look held, not on the job*. The retainage list on **By paper** has the rows.

## A window that has closed

A closed window has its own bin at the foot of each column. It shows only while a job is in it. No arrow runs into it. The bin is dashed and its chips are grey, because nothing there can be rushed.

:::cards

:::card Window closed · no notice

This bin sits under **Sent**. Every month of the job passed its notice day and no notice went. An amber chip can still be saved. A late notice counts while the job's lien day is ahead.

:::

:::card Lien day passed · not filed

This bin sits under **Served**. The last day to file the lien passed and no filing is on record. A paper stands here whatever stage it had reached. An approved affidavit past its day leaves **Ready to file**, so the print no longer counts it.

:::

:::

A paper leaves the closed bin by itself when its dates change. Record a filing that was made in time and the paper moves to *Filed · copies to mail*. Set a later last day of work and the paper goes back into the flow. A notice also leaves once someone records its months closed.

The app counts the days. It does not judge the law. Ask the attorney when a date is not clear.

### Answer a closed paper

Press a chip in a closed bin. A window opens on that job. {{button:outline|Answer 2 ›}} on the bin's title line opens the same window on the first job. The window says what closed and on which day. It also says where that day is counted from.

When the lien day has passed, the window gives three answers.

:::steps

1. **The date is wrong.** {{button:outline|Set the last day of work ›}} opens the job where that day is set. A later day puts the paper back in the flow. *Record the filing ›* is for a lien that was filed in time and never recorded.
2. **Keep collecting.** {{button:blue|Keep collecting 4127}} takes the job off the Lien desk and keeps it in Collections. A billed job that is not in Collections goes there. Nothing is filed or deleted.
3. **Let it go.** {{button:outline|Mark 4127 Uncollectible}} asks once more. Then it marks the job Uncollectible, as the Pipeline does.

:::

Type a note first when you have one. The note is kept on the job with your name and the day.

When one month's notice window closed, the lien day is still ahead. The window gives three other answers.

:::steps

1. **Send a late notice.** {{button:blue|Draft the late notice ›}} opens the notice for that month. A late notice still counts while the lien day is ahead.
2. **The date is wrong.** {{button:outline|Set the last day of work ›}} opens the job where that day is set.
3. **Record it closed.** The press writes down that no notice goes for that month. The job stays on the Lien desk for its lien day.

:::

A closed month never asks you to let the job go. The lien office answers these windows. Anyone else can read them.

With more than one job in the bin, the window counts them, like *1 of 2*. Each answer shows the next job. *Next ›* passes a job over for now.

A job that keeps collecting is listed under [A job that is off the lien path](#a-job-that-is-off-the-lien-path). **Put it back…** there undoes the answer.

## Public property

A mechanic's lien does not attach to public property. Some owners read as a city, a county, a school district or the State. Nobody can make the papers for their jobs.

- Such a job's chip carries a ⊘ mark. The chip loses its red day, because no paper can be made there.
- One amber line sits over the bins, like *2 jobs sit on public property and are still on the lien path*. The line gives the money those jobs owe.
- {{button:outline|Review them ›}} opens one window for each property in turn. The window asks whether to take the property off the lien path.

A property answered public leaves the bins. The property's jobs sit in their own bin under the affidavits, {{chip:gray|Public jobs · bond claim}}. Each row gives the job, its money, the GC and what already went out. {{chip:yellow|get the bond}} shows while nobody knows if there is a payment bond. The row also says whether the job is with the attorney. A job not billed yet cannot go on the attorney's list, and the row says so.

**Put a job back on the lien path…** at the foot of the bin puts a property back. Say why, and its jobs return to the bins. [A public property](/help/file-a-lien-and-never-miss-its-deadlines#a-public-property) has the window's steps.

## A job that is off the lien path

Some money has no lien behind it. A trip charge to pick up a check is one case. The lien office can take that one job off the lien path, with a reason.

Such a job leaves the bins. It sits in its own bin under the affidavits, {{chip:gray|Off the lien path}}. Each row gives the job, its money, the GC, who took it off and the day. The reason sits on its own line.

{{button:outline|Put it back…}} on a row puts that job back. Say why, and the job returns to the bins with its papers.

A lien affidavit that a fact holds has a quiet {{button:outline|Not lienable…}} under its row. [Take a job off the lien path](/help/take-a-job-off-the-lien-path) has the steps.

## The leader's press

A master reads every bin, as the office does. His press stands on the **Awaiting Master approval** bin of the lien affidavits, beside the bin's name. It reads like {{button:blue|Approve 2 affidavits}}. It counts the jobs he leads. It approves every affidavit that waits inside its window, one by one. Each moves to **Ready to file**. The desk asks once before it starts, with the count.

A paper past its day stays out of the press. A line under the bin's name says so, like *1 is past its day and stays here*. Press its chip to open it.

A notice is never in this press. The law wants the leader's signature on each notice, and the notice's own pane signs it. Press the second tile to see every paper that waits on the leader.

The office sees no press to approve. A dev sees one only on the jobs he leads. The old switch between **Every bin** and **Only my bins** is gone.

## On the Dashboard

The Dashboard's Needs you card carries the Board's four numbers on one line. They are the four tiles as dots. The line reads like *Lien desk · 21 by tomorrow · 3 waiting on Malachi · 7 to print and mail · $202,575 unpaid on the desk*. Press the line and the Lien desk opens at the Board. The line shows for the office while the card has lines.

## The first tab is the way back

On a computer the first tab carries the desk's name and its count. The tab reads like *⏱ Lien desk · 32 to do*. The number counts the To do rows you can move. Press the tab from any view. To do opens with the Board in view. Press it on To do and the Board comes back up. The tab is blue while To do is the view. On the other views the tab still shows the desk's name. On a phone the title **Lien desk** is the way back, and the first tab reads *To do · 32*.

## Where the old strips went

The Board took the place of two strips. One was the lien month bar with its five zones. The other was the rails named Steps of each paper. The days of the lien month still sit in {{button:outline|§ Rules}} under **The lien month**. On a computer, point at a row's bin name to read where that paper stands. On a phone every row still has its four dots.

A sent notice's reminders still run as before. The courtesy notice and the attorney's letter sit under the **Reminders** heading in the list. Read [send a courtesy notice before a lien](/help/send-a-courtesy-notice-before-a-lien) for the courtesy itself. Read [send lien notices from the Lien desk](/help/send-lien-notices-from-the-lien-desk) for the whole flow.
