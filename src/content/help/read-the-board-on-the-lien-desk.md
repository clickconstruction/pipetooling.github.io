The Lien desk's To do tab opens on the Board. The Board is the whole lien month as one picture. Four tiles sit above two columns of bins. Every job is a small chip in the bin of its stage. The list of rows sits under the Board. This guide says what each part means and what a press does.

## The four tiles

The first tile counts what is due by the next day. It reads like *21 by Monday*, then how many are to file and how many are to send. A red line under it says how many will not make that day as they stand. A paper will not make its day when it has more stages left than working days.

The second tile counts the papers waiting on the leader, by name. Press it and the list shows only those rows.

The third tile counts the notices in the mail. It asks you to type the tracking numbers. Press it and the run opens.

The fourth tile is the money still unpaid on the desk. Each job counts once.

## The two columns

The left column is the monthly notices. Its bins run down in order. They are **Needs the owner**, **To send**, **Awaiting approval**, **In the mail** and **Sent**. A thin grey arrow joins each bin to the next.

The right column is the lien affidavits. Its bins are **Needs the property facts**, **To send**, **Awaiting approval**, **Ready to file** and *Filed · Served*.

One arrow crosses from Sent on the left to Needs the property facts on the right. The notice on record is the affidavit's gate. On a phone the columns stand one over the other and that arrow is not drawn.

The busiest bin has a blue border. Each bin's count sits at its right.

## What a chip's colour means

A chip is a job's number. Its colour says how soon its day is.

- Red means the day is within a day, or already past.
- Amber means the day is this week.
- Purple means the paper waits on the leader.
- Grey means the day is later.

A chip with a red mark after its number will not make its day as it stands. It has more stages to pass than working days left. Point at a chip to read the job's name, its GC and what it owes.

A job that needs its owner stands in both columns. Point at either chip and both light up. One press on the owner clears both.

## Press a bin

Press a bin's name and the list under the Board shows only that bin's rows. A line under the bins says how many it is showing. Press {{button:outline|Show every row}} to see every row again. Pressing the same bin a second time does the same. On a phone a pressed bin scrolls the rows into view.

The Board never sends, files or moves anything. Every press on a row below still does what it did.

## Held, Missed and retainage

A **Held** bin and a **Missed** bin stand beside a column only while a job is in one. A dashed border marks them. When they are empty they are not drawn.

Retainage is one line under the notices. It counts the retainage notices by stage, like *1 to send*. The retainage list on **By paper** has the rows.

## The title is a door

Press **Lien desk** at the top of the page from any view. The To do tab opens with the Board in view.

## Where the old strips went

The Board took the place of two strips. One was the lien month bar with its five zones. The other was the rails named Steps of each paper. The days of the lien month still sit in {{button:outline|§ Rules}} under **The lien month**. Every row still has its four dots. Point at them to read where that paper stands.

A sent notice's reminders still run as before. The courtesy notice and the attorney's letter sit under the **Reminders** heading in the list. Read [send a courtesy notice before a lien](/help/send-a-courtesy-notice-before-a-lien) for the courtesy itself.
