If a company vehicle is assigned to you, a My Vehicle card shows on your Dashboard. It is how you keep the office in the loop without a phone call.

The **My Vehicle** card sits just below My Time.

## Sending an odometer reading

You type the number off your dash into **Odometer today**. The odometer is the mileage counter on your dash. Then you tap {{button:blue|Send reading}}. That is it. When the card shows a {{chip:yellow|reading requested}} chip, the office hasn't had a number in a while. Sending one clears it.

:::example What the card shows
**2019 Ram ProMaster** · {{chip:gray|121,480 mi · today}} {{chip:green|Oil OK · next 123,000}}
:::

The oil chip tracks your vehicle's oil-change schedule from the readings you send. That is another reason to keep them coming.

## Oil change prompts

When your readings show the truck closing in on its next oil change, the card tells you directly:

- {{chip:yellow|Oil change suggested}} means you're inside the warning window, for example "due in 400 mi". You work a shop visit into the week.
- {{chip:red|Oil change required}} means it is past due, for example "620 mi overdue". You get it in. The office logs the service and the prompt clears.

The mile thresholds behind these are set per vehicle by the office. The math runs off the odometer readings you send. A truck with stale readings can't warn you, so keep them coming.

## Reporting a problem

You tap {{button:outline|Report problem}}. You describe what's wrong in your own words. Then you pick how serious it is:

- {{chip:gray|Monitor}} means worth knowing, not urgent.
- {{chip:yellow|Needs service}} means book a shop visit.
- {{chip:red|Urgent}} means deal with it now.

{{button:blue|Send to the office}} files it on the vehicle's record. The office sees it immediately on the fleet board, the office's list of every truck. It stays open until someone resolves it. The card shows your vehicle's open problem count so you know it was received.
