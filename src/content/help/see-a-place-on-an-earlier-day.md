A map with a **Time** press can show a place as it looked on earlier days. You use it to see a lot before the slab, or a trench before it was filled.

:::example The presses on the map
{{button:outline|Satellite}} {{button:outline|Streets}} {{button:outline|Time}}
:::

## Turn Time on

:::steps
1. Tap the dot of the place you want to see.
2. Tap {{button:outline|Time}} on the map.
3. Read the row of dates that opens under the map.
:::

The map moves in close on that place. A map with one dot needs no tap on it. With several dots and none open, the row asks you to press a dot.

## Flip between then and now

- Tap a date to see the place on that day. The oldest date is on the left.
- Tap **Today** at the right end to see today's photo again.
- Slide the row sideways when it holds more dates than fit.

The map does not move between a date and Today. You see the same ground each time.

The line over the dates says when the photo was taken. Today's photo has a day too, and that day is rarely today.

A date reads **Before** a month while the archive finds the day the photo was taken.

## What does not change

The dots on the map are today's dots. A photo from 2019 still shows this week's tasks on it.

## Turn Time off

Tap {{button:outline|Time}} again, or tap **Satellite** or **Streets**. The map shows all its dots again.

Time also goes away when you leave the screen. The map you come back to is the map of today.

## Where the photos come from

The older photos come from Esri's photo archive, not from Google. Each place has its own dates.

Google shows its own older photos only in Google Earth. Tap **Older in Google Earth** at the left end of the row to see them there.
