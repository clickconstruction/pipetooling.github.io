Texas dropped the notary requirement for lien waivers in 2022. A signed waiver on the state's form is fully effective. This page gives you the words and the law for a GC or vendor who still asks.

A GC is the general contractor. Many GCs and title companies still ask for a notarized waiver out of habit. The law changed on January 1, 2022. You do not need to argue. Send the signed release the normal way, and send this page's link beside it. The link is public and opens without a sign in.

## The short answer

- Since January 1, 2022, a lien waiver in Texas is effective when it is signed.
- No notary is needed. House Bill 2237 struck the old notary line from the statute, *Prop. Code § 53.281(b)*.
- Texas writes the waiver wording itself. The four standard forms are in *Prop. Code § 53.284*.
- Under *§ 53.281*, a waiver is effective only when it substantially follows one of those forms.
- The statutory form protects the GC and us alike. A custom waiver that strays from that wording risks being unenforceable.

## The one case that still uses a notary

One paper still carries a notary stamp. That is a release of a lien already filed with the county clerk. A paper headed for the county records must be acknowledged before an officer such as a notary, *Prop. Code § 12.001*. So a release of record is notarized, and an ordinary waiver is not. When a filed lien must be released of record, we provide a notarized release ready for recording.

## What to tell them

- Say the law changed in 2022. House Bill 2237 removed the notary requirement from Texas lien waivers.
- Say the signed form is the protection. Our waiver follows the exact wording Texas prescribes.
- Say the one notary case is a release recorded with the county clerk. We notarize those.
- Their company policy may still require a stamp. Tell the master, and we can accommodate it. Texas simply no longer requires it.
