Every supply house bill is read line by line. **What we buy** adds those lines up. The page shows which parts the company buys, how often, how many and for how much.

Open **Materials**, then {{button:outline|What we buy}} in the Purchase group.

## Read the page

Four figures sit at the top.

- **Spent on parts** is the spend in the months you picked, and how many bills it is on.
- **Charges** is freight, fuel and fees. A charge is never counted as a part.
- **Distinct parts** is how many rows the table has. The line under it says how many have no part yet.
- **Bills not counted** is how many bills wait for a person. Each of those bills has a reason on [Supply houses](/materials?tab=supply-houses).

Each row under the figures is one thing the company buys.

- **Bought** is how many bills carry it.
- **Quantity** is how many, in the unit the bills print.
- **Spend** is what it cost in the months you picked.
- **By month** is a small bar for each month. Hold the pointer on a bar to read its month and its dollars.
- **Houses** lists where it was bought, the most spent first.
- **Last paid** is the price for one on the newest bill.

## Narrow it down

:::steps

1. Pick a house under **House** to see one house alone.

2. Pick a job under **Job** to see one job alone. A bill split across jobs gives each job its share.

3. Set **From** and **To** to change the months. The page opens on the last six.

4. Press {{button:outline|Spend}}, {{button:outline|Quantity}} or {{button:outline|How often}} to sort.

:::

Press *Clear the filters* at the foot of the page to go back to every house and every job.

## See a part's price over time

Press a row's name. **Price over time** opens on that row. You can also press {{button:outline|Price over time}} at the top and find a part by its name or its code.

Every purchase is a point. The point's height is what was paid for one. Hold the pointer on a point to read its day, its house, its price and its quantity.

- Each of the three biggest houses has its own colour. Every other house shares the grey point.
- The dashed line is the book's price, with the day it was last checked.
- **Last paid** under the chart is the newest price at each house. The line under it says how the price moved since the purchase before.
- *cheapest these 90 days* marks the house with the lowest last price, when two or more houses sold it in that time.

Every purchase is also a row under the chart, with its bill, its quantity and its job.

A return is not a price paid. Price over time leaves returns out.

## See what to stock

Press {{button:outline|What to stock}} at the top. The list shows the parts bought on three or more separate days. The part with the most trips is first.

- **Trips** is how many separate days it was bought. Two bills on one day are one trip.
- **At a time** is the fewest and the most on one bill.
- **Jobs** is how many jobs those bills were for.
- **Where** says *the counter* when most trips were small bills or carried a PO Generator code. **Where** says *delivered* when most came on big orders.
- **Read as** puts the row in one line, such as *a trip every 18 days for a $3.10 part*.

Pick **Last 30 days**, **Last 90 days** or **Last 180 days** under **Window**. Press a row's name to see its prices.

A part that is cheap and bought often is the one to keep on a shelf. The trip costs more than the part.

This list shows purchases, not use. A bill cannot say how many the jobs used.

## A row with no part yet

A house prints its own code for a part on every line. The parts book learns a house's code once. Until then the row is counted by the house and its code, and it reads *no part yet*.

The same part bought at two houses is two rows until both codes are matched. Match the codes in one place with **Codes with no part** on the Parts Book. See [Match the codes on the bills](/help/keep-a-supply-houses-number-on-a-part#match-the-codes-on-the-bills).

## What the page cannot say

Only bills whose lines add up are counted. A quote, an acknowledgement and a ship ticket are never counted.

A bill says what was bought. A bill does not say what was installed or what is on a truck.

Card purchases at big-box stores are not here. A card purchase has no lines on any paper.
