When a robot estimator finishes a draft bid, it asks for your audit. Everything happens in one place — the **Audits** tab on the Bids page — and every note you leave teaches the robot for next time.

## Start with the questions

The tab opens with one sentence that sizes today — *Today: 19 questions, about fifteen minutes. Then 31 audits. 8 more open when you send.* — and one button, {{button:blue|Answer the 19 questions}}. The questions are every one the robots have parked while working, from every bid; answering them is the quickest, highest-leverage thing on the page, so they come first.

1. The button opens a **run-through**: one question fills the screen, with its topic as a chip (like {{chip:gray|Travel bands}}) and a line saying which bids it was asked on. The answers are big numbered buttons, the robot's own pick first as {{button:blue|1 ★ About $4,000 · the robot's pick}} and the rest outlined. Tap one, or press its number; **Enter** takes the robot's pick; **→** skips to the next question; **Esc** closes. The last number is {{button:gray|Something else…}}, which swaps the buttons for a box.
2. When several robots hit the same issue on different bids — say, whether to carry travel past 200 miles — their questions are **one question** in the run-through, and it comes first: one tap answers every open copy at once, and every robot picks it up on its next run. Today's questions come next (a live bid and a waiting robot behind each), then the older ones.
3. A question with no buttons is an older or free-form one: type your ruling and hit {{button:blue|Answer all 3}}.
4. {{button:gray|Not mine}} and {{button:gray|Dismiss}} sit in the corner. **Only questions about the job belong here.** When a robot's problem is its own machine — a sandbox, a sign-in, a file it can't open — hit **Not mine** and it moves to the person who runs the robots; **Dismiss** closes a question you don't intend to answer, and the robots stop asking.
5. At the end the sheet lists what was saved, and {{button:blue|Back to the queue}} returns you to the audits.
6. Under the button, the **Questions · 19** panel (the standing rulings, in the tooltip) lists one line per question — the chip, the question cut to a line, how many bids, and the robot's pick. Tap a line to start the run-through there; **Show all 19** unfolds the rest. The bid number inside a question is a link — open it in a new tab for context before you answer. When that number is the robot's own practice copy, a green **ours b214** link beside it opens the real bid, the one with your counts and your price.
7. **A robot asking for a different plan set is not a question for the run-through.** "The file on the bid is the electrical set, attach the plumbing sheets?" is a fix on that one bid, so the panel shows one line instead, *One bid needs a different plan set*, with the bid number as a link. It opens that bid's robot needs sheet on the Bid Board, where {{button:gray|Edit bid}} and {{button:gray|Copy intake address}} sit beside the question and {{button:blue|★ Attached — rerun}} tells the robot to go again. A question a robot wrote before the one-decision rule — several decisions in one, no buttons — is not yours to untangle either: the panel says one is waiting for the owner, who splits it into one-tap questions from Robots → Console.

:::example Why the questions first
An hour spent auditing one card yields a handful of verdicts; fifteen minutes answering the questions yields doctrine that moves every future robot bid. Highest leverage on the page.
:::

## Find your pending audits

1. Your Dashboard tells you first: while robots are waiting on you, the **Needs you** card carries a {{chip:yellow|Robot training}} item that says the questions first — *19 robot questions (about 15 min) and 31 audits are waiting on you* — because the questions are the quick part; {{button:amber|Open Audits}} takes you straight to the tab. It stays until the queue is empty (locked shadow audits don't count — they aren't workable yet). The same card also shows a {{chip:blue|Robot bid}} line when the robot has sealed a number on one of our live bids — nothing to do there, it's just the head start; the score lands by itself the moment we send, the envelope opens for you right then (see *review the robot's number when you send*), and {{button:blue|Open Robot Board}} shows every sealed and scored run beside its bid.
2. Or go to **Bids** yourself. When a robot bid is waiting on you, an {{chip:yellow|Audits · 1}} tab appears near the Bid Board — the number is how many are waiting. Under the Robot Board · Audits · Scoreboard bar, a line says what the open lens is for, and a strip of six numbers heads every lens — bids with a robot run, live bids shadowed, sealed numbers, bids that need a person, audits waiting (with the oldest's age), kinds of job that earned first drafts. Each tile opens the lens that works it.
3. Open it. The audits are a **queue** in four sections: **Now** (the open card, with where it sits — *Now 5 of 31 · you picked it*), **Up next** (the waiting ones, **by what your verdict unblocks**: cards with unanswered robot questions first, then the ones that landed furthest from our number, oldest as the tiebreak — so the top row is always the one that teaches the most), **Opens when you send** (folded — the shadow audits that stay locked until our own bid goes out, each with the due date of the bid it waits on) and **Digested** (folded). Every row is named for the job — *MPH Casa Linda*, not the robot's copy number — with the delta as its one number and a why line: how many questions, notes, the kind of job and how many in a row it has (*Vet clinic, 1 of 5 in a row*), *needs a fix first* when the robot asked for a different plan set, and which slate it came from (*re-bid Sep 5*, *slate Aug 31*, *shadow · sent 09/22*). **One card is open at a time**, and it is the top one: the tab waits for the queue to load and opens the first card, then keeps whichever row you tap. On a wide screen the queue sits on the left and the card on the right, always at the top, so a tap never scrolls; on a phone a row opens the card full-screen, and {{button:green|Finish audit → next: Bonilla Law Firm}} at the bottom says what opens next.
4. The open card starts with the robot's own confession — 🤖 **Where I'm least sure**, its first two sentences with *read the rest ▸* — so you can check its suspicions first, then a comparison strip (its draft, our number, the difference) and the six biggest differences.
5. A shadow bid sits under **Opens when you send** instead of a card: the whole audit stays locked until our own bid goes out — even seeing the robot's takeoff early could sway your number. It moves into Up next by itself once we send.
6. A row whose why line reads *robot still working · no counts yet* means the robot opened the audit before pasting its counts into the bid, so there is no draft to price yet. It doesn't count toward your pending number; you can still open it to answer its questions, and the verdict rows appear once the counts land.

## Look the bid over (two quick links)

1. {{button:blue|Open takeoff (CountTooling) ↗}} opens the robot's plan markups in a new tab — no sign-in needed. Its notes are numbered pins on the plans.
2. {{button:blue|Open bid (ClickTooling) ↗}} opens the bid's Counts in a new tab, so you can see every row and price it drafted.
3. Flip between those tabs and the audit card as you go — nothing you type is lost.

Mention pin numbers in your notes — "pin 3: those are by others" — and the robot knows exactly which spot on the plans you mean.

## Answer its questions and leave your notes

1. **The six biggest differences first.** The card matches the robot's rows against ours by name and leads with the six that move the most money — *judge these first* — each with one tap:
   - {{button:red|✗ Robot's wrong}} — drafts a teaching note for you; edit if you like and hit **Post**.
   - {{button:amber|📋 Our record's off}} — the robot found a hole in OUR bid; posting files a record-repair.
   - {{button:green|✓ Both fine}} — scope difference or judgment call; posts instantly.
   A **Where the delta lives** strip above them splits the headline difference into named dollars — missed, added, counts, rates, and everything else (like the sent letter's markup over raw rows) — so you can see at a glance whether the robot miscounted or mispriced. When a row we carry and a row the robot added look like the same item under two names — *RENTALS/TRAVEL* and *Travel & Rentals (per mile)* — they show as **one row** asking *one item, two names?*: {{button:outline|Same item — teach the name}} posts a note the robot digests as an alias (no verdicts to give twice), {{button:outline|No, two things}} splits them back into two rows. Under the six sit three folds: *N more differences ▸* (the rest in four lists — {{chip:red|ROBOT MISSED}}, {{chip:yellow|ROBOT ADDED}}, {{chip:blue|QUANTITY GAPS}}, {{chip:purple|PRICED DIFFERENTLY}} — biggest dollars first), *M more pairs that look like one item ▸*, and *the system table ▸* (waste + vent, water, gas, med-gas and fixtures, robot vs ours as a ratio). Rows that match within 15% on both count and price aren't shown — there's nothing to judge there. (When there's no reference bid to compare against, the card falls back to listing the robot's biggest rows with 👍 / 🚩.)
2. **Its questions** — the ones about this bid; a job-wide question goes to the run-through at the top of the tab. The first shows with 🤖 and the plan sheet it came from; *N more questions ▸* unfolds the rest. Type in the box and hit {{button:blue|Answer}} — short is fine.
3. Anything else goes in the **one note box** at the bottom — pick a section chip (Counts / Footage / Pricing / Scope / General) if it fits, or leave it on General. Type each thing you'd tell a junior estimator — "we always carry travel past 200 miles."
4. When you're done, hit {{button:green|Finish audit}}. The card flips to {{chip:blue|Waiting on robot digest}}, the ledger records it, and the next waiting audit opens automatically.

## What happens to your notes

The robot reads every note, changes how it works (its playbook, its price and labor books, or just that one bid), and replies under your note with a receipt — 🤖 → "Learned: …" — so you can see your feedback landed. Once every note has its receipt the card moves to {{chip:green|Digested}}, kept under **Show digested audits** for reference.

## Who the robots calibrate to

A robot's shadow score is measured against whoever sent the real bid. Only a **calibration standard** estimator's numbers count toward the robot's readiness; the Scoreboard shows everyone's runs and marks the ones that count with a ✓. The company owner picks the standard under **Settings → Digital twins → ★ Calibration standard**: each estimating user shows {{chip:green|STANDARD}} or {{chip:gray|PRACTICE}} with a {{button:blue|Make standard}} switch beside it. Leave at least one standard set, or no robot score can ever count.
