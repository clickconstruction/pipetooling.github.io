When a robot estimator finishes a draft bid, it asks for your audit. Everything happens on the Audits tab on the Bids page.

Every note you leave teaches the robot for next time.

## Start with the questions

The tab opens with one sentence that sizes today. It reads like *Today: 19 questions, about fifteen minutes. Then 31 audits. 8 more open when you send.* Under it sits one button, {{button:blue|Answer the 19 questions}}. The questions are every one the robots have parked while working, from every bid. Answering them is the quickest, highest-leverage thing on the page. So they come first.

1. The button opens a **run-through**. One question fills the screen. Its topic shows as a chip, like {{chip:gray|Travel bands}}. A line says which bids it was asked on. The answers are big numbered buttons. The robot's own pick comes first, as {{button:blue|1 ★ About $4,000 · the robot's pick}}. The rest are outlined. Tap one, or press its number. **Enter** takes the robot's pick. **→** skips to the next question. **Esc** closes. The last number is {{button:gray|Something else…}}. It swaps the buttons for a box.
2. Several robots may hit the same issue on different bids. One issue is whether to carry travel past 200 miles. Their questions are **one question** in the run-through, and it comes first. One tap answers every open copy at once. Every robot picks it up on its next run. Today's questions come next. A live bid and a waiting robot sit behind each of them. Then come the older ones.
3. A question with no buttons is an older or free-form one. Type your ruling and hit {{button:blue|Answer all 3}}.
4. {{button:gray|Not mine}} and {{button:gray|Dismiss}} sit in the corner. **Only questions about the job belong here.** A robot's problem may be its own machine. That is a sandbox, a sign-in, or a file it can't open. Hit **Not mine** and it moves to the person who runs the robots. **Dismiss** closes a question you don't intend to answer. The robots stop asking.
5. At the end the sheet lists what was saved, and {{button:blue|Back to the queue}} returns you to the audits.
6. Under the button sits the *Questions · 19* panel. Its tooltip calls it the standing rulings. It lists one line per question. Each line shows the chip, the question cut to a line, how many bids, and the robot's pick. Tap a line to start the run-through there. **Show all 19** unfolds the rest. The bid number inside a question is a link. Open it in a new tab for context before you answer. That number may be the robot's own practice copy. Then a green **ours b214** link beside it opens the real bid. That is the one with your counts and your price.
7. **A robot asking for a different plan set is not a question for the run-through.** One such question is "The file on the bid is the electrical set, attach the plumbing sheets?". That is a fix on that one bid. The panel shows one line instead, *One bid needs a different plan set*, with the bid number as a link. It opens that bid's robot needs sheet on the Bid Board. There, {{button:gray|Edit bid}} and {{button:gray|Copy intake address}} sit beside the question. {{button:blue|★ Attached — rerun}} tells the robot to go again. A robot may have written a question before the one-decision rule. That question holds several decisions in one and has no buttons. It is not yours to untangle either. The panel says one is waiting for the owner. The owner splits it into one-tap questions from Robots → Console.

:::example Why the questions first
An hour spent auditing one card yields a handful of verdicts; fifteen minutes answering the questions yields doctrine that moves every future robot bid. Highest leverage on the page.
:::

## Find your pending audits

1. Your Dashboard tells you first. While robots are waiting on you, the **Needs you** card carries a {{chip:yellow|Robot training}} item. It says the questions first, like *19 robot questions (about 15 min) and 31 audits are waiting on you*. The questions are the quick part. {{button:amber|Open Audits}} takes you straight to the tab. It stays until the queue is empty. Locked shadow audits don't count. They aren't workable yet. The same card also shows a {{chip:blue|Robot bid}} line. It appears when the robot has sealed a number on one of our live bids. There is nothing to do there. It's just the head start. The score lands by itself the moment we send. The envelope opens for you right then. See *review the robot's number when you send*. {{button:blue|Open Robot Board}} shows every sealed and scored run beside its bid.
2. Or go to **Bids** yourself. When a robot bid is waiting on you, an {{chip:yellow|Audits · 1}} tab appears near the Bid Board. The number is how many are waiting. Under the *Robot Board · Audits · Scoreboard* bar, a line says what the open lens is for. A strip of six numbers heads every lens. The numbers are bids with a robot run, live bids shadowed, sealed numbers, and bids that need a person. Then come audits waiting, with the oldest's age, and kinds of job that earned first drafts. Each tile opens the lens that works it.
3. Open it. The audits are a **queue** in four sections. **Now** is the open card, with where it sits, like *Now 5 of 31 · you picked it*. **Up next** is the waiting ones, **sorted by what your verdict unblocks**. Cards with unanswered robot questions come first. Then come the ones that landed furthest from our number. Oldest is the tiebreak. So the top row is always the one that teaches the most. **Opens when you send** is folded. It holds the shadow audits that stay locked until our own bid goes out. Each shows the due date of the bid it waits on. **Digested** is folded too. Every row is named for the job, like *MPH Casa Linda*, not the robot's copy number. The delta is its one number. A why line says how many questions and notes it has. It says the kind of job and how many in a row it has, like *Vet clinic, 1 of 5 in a row*. It says *needs a fix first* when the robot asked for a different plan set. It says which slate it came from, like *re-bid Sep 5*, *slate Aug 31* or *shadow · sent 09/22*. **One card is open at a time**, and it is the top one. The tab waits for the queue to load and opens the first card. Then it keeps whichever row you tap. On a wide screen the queue sits on the left and the card on the right. The card stays at the top, so a tap never scrolls. On a phone a row opens the card full-screen. {{button:green|Finish audit → next: Bonilla Law Firm}} at the bottom says what opens next.
4. The open card starts with the robot's own confession, 🤖 **Where I'm least sure**. It shows its first two sentences, with *read the rest ▸*. So you can check its suspicions first. Then comes a comparison strip: its draft, our number, the difference. Then come the six biggest differences.
5. A shadow bid sits under **Opens when you send** instead of a card. The whole audit stays locked until our own bid goes out. Even seeing the robot's takeoff early could sway your number. A takeoff is the count of parts read off the plans. It moves into Up next by itself once we send.
6. A row's why line may read *robot still working · no counts yet*. That means the robot opened the audit before pasting its counts into the bid. So there is no draft to price yet. It doesn't count toward your pending number. You can still open it to answer its questions. The verdict rows appear once the counts land.

## Look the bid over (two quick links)

1. {{button:blue|Open takeoff (CountTooling) ↗}} opens the robot's plan markups in a new tab. No sign-in is needed. Its notes are numbered pins on the plans.
2. {{button:blue|Open bid (ClickTooling) ↗}} opens the bid's Counts in a new tab. So you can see every row and price it drafted.
3. Flip between those tabs and the audit card as you go. Nothing you type is lost.

Mention pin numbers in your notes — "pin 3: those are by others" — and the robot knows exactly which spot on the plans you mean.

## Answer its questions and leave your notes

1. **The six biggest differences first.** The card matches the robot's rows against ours by name. It leads with the six that move the most money, marked *judge these first*. Judge each with one tap:
   - {{button:red|✗ Robot's wrong}} drafts a teaching note for you. Edit if you like and hit **Post**.
   - {{button:amber|📋 Our record's off}} means the robot found a hole in OUR bid. Posting files a record-repair.
   - {{button:green|✓ Both fine}} means a scope difference or judgment call. It posts instantly.
   A **Where the delta lives** strip sits above them. The delta is the headline difference. The strip splits it into named dollars: missed, added, counts, rates, and everything else. Everything else includes the sent letter's markup over raw rows. So you can see at a glance whether the robot miscounted or mispriced. A row we carry and a row the robot added may look like the same item under two names. One pair is *RENTALS/TRAVEL* and *Travel & Rentals (per mile)*. They show as **one row** asking *one item, two names?*. {{button:outline|Same item — teach the name}} posts a note the robot digests as an alias. There are no verdicts to give twice. {{button:outline|No, two things}} splits them back into two rows. Under the six sit three folds. *N more differences ▸* holds the rest in four lists, biggest dollars first. The lists are {{chip:red|ROBOT MISSED}}, {{chip:yellow|ROBOT ADDED}}, {{chip:blue|QUANTITY GAPS}} and {{chip:purple|PRICED DIFFERENTLY}}. *M more pairs that look like one item ▸* holds the other pairs. *The system table ▸* lists waste + vent, water, gas, med-gas and fixtures, robot vs ours as a ratio. Rows that match within 15% on both count and price aren't shown. There's nothing to judge there. When there's no reference bid to compare against, the card falls back. It lists the robot's biggest rows with 👍 / 🚩.
2. **Its questions** are the ones about this bid. A job-wide question goes to the run-through at the top of the tab. The first shows with 🤖 and the plan sheet it came from. *N more questions ▸* unfolds the rest. Type in the box and hit {{button:blue|Answer}}. Short is fine.
3. Anything else goes in the **one note box** at the bottom. Pick a section chip if it fits: Counts, Footage, Pricing, Scope or General. Or leave it on General. Type each thing you'd tell a junior estimator. One might be "we always carry travel past 200 miles."
4. When you're done, hit {{button:green|Finish audit → next: …}}. The card flips to {{chip:blue|Waiting on robot digest}} under **Digesting**. The ledger records it. The top of Up next opens for you.

## What happens to your notes

The robot reads every note. It changes how it works: its playbook, its price and labor books, or just that one bid. It replies under your note with a receipt, 🤖 → "Learned: …". So you can see your feedback landed. Once every note has its receipt the card moves to {{chip:green|Digested}}. It is kept folded at the foot of the queue for reference.

## Who the robots calibrate to

A robot's shadow score is measured against whoever sent the real bid. Only a **calibration standard** estimator's numbers count toward the robot's readiness. A calibration standard is the estimator the robot is measured against. The Scoreboard shows everyone's runs and marks the ones that count with a ✓. The company owner picks the standard under **Settings → Digital twins → ★ Calibration standard**. Each estimating user shows {{chip:green|STANDARD}} or {{chip:gray|PRACTICE}} with a {{button:blue|Make standard}} switch beside it. Leave at least one standard set, or no robot score can ever count.
