On a big job the material lives in stencilled cages and boxes left on the site. The On site tab on the Tally keeps what is in each one. The office then knows what is there, what was used and what walked away.

## Open the tab

Open **Tally**. It opens on **On site**, the first tab. Pick the job at the top. On your Dashboard, the Job Mode card has **Materials on site**. Tap it and the tab opens on the job you are clocked into.

## Find the job

Tap the job at the top and the **Choose job** sheet opens. The search box sits on top. It is not focused, so the keyboard stays down until you tap it.

Type a number, a name, an address or a customer. Every section narrows as you type. What you typed lights up on each row where it landed. A number you type puts that job first. Tap the ⊗ in the box to start over.

Under the search, **Recent** lists the last three jobs you clocked in on, newest first. Each row says when, like *Clocked in today*. The press under them says how many more there are, up to ten. Tap it to see them all. With no clock-ins, the jobs your card purchases were sorted to stand in. Those rows say *Purchase sorted* and the day.

**All jobs** follows under its own heading with its count. The same sheet opens from the Materials Estimate tab.

The row under the job reads your day left to right: {{chip:blue|Unsorted 2}} → {{chip:gray|Used 6}} → {{chip:green|Count 25}}. A dot marks the one that needs you now.

A job with no cage or box offers **Add a container** to start one. Under it the screen names your other jobs that hold a box. Tap a job in that list and its boxes open.

## Find a part on any job

Type a part, its maker or a stencil in the search under the job. It looks on this job first. Tap **Every job** and the same words are searched on your other jobs too. A match on another job sits under that job's name. Tap it and that job opens on the box.

When nothing on this job matches, the screen offers **Look on every job**.

## Put away a delivery

A delivery the office lands waits in **Unsorted**. Tap it, then tap {{button:outline-blue|Put in…}} on each line and pick the cage or box it goes in. The parts move and Unsorted empties.

## Use a part, move it or say you can't find it

Tap a box to open it. Each line has four presses.

- {{button:outline|Used}} takes the number you used out of the box.
- {{button:outline|Move to…}} puts some in another box, back to the supply house, to the truck or marks them damaged.
- {{button:outline-amber|Can't find}} says the box should have it and it is not there. The office sees it that day.
- {{button:outline-blue|Short}} asks the office for more. Type how many more you need and tap **Ask the office for 4**. The request lands on the job's Parts tab, the same as Send to Office. A part from the parts book also gets a draft purchase order. A line with none left keeps Short beside its words.

The office and a job's primary can file that request. A helper or a subcontractor reads **Only the office can file a parts request on this job.** The message then gives the words to tell the office.

Every press but Short is one row in the ledger. A wrong press gets a new press. Nothing is edited. Short writes a request, not a row in the box.

## Count a box

A box is due a count after two weeks. Tap {{button:blue|Count this box}} and walk the lines. The walk shows every line that has parts, one at a time. A line marked can't find is in the walk too, even with none left. Your count clears the mark.

Tap **25 is right** when the number matches. Type the number when it does not. Tap **Skip this line** to leave a line as it is. Tap **Not in A1 any more** when the part is gone from the box.

The count becomes the truth for that box. A short count is what the office reads as missing.

## Add a container or a part

Tap {{button:outline|Add a container}} to add a cage or box with its stencil and its place on the site. On a box, tap **Add a part** for a part that came without a delivery.

Type the part's name. **Already on this job** lists the names the job has, with how many and in which box. Tap one when it is the same part. The new pieces go under that name, and the office reads one total. Type a new name only when the job has none like it.

## Take a photo

On a box, tap {{button:outline|Photo}}. For ten minutes after a count or a put away the button is blue. The picture is named for what it followed. Shoot and tap Use Photo. The phone shrinks the picture and files it in the job's Drive folder by itself.

With no signal the photo stays on the phone. Tap **Send now** when you have signal again, or **Share instead** to send it another way.

## With no signal

A press made with no signal is kept on the phone. The box shows the new number at once. A message reads *No signal. What you press is kept on this phone and sends by itself.*

The line at the top of the screen counts what waits. Each press goes out when the signal is back, in the order you made it. You can close the app. The presses are still there when you open it again.

The office sees a press only after it lands. If the office refuses one, the line turns red and gives the reason. The box then shows the number the office holds.

Read [keep working when the signal drops](/help/keep-working-when-the-signal-drops) for the line and its list.

## Find a box on the site

Tap **Map** to see the pinned boxes on the site. On a box, tap **Pin on map** and stand where it is. Tap **Move box** when it moves to another place.

## Scan a label

A printed label carries a code. Point your camera at it and the box opens. Each label prints on its own sheet.

A label printed before November 11, 2026 can open a web search on an iPhone. Print that label again and the new one opens the box. Until then, type its code with **Scan a label**.

A label the camera cannot read still opens. Tap **Scan a label** beside the search and type the eight characters under the code. Tap {{button:blue|Open the box}}. A box on another job opens on that job.

To print one box's label, open the box and tap **Print the label** under its parts. The label window opens right there. The office can print a whole job's labels from Materials, with **Print labels** at the right end of the tabs.

Under the code is its short address, like prts.co/b/4F2A-9C18. Read it out with the dash. Typed with or without the dash, it opens the same box.

## Print a tape label

The Labels window asks first what the labels print on. Pick **Brother tape** for the P-touch, or **Paper sheet** for any printer. Each box becomes one strip of tape with the QR, the stencil and the code.

A label prints the letter and number alone. A box named G12 COPPER 90 prints as G12, so the label stays true when the box holds something else. A name with no letter and number prints whole. A line under the boxes says which labels print shorter.

The app prints straight to the P-touch from Chrome or Edge on a computer. There is nothing to install. The first time, the window lists three steps. Plug the P-touch in with its USB cable and switch it on. Quit Brother's P-touch Editor if it is open. Press **Connect the P-touch** and pick the printer in the browser's list. The browser remembers it.

Once it is connected, the window names the printer and the tape inside it. Press **Print 1 test strip** before a whole run. It prints the first ticked box alone. Check that the strip is cut right and the code scans. Then press **Print 3 tape labels**. The strips print one at a time and the printer cuts each one. A run wastes one blank square of tape at its start, however many strips it holds. Print strips together to save tape.

Safari and Firefox cannot reach a USB printer. There the Brother tape choice says Needs Chrome or Edge. Press **Copy the link to this page** and paste it into Chrome or Edge. Brother makes no printer driver for a Mac, so the print window cannot print tape on one.

On Windows with Brother's driver, you can press **Use the print window instead**. After the first print it sits under **Printer setup**. In the print window, pick the P-touch and the paper that matches your tape, 18 mm or 24 mm.

Untick a box to leave it out. **None** clears them all and **Every box** picks them all again. That is the quick way to print one new cage's strip.

A box with more than one side needs more than one strip. Set **Copies of each** with the minus and plus presses. With 2, the strips come out as 1, 1, 2, 2, 3, 3. The print press names the total, as in **Print 6 tape labels**. The test strip is always one strip. The presses sit at the bottom of the window and stay in reach as you scroll.

After the first print the window opens on tape on that computer. Press **Change** to pick paper again. Press **Printer setup** to read the three steps again.

Printing at the cage from Brother's phone app? Under a box's parts, press **Copy the label code**. The address goes on the clipboard. Paste it into the Brother app as a QR code and print. The office can also press **Export the labels as a CSV** in the Labels window. That file has one row per box for a label editor.

Signed out, the scan still opens a page. The top is a dark plate with the stencil in large letters and the code under it. The foot of the plate says how fresh the count is, such as Counted 2 days ago. It says Due a count once the last count is 14 days old. The page lists what is in the box and shows the newest photo. Tap the photo to see it whole. A part that ran out since the last count stays on the list as None left. A part the crew pressed Can't find on says how many could not be found. The page never names the job.

Looking for a part that is not in that box? Type the part, the maker or a stencil in the search field and press **Find**. The page lists the boxes on that site that hold it, with their counts. It never searches another site.

Is something missing or wrong with the box? Press **Text the office**. Your phone opens a text to the office with the box's name already in it. Press **Call the office** to ring them.

To record a part used, moved or counted, press **Sign in to record a use or a count**. After you sign in you land on that box. You sign in once in a phone's browser. After that a scan opens the box's own screen.

A label from a retired box says so and offers **Scan another label**. Open prts.co with nothing after it and you get **Scan a label**. There you can type a code, or search the site you scanned today.

A box can also carry a manifest, a laminated page that lists what is in it. Under a box's parts, under the words Paper for A1, press **Print the manifest**. The manifest window opens right there. Write what you add on the page's ruled lines, and press it in the app too.

## Change a box's name or kind

A name typed wrong, or a pile saved as a cage, is fixed on the box. Open the box and tap {{button:outline|Edit}} beside its name. On the office page, press a box's name or the **Edit** press beside it.

- **Its name** takes a new name. A name another box on the job carries is refused.
- **How it sits** is Cage, Box or Loose pile. A pallet is a loose pile, and so is a thing too big for a box.
- **Where exactly** says where it stands, such as on a pallet by the north door.
- **Which place on site** moves the box to another place, or to a new one you type.

The press says what it will do, such as **Rename to G70** or **Save G70**.

A label printed with the old name still opens the box, because its code never changes. Only the large words are old. Print the label again to show the new name.

The window has two tabs. **Cage** holds those fields, the box's code and its history: when it was added, each move and each rename. **Contents** lists each part in the box. Under a part is the day it came in, who added it and where the box stood then. Open the fold under a part to read every count, use and move of it.

A part's name typed wrong is fixed on **Contents**. Tap **Fix the name** under the part, type the right name and press **Rename to 3/4 pex**. The name changes in every box on the job that holds that part. The window says which boxes before you press. The counts and the history stay as they are. **Undo the rename** puts the old name back. A part from the parts book has no such press, since its name is the book's. Fixing a name needs a signal.

Rename a box only when the same things are inside. For different things, retire the box and add a new one. Last week's count then still means what it meant.

## Retire a box

When a cage or box leaves the job, open it and press **Retire G4** under Done with this box. The app refuses while parts are still in it. Move them or mark them used first.

Retired by mistake? The toast that says G4 retired carries **Undo** for one minute. Press it and G4 is back with its label working. After the minute, the office brings it back from the Retired fold on Jobs → Materials.
