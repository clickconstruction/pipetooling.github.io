On a big job the material lives in stencilled cages and boxes left on the site. The On site tab on the Tally keeps what is in each one. The office then knows what is there, what was used and what walked away.

## Open the tab

Open **Tally**. The Tally opens on **On site**, the first tab. Pick the job at the top. On your Dashboard, the Job Mode card has **Materials on site** beside **Job calendar**. Tap **Materials on site** and the tab opens on the job the card names. The number on the press counts the cages and boxes on that job's site. A small line under the press says how many boxes are due a count. The same line says how many stickers are ready for a box.

The search box at the top of the app finds a box too. Type the stencil, a part or a place. Press the box's row and the box opens.

## Find the job

Tap the job at the top and the **Choose job** sheet opens. The search box sits on top. It is not focused, so the keyboard stays down until you tap it.

Type a number, a name, an address or a customer. Every section narrows as you type. What you typed lights up on each row where it landed. A number you type puts that job first. Tap the ⊗ in the box to start over.

Under the search, **Recent** lists the last three jobs you clocked in on, newest first. Each row says when, like *Clocked in today*. The press under them says how many more there are, up to ten. Tap it to see them all. With no clock-ins, the jobs your card purchases were sorted to stand in. Those rows say *Purchase sorted* and the day.

**All jobs** follows under its own heading with its count. The same sheet opens from the Materials Estimate tab.

The row under the job reads your day left to right: {{chip:blue|Unsorted 2}} → {{chip:gray|Used 6}} → {{chip:green|Count 25}}. A dot marks the one that needs you now.

A job with no cage or box offers **Add a container** to start one. Under it the screen names your other jobs that hold a box. Tap a job in that list and its boxes open.

## Find a part on any job

Type a part, its maker or a stencil in the search under the job. It looks on this job first. Tap **Every job** and the same words are searched on your other jobs too. A match on another job sits under that job's name. Tap it and that job opens on the box.

When nothing on this job matches, the screen offers **Look on every job**.

## Put away a delivery

A delivery the office lands waits in **Unsorted**. Tap it, then tap {{button:outline-blue|Put in…}} on each line and pick the cage or box it goes in. The parts move and Unsorted empties.

## Use a part, move it or say you can't find it

Tap a box to open it. Its parts are listed with their numbers. Tap a part and four presses open under it. Tap the part again to close them.

- {{button:outline|Used}} takes the number you used out of the box.
- {{button:outline|Move to…}} puts some in another box, back to the supply house, to the truck or marks them damaged.
- {{button:outline-amber|Can't find}} says the box should have it and it is not there. The office sees it that day.
- {{button:outline|Ask for more}} asks the office for more. Type how many more you need and tap **Ask the office for 4**. The request lands on the job's Parts tab, the same as Send to Office. A part from the parts book also gets a draft purchase order. A part with none left keeps Ask for more alone.

The office and a job's primary can file that request. A helper or a subcontractor reads **Only the office can file a parts request on this job.** The message then gives the words to tell the office.

Every press but Ask for more is one row in the ledger. A wrong press gets a new press. Nothing is edited. Ask for more writes a request, not a row in the box.

## Take parts out of a box

When you take parts out of a cage or box, the app asks for two photos. One shows the box as you found it. One shows the box after. Between them you say what you took.

Open the box. Scanning its label opens it for you. Tap {{button:blue|Take from G12}} at the bottom of the screen. The camera opens. Take a photo of the box as you found it and tap Use Photo.

Most boxes hold one kind of part. For those the screen asks **How many are you taking?** It starts on 1. Tap **+** or a quick number to change it. Tap **All 24** to take every piece.

A box with more than one part lists what it holds. Tap a part to take one. Tap it again to take one more. Quick numbers show under a part you tapped. Tap one to set the count.

Did you take a part the list does not show? Tap **Something not on the list…** and name it.

Then tap the press at the bottom. It reads your count, like **Take 3 from G12**. The take is saved at once. The camera opens again. Take a photo of the box after and tap Use Photo. You are done.

You can skip a photo. Close the camera with no shot and the app goes on. The screen reads **G12 has no photo as found.** Tap **Take the photo** to try again. After the take, tap **Finish without it** to leave the second photo out. The office sees which photo is missing.

If you walk away, the box keeps your take for twelve hours. It reads *You started a take from G12 at 2:14 PM.* Tap **Finish it** to go on. Tap **Nothing taken** to put it away.

With no signal the take and its photos are kept on the phone. They send by themselves when the signal is back.

The app's number never stops you. If you take more than the app counted, the rest is recorded as found.

While a box is open, the Tally's tabs are put away. Tap **‹** to go back to the boxes and the tabs.

**Used** on a part's line still works. It takes parts out with no photo.

The box then shows **Taken from G12**. Each take reads who took what, with its pictures side by side. Tap a picture to open it whole. Everyone on the job sees them.

## Count a box

A box is due a count after two weeks. Under the parts, tap {{button:outline|Count A1}} and walk the lines. The press says when the box was last counted. The press turns amber when a count is due. The walk shows every line that has parts, one at a time. A line marked can't find is in the walk too, even with none left. Your count clears the mark.

Tap **25 is right** when the number matches. Type the number when it does not. Tap **Skip this line** to leave a line as it is. Tap **Not in A1 any more** when the part is gone from the box.

The count becomes the truth for that box. A short count is what the office reads as missing.

## Add a container or a part

Tap {{button:outline|Add a container}} to add a cage, a box or a loose pile. The app fills in the next free name on this site, like R89. Change it only when the paint on the steel says something else. A name is one to three letters and a number from 1 to 999, like R76 or G24B. A name is one of a kind across the whole site. Words typed after the name are kept. R76 TOILETS becomes the name R76, and Toilets moves to **What is in it**. Pick how it sits, say its place on the site, and tap the press that names the box. On a box, tap **Add a part** for a part that came without a delivery.

Type the part's name. **Already on this job** lists the names the job has, with how many and in which box. Tap one when it is the same part. The new pieces go under that name, and the office reads one total. Type a new name only when the job has none like it.

## Take a photo

On a box, tap {{button:outline|Photo}} under the parts. The press says how many photos the box has. For ten minutes after a count, an added part or a put away the press is outlined in blue. The press then says what the photo will show, like Of A1 as you counted it. A put away lights the box the parts went into. The picture is named for what it followed. Shoot and tap Use Photo. The phone shrinks the picture and files it in the job's Drive folder by itself.

The picture shows on the box for everyone on the job. Tap a picture to open it whole. Tap **Close the photo** to go back. A dashed square means the picture is still on the phone that took it.

With no signal the photo stays on the phone. It sends by itself when the signal is back. Tap **Send now** to send it at once. Tap **Share instead** to send it another way.

## With no signal

A press made with no signal is kept on the phone. The box shows the new number at once. A message reads *No signal. What you press is kept on this phone and sends by itself.*

The line at the top of the screen counts what waits. Each press goes out when the signal is back, in the order you made it. You can close the app. The presses are still there when you open it again.

The office sees a press only after it lands. If the office refuses one, the line turns red and gives the reason. The box then shows the number the office holds.

Read [keep working when the signal drops](/help/keep-working-when-the-signal-drops) for the line and its list.

## Say what is in a box

Every box has one line that says what is in it. You never have to type it. Add a part and the app writes the line by itself. A box with one kind of part reads its name and count, like *Kohler toilets · 24*. A mixed box reads how many parts it holds and names the two it holds most of.

The line shows under the box's name on the list and on the office page. Open a box and your own words show under its name. A line the app wrote does not show there, because the parts are listed under the name.

Tap {{button:outline|More}} beside the box's name. Then tap **Say it better** to put the line in your own words, like PVC fittings, mixed. Then tap {{button:blue|Save the line}}. Your words show from then on. The parts under them still count.

To hand the line back to the app, clear your words. Then tap {{button:blue|Use the app’s line}}. An empty box has no line yet. Tap **Say what is in it** to give it one.

Search finds a box by your words too. Type toilets and the box that says Kohler toilets is listed. **Print labels** shows each box's name with its line. That tells you which label you are printing. The printed label still carries the name alone.

## Find a box on the site

On a box, tap {{button:outline|Where is it?}} under the parts. The press names the box's place. The sheet says what the app knows. The words name the place and the note. The words say who pinned the box and on what day. Under the words is the newest photo, then the site from above with the box's pin.

Tap {{button:blue|Show where I am}} to see your own dot and a dashed line to the pin. The sheet says about how far the pin is and which way. The line is straight. The line knows no fence, wall or gate. Indoors the phone is unsure, so go by the place, the note and the photo. Your dot stays on your phone. The dot moves while the sheet is open and the screen is on.

When you find the box, tap {{button:blue|I found it}}. The app pins the box where you stand, with your name and the day. The next person who looks starts from your pin. Then the sheet asks if the box is still in the same place. Tap **Move box** when it is not.

A box nobody has pinned says so. Stand at it and tap **I found it**. A phone that is not sure within about 150 ft is refused. Step into the open and tap **Try again**. Or tap **Move the pin by hand** and drag the map until the ring sits on the box. Then tap **Put A1 here**.

A phone that gives only the area you are in pins nothing. The sheet then reads *Precise Location looks off.*, with the steps to the switch for your phone. The open sky does not fix that. Turn the switch on and tap **Try again**.

The same line shows under **Show where I am** and **Use my location**. A phone that gives only an area gets no dot, no distance and no move of the map.

The app also asks for a pin when you are standing at a box. The app asks after a count, a photo, a scanned label or a new box. The box's screen then reads **Pin A1 where you stand?** Tap {{button:blue|Pin it here}}, or tap **Not now**. The app asks once a day for a box. A box pinned in the last week is not asked about.

A straight line can cross a fence. A painted way knows the gate, because a person drew it. Tap **Places** at the top of the tab. Under a place, tap **＋ Paint the way here**. The painting window opens on a new map named for the place, like The way to Level 2. Draw or walk the way from the gate and save it. The way then stays on the place. One way serves every box that stands there. On a box in that place, **Where is it?** reads **The way to Level 2 is painted.** Tap {{button:outline|Open the way}} to see it. [Paint a map](/help/paint-a-map) says how to draw and walk a line.

To have someone else find the box, tap {{button:outline|Send someone to it}}. The New task window opens with the words Find A1 and where the box stands. The task carries the box's pin as its place. The task carries the painted way too, when the box's place has one. Pick the person and send it. The person reads the place and the way on the task. That works even for a person who is not on the job's crew.

A pin older than 30 days carries a caution, since boxes get moved. Tap **Directions** for the drive to the pin. Tap **Map** on the tab to see every pinned box on the site.

The site map has **Satellite**, **Streets** and **Time**. Tap **Time** to see the site on earlier days. The press is not there while you drag a pin into place. [See a place on a map on an earlier day](/help/see-a-place-on-an-earlier-day) shows how.

## Scan a label

A printed label carries a code. Point your camera at it and the box opens. A label prints on a sheet of paper or on a strip of tape.

A label printed before November 11, 2026 can open a web search on an iPhone. Print that label again and the new one opens the box. Until then, type its code with **Scan a label**.

A label the camera cannot read still opens. Tap **Scan a label** beside the search and type the eight characters under the code. Tap {{button:blue|Open the box}}. A box on another job opens on that job. A sticker's code works the same way. The sticker's box opens, or a reserved sticker opens the sheet that puts it on a box.

To print one box's label, open the box, tap **More** and tap **Print the label**. The label window opens right there. A whole job's labels print from Materials, with **Print labels** at the right end of the tabs. The office, an estimator, a primary and a superintendent have that press.

Under the code is its short address, like prts.co/b/4F2A-9C18. Read it out with the dash. Typed with or without the dash, it opens the same box.

## Stick a sticker on a box

The office prints a run of stickers before the boxes exist. Each sticker is reserved for a box and carries its own code to scan. Most also carry a box's name, like G78. Stick one on a cage or box. Then scan it with your phone's camera. The On site tab opens on a sheet that reads **Sticker G78 is new**. A sticker with no name opens **This sticker is new**, with its code at the top. The sheet then asks what the sticker is on.

Tap **It is on a box I know** when the box is already in the app. The sheet lists every box on this site. Your job's boxes come first. Boxes with no sticker come before the rest. A box on another job of the site names that job. Type the digits of the stencil, like 76, and R76 is left in the list. You can type a place or a part too. Tap the box, then tap {{button:blue|Put this sticker on R76}}. The box keeps its name, its parts, its counts and its photos. The sticker is one more way to open it.

Tap **It is a new box** when the box is not in the app yet. The sheet fills in the next free name on this site, like R97. Change the name only when the paint on the steel says something else. Pick how it sits and its place on the site. **What is in it** takes your own words, and you can leave it empty. Tap {{button:blue|Save box R97}}. The box opens with the sticker on it. Tap **Add a part** and **Photo** as you do on any box.

A line under the name says which job the new box goes on. Tap **Change** to pick another job. The sticker stays in hand while you pick.

A sticker printed with a box's name reads that name, large at the top of the sheet. When the site has no G78 yet, the sheet opens on **It is a new box** with G78 filled in. Pick how it sits and its place, then tap {{button:blue|Save box G78}}. When the site already has a G78, the sheet opens on that box with it picked. Tap {{button:blue|Put this sticker on G78}} to put the sticker on it.

Sometimes the cage in front of you is a second one at the same spot. Tap **It is a different box**. The sheet offers the next letter, like G78B. Tap {{button:blue|Save box G78B}}. The sticker still reads G78, and a later scan says so.

Two stickers can sit on one box. Both open it. A sticker printed with the box's own name adds nothing beside the box's name. A sticker printed with another name reads *sticker reads G78* there. A box's own label from before still opens it too.

If someone else put the sticker on a box a moment ago, the sheet says who and when. Tap **Open R76** to see that box. A sticker printed for another job asks once. Use it here, and it belongs to the box it lands on. A sticker on a box of a job you are not on says so. Ask the office to add you to that job.

Did you stick on a whole roll of named stickers? Tap **These are on boxes** under the boxes. Type the names you used, like `G78-150`. Pick how the boxes sit and their place. Tap {{button:blue|Put 73 stickers on their boxes}}. Each name gets its box with its sticker on it. Scan one later to add what is inside.

Placing a sticker needs signal. With none, the press is grey and the sheet says so. Nothing is kept for later, because two phones might hold the same sticker. Try again when the phone has a bar.

Under the boxes, a line counts the job's reserved stickers, the ones not yet on a box. When one run holds them, the line names them, such as G92 to G200. Scan one to say which box it is on.

## Print a tape label

The Labels window asks first what the labels print on. Pick **Brother tape** for the P-touch, or **Paper sheet** for any printer. Each box becomes one strip of tape with the QR, the stencil and the code. The address prints under the stencil on two lines. The second line is the box's code. A short name makes a short strip, so a run uses less tape.

A label prints the letter and number alone. A box named G12 COPPER 90 prints as G12, so the label stays true when the box holds something else. A name with no letter and number prints whole. A line under the boxes says which labels print shorter.

The app prints straight to the P-touch from Chrome or Edge on a computer. There is nothing to install. The first time, the window lists three steps. Plug the P-touch in with its USB cable and switch it on. Quit Brother's P-touch Editor if it is open. Press **Connect the P-touch** and pick the printer in the browser's list. The browser remembers it.

Once it is connected, the window names the printer and the tape inside it. Press **Print 1 test strip** before a whole run. It prints the first ticked box alone. Check that the strip is cut right and the code scans. Then press **Print 3 tape labels**. The strips print one at a time and the printer cuts each one. A run wastes one blank square of tape at its start, however many strips it holds. Print strips together to save tape.

While a run prints, the window names the box and the strip, as in G2, strip 2 of 5. Press **Stop printing** to end a run early. One more strip prints, so the printer can cut the tape. The boxes that are done are unticked, and the window names them. A box that was part way keeps its place, and only its missing strips print. Press the print press again to print the rest. If the printer stops by itself, the window keeps the rest the same way.

Did the tape run out part way? Put in a new cassette and close the cover. The window checks the printer by itself every few seconds. When it reads ready, press the print press, and it prints the rest. You can press **Check the printer again** if you do not want to wait. The window remembers the run for the rest of the day. If you close it to change the tape, it opens on the rest. Press **Every box** to start over.

Safari and Firefox cannot reach a USB printer. There the Brother tape choice says Needs Chrome or Edge. Press **Copy the link to this page** and paste it into Chrome or Edge. Brother makes no printer driver for a Mac, so the print window cannot print tape on one.

On Windows with Brother's driver, you can press **Use the print window instead**. After the first print it sits under **Printer setup**. In the print window, pick the P-touch and the paper that matches your tape, 18 mm or 24 mm.

Untick a box to leave it out. **None** clears them all and **Every box** picks them all again. That is the quick way to print one new cage's strip. For a run of boxes in a row, pick the first box in **From** and the last box in **to**. Every box between the two is ticked. A count beside them says how many boxes that is.

A box with more than one side needs more than one strip. Set **Copies of each** with the minus and plus presses. With 2, the strips come out as 1, 1, 2, 2, 3, 3. The print press names the total, as in **Print 6 tape labels**. The test strip is always one strip. The presses sit at the bottom of the window and stay in reach as you scroll.

After the first print the window opens on tape on that computer. Press **Change** to pick paper again. Press **Printer setup** to read the three steps again.

Printing at the cage from Brother's phone app? On a box, press **More** and then **Copy the label code**. The address goes on the clipboard. Paste it into the Brother app as a QR code and print. The office can also press **Export the labels as a CSV** in the Labels window. That file has one row per box for a label editor.

Signed out, the scan still opens a page. The page does not say what is in the box. The top is a dark plate with the company's name. Under the name is the box's name in large letters, with its code beside it. The page never names the job.

Want a price from us? Press **Ask for a bid**. Your phone opens an email to the office that says which box you scanned. The press beside it says **Call** and the office's number. Press it to call.

Is the box moved, damaged or in the way? Press **Moved, damaged or in the way?** and a small window opens. Press **Send a text** or **Send an email**. Your phone opens it with the box's name already in it, ready for a photo. Looking for work? Press **Work with us** for the same two choices. Press **Not now** to close the window.

To see what is in the box, press **Sign in to see what is inside**. The press has an orange outline. After you sign in you land on that box, with its parts and counts. You sign in once in a phone's browser. After that a scan opens the box's own screen.

A label from a retired box says so and offers **Scan another label**. Signed in, it names the box and the day it left. Open prts.co with nothing after it and you get **Scan a label**. There you can type a code.

A box can also carry a manifest, a laminated page that lists what is in it. On a box, press **More** and then **Print the manifest**. The manifest window opens right there. Write what you add on the page's ruled lines, and press it in the app too.

## Change a box's name or kind

A name typed wrong, or a pile saved as a cage, is fixed on the box. Open the box and tap {{button:outline|More}} beside its name. Then tap **Edit A1**. On the office page, press a box's name or the **Edit** press beside it.

- **Its name** takes a new name. A name another box on the site carries is refused.
- **How it sits** is Cage, Box or Loose pile. A pallet is a loose pile, and so is a thing too big for a box.
- **What is in it** takes your own words for what the box holds. Clear it and the app writes the line from the parts again.
- **Where exactly** says where it stands, such as on a pallet by the north door.
- **Which place on site** moves the box to another place, or to a new one you type.

The press says what it will do, such as **Rename to G70** or **Save G70**.

A label printed with the old name still opens the box, because its code never changes. Only the large words are old. Print the label again to show the new name.

The window has two tabs. **Cage** holds those fields, the box's code and its history: when it was added, each move and each rename. **Contents** lists each part in the box. Under a part is the day it came in, who added it and where the box stood then. Open the fold under a part to read every count, use and move of it.

A part's name typed wrong is fixed on **Contents**. Tap **Fix the name** under the part, type the right name and press **Rename to 3/4 pex**. The name changes in every box on the job that holds that part. The window says which boxes before you press. The counts and the history stay as they are. **Undo the rename** puts the old name back. A part from the parts book has no such press, since its name is the book's. Fixing a name needs a signal.

Rename a box only when the same things are inside. For different things, retire the box and add a new one. Last week's count then still means what it meant.

## Retire a box

When a cage or box leaves the job, open it and press **More**. **Retire G4** is the last row. The app refuses while parts are still in it. Move them or mark them used first.

Retired by mistake? The toast that says G4 retired carries **Undo** for one minute. Press it and G4 is back with its label working. After the minute, the office brings it back from the Retired fold on Jobs → Materials.
