GC Review opens on its **This week** tab, one list of every GC with a balance. Each row has three steps: check the bills, send the statement, and write down the word.

A GC is the general contractor who owes you. The three steps are **Check** the bills, **Send** the statement, and write down the **Word**. The word is where the GC stands. Whoever is at the keyboard works every row. The GCs are grouped by the **account man** who knows them. So one call to him covers his group. The app never emails a GC on its own initiative. It emails only when a person presses **Send statement** or sets up a schedule.

## The two Pipeline cards

On **Jobs → Pipeline**, Today's Money Opportunities shows the week's two stages:

:::example Bills to check
🔏 **5 GC statements wait on sign-off — $154,166** — check each GC’s bills and its statement is ready to send. {{button:outline-blue|Check in GC Review →}}
:::

:::example Statements to send
📬 **Statements to send — 3 GCs, $103,450** — checked and ready · grouped by the account man to ask. {{button:outline-blue|Open the list →}}
:::

Both cards count every GC owing $10,000 or more, whoever its account man is. They show to everyone in the office. Both open GC Review on the week's list.

## Where the week stands

You open **GC Review** from the Billed Awaiting Payment section tools. Pinned at the top is the **stage track**. It shows the week's three steps and Done, each saying how many GCs are waiting there:

:::example The stage track
{{chip:blue|YOU ARE HERE}} **① Check** · 1 to check · 13/14 → **② Send** · 10 to send · 3/14 → **③ Word** · 1 word due · 2/9 → **Done** · 2 of 14 · due Wed
:::

- A GC is at one stage at a time: the next thing it needs. **You are here** sits on the first stage with a GC still waiting. It moves on by itself when that stage empties.
- The bar under a step is how far along it is. *13/14* is thirteen of fourteen GCs checked. A finished step turns green. The word counts only the GCs owing $10,000 or more.
- **Press a stage to see only the GCs waiting there**, with what they owe together. You press it again, or **Show all 14**, to get the whole list back. The filter clears when you close GC Review. So you never open to a list that is missing GCs.
- The track stays in place while the list scrolls. So does **Total outstanding**, at the bottom, beside **Include Collections**.

## The week's list

Under the track, **one row per GC**:

:::example One group, three rows
**Account Man Malachi** · 3 GCs · $96,000 — 2 to do
› Loberg Contracting {{chip:gray|no word yet}} · $22,000 · 4 jobs · oldest 19d — ◉ Check · ○ Send · ○ Word {{button:blue|Check bills}}
› Knight Contracting {{chip:yellow|warm · Sep 18}} · $26,000 · 4 jobs · oldest 41d — ✓ Checked · ◉ Send · ○ Word {{button:blue|Send}}
› Structura {{chip:yellow|warm · Sep 25}} · $48,000 · 12 jobs · oldest 58d — ✓ Checked · ✓ Sent Thu · ✓ warm {{chip:green|✓ Done}}
:::

- **Account Man Malachi** holds the GCs Malachi is the account man for. That is the standing pick on the GC, else the account manager on most of its jobs. Your own read ***Account Man Taunya (you)***. A GC with neither sits under the **leader**. Its opened row says *the leader, by default*, and you tap **pick an account man** there. The choice sticks week to week. GCs owing under $10,000 sit in their account man's section like any other. They are marked *word optional* in the heading. You check and send them. The word is optional. Nothing is nobody's. A GC that owes only in Collections has a statement and no steps. It sits under **Nothing to check this week** with the **Not billed to a GC** bucket.
- **The three dots are the GC's steps**: green behind it, blue where it is, grey ahead. The chip beside the name is the newest word from the last six weeks. With none, it reads *no word yet*.
- **The blue button is the next step.** {{button:blue|Check bills}} opens the checklist of that GC's bills. {{button:blue|Send}} opens the statement email, headed *Email statement to* and the GC's name. {{button:blue|Get the word}} opens the form for what was said. The dots are doors too. You press **Word** on a GC that has not been sent yet to take the word first.
- **Send stays grey until the bills are checked.** A group that changes after sign-off shows {{button:amber|Re-check}}. Share's **Draft Message**, **Copy** and **Print** wait for the check too. Until then the menu reads *Check the bills first — a statement never goes out unchecked*. A GC that owes only in Collections has nothing to check. Its Share works at once.
- **Click a row to open it.** Inside is a table of its bills. Each line has the job, the day it was billed and its age. Each line also has what is still open and its lien waivers. On a phone, each bill is a short card. The job and what is still open come first, then the address, the date and its waivers. What paid each bill shows in the **Check bills** list and on the call sheet. The GC reads the same line on the statement and on the portal. The row also holds the statement's chips: last sent, certified and the temperature. A pays by chip joins them when a date was given. There is {{button:outline|Share}}, which holds Draft Message, Copy, Print, Print unpaid invoices and **Find a check…**. Share also holds **Mark sent / spoke with them…** and **Copy portal link**. And there are the row's links: **change account man**, **or mark sent**, **undo**. You click the row again to fold it away.
- Sent it another way, by a text, your own inbox, or in person? You open the row and tap **or mark sent**. The link shows once the bills are checked, until a statement is out.
- **undo** in an opened row clears that week's mark, the statement and the word together.
- {{chip:red|promised Sep 20 — 7 days late}} means the GC gave a pay date and it has passed. They still owe. Those rows carry a red edge and sit at the top of their group. You call them first. After them come the GCs at the earliest stage. The day itself is not late. The day after is. On the row the promise is red text. The opened row shows it as this chip, and the Temperature board as *Sep 20 · 7d late*.

## Find a check

The GC is on the phone: *"what did you put #48211 against?"* You open the row and tap {{button:outline|Share}} → **Find a check…**. You type the number, the amount or the day it came in.

:::example The answer, as a sentence
**Check #48211 · $18,400.00 · received Sep 24, 2026 (mailed Sep 19)**
Applied now to $6,400.00 on 210 Maple Ct · 1058 Maple Ct, Invoice 1 of 1, which it paid in full and $12,000.00 on 4410 Oak Ridge Dr · 1041 Oak Ridge Ph 2, Invoice 2 of 3.
*$12,000.00 moved from 210 Maple Ct · 1058 Maple Ct to 4410 Oak Ridge Dr · 1041 Oak Ridge Ph 2 on Sep 26*
:::

- **Where it sits now, one line per job**, then how it got there. A payment moved to another job after it was recorded shows the move.
- **Before you type, the newest checks** show, often the one they are asking about.
- **A check recorded without its number** wears a {{chip:yellow|no number}} chip. It is found by its amount or its day. You add the number on the payment, on the job window's **Bill** tab. The next search finds it by number.
- A bill the owner pays on a GC's job is never counted as the GC's money.
- **Grouped By Development**, a development's {{button:outline|Share}} has **Find a check…** too. It reads every payment on the development's jobs, whoever paid it. The owner's checks are on it as well as the GC's.
- {{button:outline|🖨 Print the sheet}} opens *where your checks were applied* as a PDF in a new tab. It covers the last twelve months. Print it or save it from that tab. It lists every payment, where it sits now, and what moved. It lists what came in and is not yet on a bill. It shows where each job stands: billed, paid by, last applied and still open. Retainage held shows too when a job has any. Retainage is money the GC holds back until the end. The open total matches the statement. **show all** widens it to every payment on record. {{button:outline|CSV}} downloads the same rows, one per applied line. That is for a bookkeeper who reconciles in a spreadsheet.

## The word

The word is the account man's read of the GC. You can take it in any order, before the statement goes out or after. You tap {{button:outline|Word}}:

1. **Whose word is this?** The GC's account man is already picked. Change it to someone else, or to ***Mine — I talked to Knight Contracting*** when you reached the GC yourself.
2. **How did you hear it from Malachi?** Pick {{chip:gray|Email}} {{chip:blue|Text}} {{chip:gray|Call}} {{chip:gray|In person}} {{chip:gray|Other}}. Text is picked to start.
3. **Their temperature**, the sentence, and the date they said they would pay.

:::example What the record says
{{chip:yellow|warm · Thu · Malachi}} — entered by Taunya. His read shows as his on the GC's row, the Temperature board and What went out; your name stays beside it as the person who wrote it down.
:::

A word written after the statement keeps the statement's day and how it went out. A statement sent after the word keeps the read, its sentence and the pay date.

## One call, one sheet

Seven of Malachi's GCs are seven questions for one phone call. You tap {{button:outline-blue|📞 Call sheet}} on his group:

:::example The sheet
**Knight Contracting** · $26,000 · oldest 41d · {{chip:red|promised Sep 20 — 7 days late}}
Last word Sep 18 · warm · Malachi · “Check run is the 20th.”
{{chip:gray|Hot}} {{chip:gray|Warm}} {{chip:blue|Cool}} {{chip:gray|Cold}} · *Missed the 20th, now says the 10th.* · Oct 10
:::

- The GCs that broke a promise come first, then the ones with no word yet.
- **The bills behind each total are one click away.** Under a GC's total, you tap ***▸ 18 bills on 4 jobs · 1 over 90 days, $4,421.26***. The bills open under the answer, so what you are typing stays in view. {{button:outline|Show all bills}} at the top opens every GC's at once.
- You fill a row as he answers: the temperature, the sentence, the date. **A row you leave blank is left alone.** A row you start needs both a temperature and a sentence. A date of today or later goes on every bill that GC owes, as a promise.
- **no change** repeats the last read and its pay date for a quiet week. It is allowed once. After a "no change", the next word needs a fresh sentence.
- At the bottom, **Whose word** is already the account man. You pick how you heard it. {{button:blue|Save 3 answers}} writes them all.
- {{button:outline|🖨 Print sheet}} prints the list with room to write, for a call away from the desk.

Saving a word never marks a statement sent.

:::example A GC's bills, opened
**813 · Reliant Health- Electrical** · billed Jun 25, 2026 · 95d {{chip:red|said Sep 15 · late}} **$4,421.26** ▾
**898 · Reliant Health** · billed Jul 6, 2026 · 84d **$3,600.00** ▾
**18 bills — what Knight Contracting owes** · **$26,000.00**
:::

- The bills come to the total on the row. A bill 90 days old or more reads red.
- **Dig in without losing the sheet.** The job's name opens the job on top: its bill, its costs, its history. Closing it brings you back to the sheet with everything you typed still there. The **▾** at the end of a line drops that job's latest activity under it.
- {{chip:red|said Sep 15 · late}} is the date that job was promised, red once it has passed.
- Bills in **Collections** are listed under the others, apart. They are owed, and they are not in the row's total. The week's round counts active bills only. That is why a GC's total on the sheet can be less than on its row in GC Review. That happens while **Include Collections** is ticked.

### Where the answers go

{{button:blue|Save 3 answers}} writes each answered GC's **word for this week**. That is the temperature, the sentence and the pay date. It is also whose word it is, how you heard it, and that you entered it. Nothing else on the sheet is saved. A row you left blank is not touched. From then on the word shows:

- on the GC's row in GC Review, as the chip beside its name and its green **Word** dot
- on the **Temperature** tab and in **What went out**
- on the next call sheet, as *Last word*
- and a pay date that is today or later goes on each of that GC's bills as a promise. See *The date goes on their bills*.

A GC has one word a week. Saving again for the same GC this week replaces it. **undo** in the GC's opened row clears the word. It also clears a statement marked sent that week. A pay date already filed on the bills stays on them.

## No call at all: ask by link

When the account man is on a job site, you send him a link instead of phoning. On his group, you tap {{button:outline-blue|🔗 Ask by link}}:

1. Press {{button:blue|Make the link}}. It asks only about his GCs owing $10,000 or more with **no word in this week**. Nothing is sent yet.
2. {{button:blue|Copy a text for Malachi}} copies a short message with the link. Paste it into a text. Or {{button:outline|Email it to Malachi}} sends it from the app. Or use {{button:outline|Copy link}}.
3. He opens it on his phone. **No sign-in.** For each GC he sees what it owes, the last word and any broken promise. He answers with a temperature, a sentence and a pay date.
4. His group then shows {{button:green|Malachi answered 3 — review}}. It opens the call sheet with his answers filled in. Read them, change what needs changing, and press {{button:blue|Save 3 answers}}.

:::example What the record says
{{chip:blue|cool · Tue · Malachi}} — entered by Taunya. What went out lists it as *Personal · other*.
:::

- **Nothing is on the record until you save.** His answers wait for you. A row you clear stays waiting.
- The button on his group reads **His link** once there is one. You hover it to see where it stands: *asked Tue · emailed · not opened yet*.
- The link shows what his GCs owe, so it is for him only. It stops working after eight days. **turn the link off** kills it sooner. **new link** replaces it if it went to the wrong person. Answers he already gave are kept.
- Your own accounts have no link. You answer those yourself.

## Marking a statement sent

{{button:blue|Send}} on a row drafts the statement in the app. Sending it marks the GC sent for you. When it went out another way, you open the row and tap **or mark sent**. You pick **how**: {{chip:gray|Email}} {{chip:blue|Text}} {{chip:gray|Call}} {{chip:gray|In person}} {{chip:gray|Other}}. Text is picked to start. You add an optional note, then press {{button:blue|Save mark}}. Either way it counts as a statement sent everywhere the app keeps score. That is the GC's last-sent pill and the stage track at the top of GC Review. It is also the Dashboard's Wednesday "GC review is due" card and its badge.

To paste the statement into your own email instead, you open the GC's row and use **Share → Copy**. Then you mark it sent.

## Where the week finds you

You do not have to go looking for it:

- **Dashboard.** Checked GCs owing $10,000 or more may be waiting on their statement, whoever their account man is. Then the Needs you card shows {{chip:blue|2 GC statements are ready to send}}. Its **Open the list** button opens GC Review on the week's list.
- **Email.** In GC Review, on the **Scheduled** tab, you tap {{button:outline|Email me the week’s list…}}. You pick the weekday mornings you want it, the Mon to Fri chips, and a time. You press {{button:blue|Save}}. Each morning you get the week's GC statements as they stand at send time. Everyone gets the same email. The standard sits up top: a GC should never be surprised by what they owe us. The office checks the bills and sends every statement. The account man's word keeps the record current. Then come the groups. *Your accounts* is first, then one group per account man, like *Call Malachi*, then *No account man yet*. Each GC shows its one next step. That is check the bills, send the statement, get the word, or done for the week. It also shows a promise, the oldest bill, the dollars over 90 days and the last word. When the next step is Send, the AP contact shows too. AP is accounts payable, the person who pays their bills. Each group has one button, like {{button:blue|Open Malachi’s call sheet →}}. The email ends with the week's Friday deadline. {{button:outline|Preview}} shows today's email over the window, with **← Back** to return. {{button:outline|Email me a test}} sends a [TEST] copy. **Edit** and **Stop emailing** sit in the same box. The email also lists under {{icon:gear}} **Settings → Your account → My email schedule** with your other emails. Any office person can set it up for someone else, with **Set it up for someone else…** below it.

:::example Nothing waiting?
The email still comes, saying so — a quiet morning shouldn't look like a broken subscription.

## Spoke with them, but no statement went out?

Sometimes the right move this week is a conversation, not another statement. You tap {{button:outline|Word}} on the GC's row, and the form opens on ***Spoke with them · no statement***. **Share → Mark sent / spoke with them…** inside the opened row opens the same form on **Statement sent**. You switch it there. The form asks how: call, text, in person. Then it asks the question that matters: **What's their temperature?**

- You pick one. {{chip:green|Hot · pay date in hand}} or {{chip:yellow|Warm · fine, no date}}. Or {{chip:blue|Cool · dodging the date}} or {{chip:red|Cold · disputing or upset}}.
- **When did they say they'll pay?** sits right under the temperature. You tap the answer they gave: {{chip:gray|This Fri · Oct 2}} {{chip:gray|Next Fri · Oct 9}} {{chip:gray|End of Oct}}. Or you pick any day. When they gave a date before and it is still ahead, the first chip is {{chip:gray|Still Oct 9}}. No date is fine. You pick **Hot** without one and the form reminds you that hot means they gave a date.
- Then you answer in a sentence, not a word. For example: "Warm, Dave says the check run is the 10th". The app will not save a blank or a one-worder. The sentence is the whole point.

### The date goes on their bills

A date you save here is a real promise. It is the same record as a date entered from the Billed row's dates block. It shows there as the *They said Oct 3* row. It moves the money in the payment forecast. It counts toward whether this GC keeps its word. See *know whether a customer keeps their word*.

:::example Under the date
**Goes on all 4 bills · $27,499.49** {{button:outline|Choose bills}}
*1 bill had a different date — that promise goes on record as broken · 1 already says Oct 9*
:::

- The date covers **every bill the GC owes** unless you say otherwise. {{button:outline|Choose bills}} opens the list. You untick the ones the date does not cover.
- A bill that already carries that date is left alone. So saving the same word twice never counts as a second promise.
- A bill that carried a **different** date gets the new one. The earlier promise stays on record as broken. The form tells you how many before you save.
- Above the form, a line says what they told you last time. It reads {{chip:green|They said Oct 9 · 2 of 4 bills}}. It turns red once it has passed with money still owed.
- The same holds for **Statement sent** with a date, and for the call sheet. A date typed on a row goes on all of that GC's bills.

:::example What it counts as
A "spoke with them" mark fills the row's Word step for the week — {{chip:green|✓ warm}} — and leaves Send waiting. It never counts as a statement sent: the last-statement date keeps aging, and the office's "sent" count doesn't move.
:::

**The guardrail.** You talk to a GC two weeks running with no statement in between. Then the Temperature board shows a red line under the last word: *⚠ spoken to 2 weeks running · no statement since Aug 11*. Conversations are fine. A GC that never sees a written statement is exactly the surprise the standard exists to prevent.

## Review it: the Temperature board

On GC Review's **Temperature** tab, the **Temperature board** lists every GC in the round, cold first. Each row shows the account man, the current read and six weeks of dots. A grey dot means nobody talked to them that week. The row also shows the last word with who said it, and the pay date. There are three things to look for. Who is going cold. Whose dots are drifting the wrong way. And the **no read** rows, with no temperature in six weeks. When only statements went out, the last word reads *Statements only*. You click a GC's name for its full send history.

## Sent it another way? Mark it from the GC

Not every statement goes out in the round, or by email. You may have texted a GC their statement, walked one over, or talked it through on a call. Then you open the GC's row and use **Share → Mark sent / spoke with them…**. The same form opens on **Statement sent**, with Text picked. The mark counts everywhere a send counts: the last-sent pill, the week's progress, the Dashboard nudges. It works for any GC, including ones under $10,000, whose word is optional.

:::example What gets kept
Every mark keeps **who** marked it, **when**, **how** it went out, and the **note** — and for the word, **whose** it is and who entered it — for posterity. One mark per GC per week holds both the statement and the word; whichever you record second keeps the first.
:::

## Seeing what was sent before — "What went out"

The **last sent Aug 27** pill sits in each GC's opened row. It names the channel when this week's mark is what it shows, like *Sent Sep 4 · text*. You click it for **What went out**. The temperature pill and the GC's name on the Temperature board open it too. It lists every statement on record for that GC, newest first, whichever way it went. Each shows the **lane** it took, who sent it, and the details.

Aug 27 · Spoke · **Personal · call** · Malachi · warm · pays 2026-09-10

## The three lanes

A statement reaches a GC one of three ways. Every surface names the lane the same way. **Personal** is your own inbox, a text, or in person, recorded with **or mark sent**. **Draft Message** means the app sends it for you when you press **Send statement**. **Scheduled send** is one the app sends at a set time, once or every week. You set it with **Schedule…** in the statement email. GC Review's **Scheduled** tab lists the third lane. **What went out** above shows all three for one GC.

## The Scheduled tab

On GC Review's **Scheduled** tab, **Scheduled statement sends** lists every scheduled statement the office has set up. The tab shows while a send is waiting or a word is due. That is the standing *All GCs → Malachi · Mon · Wed · 7:00 AM · weekly* copies and any per-GC schedule. It shows for everyone who can open GC Review, not just the person who created them. Each line says when it goes and, when it is not yours, **by whom**. Only the person who scheduled a send, or a dev, sees its {{button:outline|Cancel}}. You ask them if a chain needs to stop.

:::example Nobody gets the same statement twice by accident
If a statement already went to the same address a few minutes ago — by any lane — {{button:blue|Send statement}} in Draft Message tells you so instead of sending again ("already went to ap@knight.com 4 minutes ago"). A scheduled send that would repeat one from the same half-day is skipped, and its weekly chain carries on next week. A scheduled send waits for the check as well. If the GC's bills are not checked this week, or changed since, it does not go. Its weekly chain still carries on.
:::

## Good to know

- Nothing goes out that a person did not ask for. You send it yourself, press **Send statement**, or set the schedule.
- **A $0 statement can't be sent.** When a GC's certified group owes nothing, Draft Message says ***Nothing owed — no statement goes out.*** The Send button stays off. **Schedule…** still works. A weekly chain simply skips the weeks the GC is at $0.
- Each group's header, like *Account Man Malachi · 3 GCs · $96,000.00*, ends with how many are still to do. That is how you see Friday afternoon what is still open. It also says whose GCs they are.
- Each opened GC row carries its portal globe. **Share → Copy portal link** and the statement's portal card point the GC at their live statement. The card itself says **"Pay online any time at …"**. It is the same card in **Share → Copy** and in the statement email's {{button:outline|Preview}}. Sent and scheduled statements carry it too.
- Every office role sees every scheduled send in GC Review. **Cancel** stays with whoever scheduled it, or a dev. Your own Settings → My email schedule still lists only sends you requested or that are addressed to you.
- The **Draft Message** dialog and scheduled sends still exist for the GCs where an app-sent statement is fine. Three things count as "that GC got their statement this week". The first is **or mark sent** or **Share → Mark sent / spoke with them…**. The second is **Draft Message**. The third is a scheduled send addressed to that one GC. Two things never do. They are ***Spoke with them · no statement***, and the office's own "All GCs" whole-report copies. Those are for you, not the GC. On Wednesday the Dashboard's GC review item turns green only when every GC with a balance is done. Done means certified and sent one of the three ways.
