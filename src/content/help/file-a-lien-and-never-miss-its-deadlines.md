When a demand letter does not shake the money loose, Texas gives you a lien. But only if the paperwork happens in the right order, on the right dates.

A lien is a legal claim on the property for unpaid work. The job's Lien window walks all of it, and the app watches every deadline. Its title reads *Liens on job* and the job's number. It opens from the orange lien icon on Billed and Collections rows.

## The clock, always visible

The top of the window draws the job's steps on one line, with today marked. The steps are last work, the § 53.056 notice and the § 53.057 retainage notice. The § 53.056 notice is the Texas form we send the owner and the GC for each unpaid month. Press a stop's title to see what that stop sends. A stop that already went out shows the paper as it went out. Under **What went out**, a card that is missing something has a press. It takes you to where that piece is entered. Then come the § 53.052 affidavit, serving it, and the year to sue. Each step shows its date and how many days are left. The § 53.056 notice step counts on subcontractor jobs only. Weekends roll forward automatically. Above the line, a tinted band names the next step in bold, with a mark for how it stands. On the Lien desk the band ends in a press that does the step. In this window it ends in the window's own door. A door that goes to the Lien desk opens the desk on this job's paper. When the move is not ours, the band says who it waits on. Opened from a Pipeline row or from the Lien desk, it names the step a notice is on there. It names an affidavit's step the same way. The switch in the corner reads **Steps** or **Windows**. Windows is a calendar of every paper, the closed windows too, from first day to last. On a phone the steps fold to one strip. Press it to see them. The same clock powers the Dashboard cards below. On **Jobs → Pipeline**, every Billed and Collections row carries the same date under its money bar. That is the **lien runway**. It names the next lien step and its last day, against the day the customer is expected to pay. So the row says whether the money lands before the lien dies. On a sub job with no recorded notice the runway puts the § 53.056 notice date first. It shows as a hollow flag. See *read the Pipeline's money view*.

On a phone the window fills the screen. Scroll the paper and the top of the window folds to its title line, so the paper has the room. Scroll back to the paper's top and the rest returns. The arrow beside **§ Rules** folds the top and brings the top back by hand. On a small phone the window opens with its top folded.

**What the clock keys on.** Each month worked gets its own notice date. The affidavit's date counts from the job's **last work month**. That is the month of the job's last work date, which is the latest **approved clock session** on the job. The app does not track which month each dollar was billed in. So it uses that one month as the basis. The notice's *months covered* is what you attest. Two consequences follow. A job with no approved clock sessions is dated from its bill. Each sent, unpaid bill gives a month, and the latest is the last month. A bill is not a record of work. A bill is sometimes written before the job is done, and sometimes weeks after. So the app says *dated from the bill · no clock hours* wherever that month shows. An affidavit on such a month carries a line that says so. Its help sheet and its notice record ask about the month too. Nothing is held. A job with no sent, unpaid bill is dated from the month it was created. Approve the hours first, or set the last day of work by hand on the Lien desk. If the crew goes back for a day, the clock moves to that later month. A last day of work set by hand is the exception. The day you set counts. Hours approved after that day do not move the clock. The Lien window says those hours are there. To count them, press **change ›** under **Last work**. Set a later day there, or go back to the clock hours.

**When the month from the bill is right.** The Lien desk asks on the affidavit pane: *Is August the month the work was done?* If it is, press **Yes, August is right ›**. Type who said so and when. Then press **Confirm August**. The app saves your name and the day on the job. No date moves and no paper changes. The flag turns quiet on the pane, the Lien window, the help sheet and the notice record. The help sheet's **Work month.** row reads ✓. If the work ran in another month, press **Set the last day of work ›** instead. That door moves every clock. Press **Change ›** on the quiet line to change the note. A last day of work set later puts the question back.

**The kind comes from the job's own record.** Every date on the line turns on the property's kind, residential or commercial. The window reads the property record linked to the job as it opens. Until the record is in, the top of the window reads *Reading the job’s property record…* with no dates. So the window agrees with the **Property record** row on Edit Job and with the Legal desk. A job with no linked record gets the residential dates, the earlier ones.

## Step 1 — the § 53.056 notice (sub jobs only)

If the job has a GC, unpaid work months need a **notice of claim**. GC means the general contractor. The notice is the statute's own form, filled in by the app. It goes to **both** the owner of record and the GC. It is due by the 15th of the 2nd month after the work on residential jobs. On commercial jobs it is the 3rd month.

- Print it for certified mail. Or pick **email** as a recipient's method and the app sends the PDF for you and keeps the send receipt.
- This tab prints a set of pages for each envelope. The press says how many, as {{button:outline-blue|Print 2 envelopes}}. A divider page sits in front of each set. It shows the envelope's face, with the name and the address. It counts the documents behind it and has a box for the tracking number. Pull the divider out before you seal the envelope. It stays on the desk.
- No signature prints from this tab. Have each set signed in ink.
- An envelope with no mailing address is held. The press then reads *Print 1 envelope · 1 held*, and the line under it gives the reason.
- Lost a set? Press {{button:outline|Reprint envelope 2 · the original contractor}} under the press. It prints that set alone behind its divider.
- Is the job's notice already approved on the Lien desk? An amber note over the buttons says so. Print that one from the desk. The desk's print carries the leader's signature and a set of pages for each envelope. Press **Open it on the Lien desk ›** in the note to go there.
- A help sheet prints first. It says what the app checked on the notice. That is the two mailing addresses, the claim, the signature and the invoices. The last day to mail is printed large. Beside it the sheet counts the days left, as *4 days from now*. It reads *MUST MAIL TOMORROW* or *MAIL BY END OF DAY* when the day is close. The sheet has a box for each envelope. Match each divider to its box. It lists the steps from the printer to the mail. Scan the code marked **Scan to record** with a phone. It opens this tab, where {{button:amber|Save & record sends…}} is. Untick **Print the help sheet first** to leave the sheet off. The sheet is never mailed and never filed on the job.
- **Enclose the invoice** is on by default when the job has unpaid bills. The statute lets the notice include the invoice. The owner learns exactly what to withhold from the GC. The notice's reference strip says it is enclosed. It prints after the notice and rides the emailed PDF, stamped INVOICE. Between the notice and the invoices sits the **pay codes** page. It has one QR code per unpaid Stripe bill under *Once these bills are paid, there will be no lien filed.* Each opens the bill's own `clicktooling.com/pay/…` address. That address is good until the bill is paid, however old the letter. Untick *Enclose the invoice* and the page leaves with the invoices.
- {{button:amber|Save & record sends…}} captures a method and tracking number **per recipient**. That is what lets the affidavit, the sworn lien statement, later swear the notices went out. It also captures a **Saved copy**: a Drive link to the paper as sent, with a note. The same two boxes sit on the affidavit's *Record filing* and the release's record. Every recorded row under *Filings on this job* has a block named **What went out**. It lists each file with its own link. Under the files, each envelope shows its number and its receipt. A chip counts the proof on file. Press {{button:outline|Add the receipt}} there to add a receipt after the record is saved. The row itself offers *change the saved copy*. When nobody kept one yet it offers *link the saved copy* instead.
- Each copy sent on paper has {{button:outline|Add the receipt}}. It takes a photo or a PDF. On a phone the press asks first. Pick **Take a photo** or **Choose a photo or a PDF**. The file goes to the job's Drive folder. The app reads the number off a photo's barcode and fills the box. Check it against the receipt. A PDF is not read, so type its number in the box. A file that does not go up says why under the press. Press {{button:outline|Try again}} to send the same file. A saved receipt has **Open**, **Replace** and **Take off**. Take off asks first, and the wrong file goes to Drive's trash. Drive keeps it there 30 days. Press {{button:outline|Put it back}} if you change your mind. Replace sends the old file to the trash too. A recorded notice shows *receipt ›* beside each copy that has one.

- {{button:outline|Already sent — record it…}} is for a notice printed here and mailed by hand. You record when, how, to whom, the claim and months as printed, and the saved copy. You also record the other unpaid jobs at the property the one paper covered. It makes one record per job, and the Lien desk reads them as sent.

Jobs where you contracted directly with the owner skip this step. The tab says so.

A § 53.056 notice goes to the **owner of record** at a mailing address. The county appraisal roll already knows both. The appraisal roll is the county's list of who owns each property. So on **Jobs → Pipeline**, the Fix-ups strip shows {{chip:yellow|Owner of record to confirm · 35}}. It shows whenever an open GC job has no confirmed owner on its property record, with or without approved hours. Click it and the list looks every property up on the roll as it opens. It reads *Looking up 12 of 31…* meanwhile. The list is grouped by property and sorted by the first notice due. So one click covers every job at that address, now and later.

## A public property

A mechanic's lien does not attach to public property. A city, a county, a school district or the State owns public property.

When the owner of record reads as a public body, the property record asks once. *Is this public property?* shows on the **Property record** row in Edit Job. The question shows on the Lien desk's owner line too. The papers on the property hold until someone answers.

- {{button:blue|Yes, public property…}} opens a window that takes the property off the lien path.
- {{button:outline|No, it is private}} keeps that answer. The holds open, and the papers can go out.

Only the office answers. Everyone else sees the question without the buttons.

The window lists each job at the address that owes money or has a lien paper. Each line says what already went out on that job. A ticked job also goes on the attorney's list for a bond claim. The attorney's list is the Collections list. A job goes on it only once it is billed. The window says so under any job that is not billed yet.

Write why the property is off the lien path. The reason goes on each ticked job. Pick whether there is a payment bond, if you know. Then press {{button:blue|Take 2 jobs off the lien path ▸}}.

Nothing is filed and nothing is deleted. Every job at the address leaves the lien bins, now and later. A job that could not go on the attorney's list is named, with the reason.

The property record then reads *Public property, answered Nov 16.* To undo it, press **Put it back on the lien path…** there. Say why, and press the button. Every job at the address returns to the Lien desk. The jobs stay on the attorney's list. If the owner still reads as a public body, the property asks once more.

A private answer can be cleared too. Press **Ask again**, and the property asks once more.

## Step 2 — the affidavit, behind its gate

The **Lien affidavit** tab refuses to generate until the paper trail is real. It needs the owner of record with mailing address ✓. The owner cannot be a city, a county, a school district or the State. A lien does not attach to public property, and the line says to ask the attorney about the payment bond. It needs the county and legal description ✓, from the property record. It needs the notice recorded ✓ on sub jobs. On a job with a GC, it needs the GC's address ✓. That address is on the GC's customer record. And it needs **not a homestead**. A homestead lien needs a pre-work contract signed by both spouses and recorded with the county. That is attorney territory the app will not paper over. A house also needs an answer to the homestead question. An unanswered question is not a no. Open the property record and pick **Yes** or **No** under *Is this the owner's homestead?*

The affidavit swears what the job's sent bills still owe. That is the amount the Lien desk shows for the job. Work not billed yet is not claimed until it is billed. A line under **The claim it swears** says how much that is. A claim set by hand on the Lien desk carries to this paper and to the notice. The line names who set it, when and why. To change it, open the job on the Lien desk. See [send lien notices from the Lien desk](/help/send-lien-notices-from-the-lien-desk). On a job you contracted with the owner, the paper says your company is the original contractor.

The affidavit is short. The affidavit says what Texas Property Code § 53.054 lists and little else. That reading is the app's, not a lawyer's. On a job under a GC the affidavit has seven numbered statements. The first three give your company and its mailing address, the trade, the property, and the GC with its address. Is your company somewhere other than where its mail goes? Type that place under **Physical address, if different** in [Settings, Jobs, bills and liens](/settings?tab=settings-jobs&focus=issuer.physicalAddressText). The affidavit then gives both addresses. The last four give the owner with a mailing address, the months, the notices and the claim. On a job you contracted with the owner the affidavit has four statements. The four name no months and no notices.

An amber line can appear under the gate list. Its first words are *Ask the attorney before this is filed*. The line names each month whose notice went late. It also names each month with no copy to the GC on record. The line does not stop the paper. It is a question for your attorney. The line also names the last work month when no notice on record names it. The paper names that month anyway, from the last day on site. The same words print on the help sheet and on the notice record. Your attorney may say the paper should name other months. Then set them by hand. Under **Months the affidavit claims**, press **Set the months by hand ›**. Tick each month the paper names, or add another. Type counsel's reason, with who said it and when. Press **Set N months by hand**. The paper then names those months. The pane, the Lien window and the help sheet say they were set by hand, with the reason. **Change ›** and **Clear ›** take it back.

An amber line can also sit above **Ask Malachi to approve**, the press that names the job's leader. It says the last work month's own notice is still due, and by which day. The paper names that month already, from the last day on site. Record the notice, and the month has one behind it. The line does not stop the draft. The months come from the job and the record. The paper always names the month of the last day on site. That day is the latest approved clock day, or the day set by hand. The paper also names each month a recorded § 53.056 notice names. A month outside the job's own dates of work is left off. The paper gives the day each notice went to the owner and how the notice was sent. Read both lines before you print. A blank there means no notice to the owner is recorded on the job. Record it on the **§ 53.056 notice** tab, and the paper follows.

Nothing prints above the title, with one exception. On a property marked homestead, a bold notice comes first. It reads *NOTICE: THIS IS NOT A LIEN. THIS IS ONLY AN AFFIDAVIT CLAIMING A LIEN.* Texas Property Code § 53.254 asks for that notice, as the app reads it. The county under **STATE OF TEXAS** is the county where you sign. It is not the property's county. The property's county is in the second statement. Beside the print press, **Signed in** holds the county the paper prints on both county lines. It opens on the county Settings hold. Signing somewhere else? Type that county for this print. Press **?** after the box to read what the box does. An empty box says *the notary writes it*. Left blank, the paper prints an empty line there. The help sheet says which county to write. The notary then writes the county by hand.

An affidavit drafted before the short form keeps the earlier form. A grey line under the claim says so. The earlier form has ten statements, and it states the dates of the work. Those dates run from the first approved clock day through the last. To move that paper to the short form, open the job on the Lien desk. On the **Lien affidavits** tab, press **Redraw on the short form…** above the paper. The top of that list has one press for every ready affidavit at once. The affidavit goes back to **To send for Master approval**. The affidavit needs approval again before you file it.

When the gate clears, click {{button:outline-blue|Print for notarization}}. A help sheet prints first. The help sheet says what the app checked on the paper: the owner, the homestead mark and the notices. A line marked **ASK FIRST** goes to the attorney before anyone signs. The help sheet also says what to write on the paper and what to do after the notary. A small picture of the paper shows where each thing is written. The steps have lines for the recording number, the day filed and the day to mail by. The sheet names who to ask: the lien leader on the job, or the dev. A code in the corner opens this guide on a phone. A second code is marked **Scan to record**. It sits beside the steps for after the notary. Scan it with a phone. It opens this job's affidavit tab. There you record the filing and the mail receipts. A phone that is not signed in is asked to sign in first. After the sign-in the app opens that same tab. Do not file the help sheet. Its striped top edge makes it easy to pull from the stack. Untick **Print the help sheet first** to leave it off. New to this paper? Press **Print a guide copy**. The guide copy shows the paper with a numbered mark beside each blank. It says GUIDE COPY across the paper. Never sign or file the guide copy. Sign the paper before a notary. File it with the County Clerk in the property's county. Then click {{button:red|Record filing…}} with the recording number. The **serve-by date stamps itself**. A copy must reach the owner and contractor within 5 days. [Mail the copies within five days](#mail-the-copies-within-five-days) says how to print them. A red Dashboard card nags until you {{button:red|Enter mail numbers…}}. That step takes the day the copies were mailed. It also has a box for each copy's certified mail number. Press **Take a photo of the receipt** beside it. The photo goes to the job's Drive folder, under Sent by us. The number and the photo can wait. Record the day first. Later, press **add the mail receipts** on the affidavit's row under *Filings on this job*. **Record filing…** has a press of its own, **Take a photo of the stamped page**. That photo becomes the filing's saved copy. Did you scan the stamped affidavit instead? Press **Upload the scanned PDF** beside it and pick the file from this computer. The scan goes to the job's Drive folder, under Sent by us. It becomes the filing's saved copy. The file can be 10 MB at most. Scan one affidavit to a file. Did several come back together? [Several scans at once](#several-scans-at-once) says how to add them in one sitting. One PDF of the whole stack works too. Is the filing already recorded? Press **link the saved copy** on its row under *Filings on this job*. Then press **Upload the scanned PDF** there.

A notice record prints next, behind the help sheet. It lists each § 53.056 notice on the job. Each copy has a row under its notice. The row says who the copy went to, the day mailed, the method and the mail number. A copy the record does not hold says *No copy on record*. A note typed beside the saved copy prints under its notice. Type the receipt and the day of delivery there. A notice mailed for several jobs shows its total as *in all*, with this job's part beside it. That total is the notice's. An affidavit claims its own job's amount. A box named **How to read this page** sits above the table. The paper swears two facts about notice. One is the months of work. The other is the day each notice was mailed to the owner. The table is the mail record behind those two facts. A line with a ✓ says *The paper matches the record*, then what the paper swears. That ✓ means the paper and the record say the same thing. That ✓ does not mean the notice went on time. A line that says *The paper and the record differ* gives what each one says. Fix one before the paper is signed. Keep the mail receipts, because they are the proof if the lien is ever questioned. A box named **Missing from the record** lists what to add, such as a mail number. Add it under *Filings on this job*. A line that starts *To add, if mailed* names a copy the record does not hold. Record that copy only if it really went out. The app does not keep the address on an envelope, and the page says so. The page ends with its verdict. **On record** means every month has a notice mailed on time. A grey box that says **Ask the attorney first** names the copy that went late or is missing.

Behind the record prints a copy of each notice. Its delivery record is at the top, with a big check. Large words say how many days ago the notice went. The note on the saved copy closes that box. The copy is the notice's own form, with the words the app kept when it was recorded. It does not show the invoices that went with the notice. It says COPY across the page. Do not file or mail the record or the copies. Their striped top edge makes them easy to pull from the stack. Untick **Print the notice record** to leave them off. A job you contracted with the owner owes no notice, so it has no notice record.

The paper prints with nothing of the browser on it. It should not show the date and time, a web address or a page count. If yours does, open **More settings** in the print window. Untick **Headers and footers** there. The last numbered statement, the signature and the notary's block print together on one page. The short affidavit fits on one sheet. A paper too long for one sheet moves that closing to the next page whole. When that happens, a line under the print buttons says how many pages the paper takes.

Was the lien filed without this tab, by counsel or on paper from elsewhere? The gate does not stop the record. Press {{button:outline|Already filed — record it…}} under the gate list. Type the county, the recording number and the day it was filed. The serve-by day and the year to sue count from that day. A hold never hides **Enter mail numbers…** on a lien that is already on file. The press sits under the gate list until the copies are recorded.

Was a filing recorded on the wrong job, or with the wrong day? Open that job's Lien window. Find the record under **Filings on this job**. Press **Void** on it, then **Confirm void**. The lien's paper goes back to the Lien desk by itself. A paper the leader approved is approved again, so nobody asks him twice. The Board shows it in **Ready to file**, or in **Held** when a fact holds it. A paper nobody had approved goes back to a draft. Then record the lien again with the right facts.

### Several liens back from the clerk

Did a stack of liens come back from the County Clerk together? Record them in one sitting. Open the Lien desk's To do. On the Board, find the bin **Ready to file**. Under its print a line reads *Back from the clerk?* Press {{button:outline|Enter document numbers · 3 ›}} beside it. The press counts the liens in the bin. One window opens. It is named **Back from the clerk: record the filings**.

Set **Filed on** to the day the clerk recorded them. Each lien has one line, with its county clerk and a box. Type the number from the clerk's stamp and press Enter. That lien is recorded as filed at once. The cursor moves to the next lien. Leaving a box records it too. The line turns green and says the day its copies must be mailed by.

A number another lien already holds is not recorded. The line says which lien holds it. A number that is not the county's usual length gets a question. Check it against the stamp. Press **Record it anyway** when it is right. A lien with no number stays in the window for later.

Do you have the clerk's PDFs? Press {{button:outline-blue|Choose the stamped liens}} and pick them all at once. A file named with its job number goes to that lien. A file named *J858 Lien* goes to job 858. A long number in the name is typed in as the document number. Check each one against the stamp. Then press the counted press at the foot, like **Record 3 filings**. Each file is kept as its lien's stamped copy. A file that names no lien here is left out, and a line says why.

One lien can take its file alone. Press **Add the stamped lien** on its line. The file can also follow on a later day.

Was a lien recorded by mistake? Press **Take it back** on its line. The line asks first. Press **Confirm take back**, or **Keep it** to leave the record alone. A take back voids the filing. The lien goes back to **Ready to file**, and its approval stands. The line opens again, so type the right number there.

When the liens are on record, press {{button:blue|Print the copies to mail ›}}. It opens the window for the next step.

## Mail the copies within five days

A filed lien owes a copy of its affidavit to the owner. A job under a GC owes the original contractor a copy too. Send the copies by the fifth day after the filing.

Press {{button:outline-blue|Print the copies to mail}}. It sits under {{button:red|Enter mail numbers…}} on the affidavit tab. Each envelope takes a cover letter and the clerk's stamped copy behind it. The owner's letter and copy come first. The original contractor's come next.

The letter names the county, the day filed, the document number and the amount. It says how to pay and get a release. It makes no threat. The letter goes to the owner's mailing address on the property record today. Untick **Print the cover letter in front of each copy** to mail the copy alone. The copy always prints.

### The copy is the clerk's stamped lien

The copy is the stamped lien saved on the filing. Every page of that file prints, as it was scanned. It shows the clerk's stamp, the signature and the notary's seal. The app draws no copy of its own.

The press reads the saved file first. For a moment it says *Reading the stamped copy…*. Then a line under it says how many pages the stamped copy has.

The print opens as one PDF in a new tab. The print window opens over it. If it does not, print from that tab. Print on one side of the paper. An envelope is often three pages. A two-sided print would put the next letter on the back of a stamped page.

**Counsel has not read the letter.** Counsel has not read the letter's words yet. A line under the press says so. Untick the letter's box to leave the letter out.

### When the press is grey

A line under the press says what is missing. A filing with no recording number is held. Type the number in the box under the press. Then press **Save the number**. A missing address is added on the property record or on the GC's customer record.

A lien with no stamped lien saved is held too. The line reads *no stamped copy saved*. Add the clerk's scan on the job's Lien window. Nothing prints in its place.

A saved copy the app cannot print is held the same way. The line says why. It may be a link typed by hand, or a file the app could not read. It may be a file that is not a PDF, a JPEG or a PNG. Put the stamped scan in its place as a PDF.

Does the saved copy have more than three pages? The app asks before it prints. A stamped lien is usually two pages, so a longer file may hold other liens. Press **Open the saved copy ›** and look. Is it this lien alone? Press **It is this lien alone. Print it**. Does it hold other liens? Put this lien's own scan in its place first.

Was the lien filed without this tab? Its stamped copy still prints. It prints with no cover letter, since the app holds none of the affidavit's words.

The print is kept on the job, with the same pages the envelopes took. Find it on the job's Documents tab under *Sent from this job*. Mail each envelope by certified mail. Then press {{button:red|Enter mail numbers…}} and type each number. Each copy has {{button:outline|Add the receipt}} there too.

### Several liens filed together

Open the Lien desk's To do. On the Board, find the bin *Filed · copies to mail*. It holds every filed lien whose copies are not mailed yet. Press the bin's own press. It reads by where the copies stand. While any lien waits to print, it reads like {{button:outline|Print 12 liens’ copies ›}}. Once they are printed, it reads like {{button:outline|Enter mail numbers · 12 ›}}. With some of each it names both, like {{button:outline|Print 1 · Enter mail numbers 10 ›}}. The list under the Board has the same press, on one row for all of them. One window opens, on the half the press named. It is named **Filed liens: copies to mail**.

One filed lien alone has a row of its own. The row reads *Lien filed · mail its copies by* and the day. Its press reads the same two ways. {{button:outline|Print the copies to mail}} opens that job's Lien window at the press of that name. After the print the row reads {{button:outline|Enter mail numbers}}. That press opens **Filed liens: copies to mail** at the job's own line. A receipt on a lien does not change its row. The row reads **Print the copies to mail** until its stamped copy is printed. A row turns red the day before its day. A row past its day says the day the copies were due. Mail them that day and record it.

The top half prints. The window reads every lien's stamped copy as it opens. Until then the press reads like **Reading 11 stamped copies…**. Then it counts the liens, like **Print 11 liens’ copies**. A mail sheet prints first. It lists every envelope in order, with its pages and its mail-by day. The name and address read as the envelope carries them. The street is on one line and the city is on the next. Under each envelope are five boxes for its certified mail number, four digits to a box. Keep the mail sheet in the office. Behind it, each envelope's letter and stamped copy print in that order. A divider page prints in front of each envelope. It shows the envelope's number on the mail sheet and how to write the envelope. Do not mail the divider. Mail only the pages behind it, up to the next divider. Each divider has a code to scan with your phone. Scan it once that envelope is mailed. It opens that envelope's number box in the app. The mail sheet has a code too. It opens the whole list of mail numbers.

A lien that cannot print is listed under the press with its reason. The rest still print. A lien with no document number has {{button:outline|Type it on its card ›}}. A lien with no stamped lien saved has {{button:outline|Add the stamped scans ›}}. Any other has **Open the Lien window**.

The bottom half takes the mailing. Everything on it saves by itself. There is no record press. A green line over the list says so.

Each lien has a card. Under its name the card says what the clerk gave back. It reads **Document no.** and the number, then the county and the day filed. **Stamped lien** opens the clerk's stamped copy. That copy is already kept on the lien. Do not add it again as a receipt.

Is the number wrong? Press **Change** beside it. Type the number from the clerk's stamp and leave the box. It saves at once. A number another lien already holds is not saved. The line says which lien holds it. A number that is not the county's usual length gets a question. Check it against the stamp. Press **Save it anyway** when it is right. A lien with no number shows the box open. Its copies cannot print without it.

Set **Mailed on** to the day the copies went out. Type or scan each certified mail number. Enter moves to the next envelope. A number is kept when you leave its box. The first number on a lien ticks **Mailed**. The tick writes the **Mailed on** day to that lien at once. A lien opened from a divider's code says which day that will be, before you type. The line beside the box reads like *Mailed Nov 18. Saved.*

Did a lien go out with no number yet? Tick **Mailed** yourself. Is a lien ticked that has not gone out? Untick it. The day comes off at once and the numbers and receipts stay. After you tick or untick a box by hand, a number does not change it.

Each envelope has {{button:outline|Add the mail receipt}}. It takes a photo or a PDF of the post office's slip. A receipt alone does not tick **Mailed**. A line under the envelope reads *Kept on this lien.* Close the window and it is still there. Until you tick **Mailed**, the lien's **What went out** block reads *Not mailed yet* on each copy. Its chip reads *Mail proof complete* only with the day, each number and each receipt on file.

The press asks first when a file does not look like a receipt. A file named with the lien's document number is the lien. So is a file whose name says lien or affidavit. The same file on both envelopes gets the question too. Press **Leave it out** to drop it. Press **It is the mail receipt** when it truly is. Nothing is saved until you answer.

A mailed lien stays in the window while an envelope lacks its number or its receipt. It moves under the heading **Mailed**. Finish it there on a later day.

### Several scans at once

The clerk's stamped affidavits often come back together. Scan each one to its own PDF when you can. Then add them all in one sitting.

Open the window named **Add the stamped scans**. There are two doors. One is a line under **Upload the scanned PDF** on Record filing. It opens the Lien desk in a new tab. The other is in **Filed liens: copies to mail**, under the mail numbers.

Press **Choose the scans** and pick every file at once. Each file is matched to a filed lien. A file named with its recording number finds its lien. So does a file named with its job number. Any other file takes the next lien in the list. A line under each file says how it was matched.

Check each file against the clerk's stamp. The lien's recording number is in the list beside its job. Press **Open** to see the file. Pick another lien from the list if the match is wrong.

Then press the save press, like **Save 12 scans**. Each scan goes to its own job's Drive folder, under Sent by us. It becomes that filing's saved copy. A file with no lien stays in the window with its reason. So does a second file for the same lien. The rest are saved.

A lien must have its filing recorded before it is listed. Tick **Also list liens that already have a scan** to replace one.

### One scan of a whole stack

Did the whole stack go through the scanner as one PDF? Pick that file in **Add the stamped scans**. A line under it says how many pages it has. Press **It holds several liens: split it**. The window shows a picture of every page.

Each page starts in order. The first page goes to the first lien in the list, the next page to the next lien. The lien's name and its recording number sit under each page. Read the number against the clerk's stamp on the picture. Press a picture to open that page full size.

Change a page with the list under it. Pick **Not an affidavit** for a clerk's receipt or a blank back. Pick **Same lien as the page before** for a second sheet. Or pick the lien by name. The pages after it follow by themselves. A page with no lien left is held and says so.

Then press the save press. Each lien's pages become a PDF of their own, in that lien's job folder. The stack itself is saved nowhere. A big stack is fine. Only one lien's pages must be under 10 MB. Press **Keep it as one file** to undo the split.

## The year after filing

A recorded lien is good for one year from the last day the affidavit could have been filed. That is § 53.158. Ninety days before that day a Needs You card appears. It reads *A filed lien's year to sue ends <date>*, with the dollars still open behind it. It turns red inside 30 days. Once the day has passed, it reads *has run out*. Paid? File the release of record and the card goes. Unpaid? Get counsel on the suit. The card names the day to have counsel by. {{button:outline|Open By job}} lands on the Lien desk's By job tab, where each job shows its date.

The Legal desk's Paper tab draws each job's timeline under *Where each job stands*. A filed lien's suit step reads *counsel by* and a date. The step reads *counsel now* inside 90 days and *the year ran out* after. The job's Lien window opens with its whole timeline under its name. So the date is one glance away wherever the job is opened.

## Step 3 — when it's paid, release it

Once a filing exists, a **Release of record** tab appears. It is prefilled with the instrument number, county, and filing date. Print, notarize, file. The recorded lien is discharged. The Lien desk walks these steps for every filed lien. See [release a filed lien after it is paid](/help/release-a-filed-lien-after-it-is-paid).
