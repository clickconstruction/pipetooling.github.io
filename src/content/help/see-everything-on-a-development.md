A development is one site with many bids and jobs on it. The Development page shows the whole site on one screen, in six looks.

You put bids and jobs into a development first. The guide [group bids and jobs into a development](/help/group-jobs-into-a-development) shows how. Then the page gathers them.

## Open the page

1. Open a bid or a job that is in a development.
2. Find the house line near the top. It names the development with a small house.
3. Press the development's name.

A Pipeline row has the same door. Press the development name under the job's customer, marked with the house.

The page opens with the site's name, its customer and its GC at the top. Under them you see who is on site and five counts.

## Put the GC contact on the bids

The GC contact line at the top names the contact from the site's bids. The office can set it here.

1. Press **add one** at the end of the GC contact line, or **Change ›** beside a contact on record.
2. Type the name, the phone and the email in the sheet.
3. Press {{button:blue|Put it on 15 bids}}. The button counts the bids it writes.

The contact goes onto every bid of the site with no contact. Change › writes onto the bids that carry the old one. The message after it offers {{button:outline|Put it back}}.

## Read the five counts

The counts are Waiting, Bids, Jobs, Bid undecided and Open on bills. Each one is a rule over the site's records.

With no bill open, the fifth count reads Not yet billed instead. It shows what is left to bill on the site. Its card lists the waiting jobs with money left, biggest first.

Bid undecided counts the bids sent or still being worked. An archived bid sits apart and is not counted. The Bids count names how many are archived.

1. Rest your pointer on a count.
2. A card drops under it with the records behind the number. Each record is a door.
3. Press the count to keep the card open. Press Escape to close it.

On a phone, tap the count. The card opens as a sheet under the counts.

## Jump between the six looks

A strip under the counts names six looks: History, Jobs & bids, Money, Plans, Questions and Photos. Each look shows its count and what in it waits on you.

1. Press a look's name to jump to it.
2. Press the chevron on a look's card to fold it. The page remembers your folds on this device.

On a phone the strip is one row with a cell for each look. Each cell shows the look's name over one number. The number is what waits on you, or the count. Money rounds to fit, so $186,116 reads as $186k. The look you are in is underlined.

## See a job's details without opening it

Every job on the page shows as a blue number, such as *JP1091*. The number opens the job when you press it.

1. Rest your pointer on a blue job number.
2. A card floats beside the pointer. The card shows the job's full name, its address and its details.
3. Move the pointer away and the card goes.

The details are the customer, the stage, the crew, the last work day and the money on the job. A job that came from a bid names the bid. An estimator sees no money on the card.

On a phone, press and hold the number for half a second, then lift your finger. The card opens under the number. Tap anywhere else to close it. A plain tap still opens the job.

The card also opens when you reach the number with the Tab key. Press Escape to close it.

## Follow the site in History

History is one dated list, newest first. It holds what the records did and what people wrote. A send, a stage move, a bill or a payment draws a line. So does a note on a job or a bid, a plan set filed, and the customer's contact log.

The crews' photos draw lines too, at the time they were taken. Photos on one job by one person within an hour read as one line with up to four small pictures. A **+ N** tile stands for the rest. Press a picture to open it at full size. Press {{chip:blue|Photos}} under the search box to see the photo lines alone. The search finds a photo by its caption.

1. Press a chip under the search box to narrow the list. Press {{chip:blue|Plans}} to see every plan set filed, newest first. On a phone the chips sit in one row. Slide the row sideways to reach the rest.
2. Type a name, a number or a word into the search box, marked with a spyglass, to find a line.
3. Press {{button:outline|Mark as looked}} when you have read the new lines. The list draws a line where you stopped.

A long line shows its first words and a **more** press. Press it and the whole line opens in a window.

Three or more like lines on one day fold into one line with the count. Like means the same act on the same record by the same person. Questions, answers and kept entries never fold. A search sees every line.

Each line starts with a small mark. Press the round **?** beside the search box to read what every mark means. Grey is a note someone wrote. Blue is a move on a job or a bid. Green is money or a bid won. Red is a bid lost. Amber is something that went out or came in. The window groups the marks under the chips that narrow to them.

## Keep what happened

An email came in, a call was made, someone walked the site. History keeps those too, at the time they happened.

1. Press {{button:outline|Write a line}} at the top of History. Type what happened in the box.
2. Pick what it is about under About. The site is picked by default. Press the box and type a number or a name to find a job, a bid or the customer. The jobs and bids written about last sit near the top.
3. Set when it happened. The box starts at now. Set it back to the email's own time.
4. Pick the source, like {{chip:blue|Email}} or {{chip:blue|Call}}.
5. Press {{button:blue|Keep it}}.

The line reads at the top at once. A kept line never changes. To correct one, keep a second line that says so.


Notes a record carries with no date sit under the chips in a block called On record. A bid's notes, a job's collections note and the customer's terms live there.

## Pair each bid with the job it became

Jobs & bids lists each bid beside the job it became. A gap in the row means the pair is not on record yet. A dev or a controller also sees a Costs column: each job's parts and sub labor as one figure. Rest on it to see the buckets.

1. Press {{button:outline|Pair bids with jobs}} above the table.
2. In a gap, press the likely pair the page names, or press {{button:outline|Pick the job}} to choose one.
3. A window shows the pair before the page keeps it. Press the same words in the window to confirm.
4. Press {{button:outline|New job from bid}} when the job does not exist yet.

The window shows each fact now and after. The ones that change are marked. The rest read the same.

Press {{button:outline|Unpair}} on a paired row to take a pair apart. A window shows what changes first. The message after it offers {{button:outline|Put it back}}.

Press {{button:outline|Mark the 2 paired bids won}} above the table to set won on paired bids with no outcome. A window shows each bid as the Bid Board will read it. The message after it offers {{button:outline|Undo}}.

The paid jobs fold under the table with their sum. Press **Show them** to see them. The archived bids fold the same way above them, with their count and what they bid.

A robot estimator's shadow bid stays in the table with a shadow chip. The counts and the people at the top leave it out.

## See what is at the address but not in the development

A line above the looks counts the bids and jobs at the site's address that are not in it yet. Rest on a chip in that line to see them.

Press {{button:blue|Put the N in}} to put them all in at once. A window shows the counts now and after, and the jobs and bids that go in. Press the same words in the window to confirm.

Press {{button:outline|Show the N here}} to draw them in the Jobs & bids table first. Edit Bid and Edit Job still put one record in at a time.

## Read the money

Money shows the site's progress as one bar, then every job by stage with what is billed, paid and left. Press a column head to sort a section. Press the band's note about jobs with no percent to sort by percent and light them. Two more views sit at the top of Money, the Scoreboard and the Timeline.

## Read the scoreboard

Press {{button:outline|Scoreboard}} at the top of Money. The column heads are the site's totals. Green heads are effort, red heads are what it cost, gold heads are the money.

Every job is a row. A folded row shows Earned, Cost and Profit. Press the arrow before a job to open all nine figures. Press a column head to sort by it. Press it again to turn the order.

Earned follows the percent done. A finished job earns its whole price. A working job earns its percent's share. A job with no percent on record earns half its price and wears ≈.

Jobs with nothing on them yet fold under one line. Press {{button:outline|Show them}} to see them.

The line under Right now names one job worth a look. A job with no price that is already costing comes first. Otherwise it names the job that carried the site. Press the job's number to open it.

Open a job to see who put something into it. Each person shows hours, days and labor. A Lead row led the crew on some of the days. Each sub, supply house and card holder shows what it cost. The bank payments, the tally, the other charges and the overhead day share have rows too.

Each row fills Cost, and a thin bar shows its share. The rows add up to the job. The Office row says who sent the bills and booked the payments. Your own rows are gold and say You.

A dev or a controller reads every column and every row. A leader or an assistant reads hours, days and earned only. Their Right now line speaks in hours and earned dollars. Under an open job they see the people alone. The page remembers the view you were in.

## Print the cost summary

Press {{button:outline|Print cost summary}} under the Scoreboard. Only a dev or a controller sees this press. The summary opens in a print window. From there you print it or save it as a PDF.

The page starts with the site's nine totals, then one row per job. Next it names who put something in, each person and each sub. Then the parts come by bucket. The buckets are supply house invoices, card charges, the tally and other charges.

The card charges are grouped by payee, largest first. The small payees fold into one line at the end. The supply house invoices are listed by date. Every other charge is listed with its day. An estimate wears an *Estimate* chip, and a note gives the estimates' total.

The page says what the total leaves out. An internal transfer is not a cost. A card charge also on a supply house invoice is counted under the invoice. A read that did not land shows a dash, never a zero. The page ends with the date and your name.

A single job prints from its own Costs tab. See [the Costs tab](/help/job-window-tabs#the-six-tabs).

## Read the timeline

Press {{button:outline|Timeline}} at the top of Money. Only a dev or a controller sees this view. The chart is the Job Summary's cost chart drawn over the whole site.

The red line is the cost to date. Every job's charges sit on it in date order. Each step wears the icon of its source. The green line is the cash position. The green line rises only when a payment comes in. The amber band is the office overhead landed on the site so far. The band sits on top of the cost, so the true cost reads at its top edge. The band starts on the day the office pool began.

Rest the pointer on a step to read its lines. Each line names the job and the kind of charge for that day. Three card charges on one job read as one line.

Press a job's chip above the chart to narrow the chart to that job. Press more chips to add jobs. Press {{button:outline|All jobs}} to see the whole site again. A job with no dated charge or payment is not a chip. One line counts how many were left out.

Tick {{button:outline|Value created (right axis)}} to see the blue line. The blue line is each job's percent done times its price, added up over the site. The line steps on every field report and every hand set percent. A job with no dated percent adds its current percent at the end of the line.

Press {{button:outline|Print or save as PDF}} under the chart. The chart opens in a print window. From there you print it or save it as a PDF.

The reads start when you open the view. On a big site the icons mark the biggest days only. The page remembers the view you were in.

## Check the plans and the questions

Plans lists each part of the site, such as the Lodge or the Bunker. Each row names the set that part builds from today. The row shows the date on the sheets, the day the set came, its memo and the jobs on it. Older sets fold under a count. Press the count to see them. The crew's as-builts read under their part. A **Papers** column counts the specs, schedules and lists kept with each part's sets. Press the count to read them. An amber chip counts the questions still waiting and the papers we are owed. On a phone each part is a card with the same facts. See [keep the papers with the plans](/help/keep-the-papers-with-the-plans).

Press {{button:outline|+ Part}} to name a part of the site. Press {{button:outline|+ New set}} on a part's row to add a set. Press {{button:blue|Add the set and build from it}} and every job on that part builds from it. Press {{button:outline|Build from this set}} on an older set to go back to it.

Two chips sit under the table. One counts the parts with no set. One counts the jobs that follow no plans yet. Press a chip to see them. Under that chip, each job is a row with its number and its name. Tick the jobs that belong on one part. Pick the part in the box. Press {{button:blue|Put 3 jobs on Lodge}}. The button counts the ticked jobs and names the part. A job can also name its part on its Documents tab, under Plans. A job that only carries bills needs no plans. A service call needs no plans either. The look leaves both out of the count. A quiet line under the chips says how many it left out and why. Ask the estimators about the rest from the card. The card keeps the question in view while it waits, and shows the answer when it comes. Bids keep their own set and are listed under the table.

Questions starts with two presses, {{button:outline|Ask the estimators}} and {{button:outline|Ask Dispatch}}. Under them sits one table for each lane with questions. Open questions come first, oldest first. Each shows how long it has waited, what it is about, the words and the answer so far. The answered ones fold under a count. With no question at all, the look says nothing is waiting.

## The photos

Photos is the last look on the page. It shows every photo the crews took on the site, newest at the left, in one row.

1. Slide the row sideways to go back in time. A mouse wheel or a trackpad moves it too.
2. Read the small day label over a tile. It marks where a new day starts.
3. Press a tile to open the photo at full size. Press {{button:outline|Next ›}} to walk along the row.
4. Press {{button:outline|Show more}} at the right end to read the next sixty.

Chips above the row narrow it. Press a job's chip to see that job's photos alone. Each chip counts its photos. Press a source chip, like {{chip:blue|Reports}} or {{chip:blue|Tally boxes}}, to see one kind.

The open photo shows the job, who took it, when and where it is filed. Press {{button:outline|Open the job}} to go to the job. Press {{button:outline|Only Deon's photos}} to see one person's photos on the row.

The row reads the same photos as Documents → Photos. The guide [find every photo that was uploaded](/help/find-every-photo-that-was-uploaded) explains where each one is saved.

## What an estimator sees

An estimator opens the same page with the jobs and bids, the history, the plans and the questions. The page shows no money. The Money look is one line that says the office reads it.

The crews' photos on the jobs read only to the office. An estimator's Photos look shows just the pictures sent by text or email. The look's heading says the crews' photos are for the office.

A job's number opens nothing for an estimator. The job's stage, crew, plans and history are on this page.

## Ask a question

1. Press {{button:blue|Ask a question}} at the top of the page.
2. Pick who answers. The estimators take a bid, its plans or its price. Dispatch takes a crew or a day on site.
3. Pick what it is about. The site is picked by default. You can pick a job or a bid.
4. Type the question and press {{button:blue|Ask the estimators}} or {{button:blue|Ask Dispatch}}.

The question lands in their inbox with the site named on it. The answer comes back here, under Questions.

## Ask from a job or a bid

You can also ask from a job's window or a bid's window.

1. Open the job or the bid.
2. Find Questions on this job or Questions on this bid.
3. Press {{button:blue|Ask the estimators}} or {{button:blue|Ask Dispatch}}.
4. Type the question and press the button with the same name.

The sheet already names the job or the bid. The question lands in their inbox with that record named on it. The answer comes back on the window and on this page, under Questions.

The window lists every question asked about the record. The open ones come first with how long each has waited. The answered ones follow with the answer's words.

A question asked on a bid also shows on the job that bid became. The job's list marks it asked on the bid.
