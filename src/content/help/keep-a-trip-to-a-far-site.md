A trip is one going out to a site away from home and coming back. You keep it on the site's Development page, apart from the hours on its jobs.

On a far site, the hours on a job do not say where the crew was. Some hours are at the site. Some are a truck on the road. Some are a person at home loading the trailer. A trip sorts them.

A trip never changes the clock. It changes no bill and no cost. It is a reading laid over the hours you already have.

## Find the Trips look

1. Open the site's Development page.
2. Find **Trips** at the end of the strip of looks.
3. Press **Trips** to jump to it.

The look shows on a site that has a trip. It also shows on a site far from the shop.

Far is 150 miles or more until a dev sets another distance. A dev sets it in Settings, on the **Trips** card under **Jobs, bills and liens**.

A nearer site with no trip has no look. Its page has one door instead. Find **Trips** in the line under the address and press *none yet · add one*.

## Add a trip

1. Press {{button:blue|+ Trip}}.
2. Answer **Who went?** Pick each person from **+ a person**.
3. Press **+ someone not in the app** for a sub or a visitor. Type the name and press **Add**.
4. Answer **Which days?** Pick the first day and the last day.
5. Answer **What for?** in a few words.
6. Press {{button:blue|Save the trip}}.

One person and one day is a whole trip. Everything else can wait.

The app gives the new trip its number when you save it.

## Say a trip by its number

Every trip has a number on its site. The first trip is Trip 1. The next trip is Trip 2.

The app hands out the number when you save a new trip. Nobody types the number, and nobody can change it.

A trip keeps its number for good. You can change its days, its people and its words, and the number stays.

A number is never given to another trip. Delete Trip 5 and the next trip is still Trip 6.

The numbers follow the order the trips were made. A trip you file late from an old record takes the next number.

Say a trip by its number and its site. *Trip 5 at Heron Point* is short enough for a text. A bill can cite the same words.

The number leads the trip everywhere the app names it. You see *Trip 5* on its row, in History and on a job. You see it on Plans by date, on the Scoreboard and on the printed log.

## Paint the days

Every day you pick starts as At the site. A tap changes one day.

{{chip:green|At the site}} {{chip:blue|On the road}} {{chip:yellow|Getting ready}}

1. Tap a day once to make it On the road.
2. Tap it again to make it Getting ready.
3. Tap it a third time to turn it off. A day off is a day nobody was on the trip.
4. Tap it once more to bring it back to At the site.

Getting ready is a day at home for the trip, such as loading the trailer. To count one, move the first day back and paint the new day.

## Give one person their own days

Everyone on a trip shares its days at first. One person can join late or drive home early.

1. Add two or more people.
2. Press **Give one person their own days ›**.
3. Tap the days in that person's row.

Press **Same days as the trip** to put the person back on the trip's days.

## Fill in the rest when you know it

Press *More · all of it can wait* on the sheet. Nothing under it is needed to save.

- **Who asked for it?** Press **Us** or **Them**. For Them, type who asked and how.
- **Work goes on.** Pick the jobs the trip's work belongs to. A trip can sit on one job or several.
- **Billing.** Press **Inside the price**, **Extra** or **Ours**. The choice bills nothing by itself.
- **How it went.** Type a sentence once the trip is back.

## Read the band

The band at the top answers one question. Of all the hours on this site, how many were at the site?

:::cards

:::card At the site
Hours on a day painted At the site, with a punch at the site.
:::

:::card On the road
Hours on a day painted On the road. A punch somewhere else on an At the site day counts here too.
:::

:::card Getting ready
Hours on a day painted Getting ready.
:::

:::card No place on the punch
Hours from a row that carries no place. An imported row or a scheduled row has none. The row says the day was paid. It does not say where.
:::

:::card At home for the site
Hours on the site's jobs outside every trip the person was on.
:::

:::

The five figures always add up to the hours on the site. No hour is counted twice.

A row with no place is never added to At the site. That keeps the At the site figure honest.

## Read where a trip stands

Nobody types where a trip stands. The page reads it from the days and today.

- **Planned** means the first day is still ahead.
- **Out now** runs from the first day through the last day.
- **Back** means the last day has passed.

The strip under the band shows the year's trips against time. A planned trip is drawn faint. Press a bar to open its trip.

## Answer Looks like a trip

Sometimes people punch in at the site on days no trip covers. The look then says **Looks like a trip** above the trips. The line names who punched in, on how many days, and when.

The app never makes a trip by itself. You answer each line once.

1. Press {{button:blue|Make it a trip}} to open a new trip.
2. Check the people and the days. Each day they punched in at the site is painted At the site.
3. Change what you need and press {{button:blue|Save the trip}}.

Nothing is saved until you press **Save the trip**. Close the sheet to leave everything as before.

Press {{button:outline|Not a trip}} when those days were not a trip. The page remembers your answer and does not offer those days again. A message offers **Undo** for one minute.

The look shows three of these lines at most. A quiet line under them counts the rest.

No line shows while the clock is being read. No line shows on a site whose address has no point on the map.

## Open a trip

1. Press a trip's row.
2. Read the heading. The heading says the trip's number, its days and what it was for.
3. Read the left side for what was typed. The left side shows the days, who went, who asked and how it went.
4. Read the right side for what was counted from the clock.

A trip with no punch at the site says so in amber. The trip still stands as painted.

## Read What shows it

An open trip says **What shows it** under the counted hours. Each chip is a count over the trip's own days.

{{chip:green|8 punches at the site}} {{chip:green|4 On my way and I'm here stamps}} {{chip:gray|3 lines in History}}

- The first chip counts clock rows by the trip's people, on their trip days, at the site.
- The second chip counts the **On my way** and **I'm here** presses on those same days.
- The third chip counts the History lines on the trip's days. The trip's own two lines are left out.

A green chip means the clock saw something. A grey chip with a zero means nothing was found.

A day a person was off is not their trip day. A punch on that day shows nothing for the trip.

The counts change nothing. They only say what stands behind the painted days.

## Copy a link to one trip

1. Open the trip.
2. Press **Copy link** beside {{button:outline|Change}}.
3. Paste the link in a text or an email.

The app says *Link copied.* The link opens the site's page with that trip open.

The person who opens the link signs in as themselves. The trip opens for the office, the leaders and the controller.

A link to a deleted trip still opens the Trips look. A message says *That trip is no longer here.*

A trip's name opens the trip in other places too.

- In History, press **Open Trip 5 ›** at the end of the trip's line.
- In a job's window, press the words for the last trip or the next trip.
- On Plans by date, press {{button:outline|Open Trip 5}} on the trip's card.
- On the Scoreboard cut by trip, press the trip's number.

The search at the top of the app does not find trips yet.

## Change or delete a trip

1. Open the trip.
2. Press {{button:outline|Change}}, or tap one of its days.
3. Change what you need and press {{button:blue|Save the trip}}.

To delete, press **Delete this trip** on the open trip. The page asks once, by the trip's number and its days. Press {{button:red|Delete this trip}} to confirm.

A message then offers **Undo** for one minute. **Undo** puts the trip back with the number it had.

After that minute the trip waits under **Deleted trips** for 90 days.

Deleting a trip changes no clock row, no bill and no cost.

A trip on a job's lines leaves its line on the job. **Undo** puts the trip back and ties it to that line again.

## Split a trip that was really two

Sometimes one trip is really two. The crew went home in the middle and came back.

1. Open the trip.
2. Press {{button:outline|Split in two}}.
3. Pick the day the second trip starts. A day that follows days at home says so.
4. Read the two trips the window shows.
5. Press {{button:blue|Split into two trips}}.

The first trip keeps its number and the days before that day. The second is a new trip with the next number.

Each person goes where their days are. A person with days on both sides is on both trips.

Both trips keep what it was for, who asked, how it went and the jobs. No clock row, bill or cost changes.

A trip on a job's lines keeps its line. The new trip then starts with no billing said.

To take a split back, open the first trip and press {{button:outline|Join with the next}}.

A trip of one day has no **Split in two**.

## Join two trips that were really one

Sometimes two trips are really one. The crew ended the trip by mistake and started a new one. A join also takes a split back.

1. Open the first of the two trips.
2. Press {{button:outline|Join with the next}}.
3. Read the two trips and the one trip they become.
4. Press {{button:blue|Join into one trip}}.

The next trip is the one that went out next on the same site. You do not pick it.

The open trip keeps its number and its words. Everyone from both trips is on it, with every day from both.

Sometimes both trips give one person the same day. The window names those days. The open trip's kind stays.

The open trip may say nothing for what it was for, who asked or how it went. The open trip then takes the next trip's words.

The joined trip names the jobs of both trips. No clock row, bill or cost changes.

The next trip goes. The next trip's number is never given to another trip.

### Billing and a trip's line

One trip may say no billing. The joined trip then takes the other trip's billing.

The two trips may say two different billings. The window says so and the press is dead. Make them match with {{button:outline|Change}}, then join.

Only the next trip may be on a job's lines. That line then moves to the joined trip.

Both trips may be on a job's lines. The window says so and the press is dead. A trip holds one line.

### Where the next trip goes

The next trip waits under **Deleted trips** for 90 days. The line there reads **Joined into Trip 7**, with the number of the joined trip.

A joined trip has no **Put back**. The joined trip holds those days already.

To take a join back, open the joined trip and press {{button:outline|Split in two}}.

The last trip on a site has no **Join with the next**.

## Put a deleted trip back

A deleted trip is kept for 90 days. It waits under **Deleted trips**, below the trips on the Trips look.

1. Press **Deleted trips**. The list opens.
2. Find the trip. Its line says who deleted it and until when it is kept.
3. Press {{button:outline|Put back}}.

The trip comes back with who went, its days and the number it had. No clock row, bill or cost changes.

A job the trip named may have been deleted since. The line says so. The trip then comes back without that job.

A trip on a job's lines comes back tied to that line again. A line that was removed since is not made again.

A site whose only trip was deleted still shows its Trips look. You can put the trip back from there.

A trip that was joined into another has no **Put back**. The line names the joined trip.

After 90 days a deleted trip is gone for good.

## Put a trip on a bill

A trip marked **Extra** is work past the job's price. You can put the trip on a job as one line. A bill takes the line later.

The press shows on an open trip that is **Extra** and has gone out. A planned trip shows no press.

1. Open the trip.
2. Press {{button:outline|Put on a bill}} beside {{button:outline|Change}}.
3. Check **Job**. A trip on two jobs asks you to pick one.
4. Type the **Amount**. A trip holds no price, so you type one.
5. Check **Line** and **Words under it**. The line and the words go on the customer's bill. You can change both.
6. Read **Job total**. The box shows the total now and the total with the line.
7. Press {{button:blue|Add the line · $6,800}}. The button shows your own amount.

The job's window opens on **Bill** with the new line picked.

- Press **Bill the 1 picked** to make a bill for the trip alone.
- Close the window to leave the line for the job's next bill.

This press sends nothing to the customer. A bill goes out only when you send it.

A trip with no job named lists the site's jobs. The job you pick joins the trip's jobs.

A job billed from the GC desk is not offered. That job's price grows only by a signed change order.

The window says so under **Job**. Put the trip's amount on a change order instead.

A leader or the controller presses {{button:outline|Open Change orders}} there to write one.

A dev or a controller sees **What it cost** under the amount. Nobody else sees a cost.

## Put a trip on a bill from the job

You do not have to open the site's page. The job's own window lists its unbilled trips.

1. Open the job and press **Bill**.
2. Find **Extra trips not on a bill** under **Line Items**.
3. Press {{button:outline|Put on a bill}} beside the trip.
4. Type the **Amount** and check the line. Press {{button:blue|Add the line · $6,800}}.

The line joins the job's lines and is picked for you. Press **Bill the 1 picked** to bill it alone.

The list shows a trip marked **Extra** that names this job. It also shows one that names no job yet.

A planned trip is listed with no press. It can go on a bill once it has gone out.

Was the trip never kept? Press {{button:outline|+ Trip}} in the same place. The sheet opens with this job picked and **Billing** on **Extra**.

The list shows only on a job that is in a development.

## Read where a trip's money stands

The billing chip on a trip says where its money stands.

An amber chip means the trip is still owed a bill.

{{chip:yellow|Extra · not billed yet}} {{chip:yellow|Extra · on J2101's lines, on no bill yet}}

{{chip:yellow|Extra · on a bill, not sent}}

A green chip means a bill went out.

{{chip:green|Extra · billed}} {{chip:green|Extra · paid}}

One trip makes one line. Once a trip is on a job's lines, the press is gone.

**Open J2101 on Bill** takes its place. That press opens the job with the line picked.

The line is a line like any other. Rename it, change its price or remove it in the job's window.

Remove the line before a bill takes it. The trip then reads *not billed yet* again, and you can put it on a bill again.

Delete the trip and the line stays on the job with its words.

The line has a door back to its trip. Open the job and find the line under **Line Items**.

Press **Open Trip 14 ›** under the line. The job's window closes and the trip opens on its site's page.

You can change a trip's billing after its line is made. The line stays, and the chip still reads the line.

A trip's line is not a trip charge. A trip charge is the fee for a turnaway.

## See a trip in History

The History look shows a line the day a trip leaves. The look shows a second line the day the trip is back.

Each line leads with the trip's number. Trip 5 writes *Trip 5 out* and then *Trip 5 back*.

The lines come from the trip's days, so they move when the days change.

Press **Open Trip 5 ›** at the end of a line to open that trip in the Trips look.

## See a job's trips in its window

A trip can name the jobs its work goes on. Each of those jobs then says its trips.

1. Open the job.
2. Find **Trips on this job** under the address.
3. Read the line. The line counts the trips that went out. The line then names the last trip and the next one planned.

The line reads like *3 trips · last Trip 5, Oct 23 to Nov 4, Luis, Priya · next Trip 6, Dec 1 to 5*.

Press the words for the last trip to open that trip on the site's page. Press the words for the next trip to open that one.

The bar under the line shows this job's hours. The bar uses the same five figures as the band.

Press **Open the site's trips** to go to the site's Trips look.

Press {{button:outline|+ Trip}} to add a trip with this job already picked. Press {{button:blue|Save the trip}} to keep the new trip.

A job that no trip names has no such line. A job with no development has no such line either.

## Read the tag on a clock line

A job's window lists its clock lines under **Crew**. On a site with trips, each clock line wears a tag.

{{chip:green|TRIP · AT THE SITE}} {{chip:blue|TRIP · ON THE ROAD}} {{chip:yellow|TRIP · GETTING READY}}

The tag says the person's trip day and where their punches were that day. Every punch a person made that day wears the same tag.

A person with a punch at the site that day reads At the site. A person whose punches were all away from the site reads On the road.

{{chip:gray|TRIP · NO PLACE ON THE PUNCH}}

The grey tag means no punch that day carries a place. The punches do not say where the person was.

{{chip:gray|at home for the site}}

The quiet chip means the person was on no trip that day.

A job on a site with no trip shows no tag. A tag changes nothing on the clock.

## See the trips on Plans by date

1. Open the site's Development page.
2. Press {{button:outline|By date}} at the top of Plans.
3. Find the **Trips** lane under the sets of drawings.

Each trip is a bar over its days. Each day wears the colour of its kind. A planned trip is drawn faint.

Press a bar to see its trip on a card. The card is headed by the trip's number and its days, like *Trip 5 · Oct 23 to Nov 4*.

Press {{button:outline|Open Trip 5}} on the card to open that trip in the Trips look.

The lane prints with the chart.

The lane shows only on a site that has a trip.

## Read What it cost

A dev or a controller sees **What it cost** on an open trip. Nobody else sees a dollar on a trip.

1. Open a trip that has gone out.
2. Find **What it cost** under **What shows it**.

:::cards

:::card The crew's hours
The pay for the hours inside the trip. A person's day off is left out. A person who did not go is left out.
:::

:::card Card and bank charges in the days
The site's card charges dated on the trip's days. A payment from the bank with no card, such as a check, counts here too. A charge counts on a day anyone was on the trip.
:::

:::card The trip
The two figures together.
:::

:::

A row says so while its figure is still being read. The sum waits for both figures.

The figures change nothing. No clock row moves, and no bill or cost changes.

## Cut the Scoreboard by trip

1. Open the site's Development page.
2. Press {{button:outline|Scoreboard}} at the top of Money.
3. Find **Cut by** above the board.
4. Press {{chip:gray|trip}}.

Each trip that has gone out is a row. The newest trip comes first.

A row leads with the trip's number. Press the number to open that trip in the Trips look.

The last row is **At home for the site**. The last row holds everything outside every trip.

A trip's row shows its hours and its days with work. A dev or a controller also sees dollars on the row.

- **Labor** is the pay for the trip's hours.
- **Parts** is the card and bank charges dated in the trip's days.
- **Cost** is the two together. The open trip shows the same figure as **The trip**.

The last row shows the rest of the hours, the labor and the parts. The last row has no cost.

Some cells stay empty. **Earned** and **Profit** belong to a job, so no row fills them.

**Subs** and **Overhead** are not cut by trip. No row fills those two columns.

**Hours**, **Labor** and **Parts** each add up to the site's total in the tile above.

The {{chip:gray|trip}} chip shows only on a site that has a trip.

## Print the trip log

1. Open the Trips look.
2. Press **Print the trip log** beside {{button:blue|+ Trip}}.
3. Print the page, or save it as a PDF from the print window.

The page lists every trip, oldest first. Each trip is headed by its number and its days.

Each trip shows who went, what it was for and who asked. Each trip also shows how it went, its jobs, its billing and its hours.

The last line counts the trips and the days at the site. The last line adds the site's hours in the five figures.

The page carries no dollars, whoever prints it.

A copy of the printed page is kept in Documents under **Sent copies**.

A browser can block the print window. The look then says so beside the link. Allow pop-ups for the site and press again.

## Who can do this

The office, the leaders and the controller read and change every trip. The same people can put a trip on a bill. Everyone who reads every trip sees **Deleted trips** and can put a trip back. The same people can split a trip in two and join a trip with the next. A primary reads every trip and cannot put one on a bill. The crew starts its own trip in Job Mode, as [start a trip from your phone](/help/start-a-trip-from-your-phone) shows. The related guide is [see everything on a development in one place](/help/see-everything-on-a-development).
