Texas counts lien deadlines from the month the work was done, not from the bill. The Lien desk keeps every notice in one queue, so no deadline passes quietly.

On a job with a GC, every unpaid month needs its own § 53.056 notice. It is due by the 15th of the third month after the work. On a residential property it is due a month sooner. The office readies and drafts each notice. The leader approves it. Then it goes out on paper.

## The Board

The Board sits across the top of **To do**. It shows the lien month as one picture. Four tiles count the day's load, what waits on the leader, the mail and the unpaid money. Two columns of bins hold every job as a chip. The monthly notices are on the left and the lien affidavits on the right. A chip's colour says how soon its day is. Press a bin's name and the list shows only its rows. Press {{button:outline|Show every row}} to see every row again. The Board never sends, files or moves anything. Read [read the Board on the Lien desk](/help/read-the-board-on-the-lien-desk) for every part of it.

The days of the lien month are the owner's rhythm. Reminders go on days 1 to 5. Notices go on days 6 to 9. Liens are filed on days 10 to 13. The 14th and 15th catch what slipped. The days sit in {{button:outline|§ Rules}} under **The lien month**.

Press the first tab from any view. On a computer it carries the desk's name. To do opens with the Board in view. On a phone, press the title **Lien desk** to do the same.

## The four views

**By paper** reopens on the tab you last used there. This guide names those three tabs often. Each one sits under **By paper**.

## A link to a job that is not on the list

A link to the desk can name one job. A bookmark does, and so does a link a teammate sends you. Sometimes that job is not on the list the link opens. Its day may be more than 30 days away with no paper started. It may be paid, or off the lien path.

The desk then opens no job. A line where the paper would be says so. It reads like *1204 · Elm Street Duplex is not on the Lien affidavits list*. The list beside it is whole. No other job is open.

Two presses follow the line. **Open its Lien window ›** shows where the job stands. When another list holds the job, a press like **Open it on Monthly notices ›** opens it there. You can also pick any row on the list, and the line goes away.

## A job paid in kind is off the desk

Some customers pay with something other than money, like a rent credit each month. That is an in kind arrangement. The desk treats a job on an open arrangement as paid.

Such a job is on no tab of the desk. It is in no count and no total. No notice run, print run or shared summary names it. Its Pipeline row carries no lien date. Put a GC on notice leaves it out too. Held for suppliers in Materials shows no lien line for it either. The law firm's portal leaves it off its lien grid.

One thing to know before you rely on this. The law's lien dates still run on that job. The desk no longer watches them, and nobody is reminded. If the arrangement stops being honoured, a deadline can pass while the job is off the desk.

The job's own Lien window still opens from the job. A line at the top reads **Paid in kind**. Every paper already on file is still there. You can still draft, sign and record a paper from that window.

To bring a job back, end the arrangement or take the job off it. The job is on the desk again the next time the desk opens. Its dates are counted the way they always were. Nothing else is needed. [Keep track of work paid in kind](/help/keep-track-of-work-paid-in-kind) shows both presses.

## The piles

The **Monthly notices** tab lists each job with a GC and an unpaid month whose window closes within 30 days. A notice someone already started stays on the list, whatever its day. The list is in piles, each under its own title with a count. The titles stay on screen as you scroll. The ones you passed stack at the top and the ones ahead wait at the bottom. Press a title to go to that pile. On a phone one title stays at the top, the title of the pile you are reading. The other titles scroll with their rows. The *Approved · to print* title carries the {{button:blue|Print and mail N}} button. A door from the Dashboard or from **To do** can open the list on one pile. The pile's title then offers **every pile**. Press it to see all the piles again. When you clear that pile, the list widens by itself. A pile that opens empty says what is no longer there and offers **Show every pile ›**. The **Lien affidavits** and **Retainage notices** lists stack their titles the same way. Their pile chips still narrow the list to one pile. That pile's title then offers **every pile**.

- {{chip:yellow|Needs the owner}} means no owner of record with a mailing address is on file. The notice goes to the owner, so this comes first. The pane looks the property up on the county appraisal roll as it opens. It shows the answer under gate 1, laid out the way the envelope will read. A note below flags anything unusual, like a landlord or a public owner. {{button:blue|Use this owner}} saves it on the property record, and the row moves to To send for Master approval. {{button:outline|Find the owner ›}} opens Edit Job. Edit Job's Property record has the paste box. Use it when the roll cannot place the address.

So the pane asks *Is this public property?* and its send buttons stay off. {{button:blue|Yes, public property…}} takes the property off the lien path. {{button:outline|No, it is private}} keeps it on, and the papers can go out. [A public property](/help/file-a-lien-and-never-miss-its-deadlines#a-public-property) has the steps. Saving that owner is still right, because it is how the desk knows.
- **An owner from the roll may be unconfirmed.** Settings, Jobs, bills and liens has a switch to save owners from the appraisal roll automatically. When it is on, the app fills empty owners overnight. The pane marks each one unconfirmed, with a link to the county and {{button:blue|Confirm}}. You can draft and send for approval on it. Recording the run refuses while any notice in it has an unconfirmed owner. Open the county link, check the name and press Confirm.
- {{chip:gray|To send for Master approval}} is ready for the office. The paper is drawn from the job and the record the moment you open it. Nothing is saved until you change something or send it on. Tick a month or type a value, and the desk saves the draft a moment later. *Saved* shows beside the state line. A save that fails says why in a toast.
- {{chip:blue|Awaiting Master approval}} is with the master. When he is beside you, {{button:blue|Leader here, Approve ▸}} on the footer ends the wait. On his phone the notice is one screen. It shows the money owed, the mail-by day and the four steps by name. {{button:green|Approve ▸}} sends it. {{button:outline|Not yet}} offers the hold reasons. **Details** unfolds the rest.
- {{chip:green|Approved · to print}} is approved. It goes out with the run, or alone through **Just this one** on its footer.
- {{chip:gray|Held}} means the master is waiting on a promise or a call. The desk asks him again three days before the deadline.
- {{chip:gray|Sent · 30d}} and {{chip:red|Missed}} keep the record in sight. Sent holds only the jobs with nothing left to notice.
- A sent job with a new month due sits under To send for Master approval, not under Sent. Its row says when the notice went. Its footer keeps {{button:outline|Send a courtesy notice ▸}} and {{button:outline|Send the attorney’s letter ▸}}. They sit on a line above {{button:blue|Ask Malachi to approve ▸}}. The next notice is drawn below that line.

**Missed** is a lens as well as a pile. A job with open months and one closed month sits under To send for Master approval. It also counts under Missed. Its row says which window closed, until someone notes it.

## Where a job stands

Pick a job, and the box under its name is its timeline. It lists every step the Texas Property Code sets for a lien, in order. Each step has its date and how it stands. A dashed today line splits what is behind from what is ahead.

Above the steps, a tinted band names the next step in bold. A mark at its left says how it stands. A red ✗ is a blocker. An amber ! is a step that is close. A green ✓ is good news. When the move is ours, the band ends in a press that does it. {{button:outline|Find the owner ›}} opens the property record at the owner. {{button:outline|Send for approval ›}} sends the paper to the leader. {{button:outline|File it ›}} opens the affidavit tab. Hold the pointer over the press to read what the step waits on. When the move is not ours, the band ends in a quiet tag instead. It reads *Waiting on the owner*, *Waiting on counsel*, or the leader's name while the paper waits on his okay.

Press a stop's title to see what that stop sends. A window opens with the paper on the left and the stop's facts on the right. Press Next stop or Previous stop to walk the stops of the same job. A stop that sends nothing of ours shows what happens there instead. The window ends with a row of dots, one per stop, with this stop ringed. Press the dots to open the path above the footer. When the stop cannot go, the footer turns amber and says what to do first.

The steps are these:

- the last month worked
- each month's § 53.056 notice
- the § 53.057 retainage notice
- the § 53.052 affidavit
- the copy served within five days
- the owner's § 53.101 hold, once the affidavit is filed
- the year to sue
- the release when the job is paid

Each step wears a mark. {{chip:green|✓}} means done. {{chip:yellow|!}} means due, and it turns red inside a week. {{chip:red|✗}} means missed. A grey dash means blocked. A dashed ring is a step the app cannot date yet, with a door to set the date.

The band names the one thing to do and how many days are left. For a notice, it names the step the notice is on. That is find the owner, draft, approve, print and mail, or enter mail numbers. For an affidavit, it names the step the affidavit is on. That is draft, approve or file. When one fact holds the affidavit, the band names that fact first. An unanswered homestead question is one such fact. **Waiting on** sits in the same band. When every window closed with nothing sent, the band says what is left. While the affidavit window is still open, it says to send the notice late and then file. Once that window closes too, it turns red. The lien is gone, and the money is still owed in Collections.

Under each step still to come, a small word says whose move it is. {{chip:blue|ours}} marks the notices, the retainage notice, the affidavit and the serve. {{chip:purple|the GC}} or {{chip:yellow|the owner}} marks the release until it is paid. {{chip:yellow|the owner}} also marks the owner's hold. {{chip:gray|counsel}} marks the year to sue, which the firm takes. **Waiting on** says who we wait on and for what.

- **A demand letter** sent from the Lien window is a square on the same rail, at its reply by day. It counts the days left, then the days overdue once that day passes with money open. It says paid in full once the bills it covers are paid.
- **A missed month** stays where it fell. Several missed months in a row fold into one stacked mark with a count. Press *show the months ›* to see each one, with its closing date and who noted it.
- **An unknown property kind** shows the residential dates, the earlier ones. The box says a commercial date would be a month later. Set the kind on gate 3, and the dates move.
- **A green line** above a date says when that paper's window opened. A month's notice can go out from the 1st of the month after the work. The affidavit opens once the notice is mailed. Every date on the strip is the last day, and the green line is the first.
- **Steps or Windows.** {{button:outline|Steps}} and {{button:blue|Windows}} switch the picture. On the Lien desk they sit at the right end of the job's title line. On a phone they sit in the box's corner. Windows is a calendar. It draws each paper as a bar from its first day to its last, with the days gone hatched. A month whose window closed is a grey bar with a red ✗ at its last day. It says *not noted* until someone writes it down. A lien that can no longer be filed is a dotted outline under the notice it needed, with no date. The calendar always shows at least three months. The choice is remembered on this device. The **Months on this job** grid follows it. The Job window's **History** tab shows the calendar only.
- **On a phone** the steps stack as a list.

The rows sort by the next date, so the top is what to do first. The amount on a row is what the job's sent bills still owe. Work not billed yet is not in it.

- **GC** turns the list into one GC's book.

- A row opens the job on the pane its next step belongs to. A job the desk does not list yet opens its Lien window.

- On a phone **GC**, **Show** and {{button:outline|⎙ Print the grid}} are each as tall as a thumb.

## Drafting a notice

Pick the row. The pane first shows what must be true before the notice can go. Then comes **Months on this job**, a grid with months down and papers across. Above the grid, one line names the last day of work and where it came from. It reads from clock hours. With none, it reads from the bill. With no sent, unpaid bill, it reads from the job's creation. Press **Change ›** to set the day by hand. {{button:blue|Save the day}} opens a window before anything is written. It says how far the day sits from the clock hours, in words. A table shows the dates that move with it, today beside the new day. A changed row is lit. A day after today is refused there. A day earlier than the clock hours is allowed. The window then says the later hours stay and do not move the day. It names each month with hours that leaves the lien clock. Some days ask for a second look, and the button then reads **Set the day anyway**. One is a day far from the hours, or before the first month with hours. One is a day that drops a month a recorded notice or the affidavit names. One is a day that puts the affidavit's last day in the past. The reason is typed in the window, and it is required. Press **Set the day** to write it. The line then shows the reason, and the day it was set. The months follow the day you set. Hours approved after that day do not move it. The line then says those hours are there. Clock hours and pay are not changed. The same line sits in Edit Job, under Our contract on this job. The pane's timeline has a press for it too, under **Last work**. It reads **change ›** over a day from clock hours or a day set by hand. It reads **set the day ›** when the day stands in from a bill or the job's creation. Press it and the same window opens, with the day to pick inside it. Over a day set by hand, the reason already saved is in it too. The affidavit's pane has the same press. The Lien window has it too. Press it under **Last work** on its timeline, and the line opens there. On the **By date** tab, the date where a row's bar starts opens the same thing. A day set in either place moves the row to its new group.

- Each row is one month worked, oldest first, with its hours and crew. A job nobody clocked in on has one row, the month it was created.
- Each row shows its window, with the day to mail by and the days left. A closed month takes one line. The line says the month was not noted, with {{button:outline|Note it as missed}}. A month skipped before the Skip button left says who skipped it and why.

## A notice sent by hand

Some notices are printed here and mailed by hand. One paper may even cover several jobs at a property. Record it with **Already mailed? Record it…**, the quiet link left of the press. On a notice past the draft, the footer is one row. A chip at the left says the state, like {{chip:green|Approved · leader's word · Oct 7}}. Hold the mouse on it to read the whole line. The side doors are links, and the one button sits at the right end.

Type when it went out, how and to whom. The day you type is the day the notice went out. Attorney’s letter, the courtesy notice and the day count all run from that day.

Each envelope has its own mail number. Certified mail or a courier to both recipients shows two **Tracking** boxes. One box is for the owner of record. The other is for the original contractor. Type the number from each envelope's label in its own box. The app checks a certified number as you type, because it has 20 or 22 digits. A line warns you when both boxes hold the same number. Leave a box empty when you do not have that number yet. The record then says the tracking is owed, and you add the number later. Mail, email and hand delivery keep one **Tracking** box.

Type the claim and the months as they were printed, and the link to the saved copy. Then tick the other unpaid jobs at the property it covered. On a phone this step opens as its own page over the desk, and **Back** returns to the notice.

The record lands on every one of those jobs, and the desk stops asking for them. When the paper's claim differs from the app's, the record shows both figures. Neither one is rewritten. The Lien window's notice tab has the same door, {{button:outline|Already sent — record it…}}.

## A notice already on record

Sometimes the paper that went is already on record. One paper went for several jobs at a property. The paper was recorded on some of those jobs and not on this one. Or the desk moved this job's money to a later month after the paper went. The desk then keeps asking this job for a notice. Do not print and mail a second one. Press **Already mailed? Record it…** on the notice.

When the property has a notice on record, the step first asks **Which paper went?** The list shows each paper on record at this property under the same contractor. Each line gives the day, how it went and its total. Under that it names the jobs, the months and who received it. Pick the paper. Then tick the month it stands for on this job.

The desk checks the paper against the job. A green line says the owner of record and the contractor match. A red line stops the record and says why. A paper that went before the month began is one. An amber line is a warning. The month may not be printed on the paper. The copy to the contractor may not be on record. With a warning, type why the paper stands for the month. The reason stays on the record with your name and the day.

Press the button, which reads like {{button:amber|Apply the Sep 22 notice to job 4107 ▸}}. One record is written on this job. The record points at the paper and keeps the paper's day and mail number. The paper's own record does not change. The month reads as noticed, and the affidavit's notice line gives the paper's day. The notice that was waiting is set aside and not mailed. The waiting notice keeps its approval and signature.

To take it back, open the job's Lien window. Under **Filings on this job** the record reads *as part of the notice on job 4101*. Press **Undo**, then **Confirm undo**. The record is voided, and the waiting notice returns to the desk as it was.

Is the paper not on the list? Press **It is not on this list. Record it now ›**. The link opens the step for a notice sent by hand. A paper that went to another property is never on the list. A notice goes to one owner about one property. Whether one paper can stand for several jobs is a question for the attorney.

## A month nobody sends

There is no button to give up a month. If the notice should wait, the master can hold it. If a month will never go out, let its window close and note it as missed.

A window that closes with nothing recorded is never silent. The Dashboard's lien card adds a line for it. While the job's affidavit window is still open, the line reads *send the late notice by Oct 15*. The late notice still carries the lien until that day. When nothing else is due, that line is a card of its own. The card is amber, and red inside a week of that day. Once the affidavit window closes too, the line reads *closed with nothing recorded*, with the count and the money. Then the lien right on that work is gone. Both lines open the desk's **Missed** lens. That month's row under **Months on this job** turns red, with {{button:outline|Note it as missed}}.

Noting a month records that a person saw the loss, with a name and a date. Then the Dashboard stops naming it. The money is still owed, and it rides on the notice for the open months.

A skipped or missed month stays as a row in the **Months** grid. A month skipped in the past reads {{chip:yellow|skipped}}, with who skipped it and why. A month whose window closed unsent reads {{chip:red|closed}} with its date. Beside the date the row says who noted the month, or {{chip:yellow|not noted}}. One line under the grid names the missed months and keeps two things apart. While the affidavit window is open, a late notice can still claim those months. The line says so, and the tick reads late. Once that window closes too, the lien is gone for those months' work. The money is still owed, and the notice claims it with the whole balance.

On the **Lien affidavits** tab, the job's pane shows **Months the affidavit claims**. Each month reads {{chip:green|on the lien}}, {{chip:blue|window open}} or {{chip:yellow|unsecured}}. A month whose window closed unsent is named and left off the lien. Its share of the money is chased in Collections.

The pane draws the affidavit under that card. A new draft is on the short form. The short form names the months and the notices from the record. On a property marked homestead, the paper opens with a bold notice that it is not a lien. The desk still holds a homestead for the attorney. ## Reading the notice before it goes

The pane is five sections tall. Each one has a thin title row that stays pinned as you scroll. The rows you have passed stack at the top. The rows still to come stack at the bottom. The one you are in has a blue bar. Press any row to jump to its section. On a computer one more row is solid blue. The blue row reads **Sign and send** and stays at the bottom of the pane until you reach it. The row's right side says where the signature stands, such as *Not signed yet*. Press the blue row to jump down to the signing box and the buttons for the next step. On an approved notice it reads **Print and mail**, or **Sign it** while the signature is missing. On a printed notice it reads **Enter mail numbers**. On a sent notice it reads **After the send**.

On a phone an open notice takes the whole desk. The tabs fold away, and the top row holds {{button:outline|← Notices}} to go back to the list. One title row stays pinned, the row of the section you are reading. A page of the notice slides sideways inside its own frame when it is wider than the phone.

On a computer the buttons for the next step are the pane's last section, under the solid blue row. You scroll through the notice to reach them, so the paper has the whole pane. An affidavit and a retainage notice keep their buttons in a bar under the pane. On a phone the buttons sit in a footer under the notice. A bar over the footer reads **Hide the next step**. Press the bar to fold the footer away and give the notice the screen. Press **Show the next step** to bring the footer back. What you typed in the footer is kept. A tall footer starts folded, so the notice is not squeezed into a strip. On a sent notice the folded bar also shows the mail proof count, such as *Mail proof: 1 of 3 on file*. Press the bar to see what went out and add what is missing. An open affidavit and an open retainage notice work the same way. The way back reads {{button:outline|← Affidavits}} or {{button:outline|← Retainage}}.

The small presses inside an open paper are easier to hit on a phone. Each one takes a tap a little above, below or beside it. Nothing on the paper moves.

Each row ends with its fact.

- **Where this notice is**. The next step and its days.
- **The four gates**. How many are clear.
- **Supply houses**. The houses and their money. This row shows only when the job bought from a house.
- **Months on this job**. The months, the claim and how many bills are behind it.
- **In the envelope**. The pages in the envelope.

The pane shows what goes in the envelope, page by page.

1. Counsel's cover letter is page 1 while its box is ticked. Untick it, and the page leaves.
   When a supply house is still owed on the job, the letter ends with a paragraph about it. A second box reads **Name the supply house owed**, with what the house is owed at its right.    A third box reads **A conditional release**, with the claim at its right. The box starts off. Ticked, the app's conditional release rides behind the letter. The letter says it is not effective until the money clears. Read more in [give a customer a lien release](/help/give-a-customer-a-lien-release#enclose-one-with-a-lien-notice).
2. The conditional release is page 2 when its box is ticked.
3. The notice comes next.
3. The pay codes page comes last when the job has unpaid bills. It shows one QR code per Stripe bill, with what is still owed on it. It says that once these bills are paid, no lien will be filed.

The job's unpaid invoices follow in the packet. On a residential or homestead property, the § 53.254 statement prints under the form on its own. The property record's kind and homestead flag decide it.

**The four gates.** Under the job's line sit four gates, always in this order. The gates are 1 owner of record, 2 original contractor, 3 property kind and 4 approved hours. A headline says {{chip:red|✗ Can't go out yet}} or {{chip:green|✓ Ready to go out}}. Each gate has its own row below, numbered to match. A row shows the fact on its first line, with its buttons at the right. One grey line sits under it. When all four gates are clear the rows fold away, because the gates already say every answer. Press a gate to open its row, or **More details** to open them all. **Fewer details** folds them again. A gate that is not clear opens its row on its own. On a sent notice with nothing left to notice, the gates fold to one line. The line reads the day the notice went, then the owner, the GC, the kind and the months it named. There is no verdict, because nothing is about to go out. Press **More details** to read the four as rows.

- A red gate is a blocker. The notice cannot go until it is fixed, and its door sits in its section.
- An amber gate is only to check. An unknown property kind moves the deadline, but it does not stop the send. On a linked property, its row reads the kind and the county on one line, with **Change ›**. Change opens the {{button:outline|Residential}} {{button:blue|Commercial}} switch and says what the other kind does to the dates. A pick saves on the property record and closes the switch. Switching takes two clicks on purpose, because every deadline on the job moves with it. While the kind is blank the switch is open already.
- A green gate's section shows the fact the notice will use. For the owner, that is the name and mailing address as the envelope will read. Beside them sit *Check on the county CAD ↗* and {{button:outline|Change ›}}.

After the gates come the months with the claim and the card that says who gets the envelopes. The card's last line is a key to the shaded boxes. Then comes the notice itself. Once you scroll into the notice, a one line strip stays pinned at the top. The strip says only what you cannot see from there, and every chip on it is a press. A red chip names a gate that blocks the send, like {{chip:red|Owner of record missing ›}}. Press it and the gate's own door opens. With nothing blocking, one green chip reads {{chip:green|Ready to go out ›}} and takes you down to the send. The months and the claim are quiet chips that take you to their sections. The next step on the path shows only when it adds something the red chips do not say. {{button:outline|Show gates ▴}} takes you back to the top.

**Preview it.** {{button:outline|Preview in a new window ↗}} opens the notice as the packet prints it, in its own tab. Each page is labelled. Yellow marks a value you can change, on the desk or right in the preview. Blue marks a value filled from the job, like the date, the GC, the claim and the claimant. Change a blue value at its source, and the paper reads it again. The marks never print.

**Export one page.** Each page bar ends in two presses. {{button:outline-blue|Open ↗}} opens that page alone in a new tab, as it prints. {{button:outline-blue|PDF ↓}} saves the same page as a file. The file is named for the job and the page, like *523 Mission Hills, cover letter.pdf*. Until the notice is approved, the foot of the page reads *Draft, not sent* with the day. Once the notice is approved, that line is gone. A saved page of an approved notice is kept under Sent from this job as *Saved as a file*. Opening a page is a look, so the desk keeps no copy of it. On a phone the two presses are the arrows alone.

While the notice is a draft, the four yellow values are boxes in the preview's side list. Type there, and both the page and the desk change as you go. The desk saves them by itself a moment later. {{button:blue|Save draft}} in the preview saves them at once. Once the notice has gone for approval, the boxes are read only. Pull it back to a draft to change the wording.

**The claim is what is billed.** The notice claims what the job's sent bills still owe. It never claims the job's price. A payment with no bill picked counts too. It pays the work not billed first, then the oldest bill. Work not billed yet is not claimed until it is billed. Under the claim box, **The bills behind the claim** lists each sent bill. Each line shows what was billed, what is paid and what is still owed. The owed column adds up to the claim. A last line in amber names the part of the job no sent bill carries.

- Press {{button:outline|Bill it from here ›}} on that line to bill it without leaving the desk. The Bill Customer window opens over the desk. Bill only work that is done.
- When the bill goes, the desk reads the job again. The claim, the row and the pay page fill in by themselves.
- A job with money on it but nothing owed on its sent bills gets a fifth gate, **Nothing billed**. It holds the send until the work is billed. The gate carries the same door.

**The claim, set by hand.** The notice claims what the sent bills still owe. When a bill is in dispute, press **Correct the claim ›** in the claim box. Type what the notice should claim, and type why. The reason is required. Tick whether it carries to later notices and the affidavit. The line under the box shows the difference from the balance. The rest stays on the books for Collections, and the app's balance never changes.

- A typed claim shows in yellow in the preview. The card says {{chip:yellow|set by hand}} with who and why, and so does the leader's card.
- A carried correction is an amount off the balance, not a fixed figure. Each later notice shows a strip naming whose correction it carries. Press {{button:outline|Still true}} or {{button:outline|Clear it}}. Nothing goes out on a standing rule until someone has looked.
- A claim above the balance turns red. Only the leader can approve it, never a rule or a spoken word.
- **Say it per month ›** is for a month with its own figure, like an invoice paid in full. A month left blank stays part of the total.
- {{button:outline|Back to the job’s figure}} undoes all of it.

The correction follows the job. Put a GC on notice claims the corrected figure, and the affidavit swears to it. The job's row in Collections says what the notice does not cover, so it gets chased there.

**Change it on the paper.** Four values may change, and each sits in a shaded box on the notice.

- the type of labor or materials, which follows the job's service type, like *Plumbing labor and materials*
- the project description
- the party contracted with, when it is not the GC
- the contact person who signs

Press a box and type in place. Enter keeps the change, and Esc puts it back. A changed value says so beneath it, with {{button:outline|Back to the job’s wording}}. The page label counts the changes. The leader sees them too, so an edited notice never reaches approval unannounced.

**Change a line on the pay page.** Each bill on the pay page has a bold line under its code. It is read from the bill, and it sits in a shaded box too. Press it and type what the owner should read. Enter keeps it, and Esc puts it back. An empty line prints nothing. The change is on this page only. The bill behind it keeps its own line, and so does its payment page. A changed line says so beneath it, with {{button:outline|Back to the bill’s line}}. A line that is only a note about mailing checks is left off on its own.

Every other value is the statute's form or is filled from somewhere else. Hover over a value to see where it comes from, and press it to go there. The original contractor, for example, opens Edit Job on its GC row. Save there, and the paper reads the job again. The date has no door, because it is the day the notice is signed. A notice not yet signed shows today.

### Signing a notice

On a draft the footer has no signing box. The line beside {{button:green|Sign and approve ▸}} says your typed name goes on it. Press it and your name goes on the paper in the cursive face, with today's date. The name is your full name. It comes from **Full name and title** on your account, up to the first comma. With that field empty, the paper carries your account name. The record says who pressed, from which sign-in, and when. The notice, the cover letter and the attorney’s letter print your name above the rule. It sits in a frame that reads **Signed electronically**. The frame carries a short record ID and the day you signed, with no time. The paper does not say how you signed. The desk shows that record under the paper, where it is not printed. Want ink instead? ### Signing a notice again

Sometimes an approved notice carries the wrong signature. The leader drew it and the office wants his name in the cursive face. Or the drawing came out poor. Open the notice in *Approved · to print*. Press **Sign it again…** in the row at its foot. A window asks first. The window names the job and says whose signature comes off. Press {{button:blue|Clear the signature}}. The signature comes off and the approval stands. The chip reads **unsigned**. The run holds the notice until it is signed again.

The signing line comes back at once. On the leader's own screen it is his signing box, with {{button:green|Sign ▸}}. On the office's screen it is his line, with his code field. The leader types his code and presses {{button:green|Sign ▸}}. The leader can press **Draw instead** and sign by hand. A line under the paper says who cleared the last signature and when. A drawing that came off stays on file.

The office and the leader both have the press. A printed notice does not have it. Press {{button:outline|Back to Approved · to print}} on that notice. Sending a notice back takes its signature off too, so the signing line is there when the notice lands. A signature by code or by pen may still wait on the leader's word. Back from the post office, type the mail numbers in the same window. The notice is recorded as it is in the run.

The link reads **held** when nothing can print yet, like a notice nobody has signed. Rest the pointer on it to read why. The window it opens has the way to sign.
:::

## Affidavits

The notice keeps a month's lien right alive. The **affidavit** is the lien itself. Its deadline is one date per job, the 15th of the fourth month after the last month worked. On a residential property it is the third month. The **Lien affidavits** tab lists every unpaid job whose deadline is within 30 days. An affidavit someone already drafted stays listed, whatever its day. When none is that close, **By date ›** shows the later ones.

- {{chip:yellow|Needs the property facts}} is the affidavit's gate. The gate needs the owner of record, the county and the legal description on the property record. A public owner, such as a city or a school district, holds the affidavit until someone answers the property's question. A job with a GC also needs a recorded § 53.056 notice. An affidavit that lacks only that notice sits under {{chip:yellow|Waiting on its notice}}. Its row says where the notice stands, like *Notice approved · not mailed yet*. The notice's gate row says the same and opens Monthly notices on the job. A job with a GC needs the GC's address too, because the affidavit gives that address. Press **Add the address ›** on that line to open the GC's customer record. A homestead is for the attorney, because its lien needs a contract recorded before the work. A house also waits until someone answers the homestead question. Press **Answer it ›** on that line. The property record opens, and you pick **Yes** or **No** under *Is this the owner's homestead?* Each missing fact has its door. Every row is a press, checked or not. A checked row shows the fact it holds after its name and ends in **Change ›**. Press anywhere on the row and that record opens at the right line. The notice row ends in **See it ›** and opens Monthly notices on the job.

Each row of the list carries the same five gates as small marks, after its last work month. They sit in the pane's order: owner, legal description, the GC's address, the § 53.056 notice, homestead. A green dot is a clear gate. A red ✕ is a hold. A hollow dot is a gate the job is not asked. A job contracted with the owner has no GC and no notice, so those two are hollow. Hover a mark and it names its gate and what is missing. The notice's mark reads *Missing a mailed notice*, because the gate is a mailing on record. Hover the strip and it reads how many are clear. On a computer the marks stand in for the row's *missing* words. On a phone the words stay, since a phone has no hover. Rows past To draft have passed every gate, so they carry no marks.
- An amber line under the gates can open with *Ask the attorney before this is filed*. The line names a month whose notice went late or never reached the GC. The line holds nothing.
- It flows like a notice. Press {{button:blue|Ask Malachi to approve ▸}}, the one press. It names the leader the job is set to. The master's phone gets one line: the job and the last day to file. With no phone set up for notifications, the same line goes by email. The words on your screen say which way the line reached him. The master approves it, holds it or sends it back. When he already told you, press {{button:outline|He already okayed it ▾}} and pick **Malachi said to file it…**. On his own sign-in the master sees {{button:green|I approve it ▸}} alone, with no signing box. The grey line beside it reads *Ready to file. You sign it on paper before a notary.*
- {{chip:green|Ready to file}} offers {{button:blue|Open the affidavit tab ›}} in the Lien window. Print it, sign it before a notary and file it with the County Clerk. Then press **Record filing…** and type the recording number. The row moves to **Filed**.
- An approved affidavit can still be stopped by a fact. The homestead question may be open, or the owner may turn out to be public. The paper then moves from **Ready to file** to **Held**, and its row names what is missing. Answer it, and the paper goes back to **Ready to file** by itself. The leader's approval stands, so he is not asked again. A paper in **Held** is left out of **Print for notarization**.
- A copy must reach the owner and the contractor within five days. The Lien window records that too.
- A filed affidavit that is still unpaid offers **Refer to the Legal desk ›**.

The Dashboard's card for a closing lien filing window opens the desk here. The whole affidavit path is in [file a lien and never miss its deadlines](/help/file-a-lien-and-never-miss-its-deadlines).

## Retainage

The monthly notice claims what is unpaid for a month's work. The **§ 53.057 retainage notice** claims the retainage the GC holds back and has not paid. There is one per job. It is due 30 days after our contract on the job is complete, terminated or abandoned. That is a short fuse, and the app cannot see the day on its own. Two facts start it, and both are typed on the job.

1. **Edit Job, Our contract on this job** sits under the Property record row on a job with a GC. Pick Still open, Complete, Terminated or Abandoned, then the day. The row suggests the last approved clock day, so confirm it or change it. Type the day the work is done, not the day the GC declares it. The day saves once it is finished. Pick it from the calendar, or type it and press Enter. A half typed date is not saved. The same row holds the retainage the GC holds in dollars, and whether a payment bond is on the project. A 0 means the subcontract holds none.
2. The **Retainage notices** tab lists every job with retainage recorded. {{chip:gray|Clock not started}} means there is no end date yet. The footer's {{button:blue|Set the day our contract ended ›}} lands on the row. From there it works like a monthly notice. {{button:blue|Ask Malachi to approve ▸}} tells the leader, on the phone or by email. The message names the job, the retainage and the day to mail by. The desk says in amber when the message did not go. It rides in the same run and is recorded as a § 53.057 filing.

3. **Where the figure comes from.** On a job with a saved pay application, the retainage is the latest application's figure. Edit Job shows it read only and says which application it reads. Change it in the AIA window. On a job with no pay application, type the figure in Edit Job. The row on the Retainage notices tab says which one it reads.
4. **Retainage nobody typed.** Some GCs pay each bill 5% or 10% short and hold the rest. The top of the Retainage notices tab lists those jobs under *Looks held · not on the job*. A job is listed when a bill was paid at exactly 90% or 95%. A job is also listed when a note on it says retainage. Press {{button:outline|Record $2,460.00 on the job}} to save the figure the bills show. Press {{button:outline|Type another figure ›}} to open Edit Job and type your own. Press {{button:outline|No retainage on this job}} when the GC holds none. The job then joins the list under {{chip:gray|Clock not started}}. Edit Job offers the same figure with {{button:outline|Use $2,460.00}}. To do shows each of these jobs under **Needs you now**. Its row starts *Retainage not on the job* and then says why. Press {{button:blue|Record the retainage}} on that row to open this list at the job. The Board counts them on its Retainage line.
5. **The release.** Once the retainage is released, the row says so under the figure. It reads *Release: release billed · awaiting payment · bill #4*. Press {{button:outline|Open the job ›}} to see the bill. The release is the final pay application, or a release bill on a job with none. See [Retainage a GC holds](/help/match-bank-deposits-to-bills#retainage-a-gc-holds).

The owner's copy opens with counsel's cover letter for retainage. It says the GC holds back our retainage and has not paid it. It asks the owner to keep both the reserved 10% and our retainage. It gives three ways to settle before they pay the GC, and none is a joint check. It ends with the signer's number to call. A homestead's letter adds the § 53.254 statement.

:::example Belt and suspenders
Once retainage is on the job, every § 53.056 notice names it inside the claim. The Months card's claim box says so too. That trap works at once, because an owner may withhold on a monthly notice the day it arrives. On a retainage notice alone, the owner may withhold only once our filed affidavit reaches them. So the retainage pane says whether a monthly notice already named the figure.
:::

The cover letters give the signing master's own phone as the number to call. It comes from his record in People. The letterhead's number is used only when he has none.

## The courtesy notice

A sent notice still needs watching. The notice's footer counts the days since the packet went out. In reminders week of the lien's month the footer offers {{button:blue|Send a courtesy notice ▸}}. Reminders week is days 1 to 5 of the month the lien is due in. The press never comes before day 7 after the packet. Before reminders week the footer says when the reminder goes. On **To do** the same press sits on the row under the **Reminders** heading. The Reminders rail counts that notice on its first step. The courtesy notice is a plain reminder by email and text. The reminder says the notice stands. The reminder names the day the lien goes on unless the claim is paid. The reminder carries the pay link. The desk holds the press when the lien cannot go on, and says why. See [send a courtesy notice before a lien](/help/send-a-courtesy-notice-before-a-lien).

## Attorney’s letter

The footer also says whether the GC has paid, and whether the GC told the owner to pay us. Counsel's rule is a second owner letter 10 to 14 days after the first packet, when neither has happened. From day 10 the job's row wears {{chip:yellow|day 12 · the attorney’s letter}}. The row sits under Sent, or under To send for Master approval when a new month is due. It turns red on day 14, and the Dashboard's lien card counts them. While no courtesy notice has gone, the row leads with the courtesy press instead.

- {{button:blue|Send the attorney’s letter ▸}} asks which letter to send.
  - **We believe the owner paid the GC out** is the paid out letter. It names the 10 percent the owner had to reserve and the affidavit date. It asks the owner three questions to answer by phone.
  - **The GC is not answering** is the letter for a GC who has gone quiet.
- Either one is drafted on the job's notice, with the same form and the same two recipients. The row moves to To send for Master approval and wears {{chip:blue|the attorney’s letter · paid-out}}. Read it, then send it for approval as usual. The run sends and records it.
- {{button:outline|The GC authorized direct pay…}} is for when the GC wrote that the owner may pay us. Note where that okay is and when it came. The attorney’s letter is then off, and the owner's check comes against a release.

:::example Paid means done
Once the job's balance is paid, the footer says there is nothing more to send. Once the owner has called and the call is recorded, the attorney’s letter is off too.
:::

## The owner's call and the three piles

Every owner letter asks the owner to call. The desk turns that call into a guided conversation. The whole call is in [answer an owner who calls about a lien letter](/help/answer-an-owner-who-calls-about-a-lien-letter).

1. **Find them.** Press the {{button:blue|☎}} button in the desk's title bar. Its hint reads *An owner is calling*. It opens counsel's words for the call at once. To find the letter they hold, type what they give you in the list's find box. Letters older than thirty days show under **Also sent**, and each opens the call sheet. **Take a practice call ›** opens the sheet on a made up letter and saves nothing. On a sent job's footer, {{button:outline|Record the owner’s call…}} opens the same sheet.
2. **Read and tap.** The letter they hold sits at the top. Each card is one line to say, with the owner's likely replies. Tap what they say, and the next card opens.
3. **Let the facts collect.** The sheet asks whether they still owe the GC, whether the 10% stayed with them and when the GC finished. {{button:blue|Save the call}} writes the answers onto the sent notice.

The last card is the **Never** strip. It lists what never to say on the call.

The **Lien affidavits** tab sorts the job into counsel's three piles from the answers.

- {{chip:green|A · owner still owes the GC}} means trap it, because the notice holds those dollars.
- {{chip:yellow|B · paid, never reserved the 10%}} means a lien on the funds that should have been held. Other unpaid claimants share it.
- {{chip:red|C · paid in full, holds nothing}} means file the property lien now, without waiting for the 15th.

The pane's **payment bond** line says whether a bond is on the project. Check it before telling an owner to hold 10 percent. With a bond, the bond claim is due the same day as the affidavit.

## A GC in trouble

When one GC stops paying everywhere at once, a month at a time is too slow. **Put a GC on notice** sends § 53.056 notices to the owners of every unpaid job under that GC. It names every month not yet noticed, in one run. Three doors open it with the GC already picked.

- **Jobs, Pipeline, ⋯ Pipeline tools**, with the board filtered to one GC, shows {{button:amber|⚠ Put <GC> on notice…}}.
- **The desk's title bar** has {{button:amber|⚠ Put a GC on notice…}} on its second line. It opens a picker of the GCs with notices due in the next 30 days, most money first. Each row shows the dollars, the jobs, the first window to close and what is stuck. A GC whose windows have all closed sits greyed under **Nothing left to claim**. It still opens, for the attorney’s letter and the grid.
- **Bids, Customer review** has {{button:amber|⚠ Put on notice…}} beside *set terms…* on the GC's row.

The run covers every unpaid job with the GC, not only the ones the picker counts.

**Jobs with unpaid work under this GC** sits above the steps. It lists every job with unpaid work under the GC, grouped by its stage. Each job shows its line items, its money and the Pipeline's progress bar. A chip flags anything that looks wrong, in the Pipeline's words. A job may be marked Waiting while a bill is out, for example. The chip opens the job over the run, on the field that fixes it. Close the job, and the run comes back where you left it. Fix the record first, because a notice claims what the record says.

The head line counts the jobs that look wrong, like *6 look wrong*. That count is a button. Point at it, or click it, to list those jobs. Each one says what looks wrong and what is still open. Click a job in the list. The table scrolls to its row and lights it for a moment. Each stage's own header row has the same count for its jobs. When a stage has one job that looks wrong, a click on its count jumps straight to that row.

The window follows the order of the work. A bar of five steps stays pinned at the top as you scroll. They are Owners, Claims, Cover letter, Decision and The grid. Each step shows its status, and a click jumps to it. Above the steps, one summary states the money once.

1. **The owners.** The app looks up every property without an owner on the appraisal roll. Each row shows the roll's answer and {{button:blue|Use}}. {{button:green|Use all found · N ▸}} takes every found row at once. A miss offers *Find the owner ›*, or you can type the owner and mailing address. A public owner is left out of the run, marked {{chip:red|public owner — bond claim, not a lien}}. Once every job has its owner, the step folds to one green line.
2. **The claims.** Every month with approved hours and no live notice is listed. A job nobody clocked in on lists the month it was created, and its notice keeps that note. Open windows show their date and days left, red inside a week. A month whose window has passed is named on the notice as information, because its lien is gone. Each notice claims what the job's sent bills still owe, as the desk's own notices do. A job not billed yet shows what it will bill. A job whose every window has closed is left out, and its row says what it still owes.
3. **The cover letter.** Counsel's letters are plain pages to an owner who paid the GC in good faith. Each job takes the letter for its kind, Commercial, Residential or Homestead. The letter names the job's trade, like the plumbing contractor on a plumbing job. Tick that the GC is not answering to send the letter for a quiet GC instead. The letter is the first page of each owner's copy, and the GC's copy is the form only. Untick **Include the cover letter** to send the form alone.
4. **The decision.** Type why now, then pick the ticks. Each tick shows what it changes. The standing rule can turn to sending without asking, from the moment this run is recorded. Payment terms can change to Winding down. A Legal desk matter can open with every job. The owners' portals can show each owner every open bill at their property. A value already in place reads {{chip:gray|already set}}.
5. **The grid.** This is counsel's spreadsheet, one row per job. A **?** marks an answer the office still owes. {{button:outline|Print the grid ↗}} prints it for counsel.

**Read a notice before you approve it.** Press a row in step 2, or press **Preview all N ›**. The preview shows the owner's copy or the GC's copy, exactly as the run will print it. The arrow keys walk the whole run. Esc closes only the preview. When a property's kind is not set, the row asks {{button:outline-amber|Residential}} or {{button:outline-amber|Commercial}}. One press saves it, and the dates read again. A job not billed yet claims its contract balance, marked {{chip:yellow|unbilled · contract balance}}.

The footer says what the run takes. Then the buttons follow the role.

- {{button:green|Sign and approve all N and send the run ▸}} is for a dev or a master technician. Every ready job is signed and approved, the ticks apply and the run opens. Each notice gets its own signature from the one press.
- {{button:outline|The leader said to send them…}} is for an assistant, a controller or a dev. Type who said it, when and how. Every notice then goes to *Approved · to print* on his word.
- {{button:blue|Send all N to the leader ▸}} puts one card on the master's Dashboard and in Quickfill. It opens the same window on his phone. His window shows the cover letter, the reason and the note as you sent them. Once he approves the set, the office prints the run.

:::example Nine jobs, one Tuesday
Taunya hears that Harborline's Harbor Ridge draw went to another job. She filters the Pipeline to Harborline and opens Put Harborline on notice. Six of the nine owners are missing. The roll finds five, so she presses Use all found and types the sixth. One job is the city's fire station, so it is left out for a bond claim. She picks that the GC is not paying its subs and writes what she heard. She ticks the rule, Winding down and the Legal desk. Then she sends all seven to the leader. Robert approves them from his phone, and the office prints the run.
:::

Nothing is mailed or recorded until the run is recorded. Afterwards, the GC's row on Bids, Customer review shows {{chip:red|on notice since Sep 15 · 7}}. The desk's Lien affidavits tab carries the same jobs with their deadlines.

### Pressed Sign and approve all by mistake

**Sign and approve all** opens the run window. A strip under its title says what you just approved. Press {{button:outline|Undo the approval…}} there.

A window asks first. It lists what goes back and what stays.

- The notices go back to drafts. Nothing is mailed or recorded.
- The standing rule and the payment terms go back to what they were.
- The bills that run showed to owners are hidden from them again.
- A Legal desk matter stays. Close it on the Legal desk if you do not want it.

{{button:blue|Keep the run}} changes nothing. {{button:red|Undo the approval}} takes it back.

The offer lasts while Put a GC on notice stays open. It ends once the run is recorded.

## Who can do what

- **Draft, send for approval and send on the leader's word:** a dev, an assistant or a controller. A master can draft and approve his own.
- **Clear a signature to sign again:** a dev, an assistant, a controller or a master technician.
- **Sign, approve, hold and set a standing rule:** a dev or a master technician. The database refuses an approval from anyone else. It refuses a signature that is not the leader's own press or his own drawing. It also refuses a send on a spoken word without a note.
- **Put a GC on notice:** the office opens it and readies the owners. Approving all of it is for the master or a dev. A send on the spoken word is for an assistant, a controller or a dev.

A storefront on a row means a supply house is still owed on that job. The card that explains it is in [see which supply houses are owed on a lien job](/help/see-which-supply-houses-are-owed-on-a-lien-job).

The Texas rules behind these dates are in [read the Texas lien rules the app follows](/help/texas-lien-rules-the-app-follows).
