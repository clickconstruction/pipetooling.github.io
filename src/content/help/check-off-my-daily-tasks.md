The Checklist → Today tab is your task list for the day. Each task is a card.

The top of the page shows the date and one line that says where you stand. It reads like *1 of 3 done today.* Your first open task sits in a card marked **Next**. The rest of today's tasks sit under **Then**. Use notes to talk about the work right on the card. Anyone who can see the task can join in.

:::example A task card
{{button:outline|☐}} Install sheet metal across the front of the office

{{button:outline|2 notes}}
:::

## Completing a task

Press {{button:blue|Mark done}} on the **Next** card. On a task under **Then**, tap its checkbox. That's it. There is no save button. Want to say something about how it went, like "used the scrap ply, looks clean"? Tap **＋ Note** and post it. Once a task has notes, the press shows their count, like *2 notes*. Open it to read the thread or add another.

A finished task moves under **Done today** as one struck line. Tapping its green check reopens the task. Every completion and reopen is remembered on the card's history, with who and when.

## Mark a round of tasks done at once

Is your day a round of the same tasks? When two or more of today's repeating tasks are open, a press under them reads like {{button:outline|Mark 3 done}}. Press it. A window asks once, and its press says the same words. Each task then finishes as if you had ticked it.

Some tasks are left out of the round, since each has steps of its own. A one-time task is left out. So is a task with lines to tick, and a task that asks for a photo. A line under the press says how many are left out. Finish those from their own cards.

## Add a photo to a task

Want to show the work? Press **📷 Photo** on the task. Take the photo, then press {{button:blue|Add the photo}}. The photo lands on the task's strip for whoever looks at it. Adding a photo does not finish the task.

You can add a photo before the task is done or after. An open task may also say what is wanted before you start. It reads like *Imani hears when it is done. It asks for a photo at the finish.*

## Say how far along it is

A card that happens once shows a ring where the box was. An empty ring means nothing has moved yet. Tap the ring to say where it stands. Pick **25**, **50** or **75**. Type what is left if you want. Tap the blue **Set** press. The ring fills to your number. The card's notes keep each move with your words.

:::example The sheet
Where does it stand?

{{button:outline|25}} {{button:outline|50}} {{button:blue|75}}

What is left? Puddy and the glue, Thursday

{{button:blue|Set 75%}}

{{button:green|✓ Done}}
:::

Tap **✓ Done** on the same sheet when the work is finished. It takes the same path as the checkbox. Tap a green ring to reopen the card. A card that repeats keeps its box. A card with lines reads its ring from the ticks.

## Tick the lines

Some cards list what done looks like. The lines sit under the title as boxes. The next one wears an amber ring. Tap a box when that line is true. Your name and the time appear under it. The card reads its percent from the ticks. Two of three ticked reads 67%.

:::example A card with lines
{{button:outline|☐}} Put the plumbing tools in a neat home

{{chip:gray|1 of 3 done when…}}

{{button:green|✓}} Pipe is racked by size

{{button:outline|☐}} Fittings are in labeled bins

{{button:outline|☐}} The cart is clear and the floor swept
:::

Tap the last box and the card is done. It takes the same path as the checkbox. Tap a ticked box to untick it. Every tick and untick is a line on the card's notes. On a computer the card shows a chip like {{chip:gray|2 of 3 done when…}}. Tap the chip to open the lines. A card that comes back starts its lines over. The notes keep who ticked what.

## Find where a task is

Some tasks have a place, like *Pump house*. The place's name sits under the task with a blue dot. A task with no place has no dot, and nothing else about it changes.

Tap the task to open it. Under its details you see a small picture of the spot from above. Tap the picture to open the map full size, where you can drag and zoom.

The person who gave the task may have added a photo of the spot. The photo sits beside the picture from above. Tap the photo to see it full size.

- {{button:blue|Directions}} opens your phone's maps app at the spot. Off a road, the maps app gets you to the nearest road. The picture does the rest.
- {{button:outline|How far am I?}} asks your phone where it is and answers, like *250 ft north-east of you*. The answer also says how sure your phone is.

The app asks your phone where it is only when you tap **How far am I?**. Your location stays on your phone. The app does not save it or send it.

The line under the buttons says who set the place and when. A machine moves and a gate gets rebuilt. If the place looks wrong, say so in a note.

With no signal the picture may not load. The task says so. **How far am I?** still works, since your phone finds itself without a signal.

A phone that gives only the area you are in gets no distance. The answer then reads *Precise Location looks off.*, with the steps to the switch for your phone. Turn the switch on and tap **How far am I?** again.

### When the way in is painted

Some places come with a painted map. An orange stroke sits beside the place's name.

Open the task. The picture shows the orange line that leads to the spot. A line under the picture names the map and says about how long it is.

- {{button:blue|Directions to the start}} opens your phone's maps app at the start of the line. The road gets you to the gate. The line takes you from there.
- {{button:outline|Open the map}} opens the painted map. It shows every place on the line, with its words and photo.

You can add a place you find to the map. [Add a place](/help/paint-a-map#add-a-place) shows how.

### When a task comes with a map

Some work is a line and not a spot, like a trench or a run of pipe. That task shows an orange stroke under its title. Beside it are the map's name, about how long it is and how many places it has.

Open the task. The picture shows the whole line from above. Under it, each numbered place is named.

- {{button:blue|Open the map}} opens the painted map. It shows every place on the line, with its words and photo.
- {{button:outline|Directions to the start}} opens your phone's maps app at the start of the line.
- {{button:outline|How far am I?}} says how far the start is from you. Your location stays on your phone.

A tap on the picture opens the map too. You can add a place you find to it.

## Add to a task's map

A task that has a painted map wears a lit **🗺 Map** press. Press it and the map opens. Press **Draw a line** to trace a line with your finger. Press **＋ Place** to leave a point with a name, words and a photo. What you add is saved on the map for everyone who can open the task.

## Draw a map for a task that has none

A task with no map shows a plain **🗺 Map** press. Press it and pick how to start.

- **Draw it by hand** lets you trace a line with your finger.
- **Walk or drive it** paints the line as you go. It shows on a phone that can say where it is.
- **Leave a point** lets you tap one spot and give it a name, words and a photo.

Save in the map window. When you close it, the map goes on the task and the press lights up. The person who gave you the task can open the map. A map closed with nothing saved changes nothing.

## Add a place to a task that has none

Some tasks come with no place. If you are standing at the task, you can add its place yourself.

1. Tap the task to open it.
2. Tap **Standing at this task? Add its place**. Your phone looks up where you are.
3. Read the line that says how sure your phone is. Type a name, like *Shop bay 2*.
4. Tap {{button:blue|Add this place}}.

A phone that is not sure within about 150 feet adds no place. Step into the open and tap **Try again**.

A line that reads *Precise Location looks off.* means your phone gave only an area. The open sky does not fix that. Follow the steps under the line, then tap **Try again**.

The next person who gets this task sees the place and can get directions.

- Your phone has to be sure within about 150 feet. If it is not, step out into the open and tap **Try again**.
- This saves where you stand as the task's place. The app does not follow you after that.
- A task that already has a place does not show this line. If its place is wrong, say so in a note.

## See all my tasks on a map

When an open task of yours has a place, the Today tab opens on a map. The map is called **Where my tasks are**. It sits above the list, in Task Mode too.

- Each place is a dot with a number. The same number sits beside the place's name on the task below.
- A red dot means a task there is overdue.
- Tasks at one place share a dot. The dot shows the first number and a plus, like *2+*.
- Tap a dot to see its tasks under the map. Tap {{button:outline|Show the task}} and the list scrolls to that task and opens it. {{button:blue|Directions}} takes you to the place.
- Tasks with no place are not on the map. A line under the map counts them, and they are still in the list.

Tap {{button:outline|Show where I am}} to add a dark dot for you. Each task's line then says how far away its place is, like *250 ft north-east*. Your location stays on your phone.

A phone that gives only an area gets no dot and no distances. The map reads *Precise Location looks off.* and lists the steps to the switch.

Tap a dot, then tap **Time** on the map to see that place on earlier days. [See a place on a map on an earlier day](/help/see-a-place-on-an-earlier-day) shows how.

**Satellite** shows the ground from above. **Streets** shows roads. Tap **Hide map** to fold the map away. Your phone remembers, and **Show map** brings it back.

## Notes are a conversation

Tap the note press to open the card's thread. The press reads **＋ Note**, or the count once there are notes. You'll see every note anyone has left, plus the card's history, in order. The history shows completed, reopened and signed off. Type in the box and hit **Post** to add yours.

## When a card comes back

If whoever assigned the task sends it back, the card shows an amber strip:

:::example A reopened card
Reopened by Robert · 9:05 AM
"Breaker keeps tripping, check the wire gauge before re-mounting."
:::

Fix it up, reply on the thread if useful, and check it off again.

## After you complete

Under your finished task a line says when you did it and who hears. It reads like *Done 12:22 PM. Imani hears it is done.* When that person signs off, the line reads *Signed off by Imani*. Nothing more is needed from you.

Right after you finish a task, a green line reads *Add a photo or a note for Imani if it helps.* The two presses sit beside it. Skip them when there is nothing to add.

## Add your own task

Found something that needs doing? Press {{button:outline|＋ Add a task for me}} under today's tasks. The **New task** window opens with you picked and today lit. Type the task and send it. The task lands on your list.

Finished a task and it turned up more work? Press **Follow-up** on the finished task. The window opens on that task's words, and you type what comes next. A task that had a place brings its place along. The person who gave you the first task hears when the follow-up is done.

[Give someone a task](/help/give-someone-a-task) covers the rest of the window.

## When yesterday's task was not ticked

A daily or weekly task that is not ticked drops off your list the next morning. The next day the top of Today asks about it once, under a label like *Yesterday · 2 not ticked*.

- Press **Did it** when you did the work and forgot the tick. That day is marked done.
- Press **Skipped** when it did not get done. The task leaves the question.

Either answer keeps the record right, and History shows a skipped day as **Skipped**. The question looks back three days, so Monday asks about Friday.

## Choose what to do first

The **Next** card is the first open task in the order the office set. Want to start on another one? Press **⋯ More** on that task, then **Do first**. The task moves into **Next**, and a line under the label reads *You picked this to do first.*

Your pick is kept on this phone or computer. Nobody else sees it. To go back to the list's own order, press **⋯ More** on the Next card, then **Stop doing it first**.

## Move a task to another day

Cannot do a one-time task today? Press **⋯ More** on the task, then **Not today**. Pick **Tomorrow**, **Monday**, or **Pick a day**. A green line says what will happen, like *It moves to Fri, Nov 20. Imani is told.* Then press the blue press, which names the day.

The task keeps its place in your list, with its notes and photos. Its row then reads the new due day and stops reading late. The person who gave you the task gets a message. A row that was moved says so, like *moved twice*.

A task that repeats has no **Not today**. A missed day of a repeating task drops off by itself.

## Say you cannot do a task

Something in the way? Press **⋯ More** on the task, then **Can't do it**. Press what is in the way, like **Waiting on a part**, or type your own words. The press names who is told, like {{button:blue|Tell Imani}}.

The task stays yours. It wears an amber line, like *Stuck: Waiting on a part*, and sits under a **Stuck** label below the tasks you can do. The person who gave you the task gets a message, and the office sees every stuck task in one list.

When the way is clear, press **⋯ More**, then **No longer stuck**. The office can also press **Sorted**, and you get a message. Finishing the task takes the mark off too.

## Hand a task to someone else

Is the task for someone else? Press **⋯ More** on the task, then **Hand it on**. Press **Back to Imani** to give it back to the person who gave it. Or type a name in **Find a person** and press that person. You can say why in the box below. The press names who takes it, like {{button:blue|Hand it to Hal}}.

The task goes as it is. Its notes, its place and its photos go with it. The person who takes it gets a message. So does the person who gave it, when someone else takes it.

A task that repeats is handed on for that day alone, and stays yours after. A roadmap task is handed on from the Roadmap.

## Close a task that no longer applies

Some tasks stop mattering. The work got done another way, or the plans changed. Press **⋯ More** on the task, then **No longer applies**. Press a reason, or type your own. Then press {{button:blue|Close this task}}.

The task leaves your list. Under **Done today** its line says it was closed, like *Closed 12:22 PM as no longer applying*, with your reason after it. The person who gave you the task gets a message. That person can put the task back. You can put it back yourself from **History**.

A task that repeats cannot be closed this way. Neither can a roadmap task or a vehicle task. For those, say why in a note.

## Still open: older work that still needs doing

Below your Today list you may see a **Still open** list. It holds one-off tasks whose day came and went without getting done. Each row says when the task was given, like *given Thu, Jul 30*. A pill at the end of the row shows its age, like *20 days*. The pill turns {{chip:yellow|amber}} after a week. A task with a due day that has passed shows a {{chip:red|red}} pill, like *3 days late*.

Check a task off right there when you knock it out. Or tap the row to read its notes first. The open row also holds **⋯ More**.

A long list is quicker to clear one task at a time. Press **Go through them** beside the list's name. Each task comes up by itself with its answers. Press **Done**, **Still on it**, **Can't do it**, **No longer applies** or **Skip**. A skipped task stays as it is. At the end a line says what changed, like *You sorted 3. 5 are as they were.* The list shows three rows and folds the rest. Press **Show 5 more** to see them all. Daily and weekly tasks never pile up here. Only work that's still genuinely waiting does.

Nothing escalates on its own. An overdue one-off doesn't page anyone or move up someone else's list. What the office does see is its age. On Checklist → Manage the open one-offs sort oldest first. A chip turns {{chip:yellow|amber}} after a week and {{chip:red|red}} after a month. A **🔔 Remind** from whoever assigned it is a person deciding to nudge you, not a timer.

## Looking back: the History tab

On your phone, **Checklist → History** shows your days newest-first. Each day says how many you finished. Every task carries a chip. {{chip:green|✓ 7:58 AM}} means done. {{chip:yellow|✓ by Maria}} means someone covered it. The other chips are {{chip:red|✗ Missed}} and {{chip:yellow|Reopened}}. The strip up top shows your week percentage and streak. Tap any task to read its whole story and add a note.

On a computer, History is two lists. **Repeating** tasks keep the day-by-day grid. Each row starts the day the task was created. It reads *since 6/14* under the title, with a blue tick on its first day. So there are no empty boxes for days a task didn't exist yet. If you have no repeating task in the months picked, one line says so in place of the grid. **Show 12 months** beside it looks back a full year. **One-off tasks** sit below as a simple ledger. A row reads {{chip:green|✓ done}} with created → done dates, {{chip:red|✗ missed}}, or open.

## Sending a task back yourself

Assigned to a task someone else marked done, but it isn't? On **History**, tap the row. Write a note saying why. The note is required. Then tap {{button:amber|Reopen}}. Whoever completed it gets notified with your reason. The task returns to **Today → Outstanding** until it's actually finished.

## ⏳ Waiting For

Roadmap tasks that are yours but not yet ready show in **Waiting For**. They are grayed, with no checkbox. On Checklist → **Today** it's its own section. On the Dashboard it's a quiet folded strip at the foot of the **My Inbox** card. The strip shows the count plus a peek at the first blocker. Tap to unfold. They're grouped under the step that's holding them. Each group names that task once, with who's on it and the stage. Your waiting tasks are listed beneath in the order they'll unlock. A small *then* marks the ones queued behind another. Only the first three groups show. Tap **…and N more steps ahead** for the rest. The moment a step ahead is done, your task moves onto your list on its own.
