Every lien letter we mail ends with a phone number, so owners call. This guide gives you the words for that call, one card at a time.

The owner is usually worried and sometimes angry. They did nothing wrong. They paid their builder. You do not need to know the Lien desk, the statute or the attorney's memo to take that call well. The **☎** button gives you the words, one card at a time, in the order the conversation goes. The find box on the list finds the letter they are holding. This guide shows you how to reach both and when to press them. It also says what to do with what they tell you.

## When to press it

You press it the moment you realise the caller is the **owner of a property**. They are talking about a letter from us. Press it before you answer anything past hello. You will hear one of these:

- "I got a letter from you about a **lien**" / "a **Notice of Claim**" / "something about **retainage**".
- "My builder says you sent me something."
- "**Am I being sued?**" / "What is this?"
- "I already **paid** my builder."
- "Can I just **pay you** and make this go away?"

It is for the **owner**, not the GC, the general contractor. If the builder is the one calling, the door will say so beside their name. A GC's call goes to the master, not to this sheet. A customer calling about an ordinary **bill** is not a lien call either. Handle that the way you always do.

## Where it is

1. Open the **Lien desk**. Open it from Jobs → Pipeline → the Collections header {{button:outline|Lien desk}}. Or open Dashboard → Needs you, the card that names the next lien deadline, and press **Open the Lien desk**.
2. On the desk's header, next to {{button:outline|§ Rules}}, press the {{button:blue|☎}} button. Its hint reads *An owner is calling*.

You can also reach the same sheet from a job you already have open on the desk. Pick the job in **Sent**. The footer has {{button:outline|Record the owner's call…}}.

## What the button opens

The button opens the words at once. Nothing is typed first. The first card is counsel's opening line, with the builder's name filled in. Under it are the six things owners say, as chips. Press the chip that matches and counsel's next line appears, with the rule behind it. Read it, then say it.

When a job is picked on the desk, the top line shows the letter in their hand. It names the day it mailed, the amount, the months and the affidavit day. {{button:blue|Record the call ›}} opens the call sheet on that notice. The sheet keeps what they tell you. With no job picked, the words still read, and **Find the job ›** puts you in the find box.

## Find the letter they are holding

The find box sits at the top of the list. Type whatever they give you. That can be the **job number** printed at the top of the notice. It can be the **street**, the **owner's name** or the **GC's name**. The piles narrow as you type.

A letter older than thirty days is not on the piles. The box still finds it. Those appear under **Also sent**, below the piles, each with the day it mailed. Press one and the call sheet opens on that notice. The § 53.056 notice is the Texas form we send the owner and the GC for each unpaid month.

:::example What a match looks like
**Priya Natarajan · owner of 9703 Lenox Hl, San Antonio** — {{chip:green|Sent Sep 22}} *§ 53.056 notice · residential letter · mailed Sep 22 · $7,902.00 for June, July and August 2026 · GC RMC · job 273 · signed Robert Douglas* — {{button:outline|Open the call ›}}
:::

A **GC's name** finds every job of theirs. If the GC itself is the one calling, the call goes to the master.

If **nothing matches**:

- Ask them to read you the **street** on the letter, or the **job number** at the top of the notice.
- The job may not be on the Lien desk at all. The desk holds jobs with an unpaid balance and a lien deadline. Search the Pipeline for any other job.
- They may be sure they hold a notice from us. Then it may have gone out **by hand** and never been recorded on the desk. Take their name, the street and a number to call back. Tell the master. Record the notice first. The guide [send lien notices from the Lien desk](?g=send-lien-notices-from-the-lien-desk) shows how, under *A notice sent by hand*. Do not guess at the letter's contents.

## Practice before a real call

At the bottom of the words, **Take a practice call ›** opens the same call sheet on a made-up letter. The letter went to Pat Sample, a homeowner. The builder is Sample Builders. Every card, chip and reply works, and the sentence at the bottom builds as you tap. There is no Save and nothing is written anywhere, so tap freely. **Start over ↺** goes back to the first card. {{button:blue|Done practicing}} closes it.

## Read the sheet

- **The top line is the letter they hold.** It says which notice, mailed when, for how much, under which GC, and who signed it. Glance at it so your words match their paper.
- **Say the green line.** It is written to be read aloud. The small **ⓘ** beside it holds the statute cites for your own reference. Do not read those out.
- **Tap what they say.** Under the line are a few replies in the owner's own words. They read like *"I already paid RMC — everything"*, *"There's still a payment to RMC"* or *"I'd have to check"*. Tap the closest one and the next card opens. Some replies open a small box first. It asks how much is left, the day the 10% went to the builder, or the day the builder finished. The builder may be *still going*. Type what they tell you, then {{button:blue|Next ›}}.
- **The chips at the top jump.** The owner may change the subject, from "I paid" to "can I pay you?". Tap that chip and the sheet goes with them. *‹ Back* undoes a tap.
- **The sentence at the bottom is the record.** It builds as you go: *Priya paid RMC in full. They let the 10% go to RMC on Sep 10, 19 days before the hold ended. RMC finished Aug 30.* Check it reads like what they told you.

You are not asking the owner three legal questions. The cards ask three things. What do they still owe the builder? Did the ten percent stay with them or go out, and when? When did the builder finish? Those three facts decide what happens next. The cards ask them in plain words at the natural moment.

## What you may and may not say

The green lines are the attorney's words, arranged for the phone. Staying on them keeps us right. In particular:

- **Say** these things. You did nothing wrong by paying the builder. You didn't hire us. This is not a lawsuit. Here is what you may hold back. Here is what happens next and when. The moment we're paid you get a release the same day.
- **Never say** these things. The last card lists them. Do not mention the builder's **other jobs** or say "they don't pay their subs". Do not mention **interest, fees** or theft of service. Do not say **the law makes you pay us**. Do not offer a **joint check**. Do not say they are **in default**.
- **Do not take a payment on the phone.** If they want to pay us directly, the card says it: *that's your decision, call me back before you write anything*. The master needs the attorney's sign-off on that job before we accept an owner's check.
- **Do not promise a release** except the one the card promises. That is the day funds clear.
- If they ask something the cards don't cover, don't improvise the law. Say *"Let me have Robert call you today"*. Then tap **Wants a call back**.

## After the call

1. Add a **note** on the last card if anything would help the master. One might read *"very calm — thought paying RMC ended it"*. Then tap {{button:blue|Save the call}}.
2. Saving writes the call onto the notice. The desk's footer, the Affidavits tab and the GC run's grid read it. The job sorts into the attorney's **pile**. Pile A is an owner who still owes the builder. Pile B paid but never held the 10%. Pile C paid in full and holds nothing. *Letter two* switches off. *Wants to pay us → counsel* flags the sign-off.
3. **Tell the master the same day** in three cases. The owner wants to pay us directly. The builder has gone quiet on them too. Anything surprised you. The sheet records it. The master decides it.

If the owner calls again, open the same sheet. It shows the last call at the top. The new one overwrites it.
