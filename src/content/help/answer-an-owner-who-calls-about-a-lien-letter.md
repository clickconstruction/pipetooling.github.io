Every lien letter we mail ends with a phone number, so owners call. They are usually worried, sometimes angry, and they did nothing wrong — they paid their builder. You do not need to know the Lien desk, the statute or the attorney's memo to take that call well. **☎ Someone's calling ›** finds the letter they are holding and gives you the words, one card at a time, in the order the conversation actually goes. This guide is how to reach it, when to press it, and what to do with what they tell you.

## When to press it

Press it the moment you realise the caller is the **owner of a property** talking about a letter from us — before you answer anything past hello. You will hear one of these:

- "I got a letter from you about a **lien**" / "a **Notice of Claim**" / "something about **retainage**".
- "My builder says you sent me something."
- "**Am I being sued?**" / "What is this?"
- "I already **paid** my builder."
- "Can I just **pay you** and make this go away?"

It is for the **owner**, not the GC. If the builder is the one calling, the door will say so beside their name — a GC's call goes to the master, not to this sheet. A customer calling about an ordinary **bill** is not a lien call either; handle that the way you always do.

## Where it is

1. Open the **Lien desk**: Jobs → Pipeline → the Collections header {{button:outline|⏱ Lien desk}}, or Dashboard → Needs you {{chip:red|N lien notices to draft}}.
2. On the desk's header, next to {{button:outline|§ The rules}}, press {{button:blue|☎ Someone's calling ›}}.

You can also reach the same sheet from a job you already have open on the desk: pick the job in **Sent**, and the footer has {{button:outline|Record the owner's call…}}.

## Find the letter they are holding

Type whatever they give you — their **name**, the **street**, or the **job number** printed at the top of the notice — and the notices we have sent line up:

:::example What a match looks like
**Priya Natarajan · owner of 9703 Lenox Hl, San Antonio** — *§ 53.056 notice · residential letter · mailed Sep 22 · $7,902.00 for June, July and August 2026 · GC RMC · job 273 · signed Robert Douglas* — {{button:outline|Open the call sheet ›}}
:::

Pick the row and the sheet opens. If **nothing matches**:

- Ask them to read you the **street** on the letter, or the **job number** at the top of the notice — one word is enough, and fewer words match more.
- If it still finds nothing, the notice may have gone out **by hand** and never been recorded on the desk. Take their name, the street and a number to call back, tell the master, and record the notice first ([send lien notices from the Lien desk](?g=send-lien-notices-from-the-lien-desk) → *A notice sent by hand*). Do not guess at the letter's contents.

## Read the sheet

- **The top line is the letter they hold** — which notice, mailed when, for how much, under which GC, who signed it. Glance at it so your words match their paper.
- **Say the green line.** It is written to be read aloud. The small **ⓘ** beside it holds the statute cites for your own reference — do not read those out.
- **Tap what they say.** Under the line are two or three replies in the owner's own words — *"I already paid RMC — everything"*, *"There's still a payment to RMC"*, *"I'd have to check"*. Tap the closest one and the next card opens. Some replies open a small box first (about how much is left; the day the 10% went to the builder; the day the builder finished, or *still going*) — type what they tell you, then {{button:blue|Next ›}}.
- **The chips at the top jump.** If the owner changes the subject — from "I paid" to "can I pay you?" — tap that chip and the sheet goes with them. *‹ Back* undoes a tap.
- **The sentence at the bottom is the record**, building as you go: *Priya paid RMC in full. They let the 10% go to RMC on Sep 10, 19 days before the hold ended. RMC finished Aug 30.* Check it reads like what they told you.

You are not asking the owner three legal questions. The cards ask about what they still owe the builder, whether the ten percent stayed with them or went out (and when), and when the builder finished — because those three facts decide what happens next — but they come up in plain words at the natural moment.

## What you may and may not say

The green lines are the attorney's words, arranged for the phone. Staying on them keeps us right. In particular:

- **Say**: you did nothing wrong by paying the builder; you didn't hire us; this is not a lawsuit; here is what you may hold back; here is what happens next and when; the moment we're paid you get a release the same day.
- **Never say** — the last card lists them: the builder's **other jobs** or "they don't pay their subs"; **interest, fees** or theft of service; that **the law makes you pay us**; a **joint check**; that they are **in default**.
- **Do not take a payment on the phone.** If they want to pay us directly, the card says it: *that's your decision, call me back before you write anything.* The master needs the attorney's sign-off on that job before we accept an owner's check.
- **Do not promise a release** except the one the card promises — the day funds clear.
- If they ask something the cards don't cover, don't improvise the law: *"Let me have Robert call you today"* — then tap **Wants a call back**.

## After the call

1. Add a **note** on the last card if anything would help the master ("very calm — thought paying RMC ended it"), then {{button:blue|Save the call}}.
2. Saving writes the call onto the notice. The desk's footer, the Affidavits tab and the GC run's grid read it: the job sorts into the attorney's **pile** (A owner still owes the builder · B paid but never held the 10% · C paid in full, holds nothing), *letter two* switches off, and *wants to pay us → counsel* flags the sign-off.
3. **Tell the master the same day** when the owner wants to pay us directly, when the builder has gone quiet on them too, or when anything surprised you. The sheet records it; the master decides it.

If the owner calls again, open the same sheet — it shows the last call at the top, and the new one overwrites it.
