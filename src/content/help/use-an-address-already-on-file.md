You pick a job's address the way the company already writes it. Then every job at that place stays together.

One place typed two ways reads as two places. Its jobs stop matching each other. The address box on **New Job** and **Edit Job** helps you avoid that.

## Pick it while you type

1. Open **New Job**, or a job's **Edit** tab.
2. Start typing in **Job Address**. Three characters are enough.
3. Look at the top of the list, under **Already on file**.
4. Press the address you mean.

Each row says how many jobs and bids are at that address. It names the development when they share one. The rows under **A new address** come from Google, as before.

:::example You type "118 old quar"
The list opens with **Already on file**. The first row reads "118 Old Quarry Trl, Sampleton, TX" and "25 jobs". Press it and the box takes that exact spelling.
:::

Under the box a green line then reads *On file*. It says how many jobs are at this address.

## When you typed it a little differently

Sometimes you type an address by hand and it is one letter off. Leave the box and the form asks once.

- It shows what you typed beside the address on file.
- The words that differ are marked.
- {{button:blue|Use the one on file}} changes the box to the spelling on file.
- {{button:gray|Keep mine}} leaves your address as it is.

Keeping yours makes a new place. You can still save either way. The form never stops you.

## When the map puts it at the same spot

Some addresses are written differently and sit at the same spot on the map. A unit, a building or a barn beside the house can read that way.

Leave the box. Once the map has placed your address, the form asks **Is this the same place?**

- The question says how many jobs and bids are on file at that spot, and under which address.
- {{button:blue|Same place, use that spelling}} changes the box to the address on file.
- {{button:gray|Same place, keep mine}} leaves your address and records that the two are one place.
- {{button:gray|Not the same}} records that they are two places.

:::example A second building
You type "412 Sample Canyon Dr Building 16". Three jobs are on file at that spot as "412 Sample Canyon Dr Building 20". Press **Not the same** if each building is its own place.
:::

Your answer is kept. Nobody is asked about that pair again, here or in **Addresses that look alike**.

After **Same place, keep mine**, a green line under the box names the address yours is kept with.

You can still save without answering. The form asks the office only, since the office's answer is the one kept.

## What it does not ask about

A different house number is a different place. So is a different unit or building number. The form never calls those a slip.

The form may still ask whether two such addresses are the same place, as above. The form asks only when the map puts them at one spot.

For two jobs that are already at one address, see [combine duplicate jobs](/help/combine-duplicate-jobs). For one place already on file under two spellings, see [answer addresses that look alike](/help/answer-addresses-that-look-alike).
