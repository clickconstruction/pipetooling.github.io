The Bids → Pricing tab has two layouts. You switch them with the Old and New pills on the selected bid.

The pills are {{chip:gray|Old}} and {{chip:gray|New}}. Old is the classic grid. **New is the Workbench**, built for pricing to a target. The tab opens on **New**. You flip to Old and your device remembers the choice.

The pills sit right beside the bid's title. The **?** next to them is the one help door. It is a short card that covers the page in four lines. The lines are typing prices, the solver, what this GC sees, and labor & cost. GC means the general contractor. It has **▶ Take the tour**. The tour spotlights each part of the Workbench in order, right on your bid. Its footer has a link back to this guide.

## GCs, prices, and the star

One sentence covers it. **Versions draft this bid for different GCs. Price options send more than one price to the same GC.** The **Send to** strip at the top shows one packet per GC. Each packet has its own counts, takeoff, prices, send date and answer. A takeoff is the list of materials counted from the plans. {{button:blue|＋ Add GC}} starts a new packet as a copy of one you already have. Inside a packet, the Workbench shows that GC's **price options**. The {{chip:green|★ base}} is what the GC sees. Cover Letter, Share, Print, CSV and the bid value all use it. Any other priced option can be **offered** to the same GC as an alternate from the card's bottom bar. An alternate is a price they may take instead of the base. It lands on their letter as an alternate section. Every card's bottom bar says who sees it. It reads *★ The price on their letter*, *On their letter · alternate*, or *Only you see this*. Everything else is yours to compare.

Most bids have one GC and one price. So the Workbench shows just one quiet line: your price, the star, and {{button:outline|＋ Add price}}. That button asks what you want. There are four answers.

- **Another price for this GC**. You name it. You offer it as an alternate right away, or you keep it to compare. It is the same takeoff with different numbers.
- **📐 Alternate with its own takeoff**. This is for "in lieu of" work that changes materials, like PEX for copper or cast iron for PVC. It starts as a copy of the bid's counts, takeoff and prices. It lands on their letter as an alternate. Its margin is calculated from **its own materials**. The card shows them with a "vs base" delta, the difference from the base. *open its takeoff →* jumps straight in to swap parts.
- **Another GC** opens the same GC-first modal as the strip. You pick the GC and which packet to start from.
- **Adopt an existing bid** pulls a bid that is already on the board in as one of this bid's packets. Its counts, takeoff, prices and sent history come along. Its old board row retires. Nothing is deleted, and the number still looks up.

Labor and the bid-wide direct costs are shared by the whole bid. An own-takeoff alternate changes materials and revenue, not labor. Once a second price or packet exists, the full structure bar appears. It reads **This GC**, ***Price options — what {{chip:gray|GC}} receives***, and **Labor & cost**.

Need the whole picture at once? The {{button:outline|Versions}} button sits at the end of the **Send to** strip, on Counts, Takeoffs, Pricing, and Cover Letter. It opens a tree of the entire bid. That is every GC packet, its versions, and every price option. Each price shows its value, its margin, and who sees it: ★ on their letter, alternate, or only you. It also shows sent dates and won or lost per GC. You click any price in the map to open it on the Pricing tab. That is viewing only, so the GC still sees their ★.

Clicking a price card just **views** it. The ★ only moves when you press **☆ make base** on a card's bottom bar and confirm. Are you viewing a different price and pressing **Share**, **Print**, or **CSV**? One question comes first. It asks for the GC's ★ price, the default, or the one you are viewing, for a teammate to check. The view never switches. A card showing {{chip:yellow|No prices yet}} cannot be starred yet. It offers a one-click **copy prices from** a priced option. Or you price it with the solver. A new packet or version starts with a copy of **every price option** on the version it was made from. Names and offers stay intact. The ★ sits on the copy of the source's ★. A packet with no prices yet says **No prices yet for {{chip:gray|GC}}** and offers to copy them. The **Counts** tab shows the same Send to strip. Each packet has its own count list. Need a same-GC variant with its own takeoff, a VE say? VE means value engineering, a cheaper way to build the same thing. You press **+ version** inside that GC's group on the strip.

## Price from the book

The Workbench reads your price book directly. A **Book entry** column on every row shows the assigned entry, its name and book price. The book's price flows straight into the sale column. Or the column shows an **assign…** search. It opens pre-filled with the row's name, minus any "ft of" style prefix. The dropdown **highlights the characters that matched**. So you can see exactly why each entry is in the list. A **Similar | Exact** toggle sits in its corner. **Similar** matches **word by word**. Entries containing the most of your words rank first. So a row like *ft of 2" water line* surfaces *feet of water line* without retyping. **Exact** keeps only entries containing every word you typed. The toggle is remembered on your device. A word that matches nothing in the book is called out. When Exact finds nothing, one tap shows the similar matches instead. Nothing quite right? **+ Add "…" to the book** sits at the foot of every search. It opens a new entry pre-filled with what you typed. It saves it into this bid's book. It is searchable immediately, and priced onto a same-named row. You pick one, and the row is priced. Typing over a book price still works and becomes an override.

Above the table sits {{button:blue|Fill N matching from book}}. One click assigns every unassigned row whose name **exactly** matches a book entry. The button counts the matches before you press it. When nothing is left to fill it says so plainly: {{button:blue|0 unassigned rows match the book}}. That means the remaining rows are already assigned or need the per-row search. The chip beside it names the book you are drawing from. It is the **one door to the book**. An older bid may have been priced straight on a shared book, before bids kept their own copy. It opens on **that** book. Its assigned entries and typed prices show even when your default for new bids is a different book. A version split from it starts from that book too. **The first price you assign freezes the bid.** A new bid shows your book's prices as they stand today, but owns nothing yet. You assign a row, type a price, fill from the book or apply the solver. At that moment the bid takes its **own copy** of that book. A toast says so. From then on, an edit to the shared book never moves this bid's numbers. Once a bid has been **marked sent**, a line under the revenue strip keeps both numbers in view. The same line sits under the Cover Letter total. It reads *Sent Jul 1 at $379,895.70 · the book prices it at $385,506.07 today (+$5,610.37)*. It is amber when they differ, and quiet when they agree. The sent number is what the customer was quoted. The grid is what the book says now. On older bids the copy was frozen after the send, so the two can drift. **A sent bid is locked**. A {{chip:gray|🔒 Sent Jul 1 · pricing locked}} chip sits beside that line. Assigning, typing, brushing, filling from the book, the solver's Apply and *Use $X on this bid* all refuse. Each shows a reminder of the sent date. You press {{button:outline|Revise…}} to unlock it for this browser session. The chip turns amber and reads *Revising a bid sent Jul 1*. Every change you make is on purpose. {{button:outline|Lock again}} closes it. The sent number never changes either way.

## The price book drawer

You click the book chip, {{chip:gray|WENDI · 111 entries ▸}}. The price book slides in beside the table. It is the whole book, without leaving the rows it prices. The old **▶ Price book** section at the page bottom is gone. So is its unused "This version's prices" mode.

- **Your book, front and center**: the row shows just your book, with ★ on the one feeding this bid. It has a **›**. You press it to reveal the others, and **‹** tucks them back. Clicking another book just **looks inside it**. The bid keeps pricing from its ★ book until you press {{button:blue|Use … on this bid}}. That switches the bid to that book. It also makes it **your default for new bids**. The line under the chips says so. Browsing never changes the bid. **Add book** creates a new one. ✎ renames.
- **This bid has its own copy of the book.** The first time a bid is priced, it takes a **copy** of the book it is priced from. It does the same when you pick a book from the chip. The copy has the same name and its own prices. So nobody's edit to the shared book can move the numbers on a bid you have already sent. The line under the book chips names that copy. On a bid that has not been priced yet it says *no copy yet*. The first price you assign takes one. It also means an edit in this drawer changes the shared book. It does not change the bid in front of you.
- **Carrying an edit across to this bid**: you change a price here with a bid open. The drawer says so. It reads *"This bid still prices Ft of Water Line at $12"*. {{button:blue|Use $13.00 on this bid}} sits beside it. One press writes the new price into this bid's copies. That is the one you are viewing. It is also **any other price option on this bid still holding the same old price**. The banner counts them. A price option you have re-priced on purpose is left alone. Every row already assigned to that entry re-prices on the spot. **Leave this bid alone** keeps the bid as it is. You add an entry the bid's copy has never seen. The same offer appears as **Add it to this bid too**. You take it, and the entry starts turning up in the row {{chip:gray|assign…}} searches. The price offer only appears when you are editing the book this bid actually prices from. Adding works from any book. Other bids are never touched either way. Each carries its own copy.
- **Combined price ⇄ Stage price**: Combined shows one price per entry. Stage shows Rough In / Top Out / Trim Set. A dim *—* shows where a stage is zero. You hover the name for the combined total.
- **Editing follows the mode**: ✎ in Stage mode offers all three stages. In Combined mode it offers one **Price**. That lands in **Rough In**, so a quick combined price is never lost. Flipping to Stage shows exactly where it sits.
- Search filters the entries. **Add entry** sits beside the Combined/Stage toggle and works in either mode. Esc or ✕ closes.

## Price to a number

1. Your **Revenue, Our cost, Profit, and Margin** stay pinned at the top. They move as you work. The bid may carry an **alternate**. That is a group marked on the Counts tab, the section a customer wants priced with and without. Then a second line reads **Base**, **+ Break room**, **With the alternate**, and the alternate's own margin. The + Break room number is what that alternate adds. Every row in the alternate wears a small **ALT** mark. Price those rows like any other. The + number moves with the whole.
2. The solver lives behind the blue {{button:blue|Solver ›}}. Press it and the controls unfold inside a **blue ring**. **‹** folds them away. Open or closed is remembered on your device. Inside, type a **margin**, or drag the slider from 20 to 95. The slider **re-prices live as you drag**. Totals, proposals, the landing chip, **and the "or total" box** all track the thumb. So you can watch the ideal total rise and fall. Then step straight into the box to fine-tune it. Clicking in selects the number for type-to-replace. While you are in it the slider leaves it alone. Small ticks under the track mark the markup multiples for reference. They are **2** at 50%, **3** at 66%, **4** at 75%, **5** at 80%. Either way it prices your costed rows at that margin, overhead included. Prices you set by hand on rows with no Takeoffs cost keep their value. They **stack on top** of the bid. A blue chip tucked under the totals names the solve you are on, like *70% on 16 costed rows*. The strip right above it shows where the bid lands. A second number runs above the slider whenever no-cost rows carry revenue. That revenue has no cost against it. Or type a **target bid total** and press Enter. That number is for the **whole bid**, hand-priced rows included. So the bid lands at what you typed. The **▾ beside Solver** holds *Price unpriced only*. It fills only rows with no sale price. Priced rows are held as-is.
3. **📌 Pin** any row to hold its price while the rest re-solve. Fixed-price rows are always held. Rows with no Takeoffs cost are never auto-priced. While a solve is pending, a banner counts them. It reads *23/39 have no cost: their $26,156.17 is unaffected*. It can filter to just those rows.
4. The **ⓘ at the end of any row** opens its **Margin breakdown**. That is sale, materials, tax, labor, profit, and margin for that fixture. It has jump chips: ***# Counts · 📐 Takeoffs · 🛠 Labor***. They take you straight to the tab a number comes from, bid in hand. They land on **that fixture's row**, scrolled into view and flashed for a moment. So it is clear where you are. The row may not exist yet over there, with no labor row or no assembly. Then the tab still opens and a note says so. Only the ⓘ opens it. So clicking around the row while you price never pops it up.
5. The **margin brush** lives left of Solver ›. Press **🖌 Margin ›** and a purple ring unfolds. It has the margin box and your recent margins, with the loaded one lit solid purple. Your cursor becomes the brush. You **press and sweep across rows**. Every row the brush crosses is priced at that margin the instant it is touched. One motion, many rows, totals moving as you go. Rows you have held with 📌, fixed-price rows, and rows with no Takeoffs cost are never painted. You let go and the sweep saves in one batch. **↩ Undo sweep** takes the whole stroke back. **‹** or Esc puts the brush down. The margin math is the same as everywhere else. It is cost divided by one minus the margin, in whole dollars. The margin you sweep with becomes your top recent chip.

## Typed prices save themselves; the solver previews

**A price you type is saved the moment you press Enter or leave the field**. A quick green *saved ✓* confirms it. There is nothing to Apply. It is the same as every other grid.

**Typing is overwrite, not surgery.** Clicking or tabbing into a **Sale price/unit** box selects the number that is there, however slow the click. So what you type replaces it, decimals and all. Esc puts the old number back. Tabbing through without typing changes nothing. At rest the box reads as money, like {{chip:gray|$1,410.00}}. The moment you are in it, it is just the number.

**Solver results preview first.** A solve re-prices many rows at once. So each proposal appears as a **purple price with an arrow to the left of the row's current price**. It looks like {{chip:purple|$640.00 →}}. The current price stays put, struck through, since it is what the proposal would replace. The card outlines purple. {{button:blue|Apply 12}} and **Discard** appear at the line's end, with a count of what is previewed beneath. Nothing is saved until you hit Apply. The button counts what it will write. Discard throws the whole preview away.

**Click a purple proposal to drop just that row.** It flips red with an ✕, like {{chip:red|$1,335.00 ✕}}. That means Apply will leave that row's price alone. The count on the Apply button and the strip's note keep score. You click it again to bring the proposal back. Clicked-off rows stay clicked off while you keep tuning the margin. So you can shape the solve row by row before committing. Typing your own price into a previewed row saves your number immediately. It takes that row out of the preview.

**Revenue, Profit, and Margin are inputs too.** You click any of the three and type. The sale price/unit follows as you type. Revenue spreads across the count. Profit adds the row's cost back. Margin solves cost divided by one minus the margin. Enter or leaving the field saves it exactly like a typed price, with *saved ✓*. Esc walks away without saving. Rows with no Takeoffs cost have no profit or margin to edit.

**Solver previews wait on this device.** You visit any page, reload, close the tab, or come back tomorrow. The preview is sitting where you left it. One from an earlier sitting says when it is from: *solve from Tue 4:12 PM — restored*. Each price option keeps its own preview. So viewing another price just sets yours aside. It is restored when you view that price again. Previews stay on the computer they were made on. They do not follow you to another machine. A GC can never see a preview. Cover Letter, Share, Print, and CSV always use saved prices only. The **?** beside the bid's name keeps a short version of this story.

Each price card's ✎ opens the **Price** modal. You rename it or delete it. The ★ cannot be deleted while the letter is built on it.

## Know where you stand

- The **coverage chip** on the solver line counts pricing at a glance. It is green *29/29 ✓* when every costed row has a price, and amber while work remains. Its caret drops the full bar with **Show unpriced only** to filter to just those rows. Open or closed is remembered on your device.
- **Where the profit lives** shows profit by item. It warns when most of it rides on one or two rows. Those are the ones a GC's value-engineering pass goes after first. You **hover** any slice and it names itself instantly: item, profit dollars, share of job. The rest of the bar fades so even the thinnest sliver reads. You **click** a slice, or a legend chip, to pin a detail card with the row's full economics. That is qty, unit cost, sale price, revenue, cost, profit, margin, and its book entry. **↑ Jump to row in worksheet** scrolls you straight to that line and flashes it. The color legend under the bar tucks away with **Hide legend ▾**. It stays how you left it.
- **Bids like this** sits beside the bid's name as three small chips. They are about decided bids like the one you are pricing. **This size** counts the bids from half to double this price. It says how many you won and how many you lost on price. The second chip names the GC and counts your decided bids for it. The last chip says where this margin stands against your wins. Press **Compare** to open a panel under the chips. A bar shows what happened to every bid this size. A bid the GC lost was never yours to win, so the bar counts it apart. Under the bar are the bids you won or lost on price, by name, closest in size first. Some carry a bid tab. A bid tab is the list of every bidder's number on a job. See *follow up with builders*. Those rows say how far over the low bid you were. Last comes the margin scale. It shows only past bids with a usable cost estimate. Green dots are wins and red dots are price losses. Amber **▽** marks are the margins that would have matched a tab's low. **Thin evidence** means fewer than ten bids are on the scale. Press **Hide**, or click anywhere else, to close the panel.

## See what the bid is made of

At the foot of the Workbench, under **Where the profit lives**, one bar splits the bid into four buckets. They are **Fixtures**, **Pipe**, **Fittings** and **Other**. Pipe is line feet. Fittings are elbows, wyes, valves and cleanouts. Other is allowances, travel and permits. The header says it in your units: *59 fixtures · 2,448 ft of pipe · 357 fittings*. Each bucket shows its margin in the strip's colors.

1. {{chip:blue|Revenue}} / {{chip:gray|Cost}} flips the bar between share of the price you are viewing and share of our cost. Where the two disagree is where the pricing posture lives. Pipe at 22% of revenue but 30% of cost is under-priced against the fixtures.
2. Hover a segment or a bucket for its revenue, cost, profit and margin, and its three biggest rows. A row name scrolls to that row and flashes it.
3. A bucket that reads *no cost on these rows* has prices but no takeoff cost behind them. Those are the same rows the strip's "have no cost" line counts.

:::example Reading it
Fixtures 65% of revenue at 57% margin, pipe 22% at 28%: the fixtures are carrying the bid. Either the footage is priced light, or that is the plan — but now it is a decision, not a surprise.
:::

## "My version is gone!" — it isn't

Landing on Pricing first **loads** the bid's packets and prices. You may land there from the board's price-tag jump, say. You will see a brief shimmer over the Send-to strip and the Workbench while that happens. Your versions, prices, and previews are exactly where you left them. The page just has not finished fetching yet. If the connection drops mid-load, Pricing says *"Couldn't load this bid's packets and prices"*. It offers {{button:gray|Retry}}. Nothing was deleted.
