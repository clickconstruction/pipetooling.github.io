A courtesy notice is a plain reminder sent after the lien notice and before the lien itself. The reminder says the notice we mailed still stands. The reminder names the day the lien goes on unless the bill is paid. The reminder carries the pay link and the office's number.

Supply houses send this letter before they lien, and it gets bills paid. Ours goes by email and text in a minute. The reminder is not a new legal notice, and the reminder never goes by certified mail.

## When the desk offers it

Open the Lien desk and go to **Do now**. A sent notice with nothing paid shows a row from day 7 after the packet. The row sits under the **Reminders** heading. The row's button reads {{button:blue|Send a courtesy notice}}. The same button sits on the notice's footer under **Sent**. The Reminders rail above the rows counts these notices on its first step, Reminder due. The rail's other steps count the reminders sent, the second letters and the notices closed.

- Day 7 is counted the way the attorney’s letter counts it. The desk uses the delivery day when the mail piece knows it.
- The attorney’s letter keeps its own day. From day 10 the desk offers counsel's certified second letter beside the courtesy notice.
- The desk sends one courtesy notice. After a week of silence it offers the attorney's letter. The footer still lets you send the courtesy again by hand when an email or a text bounced.

## When the desk holds it

A reminder may not name a lien that cannot go on. The desk holds the button and says why. The reasons are the same ones the demand letter uses.

- The property is a homestead.
- The job has no approved work month yet.
- The affidavit window has already closed.

## The affidavit's week

Seven days before the lien's day the affidavit comes first. The paper takes about a week to draft, sign before a notary and file. The desk puts the affidavit first and says so on the notice. The courtesy button stays on the footer, so you may still send one by hand.

## Press it

1. Open the notice under **Sent** on the Lien desk.
2. Press {{button:blue|Send a courtesy notice ▸}} on the footer.
3. Read who gets it. The GC's email comes from the sent notice. The GC's number comes from the GC's Messages contact when we may text that number. A GC with no Messages contact yet shows the number on the GC's record. The owner's email and phone come from the property record. Type them on the property window under **Owner mailing address**. The same two rows sit on the job's **Property record** panel in Edit Job.
4. Change the greeting if you want. Nothing else on the message can be typed.
5. Press {{button:blue|Send 3 messages ▸}}. The button counts what will go.

The toast says what went and what did not, with the reason. A refusal on one channel never stops the others.

Opening the window makes nothing. A send by email alone makes nothing either. A text makes the GC a Messages contact when it goes, and the text lands in Messages.

The owner's text goes only when the owner's number already belongs to someone in Messages. Most owners are not in Messages yet. Then the owner gets the email alone, and the Text column says so. The owner is never made a Messages contact by this window.

## What the message says

- The notice we mailed on its day stands. The message is a reminder, not a new notice.
- One number, the notice's claim.
- The day the lien affidavit is filed unless it is paid, and the property it is filed on. The day comes from the affidavit's own clock.
- That a recorded affidavit is public record on the property. A title search finds it later, even after it is released.
- Where to pay, in one line, and that we cannot take a joint check.
- The master plumber's name and number, and that a wrong bill should be answered.

The owner's copy adds the pay page's rule. The owner pays us only with the GC's written okay. Otherwise the owner holds the amount back and calls.

The message never names interest, fees, theft of service or a default. The message never mentions the GC's other jobs.

## When they pay part of it

A payment after the notice is an answer. The desk stops asking for a reminder while they pay. The row reads *paying it down* with the amount paid and what is still owed. The row's button reads {{button:blue|Record what they promised}}. The same button sits on the notice's footer under **Sent**. Type the day they gave and the amount for the rest. The desk asks again on that day. With no day the desk asks again a week after the payment. The day you type also shows on the Stages board as the job's promise.

The attorney's letter waits while they pay. The office may still send it by hand when the payment was token. The lien's day never moves for a payment that is short.

## What closes it

The button leaves the row once any of these happens.

- The claim is paid.
- The owner's call is recorded.
- The GC's written okay is recorded.
- The affidavit is filed.
- The GC or the owner answers the message in Messages.

## Where the record goes

The sent notice keeps every press with who pressed, each address and the lien day it named. The job's History shows a **Courtesy** line. The footer under **Sent** reads *courtesy sent* with the day and the channels.
