If you work with the company as a partner, your Dashboard has a Your statement card. It shows your balance in plain words and which week is open.

The balance reads **you owe Click** or **Click owes you**. You tap the card to open your **Partner statement**. The receipt icon beside the home icon at the top of every page opens it too. It is the same kind of paper the company sends its customers. It shows your running balance and everything behind it, week by week.

## Reading the statement

The letterhead's small line names the deal. It shows your company, then **partner since** the date the deal started, and your hourly rates. While the deal is still a draft in the office, there is no signed agreement yet. Then that line reads **draft since** instead. The paper never claims more than the agreement does.

The statement opens on the **current week**, laid out like the paper one. **Week opened** sits at the top. The week's lines sit in the middle. A bold total sits on a double rule where the math finishes. The headline at the top says whose money it is: **YOU OWE CLICK** or **CLICK OWES YOU**. The week's total reads **So far this week**. It is your approved hours priced at your deal rates, minus payouts and charges. It is what settling up today would actually mean. Hours still waiting on office approval show as a line with no dollars yet. They post when approved.

Charges show as a minus line **in the week they happened**. A back-charge, a cost passed back to you, is one kind. A utility overage, a utility bill over what was allowed, is another. They show that way whether or not a statement has picked them up yet. The week they hit is the week you see them.

You use the **‹ Older / Newer ›** buttons to step through past weeks, back to your very first week. Every week opens exactly where the week before it closed. Both ends of a week carry a sign. A minus means **you owe Click**. A plus means **Click owes you**. The words sit underneath, so the lines in between add up on the page. When a week's running balance crosses $0, a small note on that line says so. It reads *crossed $0 — cleared the $546.39 you owed and went $60.25 ahead*. So you can follow the money straight through. **Full ledger** at the bottom of the statement is the same history as one long list. It shows postings, payouts, and charges at their dates, with running balances. The office can also leave dated notes on the ledger. Ones shared with you appear there as quiet italic lines. On a phone the Full ledger lists each posting on two lines. The first line is the date and posting. The second is the amount and running balance. So the balance never scrolls off the edge. Each closed week shows:

1. Labor lines, your hours at the rate they were priced that week. Rate changes never reach back into old weeks.
2. Anything added, such as profit shares from your jobs and credits.
3. Anything deducted, such as back-charges and utility overage.
4. **Paid out** lines, money you were actually paid.
5. **Week opened** at the top and **Week closed** at the bottom. Every closing balance opens the next week, so the chain is the ledger.

:::example A typical closed week
Field labor 22.50 h × $50, estimating 18.00 h × $35, a profit share from a finished job, one back-charge, and Friday's payout — ending at the week's closing balance.
:::

## Printing a statement

You press {{button:outline|Print / save PDF}} to print the statement exactly as you see it. Any week that has one can be printed. A past week that only has charges says so instead. No hours were worked, so no statement was issued. There is nothing to print for it.

## Your jobs and their costs

**Your jobs** on the statement lists every job the office has confirmed as majority-yours. The **Your jobs** card on your Dashboard lists the same. Any profit share already posted shows with the job. You tap {{button:outline|Costing ›}} on a job to see its cost sheet. It shows reported **hours by person**, hours only and never anyone's wages. It shows **supply house invoices** with how much of each was allocated to the job. It shows **card charges** and **direct expenses**, including estimating moved onto the job at award. This is the same data behind your profit split. So every number in the split traces to a line you can see.

## The office reads the same page

There is one journal. The office's Partnerships tabs are Ledger, Timeline and Statements. They are built from the same list your **Full ledger** shows, row for row, just worded from their side. Their page says **we owe Bryan** or **Bryan owes us** where yours says **Click owes you** or **you owe Click**. Your hours there are the same stamped days your week cards show, to the hundredth. So the balance on your statement is the balance the office is looking at. If the two of you ever disagree, you are reading different weeks, not different books. Closing a week posts the statement to your page. There is no acknowledgment step for you to take.

## What feeds the numbers

Only **approved** hours post. Jobs appear in your profit shares only after the office confirms you did the majority of the work on them. If a week looks short, check whether hours are still pending approval before anything else.
