Messages lists each person the office works with on GC projects. Press a person to read every message with them in one thread.

The office and estimators see Messages.

## Open Messages

Press **Messages** in the header. It opens the office's one page for everyone who writes to us. [Answer everyone waiting on us from one page](/help/answer-everyone-waiting-on-us-from-one-page) shows the page. Press {{button:outline|Trade partners}} to see the people we work with on GC projects.

On [GC projects](/gc), {{button:outline|Messages}} in the row of views opens the same page with **Trade partners** picked.

Under **Went out**, press {{button:outline|Show everyone}} to see every person with a message as a list. Each person has one row. The first line has their name and when they last wrote. The second line has their company and whose move it is, such as {{chip:yellow|Our move}}. The third line has their last message.

The person with the newest message is on top. People with no message yet come last, by name.

## Find a person

- Press **Customers**, **Trades** or **Who is this?** to see one kind of person. Trades also holds the architects.
- Press **All** to see everyone again.
- Type in the search box to find a name, a company, a number or any words in their messages.
- Press {{button:outline|‹}} to fold the list into a row of faces. This browser keeps your choice.
- Press {{button:outline|›}} to open the list again.

## Read a thread

Press a person's row. The person's thread opens as a window over the page. On a phone it fills the screen.

The top of the window shows who they are and where things stand in three rows.

- The first row has the person's name. Press {{button:outline|☎}} to call them from your device.
- Under the name is what they are to us and their company. Their first email and number sit on the same line.
- An arrow after them shows when they have more numbers or addresses. Press it to show or hide the rest.
- The second row has a chip for whose move it is. Beside the chip, a sentence says when they last wrote and when we last answered.
- The third row has the person's jobs.

Each message shows how it came, who sent it and its job. A long message shows four lines. Press {{button:outline|⋯}} and then **Show all text** to read every message in full.

Press Escape or {{button:outline|×}} to close the thread.

A person waiting in **New** has a sort card. On a wide window it sits whole at the right of the messages. On a narrow one it folds to one row with the press it picked. Press **Other ways** to open it.

## A lead's two doors

A lead is a person who wrote to us and has no record yet. Their thread offers {{button:blue|Book a visit}} and {{button:outline|Start an estimate}}. Book a visit makes them a customer and opens Add Job with their name and number filled in. Start an estimate makes them a customer and opens a new estimate for them. Their thread comes along either way.

## Ask for photos of the problem

Type `/show` in the reply box to text a person a link for photos and a short video. What they send shows on a First look card above their messages. [Ask a customer to show you the problem before you go out](/help/ask-a-customer-to-show-you-the-problem) shows how.


Our estimate emails carry a button in the thread. Press {{button:outline|Open estimate #166}} to leave the thread and open that estimate. A change order's email opens the change order the same way. A text that carried the estimate's link carries the same button under its words.

## See what they did with an estimate

The thread draws the estimate's moments as the app's own lines, in order with the texts and emails. *Dana opened estimate #212* appears when they open the link. *Dana accepted estimate #212* appears when they accept, with *Signed* when they signed. *Dana declined estimate #212* appears when they decline. Each appears once.

## See the emails the app sent on its own

Some emails go out with nobody pressing Send. A reminder, a scheduled notice and a bill the app sends are like that. Each one shows as **the app** in the thread.

Two or more of them in a row fold into one line. It says how many there are and the days they span. Then it lists each email's subject.

:::example A run of the app's emails, folded
the app &nbsp; {{button:outline|3 emails, Oct 8 to Oct 10 ▸}} &nbsp; Work scheduled &nbsp; Invoice 1312 &nbsp; Paid, thank you
:::

- Press the count to open the emails in place. Press it again to fold them.
- A link to one of them opens the thread with its run already open.

An email we sent shows small print under its words when the app knows it. It can say the address it went from. It can say when it was opened and when its bill was paid.

:::example The small print under a bill
opened Oct 10, 6:12 PM &nbsp; paid Oct 11 by card
:::

No small print means the app does not know. It does not mean nobody opened it.

## Read a thread on a phone

On a phone or in a narrow window, the top of the thread folds to fit.

- Press {{button:outline|‹}} beside their face to go back to everyone.
- Press the arrow beside their company to show their numbers and addresses.
- The chips sit in one row. The sentence sits under them on a row of its own.
- The person's jobs sit in one row under the sentence.
- Swipe a row sideways to see the rest of it.
- The reply box folds to one row. Press into it to write. Press anywhere else to fold it again, with your words kept.
- A message, the job picker and the menu under {{button:outline|⋯}} open from the bottom of the screen.
- Press outside one to close it. Press **Cancel** to close the menu.

## Read a thread on a short screen

On a laptop, the window is short. The thread folds so the messages get the room.

- The top keeps the same three rows as on a tall window.
- **Who is this?** sits on the line under their name when nobody has named them.
- The reply box folds to one row. Press into it to write. Press anywhere else to fold it again, with your words kept.
- The window takes the whole height under the app's bar.

## Open one message

1. Press a message in the thread.
2. Read who sent it, when it came and the job it is tied to.
3. Press {{button:outline|Copy}}, {{button:outline|Print or save as PDF}} or {{button:outline|Share…}}.

The row above the words reads the message. The office sees one or two more presses there.

- An email on no job offers {{button:outline|Start a bid}} and {{button:outline|Put it on a bid…}}. An invitation to bid offers the same two. [Put an email on a bid](#put-an-email-on-a-bid) shows both.
- An email already on a bid offers {{button:outline|Open the bid}}.
- A customer's message tied to a job offers {{button:outline|Start a change order}}. [Start a change order from a customer's message](/help/start-a-change-order-from-a-customers-message) shows how.

## Put an email on a bid

An invitation to bid can reach you in a thread, not only in New. Or you may have made the bid already from a phone call or a bid platform. The email viewer offers two presses for that.

:::example The row on an invitation
{{button:outline|Start a bid}} {{button:outline|Put it on a bid…}} {{button:outline|Copy}} {{button:outline|Print or save as PDF}} {{button:outline|Share…}}
:::

Press {{button:outline|Start a bid}} to make a new bid from the email. A panel opens under the row.

1. Check the **Bid** box. It starts with what the email said.
2. Press {{button:blue|Start a bid on}} the sender's customer. Is the sender new? Type the company under **A new customer** and press {{button:blue|Make the customer and start the bid}}.

The bid takes the plans link and the due day from the email. The email lands on the bid. The Bid Board opens on it.

Press {{button:outline|Put it on a bid…}} when the bid exists already. A picker lists the sender's bids first. Type a name or a number to find another open bid. Press the bid. The email lands on it. The bid's history gains one line. It reads *Invitation to bid came in by email from Glenn Geling, Oct 22.* So the bid says it reached you by email too.

Press {{button:outline|Open the bid}} on an email that is on a bid already. The Bid Board opens on it.

Press Escape to go back to the thread.

## Open a link in an email's words

A web address in the words is blue with a small link mark beside it. Press it once. The address opens in a new tab.

Some mail programs write the address twice, once in angle brackets. The app shows it once.

Words the sender made bold read bold. The stars around them are gone.

The same address may also sit under **Attachments** as a chip. That chip is our filed copy of the link. Press whichever is nearer.

The link works in the thread too, under the message's line. Press the words beside it to open the message.

An email can also read as the sender laid it out. [Read an email as the sender laid it out](#read-an-email-as-the-sender-laid-it-out) shows the switch.

## Read an email as the sender laid it out

An email that came in after November 8, 2026 keeps the sender's own layout. Two chips sit above its words: {{chip:blue|Rich text}} and {{chip:gray|Plain}}.

1. Press {{chip:blue|Rich text}}. The email reads as the sender laid it out, with its tables, numbered lists and linked words.
2. Press a linked word. The link opens in a new tab.
3. Press {{button:outline|Show pictures}} to load the sender's pictures. Pictures stay off until you press it.
4. Press {{chip:gray|Plain}} to go back to the plain words.

The app remembers your choice on this device. The next email you open reads the same way.

Pictures start off for a reason. A loaded picture tells the sender you opened the email. A tracking pixel is a tiny hidden picture that does only that. The app never loads one.

The rich view keeps nothing that can run. Scripts, forms and the sender's page styles are taken out.

An older email has no chips and reads as plain words only.

{{button:outline|Copy}}, {{button:outline|Print or save as PDF}} and {{button:outline|Share…}} still use the plain words.

## Call or write to a number in an email's words

Most emails end with a signature. The signature holds the sender's phone number, their address and their office's street address. In the message, each of those is a press.

- A phone number has {{button:outline|Call}} after it. Call works as it does everywhere in the app. On a phone with a Quo seat, pick Call in Quo or Call from my phone.
- A number that can take a text has {{button:outline|Text}} after it too.
- An email address has {{button:outline|Email}} after it.
- A street address has {{button:outline|Open the map}} after it. The address opens in Google Maps in a new tab.

Press {{button:outline|Text}} or {{button:outline|Email}} and the reply box opens on that kind. When the number or the address is this person's, the box opens in this thread. When the number or the address is someone else's, the box opens in their thread. The app makes a thread for them when nobody has one yet.

Every press leaves a gray line on this thread. The line says who pressed what and where it went.

:::example The lines a press leaves
Grace pressed Call on (832) 871-4166 ext. 121 from Glenn's email of Oct 22. · 2:14 PM

Grace opened a thread for amy@maxxbuilders.com from Glenn's email of Oct 22. open it › · 2:15 PM
:::

The line lands ten seconds after the press. Press {{button:outline|Undo}} on the toast within those ten seconds and nothing is written. Press **open it ›** on a line to go to the thread it opened.

A call made in Quo still writes its own call line, as always. The pressed line says what you meant to do. The call line says what happened.

A tracking link in the signature stays a plain link. The app never reads it as a number or an address.

## Open a file that came with an email

An email's files show as chips under its words. A PDF has a red badge and a picture a green one. A link has the small blue link mark. Rest on the mark and it says it is a link, not a file.

1. Press a file's chip. The message opens.
2. Under **Attachments**, press {{button:outline|Open}} right after the file's name.

The file opens in a new tab. Open shows our copy, kept when the email came. A link opens at its own address.

A file too big to keep stays in Gmail. That file's row says so and has no {{button:outline|Open}}.

A picture on a text that the app could not copy says so too. Quo still has that picture.

## Save a file to the job's Drive

A file that came with an email can go into the job's Drive folder. Then the job's files show it with the rest.

1. Open the message. Under **Attachments**, press {{button:outline|Save to Drive}} beside the file.
2. Read where it goes. On a GC project, pick **Team only** or **Plans**.
3. Press {{button:blue|Save it}}.

Team only is for a quote or a photo. Only the team sees it there. Plans is for anything else. Anyone with the folder's link reads it, so trades see it with the plans.

A photo goes into the job's photos. A file on a bid or a Pipeline job goes into the job's folder.

The row then says where the file went. Press **Open in Drive** to see it there. A message on nobody's job asks you to tie it to a job first.

## See how a file we sent went

An email we sent lists its files under **Files**. Each one says how it went.

- **sent as the file** means the file went with the email. A copy is kept with the message.
- **sent as our link to their portal** means the link opens their portal at the right page.
- **sent as our link to the file** means our own link to that one file.

Our own link says how often it was opened. Under it is the time of each open, newest first. It never says who opened it, since a link cannot know.

To stop our link, press {{button:outline|Cut it off}}. From then on it opens nothing. The file stays where it is. Our link also stops by itself 30 days after it went.

## Save a job's photos to Drive

A message tied to a job may come with a picture. The message window then offers a tick under **Attachments**.

1. Press the message.
2. Under **Attachments**, tick **Save every photo on this job to Drive as it comes in**.

The tick is for the whole job, not for one message. Every job starts with the tick off. Once the tick is on, the window says the day it went on.

Photos that come in on the job after that day go to the job's photos in Drive. Photos that came in before that day are not copied. To stop, untick **Save every photo on this job to Drive as it comes in**.

Only the office team can change the tick. A training account cannot change the tick.

## File a paper that came by email

A trade partner may email us their certificate of insurance or their W-9. The thread offers that file under its message. The app never files it on its own.

- Press {{button:blue|File this as their certificate}}. The company window opens at **Insurance** with the file's link in the form. Fill in the day it runs out. Then press {{button:blue|File the certificate}}.
- Press {{button:outline|It is not}} when the file is something else. The offer goes away for everyone.

Once the certificate is filed, the message says who filed it and on what day.

A W-9 offer has {{button:outline|Go to their W-9 paper}}. A W-9 is not filed from an email yet.

Filing a certificate is a dev's job for now. Others see {{button:outline|Go to their insurance paper}} in its place.

## Keep a person's jobs

A person's jobs show as tiles under the top of the window. The star marks the job where a new message from them lands.

- Press {{button:outline|☆}} on a tile to move the star there.
- Press a job's number to see only the messages tied to it. Press **show all** to see every message again.
- Press {{button:outline|⧉}} to open the job.
- Press **+ add a job** to put another job on them.
- Press {{button:outline|×}} on a tile to take that job off them. You confirm first, and their messages keep their job.

A job they are on through a project has no {{button:outline|×}}.

## Tie a message to a job

- Drag a job's tile onto a message. On a phone, press and hold the tile, then press the message.
- Or press **no job** on a message and pick the job.

On a phone you can also press and hold a message.

1. Hold until the message turns darker, then lift your finger.
2. Pick a job in the list that opens. The job new messages land on comes first.
3. Press **Another job…** to find a job that is not theirs.
4. Press **Untie it** to take the message off its job. The message stays with the person.

To change a message's job, open the message and press **change** beside **Tied to**. Pick **On the person, no job** to untie it.

## Name a number nobody knows

A thread for a number we do not know has a {{button:blue|Who is this?}} button. The number shows in place of a name. Under the number, the line says nobody has named it.

1. Press {{button:blue|Who is this?}}.
2. Type three letters of their name or their company.
3. Press the person or the company in the list.
4. Pick what they are to us. A supply house or someone at one needs no pick.
5. If the person is someone else at that company, type their own name.
6. Press {{button:blue|This is who it is}}.

The number joins that person. Every message from the number and the person stays in one thread.

The list shows first the people who fit where their message came in. A message to our bids inbox shows trade partners and supply houses first. A message to the company number shows customers first.
