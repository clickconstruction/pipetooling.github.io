A signed hours statement is a paper that says what one worker did on one job for a period. It lists their days and hours from the clock, and what the company paid for those weeks. The worker signs at the bottom. The office keeps it for a case over an unpaid bill. It proves the company paid for specific work at a specific site.

## Where to open it

Open the job's window and go to **Documents**. The **Hours statements** section lists every statement made on the job. Press {{button:outline|Hours statement…}} to make one. The button says how many people clocked on the job.

You can also start from the person. Open their desk under **People** and find **Hours & approvals**. The same section sits under it. There you pick the job instead of the person.

## Making the paper

:::steps

1. Pick the worker under **Who**. The span fills in with their first and last day on the job.
2. Change **From** and **To** if you need a shorter span.
3. Leave **Show the rate, the amount and what was paid** on for the paper with the money. Turn it off for hours alone.
4. Turn on **Add days the app did not record** when the worker will fill in days by hand.
5. Read the paper on the right. It draws again after every change.

:::

A line under the span says what the app has. It counts the approved sessions and the days typed by hand. A quick add that is still waiting for approval is left off.

:::example The line under the span
The app has 23 approved sessions on this job in this span. 2 days were typed by hand.
:::

If a session is still waiting for approval, an amber line says so. The paper names that session under its day and does not count it. Approve it first if it belongs on the paper.

## The pay on the paper

The pay switch is for the payroll roles. A dev, the controller and a pay-approved leader may turn it on. An assistant makes the hours-only paper. Their switch stays off.

With the pay on, each day shows the rate and the amount. Under the table, **Paid** lists each week's pay report. It shows the job's share, what was paid, the day and the reference. An unpaid week prints as not yet paid. The paper never claims a payment it does not have.

## Days the app does not have

The app kept no clock before March 12, 2026. When your span starts before that day, the paper adds a block. The block names the span the company's records do not show. It leaves blank rows for the worker to fill in. The paper marks those days as stated by the worker, not recorded by the company.

Turn on **Add days the app did not record** to add the same block inside a recorded span. Its heading then reads *Days the worker adds*.

## Saving and printing

Press {{button:blue|Save a draft}} to keep the statement. It gets a record number like *HS-1042-0007*. The number is the job's number and a count. The statement then lists under **Hours statements** with the chip {{chip:gray|Draft}}.

Press {{button:outline|Print}} to print the paper. Print saves the statement too, so the paper carries its record number. The chip then reads {{chip:blue|Printed}}. The worker signs with a pen. Print keeps no copy in the app.

:::tryit

Open a job you worked on. Press {{button:outline|Hours statement…}}, pick yourself and press {{button:outline|Print}}. Read the paper against [People → Hours](/people?tab=hours) for the same days.

:::

## Printing one again

Each row under **Hours statements** has {{button:outline|Print}} and {{button:outline|Open}}. Print prints the paper as it was saved, with its record number. Open brings the statement back into the window with its who and span. Save there keeps the same record number.

{{button:gray|Send for signature…}} and {{button:gray|Sign here now}} are on the window but cannot be pressed yet. Signing in the app is coming. Until then, print the paper and have the worker sign it with a pen.

## Who can use this

A dev, the controller and a pay-approved leader make the paper with the money. An assistant makes the hours-only paper. Helpers and subcontractors do not see the section.
