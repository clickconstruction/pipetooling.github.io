3 The pen — press a chip when they tell you something new

New information is almost always somebody's word on a day, and the app already has the write for that: a payment promise. So the chip is the pen. Pressing one opens a small form — the day they now say, who said it, how — and the save is the Payment Chase's own promise write. A pace chip becomes their word on the new day; a word chip re-promised keeps its history, so slips and misses still count. A bank, drawer or Stripe chip takes no pen — those days come from the bank, the deposit run or Stripe, and the form says so.

5 What each piece reads

The week line draws the same reads as the lanes did — nothing changes in what is read, only where it sits. Every chip is a read the app already has, except the payout tile and the pending-deposit read, the two new pieces named in the plan.

The bar, closed

Three sums the open line already computes: Came in, Still expected, Last week still out — plus the red returned count. One row tall; remembers being left open per device.

The week line

Chips grouped by day: landed chips by paid_on, expected chips by the day each read gives (clears ~day, promised day, pace day, payout arrival). The kind is the chip's small tag, never a section title.

Last week's column

Bills whose expected day fell in last week's window with no payment landed since, promises now past, and returned checks (list_ar_return_cases). The one-week rule is new; its parts exist.

The drawer shelf

Recorded payments with no bank link whose type reads as a check — typed in, not yet driven to the bank, so they have no day yet. Door: the AR deposits window.

At the bank

Mercury rows still pending — deposited, not yet posted, placed on the day they should clear. A failed one goes red on its day with the bank's reason. (New read: today no screen shows pending money in.)

Their word · they say it's sent

Promises on their promised day and open paid claims (“check mailed Thursday”) on the day the mail should land, with the open money they cover and who heard them.

Pace chips

Bills whose expected day falls this week by the payer's own pay speed — dashed and tagged a guess so nobody reads them as a fact.

Stripe + the payout chip

Open Stripe invoices on their expected day, and the next payout on the day it lands (new edge function, get-stripe-payouts). The payout is not added to any sum — it is landed card money reaching the bank.

The pen

Pressing a chip writes the promise verbs the Payment Chase already has (add_job_payment_promise / set_job_promised_pay_date). Append-only, so slips and misses keep counting. Nothing new in the database.

Who sees it: the same roles as the cards above it — no technician, estimator or sub ever does. Dollars: the line's totals must foot to the windows its doors open; any gap is a bug in the strip, not the windows.
