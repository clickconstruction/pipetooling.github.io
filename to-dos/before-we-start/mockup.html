Before we start: what has to be true before a stage begins, and who said so

For each stage the office writes a short list of things that must be true before the crew goes. The office follows up with the customer until each one has a yes. A stage with every yes reads Clear to start. The site's stages sit on one line of time, and any bullet can be shown on the GC's own page for them to confirm.

The gap, in one picture

Today a stage has a window of dates and nothing that says the site will be ready for it. A crew can be sent on a phone call, find the slab not ready, and come home with no record of who said to come.

Today

  • A stage has our window. The GC's page can show it and ask for other dates.
  • What must be true first lives in someone's head.
  • The day they say the site will be ready lives in mail and calls. When it moves, the old day is gone.
  • Nothing tells the office which stage to call about this week.

With Before we start new

  • Each stage has a Before we start list, written in plain bullets.
  • Each bullet says who it waits on and where it stands: Open Told yes Seen.
  • Every day they give for ready is kept, with who said it and how.
  • A go or no-go day tells the office when to have every yes in hand.
  • Any bullet can show on the GC's page, where they press Yes, ready themselves.

5. On arrival: is it as told?

The first clock-in on the stage asks the crew to look. Yes turns a Told yes into Seen. No is the record that the site was not as told, with a photo, and it reaches Dispatch at once.

The rules, in plain words

A bullet

  • Words, and who it waits on: them or us.
  • A bullet on them is Open, Told yes or Seen. A bullet on us is Open or Done.
  • Told yes keeps who said it, how, and the day. Seen keeps who saw it, the day, and a photo when there is one.
  • A bullet can go back to Open. Every change is kept.

A stage

  • Clear to start when no bullet is Open. It says whether that is on their word or seen.
  • A stage with no list reads as it does today.
  • The go or no-go day is our window's first day, less the notice we need.
  • A stage that is not clear can still be scheduled. The app warns. It never blocks.

Their days

  • Each day they give for ready is a line: the day, who said it, how, and when.
  • A new day does not erase the old one. The chart shows both.
  • When their day is after our window's first day, the stage says so and offers to move our window.

What the GC's page shows

  • Only on a job whose sharing switch is on, and only the bullets with the eye on.
  • Our window, the notice we need and the day we need the answers by.
  • Never: a bullet on us without the eye, what we saw, a note, a name, a trip, a cost.
  • The office can read the page as the GC before turning anything on.

The train

Eight small pull requests. The first four give the office the list, the dates and the site's schedule. The GC's page comes sixth, behind a switch that starts off.
