Is this the best we can do? Eight changes

The first draft had the right bones. Read again from the office's chair, the owner's, the crew's and the GC's, it was missing eight things. This draft has them.

1. Who to call: the office's first screen

The office does not think in stages. It thinks in calls. So the list is by person: everything open with Ray across every stage is one row and one ask. Every ask is kept, so the row says when we last asked and whether they answered.

Development page · the Schedule look opens here · also the Dashboard's Needs you

2. One stage: yes, and how we know

A bullet is Open or Yes. A Yes always says how we know: their word, their photo, or we saw it. Our own bullets are not typed at all. They read the order log, the trip and the permit, and open the place that fixes them.

Job window · a stage · Before we start

3. Go or hold: the decision has a name on it

On the go or no-go day somebody decides. That decision is a press. It keeps who decided, when, and what was still open. A hold writes the notice for the GC, so a start that slips because of the site is on record the day it slips.

Decide · from the stage, the call list or the Needs you line

4. The GC's page: one press, and something back

A superintendent will not tick six boxes. So the text carries a link that opens on the list, one press answers all of it, and a photo can go with a yes. Once we press Go, their page says the crew is confirmed. That firm date is what they get for answering.

The GC's page · /portal · before and after we press Go

5. The site's schedule, with the days they moved

Every stage on one line of time. A diamond is the day they say ready and a hollow diamond is a day they gave before. The red figure is how many days they have moved that stage. The site's total is the tile.

Development page · the Schedule look · the chart, under the call list

6. On arrival: one press when it is as told

The crew sees only the bullets nobody of ours has seen. One press says all of it is as told. A No is the record, with a photo, and it reaches Dispatch at once.

7. The lists write themselves over time

An empty editor is the reason a list never gets written. So each stage kind offers a starter list once, and a No on a job offers its bullet to the company's list. Nothing is added without a press.

The rules, in plain words

A bullet on them

  • Open or Yes. A Yes says how we know: their word, their photo, or we saw it.
  • It keeps who said it, how and the day. It can go back to Open. Every change is kept.
  • A bullet can ask for a photo. Then their word alone leaves it Open.
  • Every ask is kept: the day, how, and whether they answered.

A bullet on us

  • Read from the app where the app knows: material from the order log, the crew from the trip, the permit.
  • Nobody ticks it. Its line opens the place that fixes it.
  • A bullet the app cannot read is ticked by hand, as Done.
  • It never shows on the GC's page unless its eye is turned on.

The decision

  • The go or no-go day is our first day less the notice we need.
  • Go keeps who decided, when and what was open. A Go with a bullet open is a Go on a promise, with a last check the day before the crew leaves.
  • Hold moves our window and writes the GC's notice for the office to send.
  • Nothing is blocked. A crew can be scheduled with no Go. The app says so in amber.

The GC's page

  • Off until the job's sharing switch and a second switch are both on, then bullet by bullet.
  • It shows our window, the notice we need and the answer-by day.
  • Their press is a Yes on their word, by name, with the time. Their photo makes it a Yes with a photo.
  • Never: our own bullets, what we saw, a note, a name, a trip, a cost, a day they gave before.
