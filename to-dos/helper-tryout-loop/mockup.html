The helper try-out loop

The purpose, in the owner's words: "for the assistant to be able to feed master plumbers helpers while the master plumbers figure out which they like and which they don't like." The board feeds and it hires. This draws the part in between.

1 · The loop, five hands

Two of the five exist. The three in the middle are new — and the only thing a master ever touches is the fourth.

Assistant
Feeds
Adds helpers to the Helper column they were shared, calls them, ranks them, presses Try out.
Office
Puts them on trial
Try out puts the person on the roster as a trial helper — schedulable like any helper, card still linked.
Dispatch
Pairs them
Schedules the trial helper with a master, as today. Nothing new here — the roster is the roster.
Master
Says yes or no
At clock-out, one card: Take Bryan again? Yes · No · Not sure, a word. By name, that day.
Office
Hires or passes
The card tallies the masters. 3 said yes — hire? The office presses Hire; the trial flag clears.

2 · The board grows a Try-out stage

Screen → Interview → Try-out → Hire → Review. A Try-out card is the helper on trial: days worked, what each master said, the nudge, and the office's three buttons. The assistant who shared the column can read this card — it is how they know whether to keep feeding.

Try out is the verb for a helper column. It runs the same roster hand-off Hire runs today (create-user, role helpers) but leaves the person flagged trial and the card linked. Advance to Interview stays for anyone who wants the phone review first; for an office column Try out is not offered.
