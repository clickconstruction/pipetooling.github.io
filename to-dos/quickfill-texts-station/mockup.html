Quickfill's Texts station: read what Messages already knows, instead of asking for a note

Today the station asks a person to remember what the app already keeps. Every company text goes through Quo into /messages, so the app knows which texts wait on a reply, which never reached a phone and which went out today. Three ways to draw the station. Desktop frames are the Roller, drawn light. Phone frames are the round, drawn dark. Dark swaps the page around them. Every press in a frame only says what it would do. Every person, number and text here is made up.

A The day's text ledger, read from Messages recommended

The body is three of Messages' own lanes, narrowed to texts, in the words the office already reads on /messages. Needs a reply is every text nobody has answered, the one waiting longest first, each row with Reply and Call. Not delivered is every text of ours from today and yesterday that Quo could not put on a phone, red, with Call them and Send it as an email. Went out today is the receipt: every text we sent today with Quo's mark, folded to one line until it is opened. A press on a row opens the thread on Messages, so there is one place to write. The box shrinks to one optional line, Anything else?, for a text the app cannot see. The Mark no longer needs a note: it writes one from the rows, so the history says who was still waiting at each look.

The count is the work: texts needing a reply plus texts not delivered, here 3 open. The receipt and Today's sends are not counted. The banner becomes a question the rows answer: Is anything on texts still waiting on us?

C Retire the station into Needs you

The Texts station goes. Needs you already carries the Customers line and Today's sends since v2.5771. It gains one line, Texts, dashed here: texts needing a reply from anyone and texts not delivered, with a door to Messages. The round is 27 stations. The texts' daily stamp and its 128 marks of history end, and the mark that covers texts is Needs you's, which covers 30 lines at once. A Needs you line shows only while it has something, so a clean day says nothing about texts. And a customer's unanswered text would sit on two lines, so the Customers line would have to give up its texts.

The count: none of its own. The Texts line adds to Needs you's count, here 3 of its 12. No banner: Needs you has none.
