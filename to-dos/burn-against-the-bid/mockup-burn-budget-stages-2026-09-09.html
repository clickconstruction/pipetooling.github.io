Burn, Budget and Stages

Two things in one mock-up: a Burn section on the Costs tab that measures daily spend against the bid and the percent complete, and a report that asks the tech which stage they are on so the percent has a real denominator. The assistant-visibility question is parked for later. The job throughout is J931 Heron Construction, worked figures, not live data.

One number carries it. Spent ÷ percent complete is the cost at completion. Price minus that is the margin you will actually make. Every other element on the section exists to explain that number.

The middle tile is the tell. Two percentages side by side, spend and progress. Green when spend trails progress, red when it leads.

Daily bars show the week the cumulative chart hides. The forecast line just extends today's burn until it meets the budget.

Decision needed · the budgetBid estimate when the job came from a bid (labor hours × rate + materials, snapshotted at job creation), else price × target margin. The mock-up assumes this. The fallback keeps service jobs from going blank.

Weight = the line's price ÷ the price of all stage lines. The permit is billed, not built, so it sits outside the percent.
A job with one line item ("as per plans") has one stage worth 100%, so its report looks exactly like today's slider. Nothing breaks on old jobs.
An Any stage counts the same way; it just isn't held to the order.
A stage reported Done is a draw ready to bill. The stage kinds you shipped this week already decide whether it can go out now or must wait its turn.

This is the right fix for the percent. Today a tech guesses a whole-job number. With stages the guess shrinks to "how far along is the piece I'm standing in", and the app does the weighting. 60% of a 35% stage is 21 points, and no one has to do that in their head.

It also makes the burn math honest. Earned value per stage means the Costs tab can say "Rough-in has burned 80% of its share of the budget at 60% done", which is where a job actually goes wrong.

The report keeps the free-text and photos it has. The stage list is one extra tap, pre-selected to the stage the crew's schedule block names.

Two rules to confirmWeights come from line-item prices, not from hours. A "—" line never carries progress. If you'd rather weight by estimated hours from the bid, say so; the mock-up uses price because every job has one.
