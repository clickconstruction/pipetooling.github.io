ClickTooling · Job window → Costs · Burn · design study · 2026-09-11

Burn against the bid

Burn today compares spend to a budget the app assumes: price × 65 %. The plan was to swap in the bid's cost estimate. Reading the real data first changed the shape of the work: the pipe exists, the water doesn't. Below: what's true, the design that follows from it (v2, after a "best we can do" pass), and the build plan.

What this meansClickTooling prices from the price book; it does not cost most bids. So "the actual bid" that exists for nearly every won job is its price and its fixture counts — not a cost. A budget that only works when the estimator filled the Cost Estimate tab would light up on a handful of jobs and stay dark on J1007.
What we build insteadA Budget that is a first-class thing on the job with a provenance: where it came from, when, and how complete it is. The bid link is the doorway; the bid's estimate is the best filler when it exists; a typed budget (hours · materials · subs) is the honest filler when it doesn't; the 35 % assumption is the floor. Every Burn readout names which one it is standing on.

Illustrative estimate: B66 has no cost estimate today. This is what the card reads once the Cost Estimate tab is filled — the point of Frame D.

Source line first. The chips say what the budget is standing on before any number is read: which bid, whether the bid's value matches the job's price (a scope change shows here), when the estimate was captured and by whom, and how complete it was.

Hours before dollars for labor. The bid priced labor at a book rate; the job pays real wages. Comparing dollars mixes the two. Hours are what the crew controls and what the labor book estimated, so the labor row leads with 1,020 of 1,180 h and keeps the dollars underneath.

One bar per component, the % done as a marker. Fill past the amber marker is spend ahead of work. The Burn tiles above keep today's whole-job answer; the table says where.

The "why" sentence is the product. A number says that; the sentence says materials blew the estimate while labor is only slightly behind — two different conversations.

What "direct" meansLabor hours × rate, materials, subs, equipment, permits, waste, other, driving and travel. Not the estimator cost — bid time is already in the overhead pool (People → Overhead, "Bid labor"), and Burn keeps overhead out of the direct signal.

This is the frame every open job shows on day one, so it has to do the linking work itself. Candidates are ranked by evidence: price equal to a bid value (15 open jobs today), then same GC customer, then address. One tap links and snapshots; nothing to type.

The typed budget is not a consolation prize. J1007 is a $250k job burning against a guess. Three numbers from the person who scoped it — hours, materials, subs — beat the 35 % rule, and the source chip says ✎ typed by Taunya · Sep 11 so nobody mistakes it for the bid.

Linking without an estimate still pays. The bid value versus the job price, the bid's GC, and the bid → job trail on the Bid Board all land immediately; and the "ask Wendi" door turns the missing estimate into a task instead of a dead end.

Ranking rule1 · bid value or agreed value equals the job's price (± $1) · 2 · same GC customer and won · 3 · address prefix match · 4 · name similarity. Show at most three; never auto-link.

One glyph, three footings. ◆ the bid's estimate · ✎ a typed budget · ≈ the assumption. It rides the Burn cell and the card, so a red 94 % against a real bid is not confused with a red 68 % against a rule of thumb.

Budget column + filter. Sort by it and the assumed jobs cluster — that list is the linking backlog. "Margin at risk" on the Pipeline card is measured against each job's own footing: the bid's margin when it has one, the target otherwise, and the subline says which.

Partial estimates stay partial. B75's estimate has hours but no materials; the job burns labor against it and materials against the assumption, and the chip says so. Silently mixing the two would produce a budget nobody can explain.

The Board is where the habit forms. A won bid with no job, or a job that matches by value but isn't linked, is a chip with a verb. A won bid with no cost estimate is the other chip — and that one is aimed at the estimator, because a job's Burn is only as honest as the bid it stands on.

Snapshot, with a refresh. The budget is what the bid said when the job took it (or when you pressed Refresh), stamped with the bid version, the date and the person. Change orders and repricing produce a visible delta, never a silent drift.

Later, not nowPersist the Pricing workbench's margin on the bid when it's priced, so a bid with no cost estimate can still hand the job "price × (1 − priced margin)" — a better assumption than 35 %. And earned value by stage (stage % × that stage's budget) once the estimate carries stage hours.
06 · The "is this the best we can do?" pass

What changed between the first cut and this one

v1 — the plan as originally scoped

  • Snapshot column jobs_ledger.bid_estimated_cost, filled when a job is created from a bid; replace the two null arguments.
  • Burn header reads "bid estimate as budget"; tiles and charts unchanged.
  • Budget as one dollar figure; labor compared in dollars.
  • Nothing for existing jobs; nothing when the bid has no estimate; nothing on the Bid Board.

Honest about the arithmetic, blind to the data: it would have shipped and lit up zero jobs.

v2 — what the review added

  • Provenance over a number. A job_budgets row with kind (bid · typed), breakdown, completeness, bid version, taken-at and by. Every readout carries the glyph.
  • Linking is the feature. Ranked candidates on the Costs tab (15 exact matches waiting today), one-tap link, Bid Board chips with verbs, the New Job carry box.
  • Hours first for labor; components each get their own bar and at-completion; a written "why".
  • Typed budgets so a $250k job never has to burn against a guess while the estimate is missing.
  • Partial estimates stay partial (labor vs bid, materials vs assumption) instead of blending into one unexplainable figure.
  • Estimator cost excluded from the direct budget — it is already overhead — and bid value ≠ job price surfaced, not hidden.
