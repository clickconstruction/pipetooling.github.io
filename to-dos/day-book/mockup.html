2 · Why the month reads by kind, and what an estimator's month says

The month answers "is the work getting done, by whom, and where are the gaps." Nobody is scored by volume.

The grid flippedv2 shaded a person's day by how many things landed. That is a click count with better manners. Now each row is a kind of work, each cell is the initials of who did it, and an empty run is the signal: three days with nothing on a row while the queue held work turns amber. That is the sentence a manager wants: nobody applied deposits Wednesday to Friday.

One person's monthPick a person and the same grid shows only their initials, so a manager reads coverage and the person reads their own rhythm. Neither view compares two people side by side.

Left, not just doneToday's lines carry the live queue: 13 open bills left to send, 48 still waiting. Activity against the pile is progress; activity alone is just activity. For past days this needs a nightly snapshot of the queue counts, which is a later PR, so history shows the outcomes without the "left" until then.

The door/people?tab=day-book&person=…&from=…&to=…, the same shape Review uses. A manager can send a link to one person's week. A person opening the tab lands on themselves; an assistant or estimator cannot pick anyone else.

4 · What the "best we can do" pass changed

  1. Was: a "Quiet days" tile in the strip and on the person door. Now: gone. The quiet line stays on the day, grey, saying what the app cannot see.
  2. Was: a month grid shaded by how many outcomes a person had. Now: kind × day with initials, and a run of empty days on a row turns amber while the queue held work. Coverage and gaps, not volume.
  3. Was: outcomes with no context. Now: today's lines end with what is left, from the live Needs You counts. History gets it after a nightly snapshot.
  4. Was: "Office days", office people only. Now: "Day book", with estimators on their clocked days, an Estimating chip, and a range strip of the measures that mean something for a bid: sent, late, decided, trailing hit rate, lost with no reason, unfollowed, quote turnaround, robot delta, hours per $100k.
  5. Kept: nothing typed; the clock-out note read, never prompted; money gated like payroll; the deleted-records line as an owner question; no person ranked against another anywhere on the tab.

5 · Decisions this design needs

  1. The name. Day book is the ledger word for a daily record and fits Crew Day, Bridge, Why we lost. Alternatives: Office days (excludes estimators), Outcomes.
  2. Who sees whom. Dev, controller and pay-approved masters see everyone with amounts; assistants and estimators see only themselves. Superintendents, subs and primaries do not see the tab.
  3. The amber gap rule. Three working days with nothing on a row, while that queue held work. Two? Five?
  4. The deleted-records line. Keep, since it is recoverable and management asks for it, or drop it.
  5. Estimator money. Confirm an estimator sees full values on their own bids on this tab, as they do on the Bid Board today.
Build order, smallest first: (1) the kernel that turns a day's records into lines per person plus one RPC for a range, gated like payroll; (2) the tab with Week view, person door and kind chips; (3) the Month rhythm grid with the gap rule; (4) the estimator lines and range strip, reusing the Bridge's sent/won counts; (5) the schedule-block trigger; (6) the Crew Day one-liner and email; (7) the nightly queue snapshot so history carries "left".
