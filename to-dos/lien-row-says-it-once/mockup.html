The short of it

The first line is the clock. It knows the legal last day and when the money is expected. The second line is the paper. It knows where the notice or the affidavit stands on the Lien desk. The second was written as a chip that stands alone, so it carries its own day and count. Under the first line it says them again.

My suggestion

  1. One lien line. Its words are the next act on the lien path. Its day is the legal last day that act protects. The count shows once.
  2. Words from the paper. Day, dot, weight and colour from the clock. So the row and the Lien calendar can never show two days.
  3. A second line only for paper that is out. Sent, delivered, held, the attorney's letter. It never carries a day and count the first line has.
  4. The line under the rule speaks of money only. It never names a lien act again.
  5. The press opens the Lien window on the right paper. Today it opens on Demand letter.

The clock gives the line its day and its colour. The paper gives it its words.

Your calls, answered 2026-11-12: "I like all of your suggestions"

  1. One line with the next act first, or only trim the repeat? My pick is the next act first. The trim alone is the first PR either way, and it fixes the row you sent.
  2. The name of the paper on the line. My pick is File the affidavit, the Lien window's and the Lien desk's own words. The other choice is File the lien everywhere.
  3. Where the press lands. My pick is the job's Lien window, on the paper the line names. The other choice sends a notice step straight to the Lien desk, which leaves the board.
  4. A closed notice window while the affidavit's is still open. The row says Lien gone today. The Lien window says a late notice can still go, the owner's reading of Oct 6. My pick is that the row follows the window.
