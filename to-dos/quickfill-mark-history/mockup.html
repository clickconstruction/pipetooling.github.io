Is A the best we can do?

  1. It borrows the grammar the owner already reads. The tiles are the ledger's, the cells are the History tab's, the rows are today's table. Nothing on the window is a second language.
  2. Every question is one glance. How often: the filled cells. The gaps: the dashed boxes, on their weekday. Who and when: the habit line. The count and its trend: the numbers in the cells, row by row.
  3. The count rule is the simplest true one. Green means nothing was open, amber means something was, and the number says how much. The third tint, red above the station's usual, is drawn above as the owner's call.
  4. The events already hold it. Every station's marks go back to mid-April, one a workday, by two people. The current marks table adds nothing a backfill could use: each of its 33 rows already has its event.
  5. Left out, on purpose: the line (B), a per-person tally as a table, a span switch between four and eight weeks. Eight weeks fits a phone, and the fold holds the rest.
