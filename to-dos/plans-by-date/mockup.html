Where the schedule is typed

A schedule is two dates on the bid, a start and an end, and a person types both. Nothing is counted from the day the bid was sent. A bid with no start has no schedule, and the chart draws no Schedule lane for it.

What each choice draws

Start empty. No schedule. The chart has no Schedule lane for this bid.

Start and End. A bar from one to the other, with the days left as the first figure. The quiet words beside the dates say how long that is.

Start, with No end date ticked. The bar runs off the right edge as an arrow, and the figure reads No end date.

Past the End. The bar keeps its end. The figure turns red and counts the days over.

An End before the Start is refused with its reason, and nothing saves.

A second door, on the chart

A bid with no schedule shows a quiet Set the schedule press on its hover line and on its row of the dated list. It opens a small sheet with the same two dates and the same tick.

A bid that has one shows Change in the same two places.

The office and the estimators can set it, since both can edit a bid today.

What each lane reads

Everything on the chart comes from what the app already records. The first build needs no change to the database.

Bids

A diamond on the day each bid was sent. A small open diamond marks the date of the plans it was priced from. The hover line names the day it was won.

One lane for each part

A dot for every set, on the date printed on its sheets. The set the part builds from wears a ring and its name in bold. A set with no date is an open dot on the day it came. An as-built of ours is a square. Papers are left out.

A dotted line joins two lanes on the day one design became two parts.

Crews on site

The clock's days on the site's jobs, joined into bars. A week with nobody on site ends a bar. A job with no clock days draws an open bar from its first work day to its last.

History

A tick for every line kept on History by hand. A letter gets a flag and its word, since paper is what people look for later.

The list under the chart

Every mark again as a dated line, oldest first, with each bid as a tinted row. It is the chart for a screen reader, a phone and a search.

Print

One press opens a landscape page, as the Scoreboard's print does. The page is drawn further down.

Who sees it

  • The office (dev, leaders, assistants, controller) sees every lane.
  • An estimator sees the same chart. It carries dates and drawings and no money. The clock's days are closed to an estimator, so the crew lane shows each job's last work day as a single mark and says so.
  • The crew does not open the Development page, and that stays. A crew member still sees the set their part builds from on the job itself.

On paper

The print is one landscape letter page: the site, the four figures, the chart and the dated list in three columns. A long list runs onto a second page. Paper is always light.

What the chart cannot draw

  • A crew trip that was entered as a job line with the date only in its name. Setting the job's first and last work day puts it on the chart as an open bar.
  • A set with no date on its sheets and no day it came. Every set has the day it came, so this should not happen.
  • A bid that was never marked sent. It has no day to stand on, so the list names it at the end.
