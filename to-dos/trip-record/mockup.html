A trip record: days at a site away from home, apart from the hours on its jobs

On a job out of state, hours are not the story. Some of those hours are a crew at the site. Some are a truck on the road. Some are a person at home loading what the trip needs. All of them may be billable to the job, but only some are a trip. This page draws a record for the trip itself, and every door that opens it.

The gap, in one picture

Today the only way to set a trip apart is to make a job for it. That splits one piece of work across two jobs, and it still cannot say which hours were at the site.

Today

  • The rough-in is one job. One trip inside it was made a second job so it could be seen alone.
  • The clock says hours on a job. It does not say whether the person was at the site, driving, or at home.
  • A trip the customer asked for and a trip we chose look the same.
  • Who went, which days, and why live in notes, mail and memory.

With a trip record new

  • A trip belongs to the site, not to a job. Its work can sit on one job or several. No job is made to hold it.
  • Each day of a trip is one of three kinds: Getting ready, On the road, At the site.
  • The clock's hours fall into those days by themselves. Hours outside any trip read At home for the site.
  • A trip says who asked for it and how it went, so an extra trip can be billed with its reason.

The rules, in plain words

What a trip is

  • One going-out to a site and coming back: people, days, a reason.
  • It belongs to the development. A job with no development can hold a trip by itself.
  • Only one thing is required: one person and one day.
  • Where it stands is read from its days: Planned, Out now, Back. Nobody types it.

How hours fall in

  • A person's clock rows on a trip day take that day's kind.
  • A punch with a place counts At the site only when the place is the site.
  • A row with no place, such as an imported or scheduled row, is shown as No place on the punch. It says the day was paid. It does not say where.
  • Hours on the site's jobs outside every trip are At home for the site.

What the app may do by itself

  • Nothing is made by itself. The app may say Looks like a trip. A person makes it, or says Not a trip once.
  • A trip with no punch at the site says so in amber. It is never blocked.
  • Changing or deleting a trip never changes a clock row, a bill or a cost. A trip is a reading laid over them.

Who sees what

  • The office roles and a leader read and change the site's trips.
  • A crew member reads and starts the trips they are on.
  • Dollars on a trip show to a dev or a controller, as on the Scoreboard.
  • An estimator does not see the look.
