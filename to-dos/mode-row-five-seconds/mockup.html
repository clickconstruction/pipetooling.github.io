Punch list #433 · a mock-up, nothing is built

The mode row shows for five seconds, then leaves

The row that switches between Task Mode, the Dashboard and Job Mode sits on top of a phone's Dashboard. The ask of 2026-11-24, after a look at it on a phone:

I like the idea of how we implemented this, but I don't love the implementation. We need to remove the new icon from job mode. And I want it to appear at the top for only five seconds at a time when a user goes to the dashboard and then I want it to slide up and out away from the page where a user can no longer see it. I also want to see a timer of sorts, like a bar that slides across the screen to let the user know when it's going to go away.

It is drawn twice. The first draw is the ask taken word for word. The second draw is the same ask after a hard look at the first, and it is the one to build. Every job, street and name on the phones is made up.

First draw: the ask, word for word

Kept for the record

The row is part of the page, as it is today. A blue bar fills across. At five seconds the row slides up and the page slides up after it. Nothing is left behind. The ring is a thumb on its way to Clock In.

What is wrong with the first draw

Read as the person holding the phone.

  1. The page jumps under a thumb.When the row goes, everything under it moves up by the row's height. A thumb on its way to Clock In lands on the card below it. It happens on every visit, five seconds in, which is about when a person presses.
  2. The row can leave under a thumb.The count does not wait for a finger resting on a third. The press lands on whatever was under the row.
  3. There is no way back.On a phone the gear menu lost its Job Mode and Task Mode rows when the row was built. With the row gone and nothing left behind, the only way to switch is to leave the Dashboard and come back.
  4. Task Mode becomes a trap.Task Mode is one page and a gear. The row is its way out. Five seconds after the switch the way out is gone. Try it on the first draw.
  5. The bar reads as loading.A blue bar that fills from the left is what a page shows while it loads. Blue is also the colour of the third that is on, so the two compete.
  6. The band under the row moves too.The View as band, and any other line above the header, rides up with the page.
  7. It plays at people who asked for less motion.A slide and a moving bar, every visit, with no other form.

Second draw: the one to build

The pick

The row lies over the top of the page, the way a phone's own notification does. It comes down over the header and goes back up, and nothing under it moves either time. The bar starts full and runs out, in grey. A small handle stays behind. The thumb's ring sits on Clock In in all four.

What it still costs. For five seconds on each visit the row covers the search box, the gear and any band above the header. A swipe up clears it at once. A person who wants the gear first will learn the swipe or wait. That is the price of a page that never jumps.

The calls

Each question the ask left open, with the pick that is drawn and the other way it could go. Say a number and a word to flip one.

1 The way back

PickThe handle stays where the row was, and a pull down at the top of the page brings the row back. Both are built today. A person who finds neither is not stuck: the row comes back by itself the next time they go to the Dashboard.

FlipNo handle, so nothing at all is left to see. Or put the three modes back in the phone's gear menu as a second door, as a computer has.

2 What counts as going to the Dashboard

PickOpening or reloading the app on it. Arriving from another page. Pressing the Dashboard link while already there. Coming back to the app after a minute or more away. And any press on the row, which gets a fresh five seconds so the way back is under the thumb.

FlipOnly opening the app and arriving from another page.

3 Task Mode and Job Mode

PickJob Mode is drawn on the Dashboard, so it gets the same five seconds. Task Mode keeps the row for good, with no count and no bar, because the row is its way out. A sub or a helper gets the same five seconds as everyone.

FlipFive seconds in Task Mode too, with the handle as the way out.

4 Does the count pause

PickYes. A finger on the row holds it, and the count starts whole again when the finger lifts. A keyboard's focus inside the row holds it too. For a screen reader the handle is a named press that never times out.

FlipThe count picks up where it stopped.

5 What the page does when the row leaves

PickNothing. The row lies over the page and never pushes it. The strip it uncovers takes no press for a third of a second, so a thumb aimed at a third does not land on the gear.

FlipThe row stays part of the page and the page slides up after it, as in the first draw.

6 The bar

PickOn the row's bottom edge, the full width of the screen. It starts full and its end runs to the left until nothing is left. It is grey, the app's muted text colour, because it is only time.

FlipBlue, to match the third that is on. Or filling from the left, as in the first draw.

7 Less motion

PickNo slide. The row is there, then fades out in a blink. The bar is five blocks that go out one a second.

FlipThe row does not come by itself for these people. The handle is their door.

8 The other bands

PickView as, Add Task, the twin line and training mode stay where they are. The row lies over the top one for five seconds. Customer Waiting is covered for those seconds too.

FlipThe row waits while Customer Waiting is up, and the handle is the door until it clears.

9 The New mark

PickOff, as asked. Nothing marks Job Mode as new after that. A row that arrives with a moving bar on every visit draws the eye more than the mark did, and the mark was wrong for anyone who had tried Job Mode on another phone.

FlipNone drawn.

What is there today

The row shows on a person's first five opens of the page, or until a third is pressed. After that the page opens with a 24 px handle in the row's place, and a tap on it or a pull down brings the row back for that visit. Job Mode wears a green New until a third is first pressed on that phone.

So the handle and the pull are already built. The ask changes one rule: the row shows on every visit, for five seconds, with a bar. The count of first opens and the phone's memory of a press both go.
