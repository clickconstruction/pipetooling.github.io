Counsel's memo says "Timeline — run it separately on every job" and "move every job onto that grid today." Rules of the tab
  • A row opens the job on the Notices or Affidavits pane, whichever its next step is. The tab itself has no verbs — the panes do the work.
  • Something due is the default lens; All includes the tail (filed, waiting) and the dead (every window closed, noted). A closed-and-noted job leaves after the desk's 7-day keep, as the Missed lens does today (v2.3680).
  • The count on the tab is jobs with something due inside the desk's 30-day lead, so it agrees with Lien desk · N on the Collections header.

Is this the best we can do?

Is the strip on the pane enough on its own? No. A filed job leaves the desk 30 days after filing and the suit clock runs a year. Without the tab and the Dashboard's fourth watch the tail is still unwatched — the same gap as today, moved one step later. The three parts are one design; PR 1 alone is a better header, not the timeline.
Is this the memo's grid, or something else? It is the grid, twice: per job (the strip) and per GC (the print). The memo's grid is a table because counsel works in tables; the office works one job at a time on the desk. One kernel draws both, so the dates cannot disagree.
Are we inventing dates the app cannot back? No. Two of the six steps need a date the app does not hold (our contract end, the original contract's completion). They are drawn dashed with the fact they need and its door. That is the honest state, and it is the frame #33 §1 and §3 slot into — without it those pieces would ship as two more unrelated dates.
Why not a calendar? Dates are sparse — one per month per job — and the question is never what is on the 15th, it is where does this job stand. A month grid answers the first. Rejected.
Why not on Job Detail or the Pipeline card? The Lien window (the per-job instruments) gets the compact one-line strip in PR 3, replacing its two-date header — and finally applies the creation fallback there. Job Detail is long already, and lien dates matter only once money is open and the GC is quiet; that is the desk's job. #34 asks for the desk's doors on the Pipeline; this does not change that.
Does the pane get taller? By one strip, in place of one line. The sticky strip keeps Next in view once the paper scrolls. Nothing under it moves. If the owner wants it tighter, the strip collapses to its Next line with a ▸ — the same fold the gates use.
Anything it must not do? Never move a missed month to a later date (v2.3681); never stamp a miss without a person's name (v2.3679); never claim a stale month on the form (v2.3745); never guess a property kind (v2.3670). The strip reads those states; it does not change them.
What does it cost? One pure kernel with the date math (most of it exists in lienDeadlines.ts), a strip component drawn twice (full and mini), a tab that reuses the desk's data hook plus one query for filings and releases, a print view, and one more branch in the Dashboard's watch. No migration, no stored rows. Three small PRs.
