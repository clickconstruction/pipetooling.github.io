What is wrong with it

1

A dropdown with nothing to drop down to. Terms lists one option because no customer document has ever been put in the Contract Book. The wording is a constant in the source code. Taunya cannot change a word of it from anywhere in the app she can reach.

2

Every outcome is an email nobody signs. Send, Send & next and Skip. There is no way to put this agreement on paper, and paper is the only way this office has ever finished one.

3

Paper only goes one direction. The app happily takes a signed contract in, through Already signed? File it and the Drive pass. It cannot produce the page to sign.

4

Terms is sweep-wide and then it locks. One choice covers all 105 jobs, and the moment a draft exists the row goes read-only and says to change it in the full editor. The full editor offers the same one-option dropdown.

After, first pass

Give her the two things she named. A Download PDF button in the footer, an Edit these terms link under the dropdown, and a stamp so a job that goes out on paper leaves the pile of 105 instead of sitting in it forever.

What it adds

A

An unsigned PDF, from the renderer that already exists. One change matters: the signature block on the current renderer is hard-coded signed electronically. A draft must print blank signature and date rules instead, or we hand a customer a page claiming a signature that does not exist.

B

Taking the PDF marks it handed over. Without this the button quietly corrupts the sweep's own count: a job only leaves the 105 when its contract is marked sent.

C

The built-in wording becomes a real Book document with a version date, so the dropdown finally has something in it and an edit door beneath it.

The hard look

Is this the best we can do?

No. It answers Taunya literally and leaves the real shape of the problem untouched. Five things it gets wrong.

It leaves the button that has never worked as the main one

Send & next stays the blue primary, and the paper lane gets a quieter button beside it. That is hedging. Zero of the sends have ever been signed and three of three finished contracts are paper. The pane should not present one house style and a workaround.

Make how this one gets signed an explicit choice on the row, the way the sweep already decides that a builder's row wants a different door.

Download is a step, not an outcome

What Taunya wants is a signed agreement. If she downloads the PDF and sends it from her own mail, the app loses the record, the reminder and the signature, and we have built a button whose success we cannot see. The first pass missed the option in the middle entirely.

The app emails the PDF as an attachment. Same paper document, still sent and tracked by the app.

"Edit these terms" walks her into a shared document, mid-sweep

That link opens the Contract library, which lives under People, and edits a document every one of the 105 agreements uses. Nothing in the first pass says so. Someone fixing a sentence for one homeowner would change the contract language for the whole pile without being told.

Two clearly separate levers: this job's wording, and the standard terms, labelled with what an edit touches.

It ignores what actually happened that day

The pain on Sep 3 was not the absence of a PDF. It was sending, needing to change it, and having no way to do that except void the contract and generate a new revision with a new email. Three times. Nothing in the first pass helps.

While a sent agreement is still unopened, Edit & re-send should be one action.

It treats a builder's row like a homeowner's

81 of the 105 need a look, and many are builders whose own subcontract is the agreement. Our service agreement as a PDF is the wrong document on those rows. A button bar that looks identical everywhere invites sending the wrong paper.

On a GC row, filing theirs is the whole block, and our three ways step back.

✓

The middle way is the one most likely to work. A paper document the customer can print and sign, sent and remembered by the app. It is the option the first pass did not think of, and it fits every finished contract in the table above.

✓

Nothing is hidden behind a workaround. Downloading is still there, now as a stated way of working rather than an escape hatch, and it says what it does to the job.

✓

The terms question is answered without a free-text box. Per-job wording and shared legal language sit in different boxes, and the shared one says out loud how far an edit reaches.

If we build it

Order, smallest first

  1. Ask Taunya which terms she means — the legal paragraphs, or the payment line. One message. It decides whether step 4 is needed at all.
  2. The unsigned PDF — teach the shared renderer a blank signature block, add a draft mode to the function that already returns contract PDFs, put Download in the pane.
  3. Email the PDF, and record the hand-off — the middle way, plus the stamp that moves a job out of the 105 when it goes out on paper.
  4. Terms as two levers — seed the built-in wording into the Book as a versioned document, split per-job from shared, say what an edit touches.
  5. How this one gets signed — the three-way block with a default per row, and the builder row collapsing to file theirs.
  6. Edit & re-send while unopened — the Sep 3 fix. Worth doing even though nobody asked for it.

Yours to decide

  • Does handing over paper count as sent? It would move the job out of the pile of 105 and into waiting on a signature. I think yes, because the pile's question is whether the customer has been asked.
  • Which way is the default on a plain homeowner row? The mock-up pre-picks emailing the PDF because that is what the record supports. Picking the signing link instead is defensible and easy to change.
  • Should a builder row be able to send ours at all? Shown here demoted rather than removed.
  • Who may edit the standard terms? Anyone in the office can write to the Book today. Contract language may deserve a narrower door.
