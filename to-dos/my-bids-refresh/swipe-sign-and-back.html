A sign that a row swipes, and a swipe back

You tried the swipe on your phone and it held. Then you asked for two more things:

I think we need to make it clear to our user that they have the option to swipe on my bids. The rules of A

  1. When. The first row of the card is in full view for half a second, no finger is down and the page is still.
  2. What. That one row slides left, holds for a second with its strip's word showing, and slides back. 1.4 seconds in all.
  3. It cannot hide anything. The slide stops short of the mark, and only a finger can pass the mark.
  4. A touch stops it. The row goes straight back. A press on the row still opens the bid.
  5. How often. Once a day at most, three times at most. It stops for good the first time the person swipes a row themselves.
  6. One sign for both cards. The count belongs to the gesture. Whichever card comes into view first shows it.
  7. Where it is kept. In the app's own store of dismissals, which follows a person to each device. A new phone does not start over.
  8. Phones only. A desk has no swipe. Each row there already has its word press.
  9. View as. It may play. Nothing is written for the person being viewed.

Where a right swipe may start

The striped band is the first 32 px of the screen. A swipe that starts there is the phone's own Back, so the row does not answer it. Try it from the band, then from the middle of the row.

What the swipe back does

  • The strip. On the left, 160 px, grey then blue at 112 px. Its words are Show again on a bid and Mark unread on a report.
  • Letting go past the mark puts the row back in its group, first in line as today.
  • A toast says where it went, with Undo for ten seconds: Shown again: Sample Retail Shell, phase 2. Today the press says nothing.
  • A swipe the wrong way answers. A left swipe on a put-away row moves nothing away. The row shows Show again on its left for a second, so the person sees which way it goes. A right swipe on a shown row answers with Hide the same way. Try it in the frames marked as my pick.
  • The line under the group's heading says the move: You hid these. Swipe one right to show it again. It sits over the rows, never under them.
  • The sign plays here too, the first time the group is opened: the first row slides right and shows its word.
  • A keyboard and a screen reader still have a real press. The strip is a button, as Hide's strip is.
  • At a desk nothing changes. Show again and Mark unread stay as word presses.
  • The screen's left edge. A swipe counts when it starts 32 px or more from the edge. A row's box starts 24 px in, so 8 px of the row is left out.

Is this the best we can do?

What is weak in A and in the swipe back, and what was done about each. Four lines are still weak, and they say so.

A person looking elsewhere misses a move that plays once.

Changed. It plays up to three times, once a day, until the person swipes a row themselves. Then never.

A row that moves as the thumb comes down.

Changed. It waits for a still page and no finger. A touch sends the row straight back, and the press still opens the bid. Only the first row moves, and it stops short of the mark.

The strip's word is at the far end, so a short slide shows half of Mark opened.

Changed. The slide goes as far as the word needs, 64 px or 108 px.

A person who asked their phone for less motion.

Changed. No slide. The words stand under the heading for that visit and count the same way.

It would start over on a new phone.

Changed. The count is kept with the person, in the store the app's dismissed alerts already use. No new table and no migration.

With no press, the way back from Hidden is a gesture.

Still weak. Four things carry it: the line over the rows, the sign the first time the group opens, a wrong-way swipe that answers with the right way, and Undo right after a hide. A person who reads nothing and never drags the row has no press to find on a phone. Way 2 keeps the press and costs the name its room.

A right swipe from the screen's edge is the phone's Back.

Still weak. The app cannot stop the phone's Back. It can only keep the row still, which the 32 px rule does. Apple does not publish how wide its edge is, so the number is checked on a phone before it ships. The Pipeline's rows already swipe right with no such rule.

The sign shows the swipe, never the mark.

Still weak. It does not teach "pull until it turns blue". The strip turning blue under the thumb teaches that, as it does today.

A sliding row loses the start of its name.

Still weak. For 1.4 seconds the first word or two are cut. It is how the swipe itself looks.

Your picks

  1. The sign: A, B or C. My pick is A. It is the one that shows the move, and it costs nothing after it has taught. The foot's line stays as the standing words.
  2. The press on a put-away row, on a phone: gone, or kept. My pick is gone, way 1. The plan of 2026-11-23 gave a bid two moves and no buttons on a phone: a press goes in and a swipe hides. This is the same plan for a hidden one. Keep it, way 2, if a way back that nobody can miss matters more than the name's room.
  3. A toast with Undo when a row comes back. My pick is yes, for a swipe and for the desk's press. A swipe can be made by accident in either direction.
  4. How often the sign plays. My pick is up to three times, once a day, ending at the person's own first swipe. The other answer is once, ever.

The build, after your pick

Three small steps, each with its kernel and tests first, its guide and its release note. The client only. No migration and no function.

  1. The swipe back on My Bids. The shared row and its kernel learn a side. Hidden's rows swipe right, with the toast, the 32 px rule and the answer to a wrong-way swipe.
  2. The swipe back on Recent Reports. Opened's rows, the same row and the same rule.
  3. The sign. One kernel says when it may play and how often. Both cards and both directions use it.

Each step is walked in the simulator before the card closes: the swipe from the screen's edge, a scroll that drifts, and the sign with a finger on the way down.
