When can each paper go out?

Every paper has a first day and a last day. The first day is usually much earlier than people think.

Jun · workJulAugSepOct
Notice
Lien
  1. 1The notice can go out on July 1. Once a month's work is done and unpaid, its notice can be mailed. Last day: Sep 15. One notice can cover several months, and the oldest month sets the date.
  2. 2The lien can be filed once the notice is in the mail and the bill is still unpaid. Last day: Oct 15, counted from the last month worked, not the first.
  3. 3Sooner holds back more money. The owner only has to hold back what they pay the GC after the notice arrives.

When each paper can go out

Every paper has a window: a first day it can go out and a last day. The app counts the last day for you. The first day is usually much earlier than people think, and nothing is gained by waiting.

PaperFirst dayLast day
NoticeThe 1st of the month after the work, once it is unpaid15th of the 3rd month after (2nd if residential)
Lien (affidavit)Once the notice for those months is in the mail15th of the 4th month after the last month worked (3rd if residential)
Example · June work, nobody has paid
The notice can go out July 1 and must go out by September 15. If June was the last month on the job, the lien can be filed any day after the notice is mailed, up to October 15. On a house, both dates move a month earlier: August 17 (the 15th is a Saturday) and September 15.
Why sooner is better

The notice tells the owner to hold back what we're owed from the GC, but only from payments the owner makes after it arrives. A notice mailed July 1 can catch the July, August and September draws. One mailed September 15 may find the owner has already paid out.

When not to rush

The law allows a lien right after the notice. The relationship with the GC might not. Filing one on a steady GC's job is the leader's decision, and the desk asks for approval before anything is filed.

the Texas lien rules the app follows · new row + three fixes
How early each paper can go

What it says. The notice may be given once a month's labor or materials are furnished and unpaid; the statute sets only the last day. The affidavit may be filed once the claim has accrued and, on a sub job, the notice has been given; the statute again sets only the last day. The owner is liable only for payments made after it receives the notice.

What the app does. The timeline and the Months table show each window from its first day; the desk's When can I send? card says it in three sentences.

Cite. § 53.056(a-1), § 53.052, § 53.053 (accrual), § 53.081, § 53.084(a).

Not yet verified, or for the attorney — three lines added

+ that a notice may go out for a month whose bill is not yet past due;

+ that the affidavit may be filed as soon as the notice is mailed, with no wait (counsel said so for a GC who had stopped answering, 2026-09-22);

+ that a sub still on the job may file for finished months.
