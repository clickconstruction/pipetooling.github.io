  • State → move is a sentence. "Work · no agreement → Get it in writing." "Pre-inspection → Schedule inspection." "Paid, queued → Pay Behar." The arrow is not decoration; it is the grammar of the column, and every row parses the same way.
  • The left half is untouched. The dots, the centered label and the sublabel are exactly what shipped today. Only their neighbor changed, so the eye that learned the rail this week keeps it.
  • The right half is the Next mock-up from before, one primary in the tone of its urgency (blue normal, amber lapsed, green money), quiet text when the sub owns the step, a ⋯ for the rest. Two buttons only at Pre-inspection.
  • Stage rows have no dots — there is no agreement yet — so the left half is their one-line standing ("Window set · no order yet") and the move is Offer to…, which opens the sub picker inline.
  • Row height stays at two lines. The dots-plus-label stack and the button-plus-hint stack are the same height, so the merged cell is no taller than either was alone. The four remaining columns fit at 1040px without a horizontal scroll.

What the width buys

This morning · 9 columns

Sub · Window · Agreed · Paid · Open · Where it stands · Next · GC portal · actions. At 1280px the rail label wrapped, the buttons column was the widest thing on the row, and the GC column was a chip most of the time.

Proposed · 4 columns

Sub · Window (with the GC) · money stack · Standing → Next. The Window track gets 220px, which is what makes the schedule readable; the merged cell gets ~330px and reads as one sentence. Nothing is hidden — the ⋯ carries what the actions column carried.
