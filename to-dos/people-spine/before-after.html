Ship order (each its own PR)
  1. Guardrail — pay rosters read the active-roster rule; a kernel test proves a sample with a pay row stays hidden.
  2. Lenses — Users gets Contact / Account / Pay; the modal and the panel render the same table.
  3. Desk finishes archive — reassign and template cleanup inside the desk dialog.
  4. Pointers — Settings and Payroll doors become links; samples and twins move to System.
  5. Born linked — invite writes the roster row with the account.
Not changed: who may see wages, who may flip training mode or archive, and every edge function behind those actions. This is where the same controls live, not what they may do.

Stronger — one spine, one view, three moments

Stronger — the person is the spine, the roster is a view, writes happen at three moments

Lenses fix where you look. This fixes how rows get inconsistent in the first place.
1 · One spine
Today the account (users) and the roster row (people) are peers, and pay tables join on the name. Make the roster row the spine: every account belongs to one person, and every pay table keys on the person id it already carries.
people
the person
name · kind · start / end · archived
↓ person_id
users · the login, 0 or 1
people_pay_config · wage, salary
workday template · time off
pay_stubs · people_hours · sessions
2 · One roster view, in the database
roster_people =
  people ⟕ users
  where kind = 'person'
  and archived_at is null
Every roster, picker, Hours grid, pay list, Draft Payroll scan and email fan-out reads this view. Samples, twins and service accounts carry another kind and are not in it by construction. No surface can forget a filter it never had to write.
The Training Helper under this model: created as kind sample, so it never appears in the view. Even with a pay row and a template, no pay list, total or scan can reach it. There is nothing to sanitize because nothing could have been polluted.
Cost: a backfill (every account gets its people row; 18 today, a handful unlinked), then the name-keyed joins move to person_id one table per PR — the same shape as the sheet-link conversion train, five PRs, each verified on prod before the next.
3 · Three moments, on the Person desk
Rows go wrong between tables because each table writes on its own schedule. Put the writes at the moments a person actually changes, and make the tables read-and-scan lenses. The desk already has Start / End employment checklists; this is those, finished.
Hire
One form: name · kind · role · start date · wage or salary · vehicle deal · invite email.
One write: person row, account (if a login), pay row, workday template (if salaried), packet assigned.
A hire cannot be half a person. A salaried hire with no wage is refused, not saved.
Change
Role · wage · salary ↔ hourly · supervision · training mode · vehicle.
Each change is one desk row with its side effects inside: salary on adds the template and syncs sessions; salary off clears them; role change re-checks grants.
The Pay lens keeps inline wage edits for raise season; they call the same Change path.
Leave
One dialog: end date · reassign customers · final pay report · hand back vehicle and housing.
One write: end date on the person, account banned, template and future sessions cleared, archived stamp on the spine so every view drops them at once.
Four clicks across three screens become one, and nothing is left running in the background.
Keeps from the lens plan: Users as the one roster with Contact / Account / Pay lenses, the Settings pointer, samples and twins out of the human list.
Order: view + kind first (one migration, every roster reads it) → Leave → Hire → person_id joins one table at a time → lenses last, because by then they are only column sets.
