5 The calls

Five, Grace's. The recommendation is first in each.

  1. The voice. First person, as Luis would say it recommended, or third person with {me}. The text already says this is Luis; a third sentence about he breaks the voice and asks the office to get the pronoun right. The office may still write a line in the third person.
  2. May a tech write? Turn a line off only recommended, or suggest one the office approves, or write freely. The veto keeps the company's voice and ships smallest; a suggestion door is one later cut.
  3. The rotation. The thread's memory, never the same fact twice to one customer while another waits recommended, or round robin per tech across every customer, or random. The customer is the one who hears a repeat.
  4. Where the office edits. The person's desk recommended, a fold per tech in the quick replies editor, or a card on Settings → Phone and texts. The desk is where the office already shapes who a person is.
  5. Day one. The migration appends {about me} to the On my way line recommended, or the office adds it by hand. Appending means the first tech with lines is heard on the first press; one edit takes it out.

6 Alternatives weighed

  1. One bio field on the user, appended whole.rejected The same paragraph every visit, and a text that runs three segments.
  2. A random fact on every press.rejected A customer on a three-visit job hears a fact twice while another waits; and unless the pick rides the request, the preview and the text can differ.
  3. Kinds the app composes (family, a team, years) through a template.rejected The he/she problem and stiff grammar; the office writes a better sentence than a template fills.
  4. The tech writes their own bio in Settings.rejected for now The voice customers hear is the company's. The tech sees every line and can turn any one off instead.
  5. A {about me} blank, the office's lines in the tech's voice, the pick by the thread's memory, the veto in Settings.picked
  6. A link to a "Meet Luis" page with a photo (Housecall Pro's way).not now {link} is off the phone until the field can make the customer's page link, and there is no photo column. The first follow-on once the On my way customer-page card exists.

7 Is this the best we can do? The second pass

The first draft took the picture as drawn: third-person lines, the office writing them in a settings card, a random one on every On my way. Read back against the customer who gets the text, the tech it is about, and the rule that the preview is the text, eight things changed.

  1. Third person became first person. No pronoun to get wrong; one voice from one person.
  2. Random became the thread's memory. One column on the message, and the pick is the same on the phone and the server.
  3. Office-only became the office writes, the tech vetoes. The line is about the tech; one switch per line is theirs.
  4. Baked into On my way became a blank the office places. Whether, in which line, and where in the sentence are the office's.
  5. A required blank became the first optional one. It vanishes with its sentence and never stops a send.
  6. No Spanish rule became skip the line. A Spanish reader never gets an English fact wedged in; the desk shows what still needs Spanish.
  7. Fixed on the sheet became Another fact / Not this time. The tech may pick a better fact for this customer, or none, with no free text.
  8. Unbounded became 120 characters. One fact plus the line stays inside two segments; the editor counts down.
