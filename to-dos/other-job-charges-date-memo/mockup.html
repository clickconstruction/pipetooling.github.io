Other job charges: a date, a memo, and amounts that read as money

Today a charge is two boxes, a description and a number, and a trash can. The number shows as it was typed, 39796.7, with no thousands and a cent missing. There is nowhere to say when the charge applies or where it came from, so the owner has been writing the date, the plan it came from and what would replace it into the description, which then runs off the end of its box. This page draws the row with an amount that reads as money, a date on the left and a quiet memo line under the description.

B Two lines: date, description and amount above, the memo underneath

The row keeps its one-line shape for the three things that are read at a glance, the date, what the charge is, how much, and gives the memo the full width of a second line, where a sentence fits. A row with no memo stays one line tall; + memo summons the line, the way + Add other charge summons a row. An ESTIMATE — prefix shows as a chip, so an estimate is told apart from a real charge at a glance and the convention the cost batches rely on is untouched.

? Is this the best we can do, are we being straightforward

QuestionAnswer
The row already has a created_at. Why a date column?Because created_at is when the row was typed, not when the charge applies, and the job form's autosave rewrites every charge row on each save (delete all, insert all), so even the typed-on date does not survive. A charged_on column the row carries through the save is the only date that holds.
An overhead share is a quarter, not a day. Should the date be a period?One date, the end of the period, says which quarter a share belongs to and sorts correctly; the period itself is one phrase in the memo. Two date boxes on every row would serve the rare case and crowd the common one.
Why a memo and not a longer description box?The description is the line's label: it prints in the Costs packet and shows in the Bill preview's line references, where it should be short. The memo is the basis and the source, read when someone asks where the number came from. Two fields, two readers.
Why show ESTIMATE — as a chip instead of a checkbox?The cost-batch function admits an other charge only when its description starts with ESTIMATE; that text is the contract. The chip is drawn from the prefix, the prefix stays in the data, and nothing that reads the text changes.
Does the amount need a dollar sign?No. The sibling rows, card charges, supply invoices, show none, and the accordion total shows none. Commas and the smaller cents do the reading; a sign on this one column would make it the odd one.
What about the row's identity?Out of scope but worth saying: because the save deletes and re-inserts every charge row, a row written by a cost batch loses its id on the next autosave, and the batch's revert then cannot find it. The right fix is a save that upserts by id; it is a separate, smaller PR, listed in the plan as PR 3.
Why not build the date and memo first and the formatting later?The formatting needs no schema change and is a morning's work with tests; it ships first on its own so the owner sees the rows read as money while the column PR goes through the migration, the types regeneration and the batch function.
