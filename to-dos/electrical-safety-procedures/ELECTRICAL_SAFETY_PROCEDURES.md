## How to read this paper

Electrical is not the company's trade. Every worker on a company truck is an **unqualified person** under NFPA 70E, the electrical safety standard OSHA cites. An unqualified person learns to see the hazard, stay outside the boundaries and know when to call. They do not test, troubleshoot or work on anything live. This paper says what a plumber does near electrical, what a plumber never does and who they call.

This paper is the company's side of the Intro to electrical course. The course says what a worker must understand. This paper is where *the company's rule* is written down. Where the two differ, this paper is wrong or the lesson is wrong, and the safety lead fixes both.

Every procedure has the same shape. **Who** does it. **When**. **What** the steps are. **Stop work when** the one line that ends the work. **Where it comes from** names the NEC article, the OSHA rule and the lesson behind it. **Confirm** lists what the owner and the electrical reader must check before the paper is handed out.

Every number in this paper came from the course, which cites the NEC. The electrical reader confirms each one against the code cycle. The owner confirms each company rule. A number that fails the check is changed here first and in the lesson second.

The words *the electrician* mean the licensed electrician the company works with, named in procedure 13. The words *the safety lead* mean the person named at the top of this paper.

## 1. Every conductor is live until tested

**Who.** Every worker.

**When.** Before a hand goes near any wire, terminal, box or appliance connection.

**What.**
1. A wire is live until a tester says it is not. A breaker off proves nothing. A switch off proves nothing. A dark light proves nothing.
2. The breaker's label can be wrong. A circuit can be fed from a second place. The tester is the only truth.
3. A 240 V circuit has two hots. Each is tested, because one dead says nothing about the other.
4. A house with solar, a generator or a battery can feed a wire from the other end. Procedure 10 holds there.
5. The lugs above the main breaker stay live with the main off. Nothing in a panel is tested by a plumber. The panel is procedure 7.
6. Under the sink, the disposal's outlet and the dishwasher's box are live with the switch off.
7. A plumber works near a wire only when it is dead and tested dead. A plumber never works on a live wire for any reason.

**Stop work when.** The tester is missing, its battery is dead or it did not beep on a known live outlet. The work near the wire waits for a tester that proves itself.

**Where it comes from.** NFPA 70E Article 120 on an electrically safe work condition, as an unqualified person reads it. 29 CFR 1926.416. The refresher's lesson 28 and Intro to electrical's lesson 26.

**Confirm.** The reader confirms that nothing here reads as a qualified person's test.

## 2. The non-contact tester and test the tester

**Who.** Every worker. Every truck carries one.

**When.** Every time procedure 1 says test.

**What.**
1. Test the tester first. Hold it to an outlet that is known to be live. It beeps and lights.
2. Then test the wire, the terminal or the whip. Hold the tip close to each conductor.
3. Then test the known live outlet again. The tester still works, so the quiet reading was true.
4. Test each hot of a 240 V circuit on its own.
5. A non-contact tester cannot see a neutral carrying current. It cannot see inside metal conduit or a metal box with the cover on. Those take the electrician's meter, not the plumber's tester.
6. A tester with a dead battery says everything is dead. A spare battery rides on the truck.
7. The company issues the tester. A worker who has none tells the safety lead before the next job.

**Stop work when.** The tester does not beep on the known live outlet before or after. The reading is not trusted and the work waits.

**Where it comes from.** NFPA 70E Article 110 on test instruments and Article 120 on verifying absence of voltage. Read for the one tool a plumber carries. Intro to electrical's lesson 26.

**Confirm.** The reader confirms that a non-contact tester is enough for the touch points in this paper. The reader names where a meter is needed instead. The owner confirms every truck has one.

## 3. Lockout and tagout as the plumber sees it

**Who.** The electrician or the owner locks and tags. The plumber checks, tests and adds their own lock.

**When.** Any time a plumber's work needs a circuit off for longer than the look under a cover. A water heater swap, a disposal swap, a pump change and any job that leaves terminals open.

**What.**
1. The person who opens the breaker locks it and tags it. The tag carries their name and the reason. On the company's own jobs the owner says who that person is.
2. The plumber checks the lock is on the breaker itself, not on the panel door. A tag with no lock is a request, not a lock.
3. The plumber tests the circuit dead at the work. Procedure 2.
4. Where there is a hasp, the plumber puts their own lock on too. One key, in their pocket.
5. A plumber never cuts, removes or works around another person's lock. Not to finish, not to go home.
6. The lock comes off only when the person who put it on takes it off. If they have left, the office finds them.
7. On a water heater job that runs past the look, the heater's breaker gets a lock. Nobody can turn it back on with the covers off.
8. Tape over a breaker, a note on a panel or a word to the customer is not a lockout.
9. Where the company does not own locks and hasps, the safety lead buys them before this paper is handed out.

**Stop work when.** A lock the plumber did not see go on is on the breaker and nobody knows whose. Or a breaker that was locked is found on. Everyone off the circuit and the safety lead is called.

**Where it comes from.** 29 CFR 1926.417 and 1910.147, and NFPA 70E Article 120 on lockout. The refresher's lessons 2 and 28 and Intro to electrical's lesson 27.

**Confirm.** The owner names who locks and who unlocks on the company's own jobs. The draft says the crew lead locks and unlocks a water heater's breaker and the electrician locks everything else.

## 4. GFCI on every cord and in every wet place

**Who.** Every worker.

**When.** Every cord on every job. Every tool near water.

**What.**
1. Every cord runs through a GFCI. On a wet floor, in a basement, outdoors, at a sink or in a crawl space, no exceptions.
2. The company's in-line GFCI rides on every truck. A house outlet that is not a GFCI gets the in-line one.
3. The GFCI is tested with its Test button before the day's first use and after any trip.
4. A GFCI trips at 5 mA. A breaker trips at 15,000 mA. The breaker protects the wire. Only the GFCI protects the person.
5. A GFCI that trips is telling the truth. First the cause. Dry the cord, unplug the tool, move the water. Then Reset, once.
6. A GFCI that will not hold after one reset is a call. A tool that trips a GFCI every time is tagged out, not the GFCI.
7. Every outlet downstream of a GFCI is dead with it. A dead outlet across the room can be this GFCI.
8. A cord with a nick or a missing ground prong leaves the job. Tape is not a fix.
9. The sump outlet, the outdoor outlets, the bathroom, the kitchen and the garage are GFCI in most code cycles. The company's rule is a GFCI wherever a plumber plugs in near water, whatever the cycle says.

**Stop work when.** A cord with no GFCI is the only cord. The work waits.

**Where it comes from.** NEC 210.8 for the required places, 29 CFR 1926.404 for cords on a job. The refresher's lesson 9 and Intro to electrical's lessons 13, 20 and 22.

**Confirm.** The reader confirms the 5 mA and the list of required places against the cycle. The owner confirms every truck has an in-line GFCI.

## 5. The bonding jumper and the metal to plastic rule

**Who.** Every worker who cuts, pulls or replaces a metal water line.

**When.** Before any cut in a metal water line. Before any meter, filter or softener comes out. Before any metal to plastic change.

**What.**
1. The metal water pipe is part of the ground. A fault can run along it to the earth any day. Water off does not make it ground off.
2. Look for the jumper before any cut. It is a bare wire with a clamp at each end. It goes around the meter, a filter, a softener or any break.
3. A plumber never removes a jumper. If it is in the way, the electrician moves it.
4. With no jumper across the section, a cut opens the ground. The person holding both ends is the path. The plumber asks the electrician before that cut. A temporary jumper across the cut is the electrician's call.
5. A plastic section in a metal run opens the ground for good. A plastic coupling, a plastic meter yoke and a plastic filter each do the same.
6. **The company's rule.** Before any metal to plastic change in a water line, the electrician is told. The electrician decides what replaces the lost ground path. The change waits for that word.
7. The ground clamp on the pipe is the electrician's. It is not a hanger. Nothing is hung on it and it is not moved.
8. When a meter goes back in, the jumper goes back on. A job is not done with the jumper off.
9. A jumper found missing on arrival is reported to the electrician. It is not the plumber's to add.

**Stop work when.** A jumper is in the way of the cut. Or a section is about to go to plastic with no word to the electrician. The cut waits.

**Where it comes from.** NEC 250.53 for the jumper around the meter and 250.104 for bonding metal water piping. Intro to electrical's lessons 7, 8 and 23.

**Confirm.** The reader confirms the two articles against the cycle. The owner confirms the company's rule in step 6 as drawn, that the change waits for the electrician's word.

## 6. The water heater rule

**Who.** A plumber on the company's truck. The electrician for everything upstream of the disconnect.

**When.** Any electric water heater with no hot water, any element, thermostat or whip job, and any swap.

**What.**
1. **The company's rule.** A plumber on the company's truck works on the whip, the elements, the thermostats and the high-limit reset. The panel, the breaker's wiring and anything upstream of the heater's disconnect are the electrician's.
2. **The company's rule.** First the breaker off. Then the circuit tested dead at the heater's terminals, procedure 2, both hots. Then the covers. Every time, with no exception for a quick look.
3. A cover off with the breaker on is live terminals at hand height. Nothing comes off live.
4. On any job past the look, the breaker gets a lock. Procedure 3.
5. No hot water starts at the breaker. A plumber may reset the heater's breaker once. A second trip is a call.
6. The high-limit button behind the top cover is pressed once, with the circuit dead. If it trips again, a thermostat is stuck or an element is grounded. That is a call.
7. An element is tested dead with a meter, and some cities call the swap a licensed job. The office knows which cities. The plumber asks before the swap.
8. A heater that trips its breaker again has a fault inside. The breaker stays off and the call goes out.
9. The whip matches the breaker. A plumber does not change the breaker, the wire size or the disconnect.
10. A 240 V heater has no neutral. A white wire in the whip is a hot and is marked so. Both are tested.
11. The bond across the heater's hot and cold pipes stays. If a flex connector breaks it, the electrician is told.
12. A gas heater's power-vent blower is a 120 V cord. No power on a gas heater is procedure 11 too.
13. A new disconnect, where a city asks for one within sight of the heater, is the electrician's.

**Stop work when.** The tester beeps at the heater's terminals with the breaker off. Something else feeds it. Nobody touches it and the electrician is called.

**Where it comes from.** NEC 422.13 and 240.4 for the common 30 A and 10 AWG pair. NEC 422.31 for the disconnect and 250.104 for the bond. The company's rule is the owner's, 2026-10-18. Intro to electrical's lessons 15 and 24.

**Confirm.** The owner confirms the scope line in step 1 and the one-reset line in steps 5 and 6. The reader confirms the articles and the 30 A and 10 AWG pair. The reader names which of the company's cities call an element swap licensed work.

## 7. Water on or near a panel

**Who.** Every worker.

**When.** Any leak above or onto a panel, a meter, a disconnect or a live box. Any water on the floor near one.

**What.**
1. Do not touch the panel. Not the cover, not the main breaker, not the door.
2. Shut the water, not the power. The house main, or the valve above the leak.
3. Do not step into water to reach a panel. Nobody throws a wet main.
4. If the power must go off, the utility kills it at the meter. That is a call to the utility, not a hand on the panel.
5. Call the electrician. The panel is live until the electrician has read it, and water inside takes days to dry.
6. A dry cover is not a dry panel. The water runs down inside to the bus bars.
7. A water heater sits in a pan with a drain for this reason. A heater with no pan above anything electrical is noted on the job's report.
8. A panel in a bathroom or under a sink is reported. The code keeps panels out of those places.

**Stop work when.** This whole procedure is a stop line. The water is shut and the call is made. Nothing else happens near the panel.

**Where it comes from.** NEC 110.26 for working space and 240.24 for panels in bathrooms. Intro to electrical's lesson 21.

**Confirm.** The reader confirms the articles. The owner confirms the utility line in Who to call.

## 8. Pumps that start by themselves

**Who.** Every worker who puts a hand in a sump pit, a well pit, a disposal or a dishwasher's box.

**When.** Before any hand goes in.

**What.**
1. A sump pump and a well pump start by themselves. A float rises, a pressure switch closes, and the pump runs.
2. The plug is pulled or the breaker is off and tested dead before a hand goes in the pit. The float alone is not off.
3. A disposal starts from a wall switch anyone can flip. The plug is pulled from the outlet under the sink first. A hard-wired disposal is the breaker, off and tested.
4. A dishwasher's box under the sink is live with the switch off. The breaker, off and tested, before the box is opened.
5. A well pump's control box on the wall is live and it is the electrician's. A plumber works on the pipe side only.
6. A sump pump's outlet is a GFCI in most cycles. Where it is not, the in-line GFCI goes on before the pump is tested.
7. A pump that trips its breaker or its GFCI again is a call.
8. The plug goes back in and the float is checked before the truck leaves. A sump left unplugged floods the house.

**Stop work when.** The pump's power cannot be found or cannot be proven off. The hand stays out and the electrician is called.

**Where it comes from.** NEC 422.31 for appliance disconnects and 210.8 for the GFCI. Intro to electrical's lessons 16 and 17.

**Confirm.** The reader confirms the sump outlet's GFCI rule against the cycle.

## 9. Drilling and cutting near cables

**Who.** Every worker who drills, cuts or screws into a wall, a floor, a joist or a stud.

**When.** Before every hole and every strap screw.

**What.**
1. Ask what runs through the wall before the hole. Cables run where the holes are easiest, and so do pipes.
2. A cable in a stud sits at least 1-1/4 in back from the stud's face. A screw or a nail shorter than that misses it.
3. Where a cable is closer than that, a steel nail plate covers it. A nail plate says a cable is right behind. It is not a hanger plate.
4. Hold the tester to the wall along the line of the hole. A beep means a cable, and the hole moves.
5. A strap screw is as bad as a nail. A strap is placed where the cable is not.
6. A hole through a top plate or a joist goes in the middle of the wood. The cable is not there.
7. A drill that catches, sparks or trips a breaker has hit a cable. Stop. Nobody pulls the bit out of a live cable. The breaker, then the electrician.
8. A cable cut by a plumber is the electrician's repair. It is reported the same hour. It is not taped and buried.
9. A core drill through a slab stops when it hits anything that is not concrete. A conduit under a slab is live.

**Stop work when.** The tester beeps on the line of the hole, or the drill has hit something. The hole waits.

**Where it comes from.** NEC 300.4 for the setback and the nail plate. Intro to electrical's lessons 28 and 30.

**Confirm.** The reader confirms the 1-1/4 in line against the cycle.

## 10. Generators and solar backfeed

**Who.** Every worker, on every house with a generator, a solar array or a battery.

**When.** On arrival at any house with a solar sticker at the meter or the panel. Or a generator inlet, or a battery cabinet.

**What.**
1. Read the sticker at the meter and the panel before any work. A house with solar or a battery says so there.
2. Never assume dead on such a house. The array makes power whenever the sun is up. A battery holds a circuit live in the dark. An automatic generator starts when the utility drops.
3. The main off is not the house dead. The tester at the work, procedure 2, is the only truth.
4. The solar disconnect outside is the utility's and the electrician's to throw. A plumber does not throw it.
5. A generator cord plugged into a wall outlet is a backfeed waiting. It can kill a lineman on a line they turned off. That is a call to the electrician, not a plumbing job.
6. A transfer switch keeps the house's source inside the house. A house with a generator and no transfer switch is reported.
7. A wire that tested dead at noon can be live at one. On these houses the test is repeated after any break in the work.

**Stop work when.** The tester beeps on a wire the breaker should have killed. Nobody touches it and the electrician is called.

**Where it comes from.** NEC Article 702 for optional standby systems and Article 705 for interconnected sources. Intro to electrical's lesson 25.

**Confirm.** The reader confirms the articles and adds any local rule on solar disconnects.

## 11. What a plumber may reset, and when to call

**Who.** Every worker.

**When.** Any tripped breaker, any tripped GFCI, any tripped high-limit.

**What.**
1. **The company's rule.** A plumber resets a breaker once, a GFCI once and a water heater's high-limit once. The second trip of any of them is a call.
2. A breaker is reset with the panel's cover on, with one hand, standing to the side. The handle goes all the way off, then on.
3. A warm breaker, a smell or a buzz means the breaker is the problem. It is not reset at all. That is a call.
4. A GFCI is reset after the cause is fixed, never before. Procedure 4.
5. The high-limit is reset only with the circuit dead and tested. Procedure 6.
6. Nothing else in the house is reset by a plumber. Not a subpanel, not a disconnect, not a solar switch, not a control box.
7. The customer is told what was reset and why. The call to the electrician is made with the customer's say so, through the office.
8. A reset that holds is written on the job's report. The electrician reads the report when they come.

**Stop work when.** The second trip. The circuit stays off and the call goes out.

**Where it comes from.** The company's rule is the owner's, 2026-10-18. Intro to electrical's lessons 19, 20 and 24.

**Confirm.** The owner confirms the once rule for all three. The owner confirms how the electrician is called on a customer's job, which the draft says is through the office.

## 12. What a plumber never does

**Who.** Every worker.

**When.** Always.

**What.**
1. Never opens a panel cover, a meter base or a disconnect. The reset in procedure 11 is done with the cover on.
2. Never lands, splices or moves a wire. Never changes a breaker, an outlet or a switch.
3. Never removes a jumper or a ground clamp. Procedure 5.
4. Never works on anything live. Never touches anything that failed its test.
5. Never cuts or works around another person's lock. Procedure 3.
6. Never crosses the arc flash boundary or the limited approach boundary at a panel. An unqualified person stays outside both. The electrician works there.
7. Never comes within 10 ft of an overhead line. The least clearance, for the smallest line, is 10 ft. A ladder, a pipe or a tape measure counts as the person.
8. Never runs a cord through water or across a walkway without a cover. Never through a door that can close on it.
9. Never tells a customer an electrical fault is fixed. The plumber says what was seen and who to call.

**Stop work when.** Any of these is about to happen. It does not.

**Where it comes from.** NFPA 70E Article 130 for the boundaries. 29 CFR 1926.416 and 1926.1408 for overhead lines. The refresher's lessons 9 and 28.

**Confirm.** The reader confirms the boundary lines read correctly for an unqualified person. The 10 ft line against the current rule.
