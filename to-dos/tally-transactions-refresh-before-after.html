What the numbers say The page is not where the work happens, and the unit of work is a person's day

The Transactions tab was built for the card holder: your card, your purchases, sort them yourself, with a phone Sort mode and a nudge at clock-out. Ninety days of production say the field does not do that. One assistant sorts almost every charge, from a Dashboard modal, one charge at a time, by search. And the charges come in days: a person buys fuel, then parts, then lunch, on the job they clocked that day.

After The team's days, sorted where they are

For an office login the tab is the team's queue, one card per person per day. The card's header is the holder's day in words: when they clocked and where. The day's chips sort every charge under it in one tap; a charge with its own rule (the store that always goes to Office) already reads its own pick and says why; a day with two jobs offers both on each line, and a day with no clock offers the neighbours and picks nothing. Sort the day writes the card; the green bar at the top writes every obvious day at once. Field holders keep the phone cards for their own card, with the same chip.

Pass 2 What pass 1 got wrong

  • It drew the wrong Before. Pass 1 showed the page as Robert sees it. The thing being replaced is the Team purchases modal and its window per charge; that is now the second Before panel, with the window drawn inside it.
  • It sorted charges, not days. Per-charge chips were a tap per row. The data says the row is the wrong unit: 382 person-days, 2.2 charges each, 64% of days to one job. A day card with one chip halves the taps again and reads the way the sorter thinks ("what was Malachi on Tuesday?").
  • It hid the two-job day. Pass 1 had no answer for fuel on a day with two jobs beyond "pick one". The day card offers both jobs and a split by hours, and the even split stays on the line.
  • It had a bar that did what the chips did. "Sort the obvious ones" now means whole days, which is a different action from the chip, not a shortcut for it.

Kept · dropped · the owner's calls

Kept

  • The write path. The staff split RPC, the allocation rows, the job cost math, the Dashboard and Quickfill counts. A day is a batch of the same writes.
  • The Assign window as Another job…: search, Recent, the holder's schedule and clock picks, uneven splits, notes.
  • Mark payroll and the rules, with the pay bar in front of them.
  • The phone cards and Sort mode for the holder's own card. They gain the day's chip; they lose nothing.
  • Show sorted too for review; the search box, under the strip; the memo, one button.
  • The date floor. The 4,200 charges before April stay under it.

Dropped

  • The Materials Estimate tab. Eight rows in February, none since; the PO flow is in Materials. Its route keeps redirecting to Transactions.
  • The card filter row as a chip strip. The people strip is the filter; a person's several cards fold under their name.
  • The "Dashboard card's count" gloss in the header. The people strip and the through-date replace both numbers.
  • The Posted-date button that opened the clock-window split modal. The day card is that modal, in place.
  • The Team purchases modal on Dashboard and Quickfill, once this ships: its door opens this tab instead. One place to sort, not two.

Owner's calls

  • Fuel on a two-job day. Today Taunya splits it evenly (Valero $113.39 → $56.69 / $56.70). The day card offers split by hours as a chip; which one leads is your call. Even is one tap away either way.
  • Let the surest rule auto-apply? "Only job clocked that day" covers 37% of charges and 64% of days. The page measures itself first: how often the sorter overrides a ✓. Under 3% for a month, and it can go the way payroll rules already do — auto-apply, every one still undoable. Not before.
  • Who sees the team view. Drawn for dev, master, assistant and controller — the roles the staff RPC already admits. Superintendents and primaries keep their own card only.
  • Retire the Materials Estimate tab, or keep it as a door in Materials. Drawn as retired.

Is this the best we can do?

The job is not a nicer table. It is every card charge landing on the right job with the fewest human minutes and the fewest wrong picks, so job cost is true while the job runs. Measured that way, pass 2 buys most of what is available: the sorter goes from a window per charge to a glance and a tap per day, and the obvious days go in one press. Roughly 70 charges a week, a minute each, becomes a few minutes twice a week.

What would beat it is the charge never needing a human at all. Most of that is already in reach: the only-job-that-day rule, the same-store rule and the pay-send rule together cover about six in ten rows. The honest path is to let the page prove them first. Every ✓ the sorter accepts or overrides is a data point, and the page shows its own accuracy on the through-date line ("guessed right 94% this month"). When a rule is right often enough for long enough, the owner flips it to auto-apply, the way payroll rules run today, and the sorter's queue becomes only the days the app could not call. That is the version of this page that gets smaller every month instead of staying the same size.

What we should not build. Receipt capture at the pump: Mercury has no receipts on these charges (4 attachments in 1,063, all check images), the field does not open the app to sort, and a photo does not name a job. A stronger clock-out nudge: the charges are in the app by then, the nudge exists, and it is skipped. A smarter search box: the search is not the slow part. And no page for the 4,200 old charges: the floor is a decision already taken.

The one thing to check before building. Ask Taunya what she does when the guess is wrong and why. The day cards and the chip reasons are drawn from the data; her ten most common overrides are the spec for the second rule set.

The plan, smallest shippable first

  1. PR 1 — the kernel. tallySortSuggestion.ts: given a person's day (its charges, the holder's clock sessions and schedule blocks for the day ±1, the holder's last N sorted charges and each store's last N), return the day's chips and each line's own pick, with a reason and a confidence (sure · likely · none) — and the split-by-hours amounts. Pure, tested on the cases above. No screen changes.
  2. PR 2 — the team queue. For office roles the Transactions tab reads the staff RPC (with include_all_unlinked) instead of the caller's own cards: the people strip, the through-date, the day cards, the evidence line, the chips, Sort the day through the existing staff RPC, Another job… opening today's Assign window. Guide amended; the Dashboard and Quickfill doors open here.
  3. PR 3 — the obvious bar and the pay bar. Sort-all for ✓ days, undo per line, the pay-send bar that marks and widens the rule.
  4. PR 4 — the holder's card gains the day's chip (phone cards, Sort mode, the pre-clock-out sheet), one component reading the same kernel.
  5. PR 5 — the page measures itself. Accepted vs overridden per rule, the accuracy line, an owner switch per rule for auto-apply, off by default. The Materials Estimate tab retires in the same PR or its own.
