What the numbers say The page is not where the work happens

The Transactions tab was built for the card holder: your card, your purchases, sort them yourself, with a phone Sort mode and a nudge at clock-out. Ninety days of production say the field does not do that. One assistant sorts almost every charge, from a Dashboard modal, one charge at a time, by search. The page itself is barely opened.

After The team's purchases, sorted where they are

For an office login the tab becomes the team's queue: every linked card, newest day first, one line per charge with the holder's name on it. Each unsorted line already carries the app's best guess and why, as a chip. One tap sorts it; a second chip splits it evenly; Another job… opens today's Assign window for the rest. A green bar at the top sorts every obvious one in one press. Field holders keep the phone cards for their own card, with the same guess on each.

Log — what each tap would write: replace_mercury_job_splits_for_linked_card_as_staff(for the holder, the charge, the rows), the same RPC the Dashboard modal uses today.
  • The people strip is the header. Who has charges to sort and how many; green zero when they are clear. "Everything sorted through Sat Sep 27" is the one date the office wants.
  • The guess is on the row, with its reason. Only job clocked that day · same as last time at this store · where the last one went. A dashed chip is a likely guess; a plain chip is a choice. No guess means no chip is picked, and the row says why.
  • One tap sorts, two chips split. A second chip on a sorted row splits it evenly; the amounts show. Uneven splits and jobs off the list go through Another job…, which is today's Assign window.
  • Sort the obvious ones in one press. Only rows with a ✓ chip. The bar says the rule; the log says what it wrote; every line keeps Undo.
  • Pay sends are one bar, not seventeen rows. Marking them widens the rule that should have caught them, so next month they never show.
  • The evidence line is the holder's day. Clock spans and scheduled jobs, in words. When the guess is wrong the sorter sees why at a glance instead of opening a window to find out.

Kept · dropped · the owner's calls

Kept

  • The write path. The staff split RPC, the allocation rows, the job cost math, the Dashboard and Quickfill counts. Nothing downstream changes.
  • The Assign window as Another job…: search, Recent, the holder's schedule and clock picks, uneven splits, notes.
  • Mark payroll and the rules, with the pay bar in front of them.
  • The phone cards and Sort mode for the holder's own card. They gain the guess; they lose nothing.
  • Show sorted too for review; the search box, under the strip; the memo, one button.
  • The date floor. The 4,200 charges before April stay under it.

Dropped

  • The Materials Estimate tab. Eight rows in February, none since; the PO flow is in Materials. Its route keeps redirecting to Transactions.
  • The card filter row as a chip strip. The people strip is the filter; a person's several cards fold under their name.
  • The "Dashboard card's count" gloss in the header. The people strip and the through-date replace both numbers.
  • The Posted-date button that opened the clock-window split modal. The evidence line and the chips do that job on the row.
  • The Team purchases modal on Dashboard and Quickfill, once this ships: its door opens this tab instead. One place to sort, not two.

Owner's calls

  • Fuel on a two-job day. Today Taunya splits it evenly (Valero $113.39 → $56.69 / $56.70). The chips could offer split by hours on each job as the pick instead. Even is drawn; by hours is one line in the kernel.
  • Let the surest rule auto-apply? "Only job clocked that day" covers 37% of charges. The page can measure itself first: how often the sorter overrides the ✓ chip. Under 3% for a month, and it can go the way payroll rules already do — auto-apply, every one of them still undoable. Not before.
  • Who sees the team view. Drawn for dev, master, assistant and controller — the roles the staff RPC already admits. Superintendents and primaries keep their own card only.
  • Retire the Materials Estimate tab, or keep it as a door in Materials. Drawn as retired.

Is this the best we can do?

The job is not a nicer table. It is every card charge landing on the right job with the fewest human minutes and the fewest wrong picks, so job cost is true while the job runs. Measured that way, the redraw above buys most of what is available: the sorter goes from open-search-pick-save-close per charge to a glance and a tap, and the obvious half of the queue goes in one press. Roughly 70 charges a week at a minute each becomes a few minutes, twice a week.

What would beat it is the charge never needing a human at all. Three quarters of that is already in reach: the only-job-that-day rule, the same-store-same-job rule and the pay-send rule together cover about six in ten rows. The honest path is to let the page prove them first. Every ✓ chip the sorter accepts or overrides is a data point; the page shows its own accuracy on the through-date line ("guessed right 94% this month"). When a rule is right often enough for long enough, the owner flips it to auto-apply, the way payroll rules run today, and the sorter's queue becomes only the rows the app could not call. That is the version of this page that gets smaller every month instead of staying the same size.

What we should not build. Receipt capture at the pump: Mercury has no receipts on these charges (4 attachments in 1,063, all check images), the field does not open the app to sort, and a photo does not name a job. A stronger clock-out nudge: the charges are in the app by then, the nudge exists, and it is skipped. A smarter search box: the search is not the slow part. And no page for the 4,200 old charges: the floor is a decision already taken.

The one thing to check before building. Ask Taunya what she does when the guess is wrong and why. The evidence line and the chip reasons are drawn from the data; her ten most common overrides are the spec for the second rule set.

The plan, smallest shippable first

  1. PR 1 — the kernel. tallySortSuggestion.ts: given a charge, the holder's clock sessions and schedule blocks for posted ±1 day, the holder's last N sorted charges and the store's last N, return the ranked chips with a reason each and a confidence (sure · likely · none). Pure, tested on the cases above. No screen changes.
  2. PR 2 — the team queue. For office roles the Transactions tab reads the staff RPC (with include_all_unlinked) instead of the caller's own cards: the people strip, the through-date, day groups, the evidence line, the chips, one-tap sort and even split through the existing staff RPC, Another job… opening today's Assign window. Guide amended; the Dashboard and Quickfill doors open here.
  3. PR 3 — the obvious bar and the pay bar. Sort-all for ✓ rows, Undo per line, the pay-send bar that marks and widens the rule.
  4. PR 4 — the holder's card gains the chip (phone cards, Sort mode, the pre-clock-out sheet), one component reading the same kernel.
  5. PR 5 — the page measures itself. Accepted vs overridden per rule, the accuracy line, an owner switch per rule for auto-apply, off by default. The Materials Estimate tab retires in the same PR or its own.
