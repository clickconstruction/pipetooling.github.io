Money that goes back the other way

Taunya needs to enter a return. The app has no way to record one, so the number gets folded into an invoice, zeroed out, or left positive on a job that never kept the parts.

01

What happens today

A return from Reece for −888.10, against purchase order Return, with the credit memo scanned and linked on Drive. Everything about it is a real document. The form refuses it.

! Value must be greater than or equal to 0.

Three separate gates say no: the amount box has a floor of zero, the save path rejects a negative before it reaches the network, and the database carries a constraint requiring the amount to be zero or more. None of them is wrong on its own. Together they mean a credit memo has nowhere to live.

02

Two different things are called a refund

They need different answers, and only one of them is Taunya’s ask. Keeping them apart is what stops the same credit being counted twice.

A credit memo on a house account

Reece, Ferguson, Hughes, Texas Plumbing. Parts go back, the house issues a numbered credit, no money moves until the statement is paid net.

Nowhere in the app.
This is what we are building.

A card refund at the counter

Lowe’s, Home Depot, O’Reilly. Pay-at-the-register houses with no invoices at all. The money comes back to the card.

Already in the bank feed.
46 allocated to jobs, $2,494.

The rule that keeps them straight: a credit belongs on a supply house only when the house issues paper for it. If the money came back to the card, the bank feed already has it, and typing it here would credit the job twice.

03

The form

The amount box does not learn to accept a minus sign. Instead the form asks what the paper is, and the sign follows from the answer.

That distinction is the whole safety argument. A form that simply allows negatives cannot tell a credit memo from a mistyped invoice, and a stray minus moves a job’s cost by twice the amount with nothing on screen to catch it.

1

The sign comes from the choice, never from typing

The amount stays a positive number in the box. Picking Credit is what makes it negative on the way to the database, so a slipped minus key cannot invent a credit and a real credit cannot be lost to a missing one.

2

Every label follows the document

Invoice # becomes Credit #, Paying it becomes Applying it, and the save button reads Save credit. Nothing on screen calls a credit memo an invoice.

3

The effect is stated before it is saved

One line naming both consequences in the office’s own words. This is the sentence that makes a wrong job or a wrong house obvious while it can still be fixed.

4

Which invoice it credits, if the office knows

Optional, because a return is not always traceable to one invoice. The prod data argues for it, since the office already typed both document numbers into a single field to hold the pairing.

04

What the second look changed

Revised after a full sweep of every reader

The first draft of this note said the job side needs no code and the real work is the aging table. The first half is true. The second half was too small, and it made the plan unsafe.

A negative amount reaches about twenty-five readers, not one screen. Most of them do the arithmetic correctly, because job cost is the amount times an allocation percentage and a negative flows through it honestly. What breaks is everything built on top of the assumption that the number is positive.

Every sibling money stream is protected. This one never had to be.

The constraint did the work, so nothing downstream ever learned to handle a sign.

Card charges from the bankabsolute value, everywhere
Customer paymentsonly amounts above zero
Payroll remainingfloored at zero
Collections balancefloored at zero
Supply house invoicesnothing but the constraint

It hides money

Five surfaces drop a balance rather than show it as negative, and each one removes a real debt from view along with the credit.

  • The dashboard payables bucket skips any invoice at or below zero outright, so the credit never lands and no count says it was excluded.
  • The aging table keeps only houses with a positive total, so one credit can remove a house holding tens of thousands at ninety days.
  • Job Accounts drops a job out of “owe suppliers” when a credit offsets a real unpaid invoice.
  • The week-close queue floors the gap at zero and reports an unallocated credit as “1 invoice · $0”.

It prints or ranks the wrong thing

Three outputs lose the sign after the arithmetic was right.

  • The weekly money email formats with absolute value and no minus, so a negative week of spend prints as spend.
  • The paid-job email ranks biggest charges by absolute size, so a credit memo sorts above a real purchase.
  • The phone balance bar divides by a house total that a credit can drive to exactly zero.

It moves a number that is hard to take back

Two of these are not display at all.

  • Partner profit share reads a materials bucket that includes the credit, and a split gets posted from it.
  • A trigger freezes a job’s materials cost at billing, and future estimating rates are derived from that frozen figure.

It reaches places a credit should not go

Two edges should simply be closed rather than taught.

  • A workflow step copies the amount into a second table, where the sign outlives the invoice it came from.
  • A card charge linked to a credit is excluded from parts cost and credited again, hitting the job twice the same way.

06

How it ships, in the safe order

The first draft opened the gate first and repaired the readers second. That is backwards. Reverse it and every step before the last one is provably harmless, because no negative row can exist while they land.

Only one file writes these invoices, so the gate is a single place to hold shut until everything behind it is ready.

PR 1

Teach the readers about a sign, while none exists

The aging kernel stops dropping houses and counts only invoices in the due-date nudge. The payables bucket stops discarding non-positive rows. Job Accounts keeps a job in the queue on either kind of paper. The week-close gap stops flooring at zero, and the two emails print a minus. None of this can change a number today, which is exactly what makes it safe to ship first.

Proof it worked: every live figure is identical, and the new tests fail without the fix.

client & SQL
PR 2

Close the two edges a credit should never reach

A credit cannot be added to a workflow step, where the amount would be copied into a table that never learns it was a credit. A card charge cannot be linked to a credit, which would take the money off the job twice. Both are refusals with a reason, not silent filters.

Proof it worked: the two doors say why, and nothing else changes.

client only
PR 3

The document kind, the form, and the gate

A column recording whether the paper is an invoice or a credit, defaulting to invoice so all 466 existing rows are already right. The old constraint is replaced by one that still forbids a negative invoice and permits a negative credit. The form gains the choice, the labels follow it, and the sign math lands in the kernel that already backs this form.

Proof it worked: Taunya’s Reece credit, entered live, lands on J878 and on Reece’s balance.

migration · the gate
PR 4

Which invoice a credit credits

The optional pairing, shown on both documents so a credit and its invoice read as one story. Worth doing only once the office has used the first three for a few weeks and said whether it reaches for the field.

later

One thing this order gives up. The repairs in the first two changes cannot be seen working on real data, because nothing negative exists yet to make them visible. Their live test is that no number moved, and their real proof is unit tests written against negative fixtures. That is a weaker demonstration than usual, and it is the right trade for not putting a sign into twenty-five unguarded readers first.

07

What I need decided

Does an open credit reduce what the aging table says we owe?

Its own column, as drawn above, or netted into the buckets. This is the only decision that changes what gets built.

My recommendation: its own column. A credit has a size but not an age, and netting is what makes the sixty-day number lie.

What does “paid” mean on a credit?

An invoice is paid when money leaves. A credit is spent when it comes off a statement, which is not the same event.

My recommendation: read it as applied, default it to open, and label it that way.

Must a credit always name a job?

A credit with no job reduces the house balance and no job’s cost. That is right for a freight adjustment and wrong for returned parts.

My recommendation: allow it, but say plainly on the form that no job gets money back.

Who may enter one?

A credit moves job cost, a partner split and an estimating baseline. Today anyone who can add an invoice can add one.

My recommendation: same people as invoices, since narrowing it would put Taunya through someone else to do her own job.

Separately: card refunds are being added to job cost, not taken off

Refunds from Lowe’s, Home Depot and O’Reilly arrive in the bank feed as money in, and the kernel that turns card charges into job cost takes the absolute value of every row. Forty-six such allocations worth about $2,494 sit on jobs as cost rather than credit.

A separate bug on a separate channel. Worth its own change, not this one. Say the word and I will size it.
