Put a GC on notice: every owner on every job, in one run

The situation: a GC has been taking money from each house to pay for the last one, and will not be able to pay any of its bills. Texas already has the instrument for this. The § 53.056 notice is the notice of intent to lien, and its teeth are fund trapping: once an owner receives it, the owner must withhold that money from the GC or become liable for it themselves (§ 53.081). The Lien desk sends those notices one unpaid month at a time, thirty days ahead of each deadline. This button sends them for every unpaid month on every job with that GC, today, with one approval. Names and numbers below are an example GC, not a real customer.

What it does

Picks a GC, finds every job of theirs with unpaid work (billed or not), fills a § 53.056 notice per job naming every unnoticed month, gets one approval from the master, and hands the whole set to the run. The owners are the recipients, the GC gets its statutory copy.

What it does not do

It does not sue, and it does not file liens. Affidavits still come per job, on their own dates, from the desk's Affidavits pile. It does not change the statutory text. It does not send anything the desk could not already send one at a time.

Why one approval

This is one decision about one relationship, made under pressure. The master decides once; the reason he gives is kept on every notice's record, and the GC's standing rule flips to send without asking so the months that follow go out on schedule.

Is this the best we can do?

What Draft A gets right

One place, one GC, one action. The count and the dollars are the two numbers the master wants before he says go.

Where it falls short

The owners are the whole point, and most are missing. On the live desk today, none of the seven jobs has an owner of record on file. A GC-in-trouble button has to make finding nine owners fast: every job's property record and county lookup in one list, done before anything is approved.

Unbilled work needs a number. A notice claims an amount. Jobs still Working have hours but no bill. Either bill the finished work first (the Pipeline already has "Bill the finished work"), or claim the unpaid contract balance and say so.

The letter to a homeowner needs a human paragraph. The statutory form is a form. These owners paid the GC in good faith; a plain cover letter that says what happened, what the law asks them to do, and that we will release the moment we are paid, is what keeps the phone civil.

A decision this size leaves a trail. Why, who decided, when, and what else changed: the GC's standing rule, its payment terms, the Legal desk account. One record, not five.

It should not stop at notices. The affidavit dates for these jobs are now the calendar. The modal should show them, and the desk's Affidavits pile should already be watching.

To the owner of 212 Kettle Dr, Buda
We are the plumbing contractor on your home, working under Harborline Builders. Harborline has not paid us for work we completed in May through August, and we have reason to believe it is not paying its subcontractors generally.
Texas law asks us to send you the enclosed notice. It is not a claim against you, and it does not say you have done anything wrong. The plan

Two PRs on top of the Lien desk. No new tables.

Data

Nothing new to store. A run for a GC is N desk items (kind notice) with the same approval_mode and a shared batch_reason in each item's fields; the cover letter rides in fields.cover_letter and replaces the standard cover note for those items. The GC's rule and terms are the existing columns; the Legal desk account is the existing matter.

One RPC change. list_lien_notice_months(p_gc_customer_id): for a chosen GC, every job with unpaid work (billed, or Working with approved hours), every month with approved hours and no live notice, no 30-day window, plus the unbilled balance per job.

PR train
  1. PR 1 — the modal and the three doors. The GC picker, Steps 1–2 (owners list with CAD links and inline owner entry; months and claim amounts; closed windows named), Step 4's reason + the three ticks, and Approve all → N approved desk items → the run. Kernel gcOnNotice.ts (+tests) folds the RPC rows per job.
  2. PR 2 — the cover letter. The one-letter editor, stored per item, printed as the cover page of each notice in the run; the master's signature block from the letterhead; the demand-letter tick.
