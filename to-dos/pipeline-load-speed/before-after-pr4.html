Pipeline · remember the last board (PR 4) — the two questions

Jobs → Pipeline · drawn from the real screen 2026-09-19 (21 Waiting · 34 Working · 0 Ready to Bill · 69 Billed · 7 Collections; rows are the newest three in Billed Awaiting Payment) · for the owner's read

What changes

Today every cold load of Pipeline — a new browser tab, a reload, a phone that reopened the app after the OS killed it — shows Loading jobs… on an empty page for about 0.7 s (measured after PRs 1–3), then the sections fill. A tab switch within 30 s is already free (v2.3603). PR 4 keeps the last board the device saw (the rows, the money cards, the section totals) in the browser's own store and paints it at once on the next cold load, with Updating jobs… under the tabs while the real fetch runs. When the fresh board lands (~1 s) it replaces the remembered one without a flash. Nothing is written from the snapshot; row buttons wait for the live board.

The two things only you can decide

  • 1 · How old may a remembered board be? Older than that and the page loads the way it does today (blank + Loading jobs…). Proposal: 24 hours.
  • 2 · While the fresh board is on its way, do the money numbers read muted? Option A greys every dollar and the progress bars until the live board lands. Option B shows the remembered board exactly as it was, and only the status line says it is being updated.

Question 1 — how old may the remembered board be?

The same cold load, three ages of snapshot, under the 24-hour proposal. The status line always says how old the board on screen is; the age is the only thing that changes what you see.

Snapshot 4 min oldClosed the tab, opened it again. The board paints instantly, reads Updating jobs… · board from 4 min ago, fresh board lands ~1 s later. Almost nothing moves.
Snapshot 6 h oldLooked this morning, reopened after lunch. Same paint, board from 6 h ago. A payment applied at 11:00 shows a second later than it would with a blank page — but you had the rest of the board 0.7 s sooner.
Snapshot 26 h old — over the lineYesterday's board is not shown. The page loads as it does today: blank + Loading jobs…, then the live board. The snapshot is dropped and rewritten from the fresh fetch.
The linewhat each choice means on a real week
1 hourOnly a reopen within the hour paints early. Most mornings start blank as today. Safe, small payoff.
4 hoursCovers a lunch break and a phone that slept. The morning's first open is blank.
24 hours — proposedEvery workday opens on yesterday's board for a second, then the live one. Overnight changes are the only ones you could see stale, and only for that second.
7 daysMonday opens on Friday's board. A week of payments and billing shown as it was, for a second. Not proposed.
Never expireAlways paint whatever was last seen, however old. Not proposed: a board from a vacation ago reads wrong even for a second.
My read: 24 h. The snapshot only ever shows for the ~1 s the live fetch takes, so the question is not "how stale can the data be" but "how stale is worth a one-second glimpse". A day covers the case that matters (the first open of the morning); longer buys nothing.

Not on the drawing: the snapshot is per device and per account (signing in as someone else never shows your board), it is dropped on sign-out, and a device that has never loaded Pipeline behaves exactly as today.

Quieter: no grey flash on every cold load. The cost is that for one second a number six hours old is drawn exactly like a live one.

The trade
  • A (muted) — honest by construction: a dimmed dollar can never be mistaken for a live one, and the office's first glance at Pipeline is at the money. The cost is a one-second grey flash on each cold load, which reads as "loading", which it is.
  • B (plain) — the calmer screen, and for a snapshot minutes old the numbers are almost certainly right. The cost is that a 6 h or 23 h board looks finished when it is not; someone who reads a total and looks away has read yesterday.
  • A middle path, if wanted — plain when the snapshot is under an hour old, muted past that. One more rule to explain; drawn here only as an option.
My read: A. The whole point of the page is the numbers, the flash lasts about as long as Loading jobs… does today, and the age chip does the explaining. If the grey turns out to be irritating in daily use, dropping it is a one-line change.

What the drawing assumes (so it can be corrected)

  • The snapshot is the last successful board for the device's open sections, keyed by account + the sections open, stored in IndexedDB; painted only on a cold load, never over a live board.
  • The status line reuses today's Updating jobs… (already shown when a board refreshes in place) and gains the age chip. Loading jobs… stays for a device with no usable snapshot.
  • Row buttons (Mark Paid, View Bill, Send back) carry the row's own data with them, so on a remembered row they are inert until the live board lands — about a second. Nothing can act on a remembered invoice.
  • Ages are relative and rounded (4 min, 6 h, yesterday); the chip goes away with the status line when the live board lands.
