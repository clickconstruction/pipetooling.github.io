A lien payoff: one check in, every lien at the place released

A property owner pays off every lien the company filed at one place with one check. Today that is about fifty presses across three jobs, and nothing ties the releases to the money. This page says what exists, what to build, and what must never happen. Every name and number on it is made up.

  1. 1A payoff is one paper on the Lien desk, kept by place. It takes one check, places it on the liened bills first, and checks the total against the check.
  2. 2It lines up one release for each filed lien the money covers. Each waits for the check to clear and shows the day it is due. The releases print in the desk’s one run.
  3. 3The card stays until the county has recorded each release and the copies are out. A check that comes back holds every release that has not left.

The law’s shape, for counsel

This is a reading of the published text, not legal advice. Each section was read on 2026-11-24 in a public copy of the Code that dates itself May 2025.

  • Tex. Prop. Code § 53.152(a). Once the debt is “paid by collected funds”, the claimant must furnish a release within ten days of receiving a written request, to the extent of what was paid. The owner, the original contractor or whoever paid may ask.
  • § 53.152(b). The release must be in a form that can be filed of record. § 53.157(1) says recording it discharges the filed affidavit.
  • Prop. Code § 12.001(a). A paper can be recorded if it is acknowledged, sworn to with a proper jurat, or proved.
  • What lateness risks. § 53.152 names no penalty of its own. § 53.156 has a court award costs and reasonable attorney’s fees in a suit to declare a lien invalid or unenforceable. § 53.160 is the summary motion that removes one.
  • § 53.283 and § 53.284. No one may demand an unconditional waiver before good funds. The conditional forms take effect when the bank has paid the check.

The shape to build

A payoff, kept by place, on the Lien desk. A lien sits on a property, so the door is the place: every filed lien there, every open bill behind each, and what is open and not liened. The office types one check. The app places it on the liened bills first, oldest first, and shows the check, what is placed and what is left as three values. One press records a payment on each bill. From then on the payoff is a card on the desk with five stops: money recorded, check cleared, releases signed and notarized, recorded at the county, copies sent. The card draws one release for each filed lien the money covers in full, from that affidavit’s own facts. It holds each release until its money is recorded and the check has cleared, and it shows the day the release is due beside the day the check clears. The releases print in the desk’s one run with the day’s other papers. Money recorded anywhere else in the app starts the same card by itself.

What else was weighed

Mend the job’s tab and stop.

Give the Release of record tab a money check and a place for the county’s stamp. It is cheap, and it is the first two pieces of the build anyway. Alone it leaves three trips, a check typed four times, and a desk that never sees the payoff.

A receive-a-payment door in Accounts Receivable.

Good for any check over several jobs. Accounts Receivable already spreads a deposit once the bank shows it. The gap is the release, its clock and its run, and those belong to the desk. The build takes this door’s kernel and puts it where the lien is.

No door: draft the release when a bill reads paid.

The app cannot know of a written request, a cashier’s check, or which bills the payer meant. A release drawn from a payment placed on the wrong job is the worst mistake on offer. The build keeps the good half: the card appears by itself.

    What must never happen

    The mistakeThe guard
    A release goes out for a lien whose money is not recordedA release is drawn only from payment rows. It is ready when every bill behind its lien reads paid and the check has cleared, or a leader has confirmed the funds. The print press counts only ready releases. The job’s own tab gets the same gate.
    A release for the wrong filingEach release holds a link to one affidavit row and copies nothing by hand. The paper’s instrument number, day and county come through that link. A job with two filed liens shows two releases, never the first one found.
    A release wider than the moneyThe release names one instrument and its claim. Where other bills stay open at the place, the app does not offer a final unconditional waiver there until counsel has said how it should read.
    A paid bill left showing as lienedA filed lien has three states the desk can see: on record, paid and release owed, released. A released lien leaves every pile, the clock for copies and the year’s suit reminder.
    A lien paid and forgottenA filed lien whose bills read paid shows in the Payoffs pile by itself, even when the job has left the desk’s other lists. It stays until the county’s stamp is recorded.
    A check comes back after the release is filedBefore recording: the returned-check case holds every release on that check, and a printed one reads Do not record. After recording the app cannot undo it. The row reads Released, money came back, the bills go back to open, and one press opens the matter for counsel.

    The calls

    For the company’s owner
    1. Does a release wait for the check to clear? Pick: yes, the app’s seven days, with a leader’s press when the bank confirms the funds.
    2. One release for each lien, or one paper for the place? Pick: one for each lien. Each matches one record at the clerk, and one can be held while the others go.
    3. What does the payer get on the day of the check? Pick: a receipt that names the instruments. Nothing signed that waives.
    4. Who signs a release? Pick: the affidavit’s signer.
    For Grace
    1. Do releases print in the day’s run or by themselves? Pick: in the run.
    2. Who types the check? Pick: whoever holds it. The desk picks the money up from anywhere it was recorded.
    3. Is a payoff kept by place or by payer? Pick: by place. A payer with two properties has two payoffs.
    4. What is the pile called? Pick: Payoffs.
    For counsel questions only; the build takes the cautious reading until answered
    1. May the company wait for a check to clear before it releases, and what if the tenth day comes first?
    2. If the payer asks for the release before or as the check changes hands, what may be given?
    3. If no written request ever comes, is a release still owed, and by when?
    4. Does a release take an acknowledgment or a jurat, and will the clerk record either?
    5. May one release name several instruments?
    6. With other bills still open at the place, how must a release or a waiver read so it gives up the lien paid and no more? Is a waiver needed at all beside a release?
    7. The property owner paid and the builder owes the bills. Does that change the wording or who gets a copy?
    8. A release is recorded and the check comes back. What can be done, and how fast?
    9. What does a late release risk beyond costs and fees?
