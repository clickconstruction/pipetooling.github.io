Who Sees Whose Bills

A GC's portal shows the bills their homeowners still owe us. A homeowner's portal never shows what their builder owes. One rule, applied on the server, drawn as two statements.

The ask, in the owner's words. "Now that jobs can be paid by the customer or the GC I would like for GCs (not customers) to see jobs their customers need to pay us. At the same time if a bill is assigned to a customer and they look at their portal, they should not see bills their GCs need to pay."

What the portal does today

Since v2.3346 the statement only counts what the viewer owes. Everything else on their jobs is listed under one shared strip, On your jobs, billed to someone else, in both directions: the builder sees the owner's bills there, and the owner sees the builder's. The strip is one code path (billedToFor), so the two directions cannot be changed independently without a viewer-role signal. And the amounts ride in the payload either way: hiding the strip in the page alone would leave the builder's invoices in the homeowner's JSON.

The rule

Per bill, two facts decide everything: who the viewer is on that job (the customer row, the GC row, or both when a GC was entered as the customer) and who pays that bill (effectiveInvoiceParty, unchanged).

The train

  1. Close the leak, hide the builder's bills from the owner.

    Kernel role function + tests · buildPortalBills owed-only · function filters before the payload and emits customersBills · payload parser tolerant. The page still renders from bills, so the old strip simply empties. Deploy the function first, then merge. Ships (b) and closes the payload leak in one step.

  2. Your customers' open bills on the GC's statement.

    The card (summary line, owner rows, age with the 30-day tint the ledger uses, received-so-far, total), the print page, the office mirror, the two guides, the glossary line. Render tests for present-on-GC / absent-on-owner / absent-on-customer-link. Ships (a).

  3. Optional: Ask the office.

    Two request kinds through submit-portal-request (bill this to us instead · remind the owner) landing as inbox requests; the office acts with Bill to ▾ or the Followup tools. Recommend waiting until the card has been seen live for a few weeks.

  4. Decision: the same rule for the non-money cards.

    Agreements (contract amounts), test reports and the visit picker still run over the whole customer-or-GC job union. Apply the viewer-role rule there too, or leave them as the shared job record. Not in the ask; raised because the exhibits made it visible.

Open decisions

  • Scoped gc links. The office mints these to give a GC's AP inbox "only their GC bills". Recommend the customers' card shows there too, since the owner asked for GCs to see it, and the copy explains it is not theirs to pay.
  • How much of the owner's status the GC sees. Recommend facts about the bill only: billed date, age, amount, received so far. Not the owner's promise, not collections, not the owner's contact details.
  • Paid repairs. Only open bills are listed. A "paid this year" fold would make the card a ledger; recommend no.
  • Typed "someone else" bills. Hidden from both parties. Unused in the data (0 of 524), so nothing changes today.
  • PR 4. Yes or no on scoping agreements and reports by the same rule.

Is this the best we can do?

What holds up

The rule is one function beside the one that already exists, decided per bill, so split jobs, invoice picks and the 72 GC-as-customer jobs all fall out without special cases. The owner side is a deletion. The leak closes on the server, not in CSS. Everything reuses the statement's own paper, grid and words.

Where it is thin, honestly

The card is nearly empty today. One open owner-paid bill under a GC in all of prod. Its value depends on the office recording the site owner as the customer and the GC as the GC, which the 72 legacy jobs never did. The plan should ship with the data lever, not just the UI: the Customers page could flag GCs whose jobs carry no owner.

The GC cannot act. A list of other people's debts with no verb is a report, not a tool. PR 3 is the fix, and it is small, but it is deferred on purpose so the first version cannot spam owners. If the card goes live and the first thing Done Right asks is "can we cover this," PR 3 moves up.

An adjacent bug the exhibits exposed. The server writers for payment promises still snapshot the payer as "the GC when the job has one" (add_job_payment_promise and the trigger), while every client reader follows the who-pays rule. On exactly these repair jobs a homeowner's promise is filed under Done Right. Flagged as its own task; not part of this train.

Alternative considered and rejected: a separate GC page (/gc/:slug) with its own function. It would duplicate the letterhead, the link machinery, view counting and the request forms for one extra card, and GCs already hold the merged link. The section belongs on the statement they already open.
