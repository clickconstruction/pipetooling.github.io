Affidavits from the desk: one window, two forms, three print faces

The office made one affidavit by hand this month in nine steps across four tools, and a date in it was wrong. One window on the Legal desk does it in four presses: type the statement with its title, who swears it, the day it is about and the county it is signed in; pick how it is signed, before a notary or under penalty of perjury with no notary; print the whole paper, the statement alone, or the signing page alone to staple on a handwritten statement; file the signed scan and confirm the two days. The text lands on the Timeline's Said lane, the scan on Evidence, linked. The blue Second pass box under each section says what the first render showed and what changed.

Where the words come from. Tex. Gov't Code § 312.011(1): "Affidavit" means a statement in writing of a fact or facts signed by the party making it, sworn to before an officer authorized to administer oaths, and officially certified to by the officer under his seal of office. The jurat is that certificate. The Secretary of State's sample form (Sept 2019): State of Texas · County of ___ · Sworn to and subscribed before me on the ___ day of ___, (year), by (name of signer). · (Personalized Seal) · Notary Public's Signature. § 406.013(a): the seal carries the words "Notary Public, State of Texas" around a star of five points and the notary public's name, the notary public's identifying number, and the date the notary public's commission expires, so the printed block needs the signature line and room for the seal; the printed-name and commission lines are a courtesy. The Lien desk prints these same words under the § 53.054 lien affidavit today; the kernel lifts them so both papers read one jurat.
The declaration. Tex. Civ. Prac. & Rem. Code § 132.001(a): Except as provided by Subsection (b), an unsworn declaration may be used in lieu of a written sworn declaration, verification, certification, oath, or affidavit required by statute or required by a rule, order, or requirement adopted as provided by law. (b): This section does not apply to a lien required to be filed with a county clerk, an instrument concerning real or personal property required to be filed with a county clerk, or an oath of office or an oath required to be taken before a specified official other than a notary public. (c): it must be in writing; and subscribed by the person making the declaration as true under penalty of perjury. (d): the jurat, quoted whole on the paper at right. So the § 53.054 lien affidavit stays a notarized paper on the Lien desk, and this window never offers the declaration for it.

7 Is this the best we can do?

The questions a first-timer, the witness, the firm and a bad week put to the design, and what the drawing answers.

What a first-timer presses firstSwear a statement… beside Choose files on the Evidence tab, because the scan lands there and that is where the office already goes with a paper. The Timeline composer's kind tag offers it too, and the Said lane's empty words name it. One window, two doors. The first field asks who swears it, and the roster pick fills the role line, so the first thing typed is the title and the second is the story.
What the witness holdsTwo Letter pages at most: numbered paragraphs in a serif, a signature line with their name and role under it, then the signing block on the same page as the signature, never alone on a page of its own. When they wrote their own, the signing page names what it is stapled to and asks for their initials on each page, so no notary or court calls it a loose jurat.
What the firm seesThe statement's text in its Timeline's Said lane with the chip sworn statement or declaration, and the scan under Evidence with the sworn line, linked. A draft shows as a draft with a loud chip, as a letter's draft does today, so counsel can ask for a change before the signing. Open call for the owner: some firms would rather never see an unsigned statement. The hold works per statement either way.
The scan never comes backThe draft stays on the Timeline as a draft chip on the day it is about, turns amber after fourteen days (printed 14 days ago · no signed copy), and sits under the Evidence tab's Drafts filter. Not signed… voids it with a reason the line keeps: Undone: would not sign the September paragraph. Nothing pretends it was sworn.
The text changed after the printEach print keeps its text. Filing asks whether the signed paper reads as last printed. They changed it by hand keeps the printed text and marks it, so the Timeline never quotes a sentence nobody swore to without saying so. The scan governs.
A birth date and a home addressThe declaration must carry them. They are typed at print time, held in the window for the session so a reprint after an edit does not ask twice, and never saved: not in the entry, not on the Timeline, not in the Evidence line. The scan carries them to the firm, as it must.
Fewer presses still?The desk could offer Swear it on a field report's row and prefill the day and the first paragraph from the report, and on a clock session the same. That is a later door, not this card. The record check already puts the report beside the day.
