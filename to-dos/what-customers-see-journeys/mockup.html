Settings → What customers see · dev-only · mock-up 2026-09-16

Every outside surface, in the order they meet it

The tab was built to show every email and page a customer, a GC or a sub gets. Since then the app grew a customer contract page, a submittal review room, a law firm's portal and a supply house's quote page, and none of them appear. This is what the tab looks like with all of them in, and the plan to get there without it drifting again.

Second pass · after "is this the best we can do?"

Not a gallery for devs. The one place the office sees a customer's whole journey, and opens any door on it

The first version below completes the tab as built: every surface rendered with sample data, guarded by a test. It is right and it is small. But everything is now in one place, and that is a bigger opportunity than a preview. The same strips, fed a real person instead of Sam Sample, become the journey that person has actually had with the company: what was sent, when they opened it, where they stopped, and one tap to the page they hold. The app already records all of it and shows it nowhere together.

Figures on this strip are illustrative except the agreement story, which is J363's real record (rev 1 opened once, revised twice, signed on paper Sep 4). The health row's numbers come from the same tables the Needs You cards read; the counts shown are placeholders for the mock-up.

1

Dev-only wastes the map. The tab is gated to the dev role, so the people who send these pages every day, Taunya and the office, have never seen them from the customer's side. That is how a plain-text estimate email went unnoticed for months. Open it to dev, master and the assistant-like roles; keep the sample mode for training-mode users, who cannot write anyway.

2

A sample answers "what does it look like"; a person answers "what happened". The strips take a search box. Sample stays the default in Settings. Pick a customer, a GC, a sub, a house or the firm and every step turns into a status card: sent, opened, signed, paid, or not yet, with the date, read from the stamps the tables already carry (viewed_at, first_viewed_at, signed_at, opened_at, the page-views log). No new tracking.

3

Swift access means the real link, not a preview of it. Each rendered card on a person's strip opens the page that person holds, the same token, so the office sees exactly their portal, their bid room, their unsigned agreement. Today those links are copied from six different places (People → Subs → 🌐, Cover Letter → Publish, the contract modal, and so on). One strip, every door.

4

The step that is missing is a door too. A card that says never sent or never opened carries the app's own next action: Start the sweep, Edit & re-send, Copy link, Ask when they'll pay. The journey view is where a stall is obvious, so the fix belongs on it.

5

Teaching lives on the card, not in a manual. Every step already carries a reflects list (which Settings shape it). Add two lines: what triggers it, and what the customer can do there. Link each card to the help guide that covers it. A new office hire reads the customer journey in an hour by clicking along a strip.

6

The health row is the journey for everyone at once. Per step, how many people are at it and how many are stuck: agreements unopened, bid rooms never opened, portals never visited, statements uncertified. These are the Needs You cards regrouped by where the customer is, with the same doors, and the tab becomes the place that answers "where do customers stop?".

✓

Where it lives. Settings keeps the tab (sample mode, the config check, the count and the test). The same strips component also renders on the Customer page and the Job page as Their journey, preselected, which is where the office is standing when the question comes up. One component, two doors, no second truth.

✓

What does not change. The first version's registry test, the sample fixtures and the six-PR list all stay; the person mode is layered on the same journeys data. Nothing here adds tracking, exposes a page to anyone who could not already open it, or writes anything the office could not already do from the surface that owns the action.

✓

The count at the top is a test, not a promise. customerSurfaceRegistry.test.ts lists every route in App.tsx outside ProtectedRoute and every function that calls sendEmailViaResend to a non-staff address, and fails when one has no journey step or an explicit exemption with a reason. The next room someone builds cannot ship without a place on this page, the same way a feature cannot ship without a release note.

Is this the best we can do?

The obvious build, and what it gets wrong

The first cut was "add the six pages as steps". It is kept here because its mistakes shaped the version above.

1

Adding pages fixes today and not next month. The tab is behind because nothing checks it. Without the registry test, the seventh room will be missing by October. The test is the smallest PR and ships first.

2

"Customers" is the wrong word for two of the audiences. A supply house and a law firm are not customers, and putting them under that title invites someone to trim them later. The tab keeps its name (it is searchable and known), but its hint line becomes "Every email and page anyone outside the company gets", and the count says outside surfaces.

3

Copying the email builders into the browser would create a second truth. Four senders build their HTML inline in the function. Duplicating that into src/lib means the preview and the real email diverge on the first edit. A sample mode on the sender costs one deploy each and keeps one builder.

4

The collections paper is not a page and should not pretend to be one. The demand letter and the § 53.056 notice are PDFs the customer opens from an email. Rendering them as iframes would show a blank frame; the test-report card already solved this, so they take that shape.

5

The first cut put the contract in the wrong strip. The customer's agreement is a homeowner step between the thank-you and the bill, not a sub step. Placing it there is what makes the Contract sweep's paper lane visible from the customer's side for the first time, which is the whole point of the tab.

✓

Left out on purpose. The texted job-share card (job number, name, address and status for whoever holds a link, then a redirect into the app), sign-in, invite and password-reset emails, and the two staff preview routes. They are staff or near-staff surfaces. The registry lists them as exempt with that reason, so the count stays honest.

If we build it

Nine PRs, smallest first, each one alone useful

Client-only PRs merge and deploy with Pages. A PR that touches a function needs that function deployed after merge, and any edit to _shared/customerSampleFixtures.ts means redeploying every importer by hand, because check:edge-drift cannot see shared code.

  1. PR 1 · The honest tab. The surface registry and its test; every missing surface added now as a Next release card (the soon render kind already exists); the two new strips; the hint line reworded; the count at the top. Client only · S · the tab shows every gap the day it merges, and CI guards it from then on.
  2. PR 2 · The homeowner's agreement. Sample branch in get-job-contract (plus a sample-done state), /contract/sign honouring the sample token, mode: 'sample' on send-job-contract and remind-job-contracts. Client + 3 functions · M · this is the surface the Contract sweep to-do is about, so it goes second.
  3. PR 3 · The money paper. Bill by email, the hazmat notice (email and page, client fixture), the terms page. All three builders are client-side already. Client only · S.
  4. PR 4 · The GC's rooms. Sample branch in get-submittal-room with a decided state, the room honouring it, mode: 'sample' on send-test-report, the statement email from the existing client builder; the test-report card becomes a step. Client + 2 functions · M.
  5. PR 5 · When it goes wrong. The demand letter and the notice to the owner of record as PDF steps at the end of their journeys, from demandLetter.ts and lienFilingDocuments.ts over the sample job. Client only · S.
  6. PR 6 · The two new audiences. Sample branches in get-rfq-quote-page (with a submitted state) and legal-portal; mode: 'sample' on legal-notify-dispatch for the confirm, now and digest emails and the confirmed page. Client + 3 functions · M · the firm's sample must be built from the fixture only; a real matter can never reach this frame.
  7. PR 7 · Open the tab to the office. Role gate widened to dev, master and the assistant-like roles; training-mode users see sample only. Every step gains its two teaching lines and a help-guide link. Client only · S.
  8. PR 8 · A person on the strips. The search box; a kernel journeyForPerson.ts that turns one customer, GC, sub, house or firm's rows into per-step status cards from the existing stamps; each card opens the real page with the link they hold, and carries the owning surface's next action. The same component renders as Their journey on the Customer and Job pages. Client only, reads existing tables through RLS · L · the one large PR, and the one that changes what the tab is.
  9. PR 9 · The health row. One RPC counting people per step and stalls per step, the doors reused from Needs You. Client + one migration (a read-only RPC) · M.

Each PR: npm run claim, a release note and fragment, the what customers see section of the dev docs, and a live pass in the tab at phone and desktop width before it merges.

Yours to decide

Seven calls, three of them before PR 1

  • Does the tab open to the office? Proposed yes: dev, master and the assistant-like roles, with training-mode users held to sample. It is the single change that makes the rest worth building. Decide before PR 1 so the wording and the count are written for the office, not for devs.
  • May the office open a customer's real page from here? Proposed yes: the office can already copy every one of these links from the surface that made it; the strip only gathers them. If no, person mode shows status and dates only and the door reads Copy link.
  • Where does person mode live? Proposed both: Settings keeps sample as the default with the search beside it, and the Customer and Job pages get Their journey preselected. If only one, the Customer page wins; Settings is not where the office stands when they wonder what a customer saw.
  • Do the supply house and the law firm belong on this tab? Proposed yes, as their own strips, with the hint line widened to "anyone outside the company". The alternative is a second tab, which splits the one place that answers "what do they see".
  • Do the collections documents sit inside the journeys or in a strip of their own? Proposed inside, after a dashed break, because a customer meets the demand letter as the end of their story. A separate When it goes wrong strip is a one-line change if the office would rather see the legal paper together.
  • Should a sample mode on a sender be allowed at all? It is one more branch in a function that sends real email. The guard is the one the pages already use: the office JWT is required and the mode never touches Resend. If the answer is no, the four inline builders move to _shared and get re-exported, at the cost of a parity test each.
  • Does the sample GC job carry an owner of record? The notice step needs one in the fixture (Sample Contracting builds for a Sample Owner LLC). Naming it is a fixture edit; leaving it out drops that one step to "not rendered".
Every surface named here was read from the code on 2026-09-16 (src/App.tsx public routes, src/lib/customerJourneys.ts, and the edge functions that serve or send each one). The mock-up draws thumbnails as wireframes; the built tab shows the real frames.
