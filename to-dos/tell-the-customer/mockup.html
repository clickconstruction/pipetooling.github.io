Punch list #115 · mock-up · 2026-10-16

Tell the customer: every text or email is pressed by a person, seen before it goes, and followed by a receipt that suggests the call

The principle: nothing reaches a customer without a person pressing it, or seeing it the moment after. When Rosa schedules a job's first visit or sends an estimate, a Tell the customer sheet opens with the step's line filled, the channels that apply ticked, the words hers to edit, and Send or Skip. The reminders the app would send on its own become offers she presses from Today's sends. After anything goes, a receipt shows what went and suggests the call, with the script and one tap to dial. The people, the job and the estimate are made up.

6 The five calls, settled

  1. Preview on the press, offers for the app's own sends, a receipt either way.settled The principle wants a person in every send: the preview is where they see the words first, the receipt is where they learn what the app did.
  2. The estimate cadence: day 1 text, day 3 call, day 7 text, then rest in They owe us.settled
  3. Call scripts, office‑written per step in both languages, seeded.settled
  4. The page link rides in the estimate's texts, never the booking's.settled
  5. Booked on the first visit; Rescheduled when it moves; later visits by hand from the thread.settled

7 Alternatives weighed

  1. The app sends on its own with no receipt (today's stage sends and morning run once switched on).rejected The assistant would learn of a text from the customer.
  2. A receipt only, no preview.rejected When the assistant caused the message they should see the words first; the receipt alone fits only the app's own sends.
  3. A call the app places after every send.not possible Quo's API sends texts and reads calls; it places none. An automated call is also a different consent regime. A call is a person's, with a script and a task.
  4. Preview on the press, offers for the app's sends, the receipt with the call, the task Quo closes.picked
  5. A separate Calls page.rejected Follow up's We owe them already holds what we owe people, and the Dashboard's Customers line counts it.
  6. The receipt on the Messages page too.rejected The thread shows the send; one quiet line under the bubble.

8 Is this the best we can do? The second pass

The first pass took the ask as written: a modal after every send suggesting a call to say it went. Read back against the assistant with six jobs to schedule, the customer who gets a call about a text, and the morning run that sends with nobody on a page, seven things changed.

  1. A modal after every send became a receipt that never stacks. One per press; the morning run's sends are listed on the Dashboard instead of popping windows nobody is there to see.
  2. "Call to say it went" became "call to walk them through it." The script says why we call; the office can turn the suggestion off for a step where a call would be odd.
  3. The app's own sends became offers by default. The three-way switch keeps the app's memory and loses the surprise; Sends stays for the owner who wants it on a step.
  4. The call became a task Quo closes. The webhook already logs every call; a call to that number the same day marks the task done, so nobody records a call twice.
  5. The preview became editable for the office. The office writes free text in the reply box today; the step's line is the starting words, not a cage. Techs keep the no‑free‑text rule.
  6. Both channels became one press. The estimate's email and its text go together, each ticked, and the receipt names both.
  7. A changed first visit became a Rescheduled line instead of silence.
