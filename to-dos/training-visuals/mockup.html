Punch list #116 · Training visuals · the course's own sentences, no made-up facts

Training visuals

Read as a fourteen-year-old on a phone, the OSHA 30 refresher fails in four kinds of places: shapes in space, things you recognise by sight, scales, and motions. Each spot below is the sentence as it reads today beside the same sentence with its drawing under it. The drawing sits inside the lesson, in the page's ink, and reads in both themes. Ten are drawn here; the catalog at the foot names all thirty-five, and the plan says how they ship.

Now

  • Engineering controls work without you. Guards, ventilation and dust collection are engineering, while training and permits are administrative.

2The clearance stack · lesson 8

Now
Must know
  • Free fall is the lanyard length plus or minus the distance from D-ring to anchor. It is never over 6 ft. A 6-ft lanyard anchored at your feet means 11 ft.
  • Clearance adds four things to the free fall. 3.5 ft to slow, 1 ft of D-ring shift, 5 ft of D-ring height and 2 ft of safety. Rule of thumb: 18 ft below you.
Suggestion
Must know
  • Clearance adds four things to the free fall. 3.5 ft to slow, 1 ft of D-ring shift, 5 ft of D-ring height and 2 ft of safety. Rule of thumb: 18 ft below you.

In words it is a sum to do in your head. Drawn, it is a stack with the ground at the bottom, and the question "is there 18 ft under me?" asks itself.

3The guardrail · lesson 8, used again in 18

Now
Must know

  • A parapet is the short wall at a roof edge. A 24-in parapet can serve as midrail and toeboard under an added top rail.

6The swing radius · lesson 11, used again in 22 and 30

Now
Must know
  • The swing radius is the full 360 degrees out to the furthest moving point. It includes loads, cabs, tracks and buckets. Red rope on delineators marks it.
  • Vehicle blind spots: directly behind, under a raised bucket, the far side of a side-set cab. Reversing needs a back-up alarm and a spotter. Inspect each shift.

Now
Must know

  • A breaker trips at 15,000 mA. It protects the wiring, not you.

8The signs sheet · lessons 14, 24 and 9

Now
Must know
  • The nine pictograms include the flame, health hazard, skull and crossbones, corrosion, and flame over circle for an oxidizer. The signal word Danger is more severe than Warning.
  • Red signs mean danger, the most severe hazard. Orange or yellow means warning. Yellow and black means caution. Blue is a safety notice.
  • Underground flags: yellow gas or oil, blue water, red electric.

9The hard hat's limits · lesson 11

Now
Must know
  • A hard hat has limits. A 1-lb wrench injures from 30 ft and a 2-lb hammer from 15 ft. A 5-lb brick from 8 ft and a 10-lb drill from 4 ft. A 20-lb sledge from 2 ft, a 50-lb block from 1 ft.

10PASS and the fire classes · lesson 25

Now
Must know
  • Fire classes: A ordinary combustibles, B flammable liquids and gases, C energized electrical equipment, D metals, K cooking oils.
  • An electrical fire takes dry chemical or CO2, never water. A flammable liquid takes CO2, dry chemical or foam.
  • Use PASS from 6 to 8 ft away: Pull, Aim at the base, Squeeze, Sweep. If the fire does not shrink right away, get out.

How a drawing rides in a lesson

One line in a lesson's Must know or Traps list draws a figure: - {{figure:clearance-stack|A 6-ft lanyard needs 18 ft of clear space below you.}}. The parser reads it into a figure item; the page draws the named SVG at full width with the caption under it, and the list shows no bullet for that item. The caption goes through the same plain-words rules as every sentence, and a reader who cannot see the drawing hears the caption.

Each figure is one small React component returning an inline SVG in the page's ink: currentColor strokes and the house tints, so the same drawing reads in dark with no second copy. Under 4 KB each, the whole catalog under 120 KB, all of it in the course page's own chunk and none in the Dashboard's. A test holds the registry and the lessons to each other: every figure a lesson names exists, every drawn figure is named somewhere, every figure renders with its name. The content version hashes the figure's name and caption with the words, so a changed caption is a changed lesson and, by the rule already in place, never voids a pass.

The plan · six small PRs

  1. PR 0 · the mechanism, with the controls ladder in lesson 1 and the clearance stack in lesson 8 as the proof, the registry, the tests, one sentence in the guide, a production build before the merge.
  2. PR 1 · space: figures 3 to 10 and the lesson 30 site map.
  3. PR 2 · sight: figures 11 to 19.
  4. PR 3 · scales: figures 20 to 28.
  5. PR 4 · motions: figures 29 to 35.
  6. PR 5 · reuse: each figure named in every other lesson that teaches the same fact.

Each figure PR is cut from fresh main, carries its release note and fragment, builds before it merges, and is walked as the sample helper on a 375 px phone in both themes.

Is this the best we can do?

Photos were the first thought. A photo of a guardrail says less than a drawing with three dimension lines, costs a licence or a site visit, weighs a hundred times more against the app's offline cache, and cannot follow the dark theme. The line drawing does all four.

Recordings are what the help guides use for the app's own screens, because a screen goes stale. A ladder never goes stale, and a motion in four still frames reads at a glance where a loop has to be watched through.

A Pictures section at the foot of each lesson makes the reader scroll away from the sentence and match the picture back to it. The figure sits under its sentence and nowhere else.

One poster per lesson says everything and teaches nothing. One figure per fact, only where words fail. Lessons 3, 4, 6 and 29 get none.

Interactive figures, a tap to reveal a label, are worth doing for two or three later, the trench first. Static first, because a figure that needs a tap is invisible to a skim and to a screen reader.

The Numbers chips already list the values at the foot of a lesson. They are a summary for the retake, not a way to understand the first time.

Grace's calls

  1. Line drawings or photos. Drawn: line drawings in the app's ink. Photos only where a drawing cannot do it, which the catalog does not find.
  2. The first ten or all thirty-five. The ten above carry the most weight. All thirty-five is five small PRs over a few days.
  3. A figure inside a question. "Which slope is Type C?" with the three drawn. Not in this plan; a later PR once the figures exist.
  4. The pictograms. Ours resemble the GHS set, enough to recognise the real one on a label. The lesson does not link out.
  5. Spanish. Captions ride with the Spanish course when it comes; the drawings need no second copy.
