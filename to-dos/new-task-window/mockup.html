The window that gives a task

Yes, it is worth redrawing. The window works, but it shows every choice at once, at the same weight. Seven tasks in ten use none of them. My suggestion keeps every field and puts the common task on one screen.

The short of it

My suggestion

  • The window is called New task.
  • It reads top to bottom as a sentence: what, for whom, where, when.
  • Each row has its own shape. People are faces, the day is one bar, places are a shelf.
  • The people row holds Me, the people given to last, the crews, then Someone else.
  • The rare things are quiet presses in one row called Add. Each opens in place.
  • The bar at the bottom says in words what will happen. Its button names who gets the task.
  • On a phone the window is about 570 px tall, under half of today's. Nothing is below the screen's edge.

Same fields, same save, no change to the database.

Now

  • The window is called Add checklist item:, a name the app no longer uses for a task.
  • It is 1,267 px tall on a phone. That is one and a half screens.
  • The day sits 711 px down. The reminder and the bottom Send are below the screen's edge.
  • The list of people is always open and takes 260 px.
  • Place and Photo of the spot, new this week, added 245 px.
  • 53 boxes and presses show before a word is typed.

96 of 137 tasks used none of the eight extras. 110 of 129 one-time tasks were for today.

What I would not claim. The row of faces does not hold everyone. I replayed it on the 137 tasks on record. A phone shows Me and the last four people, and that held everyone on 87 tasks. A swipe to a fifth face makes it 93. On the rest the giver presses Someone else first, one press more than today. Me and three names, about what the Recent line offers now, held 80.

Your calls

  1. The name. My pick is New task. The button that opens the window already says Task, and the guide says "give someone a task".
  2. People. My pick is the row of faces with Someone else. The other way keeps the list open always, as now, and costs 180 px on every task.
  3. The rare things. My pick is the Add row, each thing named. The other way is one More fold, which is shorter and hides them all.
  4. The button. My pick is that Send names who gets the task: Send to Theo, Send to 3 people, Add to my list. Cancel leaves the bar, and the ✕ at the top closes the window as now.
  5. Tomorrow. My pick is a Tomorrow press. Ten tasks were for the next day, and each took a date box.
  6. The other two windows. My pick is that Change a task and the roadmap's Add task take the same rows next, so the three read alike.
