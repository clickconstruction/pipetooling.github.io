Sub payments as a ledger

Every payment made to a sub, in date order, on Jobs → Subs and on People → Subs. Built to sit beside a bank statement and be checked off line by line.

The short of it1 · Dates on the Pay rows2 · The ledger, by date3 · By sub, with a running balance4 · On a phone5 · Checking it against the bank6 · Where each fact comes from7 · Your calls8 · The plan

The short of it

  • Today the date of a payment is two presses deep. The Pay view is one row per sheet. A payment's date shows only after the row is opened, one sheet at a time, and never on a phone. People → Subs loads every payment and shows none.
  • The fix is a third view, the Ledger: one line per payment, newest first, grouped by month with the month's total. The same view groups by sub, oldest first, with a running balance. One screen, two doors.
  • The first three steps need no database change. Every payment and its date are already loaded on the Pay view. The check mark against the bank is the first step that stores anything new.

What a line is. One row of the payments table: the day sent, who, which job and sheet, the memo as typed, the amount, and what the sheet still owes after it. A back-charge is a line in red. A payment that was moved between sheets leaves its grey trace, with who moved it and why. A job's name opens the sheet. A sub's name, pressed in By sub, opens their statement, drawn next.

5 · Checking it against the bank three depths, the first is free

The app does not know today how a sub was paid or whether the bank shows it. A payment row holds an amount, a date and a memo. So reconciling comes in three depths.

DepthWhat you getWhat it costs
A · Read and exportThe ledger above, in date order with month totals, and a CSV to sort beside the bank's own export.No database change. Steps 2 and 3 of the plan.
B · A check mark and how it was paidThe ✓ column: tick a line once you have seen it at the bank. The line keeps who ticked it and when. A Not checked chip shows what is left. Each payment also gets How paid (check, ACH, Zelle, Cash App, cash) and a reference, so one account can be checked at a time.One small migration: four columns on the payments table. Changing a ticked payment's amount or date clears its tick and says so.
C · Match to the bank lineFor a payment that left a Mercury account, the ledger offers the bank line with the same amount within three days: Mercury · Nov 10 · −$3,000.00 · CHECK 1187 Match. A matched line ticks itself.A link table and a matching kernel. Worth it only if most sub money leaves Mercury. Your call 2.

7 · Your calls

  1. Which date leads? My pick: Date sent, the day the money left. A payment with none shows the day it was recorded, marked no date sent, with a door to set it. A back-charge has no date field today and gets one.
  2. How are subs paid, and from where? If most of it leaves Mercury, depth C is worth building. If it is Zelle, Cash App and checks from several places, depth B is the right stop. My pick until you say: stop at B.
  3. Crew pay sheets in the ledger? They are payments made to people, so my pick is yes, with a crew chip and a filter. They stay out of every We owe.
  4. Who sees it? My pick: whoever opens Jobs → Subs → Pay today, the office. A superintendent sees the Subs tab and no payments now, and would not see the Ledger.
  5. Newest first or oldest first, by date? My pick: newest first by date, oldest first on one sub's statement, since a running balance reads forward.

8 · The plan each step ships by itself

  1. Dates on the Pay rows. Section 1. No database change.
  2. The Ledger on Jobs → Subs, by date. Section 2 without the ✓ column: periods, search, month totals, Print, CSV, the phone cards. No database change.
  3. By sub, and People → Subs. Section 3: the Roster | Ledger switch, one sub's statement with its running balance, the print. No database change.
  4. The check mark and How paid. Depth B. One migration.
  5. Match to the bank. Depth C. Only on your yes to call 2.
