Prospect fields: who we are talking to

Grace, 2026-10-17: "when it is an owner it is not always a company it is sometimes a person, and when it is a sub they'll usually have a google maps business profile, how could we refine the prospects fields to better accommodate this and other things?" Then: make a mock-up, and ask whether it is the best we can do, whether it is holistic, and whether it thinks about the customer. This page draws today, a first cut, the critique, and the revised cut. Every name, street and number is made up.

Recommendation, before the drawings: build B. The first cut answers the two asks with a tick and a box; the second pass found the real shape under them. Every prospect is a person we call, sometimes at a company, and the facts we gather on the call are the same facts the customer record, the bench and Messages keep. Gather them once, in the shape those records want, and the person on the other end is greeted by their own name, texted on the phone they gave us, in their language, and never asked twice.

Is this the best we can do? Is it holistic? Does it think about the customer?

A answers the two asks and adds five boxes. Read back from the other end of the phone, and from the three records a prospect turns into, it stops short in six places. The customer here is three people: the homeowner we call, the sub we want on our bench, and the GC estimator we want plans from. B is built for them.

Grace's calls

  1. The person first, on every kind. Drawn as B. The smaller cut is A's tick on owners only; it fixes the homeowner and nothing else.
  2. May we text? Drawn as a question on the call with Quo refusing a text without a yes. The lighter rule is that a number they gave us counts as yes. The owner's call, since it is his number texting.
  3. Cell and office, or one phone. Drawn as two labeled numbers. One phone is simpler and keeps texting a landline possible.
  4. The Places read. Not in this train. Rating, hours and the sub's point for distance come from a paid Google read and wait for the owner's yes.
  5. Also reach. Drawn as one free line. The fuller shape is the customers' contact-persons list on the prospect, which is a table.
  6. Order of work. PR 1 the migration and the person block on the form and card; PR 2 the three seeds; PR 3 the Maps paste, link and bench column; PR 4 the texting rule in Quo's path; then the guides.
Nothing here changes the lanes or the deck's machine. The one write that grows is the prospect row, by seven columns, and the one new rule outside Prospects is a text refused without a yes.
