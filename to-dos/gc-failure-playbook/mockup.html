GC failure playbook: what counsel's memo asks around the notice

The letters shipped (v2.3744, v2.3745). Counsel's memo of Sep 22 also tells the office to do four things the app cannot do today: send the § 53.057 retainage notice inside its 30-day fuse, send a second owner letter at day 10–14, keep four facts per job the desk does not hold (bond, paid-out, the 10% reserve, the original contract's completion), and print the letter with the signer's own phone. Below, each piece drawn where it lands. Harborline Builders and its jobs are an example GC, not a real customer. Amber marks what is new.

Built (v2.3744 · 3745)

The § 53.056 notice per job per month, the three cover letters by property kind plus the unresponsive one, the timely-only claim with the stale footnote, the § 53.254(g) statement on a home, one envelope per property, the run.

Not built · this page

A the § 53.057 form and its clock · B letter two and its reminder · C the owner's answers, the bond, and the three piles · D the signer's phone. Three PRs, two of them with a migration.

Kept out on purpose

No interest, fee or Penal Code line on the owner's envelope (those stay on the GC demand). No joint check. Counsel signs off per job before an owner's direct payment is accepted. Nothing here changes the statutory text.

To the owner of 44 Lantern Row, New Braunfels,
This page is a cover letter. The enclosed notice is given under Texas Property Code § 53.056.
We are the plumbing contractor on your project under Harborline Builders. Harborline has not paid us $9,800.00 for work in July and August 2026 and has not responded to us. A copy of this letter goes to Harborline.
We have reason to believe you may already have paid Harborline in full. If that is true, there may be no further draw to withhold. Two things still matter:
  1. Texas law required you to reserve 10% of the original contract during construction and for 30 days after that contract was completed (§ 53.101). If that 10% is still in your hands, you may hold our claim out of it. If it was paid to Harborline when it should have been reserved, the property can still be reached for that reserved amount (§ 53.105).
  2. This notice preserves our right to file a lien affidavit on the property. If $9,800.00 is not paid, we will file that affidavit by the 15th day of the third month after our last work month. A recorded affidavit is harder to take off than this notice.
Please call Rey Salinas at (830) 555-0142 6 this week and tell us:
  • whether you still owe Harborline any amount, including retainage;
  • whether you reserved the statutory 10%, and whether it is still in your hands; and
  • the date the original contract was completed, if it is done.
If you still owe Harborline, do not send Harborline those dollars until this is cleared. If you want the notice released, the payment we can take is a check or wire payable only to Click Plumbing. We cannot deposit a joint check. We will send you a release the day funds clear and mail a copy to Harborline.
We would rather pick up a check than record an affidavit.

Is this the best we can do?

What it gets right

Every piece lands on a surface the office already uses: Edit Job, the desk's tabs and piles, the sent notice, the run. No new page, no new notification channel. The dollars are named per pot the way counsel named them: the trapped draw, the 10% reserve, the property.

The grid is not typed into a spreadsheet; it is what the app already computes plus four facts, and it prints.

Where it could fall short

Contract-ended is a typed date. If nobody types it, no § 53.057 clock starts. The pile "Clock not started" and the suggestion from the last clock day are the guard; a nightly nudge is not drawn here.

Retainage only exists if the subcontract holds it. If Click's subcontracts rarely carry retainage, section A shrinks to the claim-box line and the form waits. That is the first question for the owner.

The owner's answers are hearsay. A call is not a draw sheet. The grid marks them as the owner's word with a date; the affidavit decision is still the master's.

The plan

Three PRs, smallest instrument first. Each with its release note, fragment and guide update; PRs 1 and 3 carry one migration each.

  1. PR 1 · § 53.057 + the clock + the signer's phone (M). Migration: two columns on the job (contract ended on / how) and the subcontract retainage, a payment-bond flag, and one RPC for the retainage windows in the same shape as the notice months. Client: the Edit Job row, gates 4 and 5, the Retainage tab with its piles, the form as a third document kind on the Lien window, the retainage line inside the § 53.056 claim box with the split enclosure, signerPhoneFor. Guide: send lien notices from the Lien desk gains a Retainage section. Counsel reads the drafted form before the first goes out.
  2. PR 2 · Letter two (S). Client-only if the "GC authorized direct pay" tick can live on the existing filing record's notes; otherwise one small column. The days-since kernel, the Sent footer's two doors, the paid-out letter as a second letter kind in the run, the Needs-you count, the claims-table column.
  3. PR 3 · The owner's call, the piles, the grid (M). Migration: the owner's three answers on the filing record. Client: the call dialog, the pile chips and next-step lines on the Affidavits tab, the bond gate, step 5 on the run with print.
