What the rows say. A superintendent and a lead never meet the empty window. Most of the office does, and so do the field techs who are scheduled and do not clock. Three reads fill the list for nearly everyone, and each is already a quick read: older clocks, past days on the schedule, and the team. Two new helpers have nothing in any of them. What needs a decision. For the office, the truest answer is the jobs they changed in the app. That record has no quick way to be read by person today. Reading it needs one small index on the database, which is a migration. The mock-ups below do not lean on it.

Is this the best we can do?

Asked after the page was rendered at 375 by 812 and at 1180 by 720, light and dark. Nothing ran off the side of a screen. No window was taller than its screen.

What the look changed

  • The desk window starts near the top, not in the middle. Six rows did not fit under a centred window on a short screen. A window that hangs from one height also stays put while typing shortens the list.
  • A row says how it knows, only when it is not a clock. A plain day means they clocked onto it. scheduled Nov 14 means it was on their schedule, which does not prove they went. Tally's Choose job sheet already says how it knows on each Recent row (#216).
  • On the team, not your team. The job is not theirs. They are on its team.
  • B is shorter than A on a phone: 603 tall against 696, with the same six rows. The link's two lines and the search's label go.

What was weighed and lost

  • One flat list with no headings. Today's jobs carry times, and a heading is how a tech finds them in one look.
  • A menu that drops from the press at a desk. The same window opens from four doors, and three of them have no press to hang it from.
  • Rows as one ruled list, not boxes. The boxed row is the one the search and Near you draw, and a box 52 tall is what a press looks like across the app.
  • Cutting the first sentence. It is the owner's rule in words, and it tells a first-timer why they are asked.
  • C, the guess. The Dashboard's square has no second line to say where the map will land. A job from last week is a weak guess. A wrong one is a map on the wrong job.

What is still weak

  • The office's truest list is the jobs they changed in the app. That waits on the owner's call about one small index.
  • A person nobody has put on a job still meets a sentence and no rows. It now names the two presses above it.
  • That the search belongs on top on a phone rests on one belief: what is found today is drawn under the box, where the keyboard sits. That has not been tried on a real phone.
  • Jobs coming up on the schedule are not listed. A way-in map is most use the day before, so this is worth its own call.
