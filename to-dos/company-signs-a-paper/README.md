# The company signs a paper too (#412)

Taunya asked on 2026-11-21 that this be its own card. It was the last unbuilt row under *What is left*
on card #376 (`to-dos/documents-agreements-tab/README.md`), and that card closes once its two built
pieces land. Nothing here is designed or built.

## What is true today

- A paper sent for signature is signed by the other side alone. `accept-contract` records their name,
  the time they agreed and their drawn or typed signature.
- The signed record and the PDF show that one signature. The company is named in the words and signs
  nowhere.
- The one exception is a two-party form. A form whose template has boxes for the office, such as an
  I-9's second section, is finished by the office through `complete-contract-form-office`, which keeps
  the office signer's printed name and the time.

## What it takes

Each line is a guess at the shape, to be redrawn once the calls are made.

1. **Who signs for the company.** A short list of people who may sign, kept in Settings.
2. **A signature on file.** Each of them draws or types a signature once, and the app keeps it.
3. **A countersign step.** After the other side signs, the paper waits on the company. One press by a
   person on the list signs it, with their name, their title and the time.
4. **Both names on the record.** The signed record and the PDF show both signatures. A copy that goes
   to the signer goes once both have signed, or says the company has yet to sign.
5. **The Agreements tab** says which papers wait on the company, as it says which wait on the other
   side.

## The calls, Taunya's to make

1. **Who may sign for the company.** One named person, a few, or a role.
2. **Which papers need it.** Every paper, or only a mutual one, where both sides take on duties. A
   one-sided paper, such as a worker's acknowledgement, may need none.

Two more follow from the second call and wait on it: whether a paper counts as signed before the
company signs, and whether a paper already signed gets the company's signature added.
