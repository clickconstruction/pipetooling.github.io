One place for work paid in kind

In kind became a kind of payment in v2.6816. It pays a bill down and is never counted as money. What the app still does not know is the deal behind the payments: who pays this way, with what, how much is left, and that a month's credit is due. Each month someone must remember, work out the amount, and split it across bills by hand.

Two cases are drawn. Calloway Holdings is a customer and also our landlord. It pays $19,875.40 of finished work with a rent credit each month. Pecan Creek Gravel paid part of one bill with three loads of road base. Frames A to G are the shape I would build. Frame H is the smallest alternative. The last two sections are the second look and the calls that are yours.

Why "Went to" matters. Today a month's $1,500.00 is typed as two payments on two jobs when a bill has less than that open. The ledger keeps the month whole and shows the split under it. A reader sees six credits, not nine payment rows.

The terms, written down once. Change the terms holds what is in people's heads today: who, what is given, the rent, what we pay, when it started, which bills it pays and in what order, and the name the rent goes out under at the bank.

Is this the best we can do?

A second look at the first draft, written after the frames were rendered at desk and phone width, light and dark.

  • The page is the obvious first build, and I think it is the wrong one. The ask was "an in kind page", and frame G is that page. Then ask what went wrong in November. Nobody was missing a page. Nobody was told. A page answers a person who already remembered. So the reminder and the one press go first, and the list goes second.
  • The amount should be a reason, not a number. The plain version of frame D is a box with $1,500.00 already in it. That hides why. The window shows the bank line and three plain answers instead. A person checks a fact instead of trusting a figure.
  • "No credit this month" had to be a real answer. Without it, a month with full rent paid looks the same as a month someone forgot. The reminder would nag for ever, or it would have to guess. Now the month is marked settled and the ledger shows it.
  • Suggested, not entered by itself. The app could write the credit when the rent leaves the bank. I would not start there. A payment to the landlord is not always rent. A month with no rent is only known when it ends. The press writes payments on real bills. One press a month is cheap, and it keeps a person looking at the ledger.
  • The gravel window asks for more than the rent credit did. Five questions is the most I could cut it to. The fifth, where it went, is the one I would defend. It is the only place the app can learn a cost that never touched a card. It is also the biggest piece of new work, so it could come after the first build.
  • What the frames do not solve. F3, a partner who gave first, has no bill to sit on. I drew it so the gap is visible. It needs the list of frame G, or it has no home. The rent's hidden half, the note under frame F, is shown as a fact and not fixed.
  • What the render showed. At desk width every frame fits. On a 375 phone the page itself does not scroll sideways. The desk frames scroll inside their own box there, and the phone frames draw whole. On the phone the ledger leads with what is due, since the table of months does not fit. In dark, the green meter and the amber line stay readable.

The calls that are the owner's

Decided 2026-11-10: where it lives. The card on the customer and a line on Needs you first, then the list. The calls below are still open.

  1. The monthly credit. Suggested with one press, as in frame D? Or entered by itself when exactly the usual rent leaves the bank, with a line that says it was done?
  2. When the reminder speaks. The day the rent leaves the bank, and the last day of a month with no rent? Or a fixed day each month?
  3. What a trade for goods must record. Who, what, the worth and the bill are the floor. Is the ticket photo required or only asked for? Must the goods be placed on a job, the yard or the company before it saves?
  4. Whether the worth counts as a cost. Should gravel placed on a job raise that job's material cost? Should the rent's credited half show as overhead? Both change profit numbers the owner reads today.
  5. A partner who gave first. Is frame F3 a real case, goods taken before any work is done? If not, it is dropped and every arrangement starts from a bill.
  6. Who may see and enter. The same people who can record a payment today? Or the owner only?
  7. The words. The page is called In kind and each row an arrangement. Trade already means plumbing or electrical in the app. Is In kind the word the office would look for?
