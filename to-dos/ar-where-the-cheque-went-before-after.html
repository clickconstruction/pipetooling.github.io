Is this the best we can do? Draft 1 against the 30 days of real rows

Draft 1 answers the literal ask. Checked against what actually happened this month, it misses the question the office actually had.

  • It lists payments; the office thinks in cheques. KCG's one $17,752.65 deposit is six rows, one of them $0.30. 38 rows for 27 cheques.
  • A move reads as unrelated rows. Today's fix is three lines far apart: Lober's off #878, Lober's onto #650, Loberg off #650. Nothing says the $5,622.49 is now back with nowhere to go.
  • The question came as a search. "$6,077.51, where is it?" A third tab is one more place to look. The search should answer from every list and say where it went.
  • The dangerous rows are buried. A cheque the bank failed while it's still paying a bill, or one taken off a job and never put back, should sit on top, not scroll by as history.
  • "Mine" and "By" can't be trusted yet. 15 of 38 rows have no name: the job history records who, but not for every path that applies a payment. A Mine filter would quietly hide real work.
  • A table on a laptop-width column. Six columns squeeze the column the matching work lives in; on a phone it scrolls sideways.

Second pass: is this the best we can do? The first revision against what the app already has

Read against the code before drawing anything more. The first revision is three features; two of them exist, and the third is a new shape for a row that already exists.

  • Half of it shipped last week, from this very cheque. Southern Post's bounce is punch list #40. Since 9/24 a deposit the bank returns while a payment still carries it raises a Needs You card (v2.3795), an email and a push to the office the moment Mercury reports it (v2.3804), a badge on the Pipeline row (v2.3806) and a chip on the AR row. "Needs a look" rule 1 is that card, drawn again.
  • Rule 2 is To match. A cheque taken off a bill has money left, so it is already in To match and in the dashboard's AR count. The only new fact is "it used to be somewhere": that is a trail on the row, not a queue.
  • The answer card is a new shape for an old row. A deposit row already carries a state chip (applied, returned by the bank · Insufficient funds) and, once selected, the bills it paid with a click into the job. The search should surface that row, not draw a card beside it.
  • "Applied lately" is All in a different order. Same cheques, same rows. A card per cheque with bill lines duplicates the selected-row pane. What All lacks is the trail, and an order by last action instead of bank date.
  • "It's fine" wants a table for a case with no decision in it. It goes with the strip.
  • Folding DRF × 4 hides the one that goes wrong. One cheque, one row.
  • The 15 blanks are a path, not a missing column. Every one of the 13 live rows has its history row; the actor is empty. Whatever applied them ran with no signed-in user. A "who" has to read the app there, never blank, and the path has to be found before a column is added.

  • Nothing new to learn. The same two lists, the same rows, the same selected-row pane. One line more per row.
  • The trail is the whole history. Applied, moved, taken off, returned, who, when. A move reads as a move.
  • The search never says "not here" when it is. A miss on To match shows the All rows that match, in place.
  • Cut from the first pass: the answer card, the Needs a look strip and its "It's fine" table, the Applied lately cards, the folding, the Mine filter.

What it takes Smallest first

  1. The search looks in All. Client only. On a To match miss, run the same search over the All rows (the modal already loads them in its All mode) and list the hits under Nothing to match · found in All as ordinary rows.
  2. The trail. One read-only RPC keyed by deposit ids: the payments that carry each deposit, live ones and ones taken off (the deleted-records archive), with the actor from the job history. A kernel (arDepositTrail.ts, unit-tested) turns them into one sentence; the row shows it. Depends on the separate fix that dates a removal when it happens, running in its own session.
  3. All by last action. Client only, over the trail's last-touched time: day headings for the last 30 days, then bank date as today.
  4. Who. Find the path that applies payments with no signed-in user; make the trail say the app for it. Then a "created by" on the payment so the trail stops depending on the history table.

Your calls

  • Order All by last action? Proposed, for the last 30 days, then bank date. Or keep bank date and add a toggle.
  • The trail on To match rows too? Proposed: a cheque back in the queue says where it was.
  • "By the app" or the rule's name? Once the path is found, the trail could say which rule applied it (exact-amount match).
