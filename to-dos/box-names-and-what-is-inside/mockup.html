Box names: a letter and a number

My recommendation: split the one name field into two things. The name is a letter and a number, like R89, and the app fills in the next free one so nobody types it. What is in it is its own line. The app writes that line from the box's parts, and a person can say it better at any time. Words typed into the name are not refused. They move to the line below.

The short of it

Today

  • One field, The stencil, takes any 24 characters.
  • The phone offers A1 on every site, whatever letter the site paints.
  • There is no place to say what is in a box, so people type it into the name.
  • A name is checked against one job. A site with two jobs can hold two R4s.
  • The office page adds every box as a cage. It has no kind to pick.

My suggestion

  • A name is one to three letters and a number from 1 to 999.
  • The app offers the next free name on the site. One press accepts it.
  • What is in it is a line of its own. It fills in from the parts.
  • A name is one of a kind across the whole site.
  • Both pages ask how it sits and remember the last answer.

A new box is one press when the defaults are right.

Your calls six, each with my pick

1 · The shape of a name

My pick: one to three letters, then 1 to 999. No letter after the number, no zeros in front. R7, not R007.

Also possible: allow one letter after the number, so R24B stays a name.

A second thing at the same spot is a second box, and the next free number says so without a rule to learn.

2 · One of a kind across what

My pick: the whole site. A name is offered and checked against every job of the development.

Also possible: one job, as today.

The real site holds the same name twice, on two jobs. A person at the cage reads one site.

3 · Who writes the line

My pick: the app writes it from the parts until a person says it better. It is never asked for.

Also possible: always typed, and empty until someone types it.

Most boxes hold one kind of part, so the app's line is right without a keystroke.

4 · The line on a printed label

My pick: on a loose pile's label only. A cage or a box prints its name and the code.

Also possible: on every label, or on none.

Printed words go stale when a cage is refilled. On a pallet of toilets they never do.

5 · The old names

My pick: the Tidy window, pressed by the office when it suits. Old names work until then.

Also possible: leave them, and hold only new names to the rule.

6 · The site's letter

My pick: the app offers the letter most boxes on the site already carry. The first box on a new site asks once.

Also possible: a letter for each place on the site, such as A for Arena.

One letter a site keeps the next number obvious. A second letter is still one tap away.

The plan four small PRs, each useful alone

1 · MThe nameThe rule, the next free name across the site, words moved out of the name. Add on both pages, with the kind on the office page. Edit's name field. A guard in the database.
2 · MWhat is in itOne new column. The line from the parts, and a person's own words over it. The box's screen, both lists, the search, the scanned page, the Print labels chips.
thenStop and use itAdd ten boxes on a real site. See whether the app's line reads right.
3 · STidy the old namesThe window above. It acts on what is clean and holds the rest with a reason.
4 · SThe line on a labelOnly if call 4 says to print it.

What I would not build: a line that must be typed before a box can be added. A code for each single item on a pallet. A free-text name kept as a setting, since one site that opts out brings every problem back.
