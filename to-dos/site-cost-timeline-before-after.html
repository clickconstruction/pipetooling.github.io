What changes. The left-out jobs list under the chart, each with its cost and the reason it is off the time line. Each job name opens the job. The last line adds the chart's end and the strip, and that sum is the Scoreboard's Cost figure.

What it costs. One grey strip, as tall as its rows. On a site where every job is dated the strip is not drawn and the view reads as it does today. The print gains the same rows under the chart.

  • Why the strip and not a barThe chart is time. A job with no date has no place on its axis, so the strip puts the money beside the chart instead of inventing a day for it. The count line's words stay, and now the dev can act on them.
  • Not a flat bar at the right edgeA bar at the right edge would say the cost landed today. It would also lift the red line's end above the drawn jobs' own sum, which the parity test holds.
  • Not the Job Summary's “No date” bucket in frontThe kernel already draws a job's undated items in a bucket before the first day. Letting the undated jobs in would start the red line above zero and push every job's steps up by a figure that belongs to no day.
