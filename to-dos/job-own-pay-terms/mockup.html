A job's own pay terms

A job can already carry its own Pay calendar, and it wins over its payer's. This page shows what is missing: the way in, and the way to see it. Today on this page is Friday, Nov 13. Every name and number is made up.

The short of it

My recommendation: build the door and the mark. They are two small steps with no database change. Hold the rest until a real contract needs it.

Now

  • A job can carry its own calendar, or none. It wins on every screen that reads one.
  • The press sits on a row only when the payer already has a calendar. No payer has one yet.
  • No row says a job is on its own calendar.

The one door today is inside the Their paper sheet.

My suggestion

  • The job window gets a Pay calendar line. It is always there for the office.
  • A row on its own calendar says This job's own.
  • A job switched off says so, and the words lead back.

One press from the job. One glance to see it.

Which terms win for one bill

A bill has one payer: the GC or the customer, by the job's Bills go to. Only that payer's terms are read. The first rung that holds something wins.

1

This bill's own due day Not built

One bill due on a named date. A retainage release, a dated draw. Step 6 below, on your call.
2

The job's own calendar, or its off switch

Built. Hard to find. Steps 1 to 3 below.
3

The payer's calendar

Built. On the GC's or the customer's profile.
4

The usual guess

The bill date plus how long this payer takes.

Two things sit outside the ladder. A date the customer named, They said, still wins the expected day. And a bill typed to another email has no payer on file, so it reads no calendar at all.
