Signed hours statement: a worker's recorded hours on one job, as a paper they sign

The ask: a print out like a pay report of a contractor's recorded hours on a job, which they sign at the bottom, so that in a case over an unpaid bill the company can prove it paid for specific work on a specific site. Every job, the office job included. The app already holds the rows (each punch has a day, a start, an end, a job and a note, and an approver), the pay report print, the ink-signing page the lien waivers use, and the Legal desk's Evidence tab. What is new is one paper and one window. The hard part is not the paper: it is the span before the app, where the paper can only carry what the worker states.

1Where it opens: the job's Documents tab, and the person's desk

The paper is a job paper, so its first door is the job window's Documents tab beside the contract, the bills and the lien paper. The same window opens the other way round from a person's desk under Hours. The office job is a job like any other, so it needs no door of its own.

4Three kinds of worker, one paper

The rows come from three places and read as one table; the owner took the Record column off the paper on 2026-10-29, so nothing on it says whether a row was a punch, a typed day, a quick add or a sheet line. The app keeps that, and the office reads it in the window. An employee's or a helper's hours are punches and typed days on the clock. A sub's hours are the sheet's lines (units × hours a unit), never a punch, and the sheet's own payments with their memo. A salaried person's days are the schedule's blocks, which the app marks as such; the pay is the salary, so the paper carries the days and no rate.
