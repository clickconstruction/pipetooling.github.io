4. Is this the best we can do?

  • Why not a status? A seventh status touches every trigger, report and send-back. Collections is a flag on a billed job and has worked for a year; this is the same shape, one step further.
  • Why not the Legal desk's write-down? That is an agreed reduction with a Stripe credit note — the customer said yes to a smaller number. Here nobody agreed to anything; the bill stays what it was, and the books should show it was given up, not shrunk.
  • Why not delete the bill or Mark Paid? Both lie to the ledger, the customer's history and the accountant.
  • Why inside Collections and not a seventh section? The owner asked for a space in Collections. A new section means a new strip chip, map pin colour, section pref, deep link and header; a band at the bottom of Collections costs none of that and keeps "given up" one scroll from "still chasing".
  • Why job-level, not bill-level? Collections is job-level; the row the office reads is the job's; a job with one live bill and one given-up bill is rare and the reason can name the bill. Bill-level is the fallback if the first month proves otherwise.
  • What this adds beyond the ask: the accountant's bad-debt list (a year's given-up dollars with the reasons — a deduction the office has never been able to name), the self-clearing stamp when money turns up, and the Legal desk hand-off so a matter the firm holds is not given up behind the firm's back.
  • The owner's four calls: the word on the button (Give up on it… / Write it off… / Uncollectible); who may do it (the Collections managers, or a dev and the controller only); whether Stripe's own invoice is marked uncollectible too (closes the pay link; Stripe still accepts a late payment); and whether a customer with a given-up bill gets a payment-terms nudge (Deposit required) on the next job.
