The Board is now the top of To do. This is the same day's work drawn four times, each with the Board in place. B, A and D build on each other: B is the start of A, and A is the first half of D. Taunya picked the direction of B, and B is drawn here after one round of asking whether it is the best we can do. Nothing here is built.

Today. The Board takes most of the screen, and it should: it is the picture of the month. The list is pinned in what is left under it.
  • The list is its own scroller, about three rows tall. On a smaller window it starts below the bottom of the screen.
  • The Board orders the papers by stage. The list orders the same papers by day. One desk says it twice, in two orders.
  • Ten rows read the same and carry the same button. No row carries dollars.
  • The held paper reads "the property record is not complete". Its one question is a press away.

A · The list follows the Board. One page, as in B. Under the Board the list is drawn in the Board's own order: one group for each bin, under the bin's own name.
  • Press a bin's name and the page goes to its group. The picture and the list say one thing, once.
  • Papers that share one act fold into one row with a total and one button. Sixteen rows become seven.
  • Tomorrow's papers come first. Everything later folds under one line.
  • The rows are B's rows: dollars, the notice line, the one question.

D · One run. The Board's first tile is the run: every paper due by tomorrow. Under the Board the list becomes three steps in order: get every paper right, print them all at once, then mail and file them together.
  • Step 1 is the tile's own red line, written out. The seven papers that will not make tomorrow, and one flagged for the attorney, become six questions.
  • The print stays locked until every question is settled or its paper is left out. One stuck paper never holds the run.
  • The chips move on the Board as the run moves. Press the three states below and watch In the run.
  • After the print, a paper whose record changed turns amber on the Board and says what changed.
