Permits: skim first, then dig in

Today the tab is a ledger. It is good for finding a permit and slow for answering one. On the live tab on 2026-11-10, 163 permits wait on a look and none has a verdict. This page draws a first step that takes a few seconds a permit and a second step that takes as long as the permit deserves. Then it draws the builder behind the name, with a mark and our own contacts. Every name, street and number here is made up.

The two steps

Step one is a yes or a no on one screen. Step two is the research the ledger already does, on the few that got a yes. A permit is always in exactly one of four places, and the counts on the step strip add up to every permit the feed brought in.

what the card carries, top to bottom
  1. The look. A street view of the address beside a map with the office and its 25 mile ring. Added on the owner's look at the first frames.
  2. What. The kind of work, the size and the declared value in the biggest type on the card.
  3. Where. The street, and the miles from the office on the map.
  4. When. Applied or issued, and how many days ago. An application is the early signal.
  5. Who. The builder, their mark, our bids with them, and how many permits they pulled this year. A tap opens everything public about them (frame L1), and the small Google press searches the name.
  6. Plumber. None yet, on file with no name, or the plumber's name.
  7. The property. The county's owner of record and what stands on the lot now. A tap opens everything public about the address (frame L2).

The pictures are the app's own. The job window already shows a street view of a job's address, read through the app's own function with the key kept on the server. The On site map already draws satellite. The card reuses both. The date on the street view matters: on a new build an old picture of an empty lot is the signal, and where the car never drove the tile shows satellite and says so.

Nothing is typed on the skim. Today every verdict needs a typed reason, which is right for a decision and wrong for a glance. A pass offers four one-tap reasons in the dark bar after the card has gone. Tapping one is optional. The bar is also the undo.

A permit skimmed by one person is skimmed for everyone. The queue is the company's, like the ledger's verdicts are today. Each answer keeps the name and the day.

how the queue is counted
  • To skim is every building permit with no answer and no plumbing permit on it, however old. Today the list reads a 14, 30 or 90 day window, so an unanswered permit drops out of sight when its day passes.
  • The deck keeps today's two filters as its own: commercial only, and $25k and up or not declared. A permit under the filter is not in any count.
  • A permit the feed pairs with a plumbing permit never enters the deck. It goes to Closed as Has a plumber, and it still counts on the builder's page.
  • Skim, Kept, Pursuing and Closed add up to every permit above the filter. The step strip shows all four.

Is this the best we can do?

Asked twice. First of the plan while the frames were being drawn, which changed the six things below. Then of the page once it was rendered, which is the box after them.

  1. The first draft had three answers. Pass, Later and Pursue now. Pursue on a glance skips the dig in that makes a call worth making. The card has two answers and a small Open it now for the rare permit that cannot wait.
  2. The first draft kept the typed reason. The ledger refuses a verdict with no reason. On 163 cards that is 163 sentences, and it is why nothing has been answered. The reason became four taps in the bar, optional, after the card is gone.
  3. The first draft was one card per permit. Then the live counts showed 26 names behind 70 of the 163. The stack came from that.
  4. The first draft read a window. A deck that forgets a permit after 30 days cannot promise that every permit is closed out. The deck has no window, and the first day gets its own card.
  5. The first draft swiped on a desk. Dragging a card with a mouse is slower than a key and reads as a toy. The desk has keys.
  6. The first draft put the mark on the skim card as a press. A mark is a judgment about a company, with a reason. It moved to the builder and the dig in. The skim only shows it.

The owner's look, 2026-11-10. Good as drawn, and better with a map and a street view. Both are now on every skim card (frames A to C and I), bigger on the desk (frame F) and side by side on the dig in (frame H), with a tap to open either one big (frame B4). The first draft had only a small map strip, which said where and nothing about what is there. Two tiles of equal size were chosen over a street view with a small map in its corner, since the miles are read as often as the picture. A swipe between the two pictures was not drawn: it would fight the card's own swipe.

The owner's second look, 2026-11-10. Much better, and two more asks: a tap on Who or on the building that shows as much public information as there is, and a small Google button beside a name. Both are section L. The first draft made the reader go to the dig in for any of this, which is one step too late: the facts that decide a keep are the ones on these sheets. They are sheets over the card, not pages, so the deck is never left and the two answers stay under the thumb.

What the render showed. Checked in the browser at desk width and at 375. The live deck in frame A answers by button, by drag and by key, takes a reason, undoes, and ends on the caught-up card. Three things were fixed after seeing them: the stack's blue line broke into two columns, a dashed box sat on top of the address on two cards, and the triage table used K for both keep and move. One thing stays as it is on purpose: the desk card in frame F is short enough that the Up next column, not the card, is where the eye goes first. That may be right. It is the owner's to judge on the frame.

Still weak, and said plainly. The card can only show what the feed and the county give. On the live tab 137 of the 163 waiting permits are applications whose work reads only Commercial project, 70 declare no value, and the history word reads new name on 162. The first weeks of the skim will be made on the street, the size, the owner of record and the miles. The card gets better as builders collect permits, marks and people, which is the reason the builder work is in the same plan and not a later one. The other weak spot is the swipe itself: a drag that starts at the left edge of a phone is the browser's own Back. The drag starts on the card, the card sits inside the page's gutter, and the buttons never go away.

The owner's calls

  1. The two answers. Right keeps for a closer look and left passes, under the words Keep and Pass. Or other words?
  2. A reason on a pass. Optional and one tap, as drawn. Or required, or none at all?
  3. The backlog. Skim all 163, or start from the last 14 days and pass the older ones in one press as Too old?
  4. What reaches the deck. Commercial only, $25k and up or not declared, no houses, nothing that already has a plumber. That is today's default list. Keep it?
  5. One queue or one each. As drawn, a permit skimmed by one person is done for everyone. Or does each estimator skim their own?
  6. Where a pursued permit lands. Today the lead goes on the Jobs to GC deck. A general contractor who needs a plumber reads more like the Customers deck. Which one, or by the builder's kind?
  7. What Avoid does. As drawn it is a red word on the card. Or should an Avoid builder's permits pass by themselves, counted and listed under Closed?
  8. Who may mark a builder. The office and every estimator, as with verdicts. Or the office alone?
