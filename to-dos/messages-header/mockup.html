Punch list #151 · Messages

A tighter top for Messages, with a jump strip like the Pipeline's

Three ways to draw the top of /messages. Every frame scrolls on its own. The strip sticks under the top and lights the lane you are in. Press a lane on the strip to jump to it. Desktop frames are drawn light and phone frames dark. Dark swaps them. The number on each frame's caption is the distance from the title's top to the New heading, measured in this drawing. Every person, subject and send here is made up.

0 Today, as it stands

Five rows of chrome before the first person: the title, the kind chips, Show, the search box, and the week's four cards. Then the first lane's heading and its sentence. Measured on the page as the dev on 2026-10-23: from the title's top to the New heading 319px at 1280 wide and 480px on a phone, and to the first person 365px and 641px, so on a phone the first person is below the fold.

A The strip, and the cards fold to a line

The smallest change. The title, the chips, Show and the search box stay as they are. The week's four cards fold to one line of figures, each figure still a door to its rows, and the line's name, The last 7 days, opens the four. The strip goes under that line and sticks. What it saves. Little. The cards were 125px tall on a desktop and 219px on a phone; the line is one row and the strip is 56px, so the two nearly cancel: about 30px on a desktop and 70px on a phone. Is this the best we can do? No. It gives the table of contents and nothing else moves. Four rows of chrome still stand over the strip.

B Three short rows, then the strip

The title shares its row with a short search box. The kind chips share theirs with Show. The week is one line of figures under them, each a door, its name opening the four cards. The strip sticks under that. Each lane's heading becomes a band in the strip's color, as the Pipeline's sections are, with the count in a pill. A lane's sentence sits behind a small ? on its heading: press it and the sentence opens under the name.

What it saves. About a third of the desktop chrome, and the lanes' sentences. On a phone the chips still wrap to three rows and the figures to four, so the gain there is small. Is this the best we can do? It is the honest middle: nothing goes behind a press except the four cards, which the figures line already says in full. The chips are the one thing left that wants room.

C One row recommended

The Pipeline's shape pressed into one bar that sticks: the title, a picker for the kind, Mine · Everyone, a search glass, the five lanes with their counts, 7 days and one ?. 7 days opens the week's four cards under the bar, and they stay open on your browser until you close them, like Show. The glass opens the search box under the bar. The ? opens one card that says what each lane holds. The lanes' headings are bands. Where the row is too narrow for everything, the lanes drop to the bar's second line.

What it saves. Nearly all of it: about 80px from the title to New on a desktop, a quarter of today, and about 175px on a phone, a third of today, so the first person is on the screen when the page opens. From about 1000px wide the row holds everything; narrower, the lanes take the bar's second line and the top is about 120px. Is this the best we can do? Are we being straightforward?
