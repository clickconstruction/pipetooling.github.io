Permits: a Market page. Who builds, and who to call.

Prospects → Permits · punch list #405 · the owner's go is given, nothing is built yet · every company, person, address and number on this page is made up, as on every mock-up here. The live numbers were read on Nov 19, 2026 and given to the owner. Nothing was written to the live data.

The idea in one line. Today the only way in is to ask a company to let us bid. This page says which companies to ask, why now, who to ask for, and whether the companies we already bid actually build. It leads with a call list of five names a week, sized to the one person who makes the calls. The charts come last.

What a first read of the data found

  • Few of the companies we bid pull permits here. The companies we priced for in two years, and the names on the permits, barely overlap. The match is by exact name, so the real overlap is larger. It is still the finding to explain.
  • Hundreds of names pull three or more commercial permits a year in San Antonio. We have a bid on record with a handful.
  • A San Antonio application comes weeks before its permit. That is the window to call in.
  • In Austin the plumber's permit lands within days of the building permit. By then the job is gone. The permit is news about the builder, not the job.
  • In Austin a few plumbers hold a large share of the jobs, and some builders use a different plumber on every job. Those builders are the open doors.
  • The office has marked no builder Good or Avoid yet. The page starts without that judgement and gains it as marks are said.

Is this the best we can do?

A page that leads with a call list my pick

What is drawn above. It wins because the question is "who do we ask?", and a list of twelve names with reasons answers it. Charts alone would be interesting and change nothing.

Grow the Builders page instead of adding one

Builders already lists every name with its permits and our bids. It lost as the home for this because it is a place to look a name up. It holds over a thousand rows in San Antonio and has no opinion. Market is the opinion, and every row on it opens the builder's sheet, so nothing is kept twice.

A Monday note with the top five, and no page

The strongest objection to any page: almost none of the permits that arrived since August were skimmed. People do not open this tab. A note that arrives says more than a page that waits. It lost as the first build only because the note needs the same list behind it. I would send the top block as a Monday email as the second step.

What asking the question changed

  • No score. A first draft ranked builders by one number. A number hides its reasons and invites argument. The tags are the reasons.
  • The bid table moved up to second. It is the only part that says where to stop spending estimating time, and it needs no research to build.
  • No map. The Permits tab has one. A second map here would repeat it.
  • The plumber box is drawn only where the city names plumbers. In San Antonio it would be empty.
  • A name leaves the list when it is answered. Otherwise the same names sit there every week and the list stops being read.
  • Five names, not twelve. The owner makes the calls himself. A list he can finish in a week gets finished. A longer one gets skipped.

Decided, and still yours to say

  • Who makes the calls: the owner. His word, 2026-11-19. So the list is five names a week, and the Monday note goes to him.
  • Go: built by someone else and shown to the owner the next day. His word, 2026-11-19. Steps one and two are the target. The card beside this page holds each step.
  • May an agent look up the top names once? About 150 names a city, a minute each, public sources only, nobody contacted, every fact with its source and a person's check before it reads as checked.
  • The order of the build: the bid table and the clock, which need no research. Then the call list with its reasons. Then Ask for, once the lookups exist.
  • Which city? San Antonio has the history and the applications. I would start there and let Austin follow.
