The newest press on a fact is its state, the same way the newest answer on a name is. Nothing is edited or deleted, so who said what stays on record. A newer lookup of the same fact replaces the older one on the row and starts as not checked.

3. How the lookups get written, and who may write

  • A fact: only a dev account may add one, and only under its own name. The table's own rule holds that, not the screen. Nobody may change or delete one.
  • A press (Checked, Wrong, Put back): anyone who reads Permits, as themself. That is the office and the estimators, the same people who answer the call list.
  • Reading: the same people. Closed to the public, to a read-only training account, to a twin and to an archived account, as every new table is.
  • The run: one agent session, signed in to the app as the dev, adds the rows through the app's own connection. The call-list answers table was proven the same way. No new function, no nightly job, no new dev-seat verb, no paid service.
  • Before anything is written, the 25 names and every fact with its page are put in one list for you, outside the repo. The rows written are that list and nothing else.

4. What the run reads, and what it never keeps

  • Which 25: the first 25 names on San Antonio's call list, in the list's own order, once today's order change is live. A name that reads as one person is skipped and never searched.
  • Reads: the company's own website, its Google Maps business listing, a state licence or registration record, a trade association's member page. Public business pages only.
  • Keeps: the kind, one office phone, one person with a title. Each with its page and day.
  • Never keeps: a mobile or home number, a home address, a personal email, a social profile, or a person named only on a social site. No email at all in this step.
  • Never guesses: a kind is kept only when a page says it in plain words. A person is kept only when a business page names them in a role that takes bids.
  • Contacts nobody. No form is filled, no call is made, no mail is sent.

5. Is the app's own Google lookup reused?

As a source, yes. As the place to keep these facts, no.

  • The skim card's Who pulled it already asks Google Places for a company and keeps the answer a month (permit_lookups). It keeps one answer a name and writes over it. It has no page for each fact and no check. Its match can be loose, which may be another company.
  • So the run may read that answer for a name. It takes the office phone from it only on an exact match, and cites the Google Maps listing as the page. A loose match is not used.
  • That is about 25 asks on the Google key the app already has. Nothing is copied from it by itself, then or later.

6. What a looked-up fact may do to the list

  • Tag the name. Looked up: a design firm is an outline until checked, then a filled tag like the others.
  • Order, only once checked. A checked builder leads with the names that say they build. A checked design firm, permit service or trade waits behind the other companies. A fact nobody checked moves nothing.
  • Never remove. Every name stays on the list until you answer it.
  • Never the office's word. Where the office has said a kind on the builder's sheet, that tag shows and that kind orders. The looked-up kind still sits in the block, with its page.

7. The build, once you say yes

  1. The two tables, alone. One migration, merged and pushed before any screen names them.
  2. The screen. The block as drawn, the tag, the order rule, the guide's lines, with tests.
