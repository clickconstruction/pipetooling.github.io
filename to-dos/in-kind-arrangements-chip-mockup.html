The in kind chip beside a customer's name

The deal has a home now: a card on the customer's page, a ledger, and the In kind page. What is missing is the reminder everywhere else. On the Customers list, on a job and in the Bill window, a customer who pays in kind looks like any other, and the open balance still reads as money owed.

The owner's ask: where in the app should a user be able to see the in kind balance due or credit for a customer, such as next to a customer's name? This page draws one small chip that travels with the name, and the four places it earns its room. Calloway Holdings pays $19,875.40 of finished work with a rent credit. Pecan Creek Gravel gave gravel before its job was billed. The dashed copper outline marks what is new.

Where it goes, and where it does not

Build first

The Customers list, the customer's page, a job and the Bill window

Frames B, C and D. These are the places with no sign of the deal today. D is the one that prevents a mistake.

Build second

Who owes what, the Dashboard's receivables card, the Bridge's Collect pile

Frame E. This is the sweep already listed as left on the card. The chip and its two numbers give all three one shared piece.

Leave it off

What the customer sees, the crew's screens, search and Messages

A bill, the portal and a statement are the customer's own papers. Crew screens show no money owed. In search and Messages it would be one more chip that changes nothing a person does there.

A correction to what I said in chat. I listed Accounts Receivable as a place for the chip. That page is the window that matches deposits to bills. It has no list of customers and what each owes, so there is no row for a chip to sit on. It keeps its In kind link and nothing more.

Is this the best we can do?

  • Fix the numbers before the chip. A chip beside a name does nothing if the total above it still counts the deal as money owed. In B and E the split total is the real change, and the chip is its label. If only one thing ships, it should be the totals.
  • A chip everywhere the name appears lost. The name shows on the schedule, in search, in Messages, on dispatch. A chip in each would be seen so often it would stop being read. It stays where money or billing is the subject.
  • An icon with no words lost. Nobody knows what a handshake means until they have been told once, and a tooltip does not exist on a phone.
  • The amount in every chip lost on a job. Beside the name on one job, $9,375.40 reads as that job's balance. The short form goes there.
  • Green or red both lost. Green says paid and red says late, and the deal is neither. Quiet blue for on schedule and amber for a month due are the two states a person acts on.
  • Guessing at the bill lost. D2 could default to money, and the landlord gets a late notice. It could default to the arrangement, and a real bill is never chased. Asking costs one press on a rare customer.
  • What I am least sure of: the strip in D1. It may be one line too many on a job window that is already full. The chip plus the question in D2 may be enough.

The calls that are the owner's

  1. The amount on the Customers list. Drawn with the amount. The word alone is quieter, and the amount is one press away.
  2. A new bill for a customer who pays in kind. Drawn as a question that must be answered. The other choice is to default to money and only offer the arrangement.
  3. Owes money on the Customers list. Drawn leaving out a customer whose whole balance is scheduled in kind. A customer with both kinds stays in, counted for the money part only.
  4. Who sees the chip. Drawn for the office. The deal's lines are the office's to read today, so a superintendent or a crew lead would see no chip.
  5. The strip on the job window (D1). Keep it, or let the chip and the Bill window carry it.
