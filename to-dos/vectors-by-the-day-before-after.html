The owner's ask: a view that shows people every day, and whether their contribution to jobs was profitable or unprofitable, every day, every week and every month. Today the Bridge's Vectors panel answers it for one pay week at a time; People → Review for one picked period. Neither draws a day, and nothing adds up a month. The after is one grid under Vectors with three zooms, fed by the kernel Vectors already runs.

The past moves. A job's rate is its contract ÷ (hours to date ÷ % complete). Every new hour and every % update re-prices every day ever worked on that job, so a green March turns red when a job runs long in September. That is the honest reading, not a bug — the grid is always as of today, and the stamp says so. A day whose sign flipped in the last 7 days carries a small ↻ (not drawn here) so a change on the grid is a change on a job, not a surprise.

Is this the best we can do? what I questioned

keptIt lives under Vectors on the Bridge, not as a third view on People → Review.

To-do #15 is retiring People → Review into the Bridge once the two tables agree; a new view on the surface being retired would be a second copy of the same math. Vectors already has the per-session inputs, the earned rate per job and the wage per person. The name still opens Review's per-person panel for the drilldown until #15 lands.

owner's callRead recorded time, not approved only.

Vectors is approved-only, and approval runs days to weeks behind. A daily grid on approved hours would be blank for this week and thin for last week — the days you most want to read. Job costing switched to recorded time in v2.3178 for the same reason. Proposal: recorded by default, dashed until approved, with the toggle in the bar. The pay-week figure in Vectors' own table stays approved-only, so payroll and the grid never disagree on what was paid.

kept, and made visibleA red day is nearly always a job's verdict, not a person's.

Under the earned convention every hour on a job earns the same rate, so a day goes red for exactly one reason: the job's rate per hour is under the person's wage — because the job was priced low, has no contract price, or has run past its expected hours. The same job colors everyone on it. So the cell's click (drawn at right) names the job and puts the rate beside the wage, and the row's Why column counts red days by job, so "6 red days, 5 on J1044" reads as a pricing problem in one glance. Without that column the grid would invite the wrong conclusion.

droppedA daily score, rank or streak.

Days are noisy: a 2-hour callback, a rain day, a drive. Ranking people on them would reward whoever drew the well-priced job. The week and month columns are the reading to act on; days are for finding where a week went wrong. And by the owner's standing decision no financial number reaches a teammate, so this stays a dev surface and never becomes a scoreboard on anyone's Dashboard.

droppedThree tabs (a day list, a week list, a month list).

One grid with week subtotals and a month total answers all three questions without leaving the screen; the Weeks and Months zooms exist for the longer look, not as separate pages.

keptOffice and bid days stay on the grid, grey.

A field person's office day costs a wage and earns nothing here; hiding it would make their week look better than it was. Grey with the hours says what happened without pretending to judge it. Pure office people are not rows — nothing in the earned convention reads their day, and the Day book already does.

owner's callSalaried people.

Vectors carries an isSalary flag and reads no wage for them. Review prices a salaried day through the workday template (salariedEffectiveHours). The grid should do the same or leave their cells grey with the hours; drawn here as priced.

Where it plugs in

Exists: src/lib/bridge/vectors.ts — buildVectors resolves one pay week from sessions (person, day, hours, job, approved / pending), ratePerHourByJob and wages; earnedRevenue.ts — the rate and the assumed-half list; loadBridgeVectors.ts — the loader for the Bridge's 8-week window; BridgeVectorsPanel.tsx — the table; People → Review's per-person day rows as the drilldown.
New: a pure vectorDays.ts beside vectors.ts: buildVectorDays(sessions, rates, wages, range) → one cell per person × day (earned, labor, contribution, hours, pending, guessed, jobs with rate vs wage), and foldVectorDays(cells, 'week' | 'month'); the loader's window widened to the zoom (a year of sessions for Months — the rates already use lifetime hours); the zoom row and the grid in the panel; the cell popover; the Why column.

The train

  1. Kernel + Days zoom — vectorDays.ts with tests (a day on a priced job, on a no-price job, on an assumed-half job, a pending day, an office day, a salaried day); the grid for one month under Vectors; the month stepper.
  2. Weeks and Months — the fold, the two zooms, the loader's wider window.
