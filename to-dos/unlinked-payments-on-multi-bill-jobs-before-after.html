After Three rules — pick one, and one kernel answers for the invoice, the letter and the lien

Each panel is job 273 under that rule: what the three bills print, what the letter claims, and what the office sees. The dangerous direction is overstating — the letter and the § 53.056 notice are legal instruments — so each panel says which way it can be wrong.

RULE 1

Oldest bill first

Job-level money fills open bills in sequence, never past a bill's amount. No new data. The surplus shows once, on the job.

Billapplieddue #…0916$13,420 of $38,780$0paid #…1718$3,500$0paid #…1716$665$0paid On the job$21,195 unappliedprepaid
Each dollar counts once. The Exhibit A on #…0916 lists $13,420 applied, not four raw payments; the other two bills show their own share.
One sub-question inside this rule: "oldest bill first" by the bills' order, or by matching each payment's date to the bills open on that day? On 273 both give the same answer; on a job where a bill is raised after a payment, they differ. The kernel needs the owner to say which — bill order is simpler and is what the office describes.

RULE 2

Single-bill jobs only

Attribute only when there is exactly one bill (v2.3515 already does). On a multi-bill job, job-level money lands on no bill and every document says so.

RULE 3

Make the office link it

A Needs You item works the pile down; the invoice and the letter count only linked money — once, on the bill it was linked to.

Whichever is chosen, one kernel — the invoice's payment history, paymentsAppliedToInvoice, and the lien claim — must give the same answer, or the two halves of this window drift apart again.
