Link a customer to its names on permits

The owner: our names for customers may not match the permits, so let a person link one of our customers to a name the city lists, and see what the two have in common. The app suggests, with its reasons. A person ties. Nothing is tied on a likeness alone.

Is this the best we can do?

Not by itself. Three things from the data shaped the second look.

  1. A look-alike is a question, never an answer. One of the three was a different company with one word in common. So the press says what it does, Same company or Not them, and a No is kept so the name is not offered again.
  2. The name is the weakest evidence. Compare puts ours beside the city's and marks what is truly shared: an address you bid to them, a zip, a phone. A shared word alone is drawn as the least of them.
  3. One customer at a time leaves most of them unlinked. Nobody opens forty bids to tie forty names. The same row, drawn as one list for every customer, lets one sitting do it. That is direction 2.

What is built, and what is new

The link itself already exists. It is the answer table that ships with the bid's name question, where works for, every permit is a tie. Linking a customer to a name writes the same row from a different door. No new table.

New, small: a pure kernel that grades two names and says why (the same name, one begins the other, a shared telling word, one letter apart, initials), with the common trade words counted for little. A read of the names on permits. The sheet. The list.

Never: a tie made by the app on a likeness. Only the exact same name is joined without a person, as it is today.
