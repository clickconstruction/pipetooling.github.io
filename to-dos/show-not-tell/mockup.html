Show it, don't say it: seven shapes for the places the app still tells

"We want the user to see processes that they feel like they can understand and trust instead of wondering where text comes from." — Taunya, 2026-10-30

Eight read-only scans walked every area of the app (the Jobs hub twice, Bids, GC mode and Messages, the office's day and Settings, the phone, People and Documents, and everything a stranger reads or receives) and wrote up 131 places where a sentence stands where a shape should. Every one of them wants one of seven shapes, and every shape already lives somewhere in the app since punch list #214 drew the Legal desk's four. This page teaches each shape once with its strongest before-and-after pairs, then lists every other place that takes it, so a yes can be a yes to a shape as much as to a screen.

How to read a pair. The dashed frame on the left is the screen as it draws today, its words close to verbatim. The solid frame on the right is the same screen with the shape drawn. The lines under them say what the shape gains and what rule it keeps, and the blue box is the second pass: what the first render got wrong and what moved.

How to answer. Every pair and every row has Build · Later · Drop and a line for a note. On the claude.ai copy of this page the picks save as you press them and come straight to me; on the repo copy they stay in this browser, and Copy my picks puts them on the clipboard to paste into a message. A pick on a pair is a pick on that screen; a shape's Build all picks the pair and every row under it.

1A date against today

A time said in words is a position left undrawn. Expected Nov 8 · in 9d, No field work in 59 days, quiet 5d, No round Thu, Oct 29, Late 6× · median 18m: each is a count from a day the screen never shows, so the reader cannot see what started the clock, how near the limit sits, or whether the pattern is every Thursday or every day. The shape is a strip of days with today marked and the counting day labelled, or three dates on one track against today.

Lives in the app already as the Legal desk's Timeline strip (timelineStrip.ts, v2.6107), the Lien desk's Deadlines track, the Lien window's Windows calendar (v2.3815) and Quickfill's Mark history tiles. 24 of the 131 places take it; every one is drawn here, the six that matter most first.
