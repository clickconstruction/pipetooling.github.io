What this step is

The second letter after a notice, sent certified by counsel. The desk offers it from day 10 after the packet and calls it overdue from day 14.

The usual move
Send letter twothe leader presses it, drafted on the notice and run like any notice
Why a notice is here

Letter two is due and the courtesy notice could not go. A reminder may never name a lien day the law would not allow, so the courtesy waits when the affidavit is inside a week, the property is a homestead, no work month is approved, or the window closed. A notice is also here while a letter two is drafting, awaiting the leader, in the run or sent.

You may instead
Send a courtesy noticeThe owner calledHold itOpen the notice

The courtesy goes only once its hold has cleared. Each press is on the row and on the notice's footer.

It closes by itself when

The bill is paid, the GC authorizes direct pay in writing, the owner's call is recorded, they answer, or the affidavit is filed.

What changes and what does not

  • Changes: a pressed rung draws a step brief under the rail in place of the Showing line and the one-line rule; the rows re-sort into On this step and On other steps at full strength, and the fade goes. The Notices, Affidavits and Retainage rails get briefs of their own in the same shape.
  • Does not: any rule, any press, who approves what. The alternatives are the row's and the footer's existing presses, named. A second press on the rung, or Every step, clears the sort.
  • Size: two small PRs. The first is the Reminders brief and the re-sort. The second writes the briefs for the other three rails.
