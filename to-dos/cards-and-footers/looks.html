Other looks, and how an approved one is offered

The card and the footer are code now, so a second look is a handful of colours over the same layouts. This page draws three candidates beside the look in use. Then it draws how the owner approves a look and how a person picks one.

Everyone here is made up. The layouts do not move between looks: only the ground, the accent, the ink on the accent and the mark change. The yellow hand is the logo's picture recoloured for this page.

2Can it be read?

How strongly each pair of colours stands apart. Small words need 4.5 to 1 or better. Large words need 3 to 1.

Navy and yellow reads best. Yellow on navy is the strongest pair on the page, and navy words on a yellow panel are strong too.

White words on orange are the weakest pair, in the look in use as well. They are the logo's two words on the card front, which are large, so they pass.

The yellow hand changes the logo. It is not a colour swap on a card. It is a second version of the company's mark, and the designer should draw it.

5The rules a look must pass

The same bones. A look changes colours and art, never where a thing sits. Then every look fits the same long names and the same lines, and one test covers them all.

It reads. Every pair of colours in it passes the numbers in section 2. A test holds that, so a look that cannot be read never reaches the owner's card.

A short list. Three looks at most are on at once. A customer who gets two cards from one company should know they are from one company.

The footer and the card go together. A person picks a look, not a footer and a card apart.

The owner approves, a person picks, the office can pick for a teammate. The team's batch prints each person in their own look.
