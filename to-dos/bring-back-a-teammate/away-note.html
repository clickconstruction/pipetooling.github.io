Is this the best we can do? The second pass

  • The countdown was the biggest thing on the screen. Three years in days reads as more than a thousand, a number that hardly moves. A thin bar under it says how far along they are, and the note stays above it, so the first thing they read is the team's words.
  • The location press asks before it asks. The browser's own permission prompt only shows after they press Use my location, never on open. The miles are worked out on the phone from the office's map pin and never saved.
  • Seconds were cut. A ticking seconds digit on a three-year wait looks like a timer for a bomb. Days, hours and minutes.
  • The note shows who wrote it as "The team". Not the name of whoever pressed Save, since that person may be gone in three years too.
  • The end-of-countdown alert is off by default. A return date three years out is a hope, not a promise. The office turns it on when they mean it.
  • The comparison squeezed B. The first render gave column A most of the table and wrapped B one word a line. The two options now get equal columns.
  • Email me my note says the same thing for every address. If the team left you a note, it is on its way. Otherwise anyone could type an email and learn who has left.
  • I would have picked B; you picked A. The screen they see is the same. A live login for three years has four doors into company data, so A ships with a lock on each and a probe that tries every table, view, RPC and file as a sealed account before anyone is sealed. B stays the fallback if the probe cannot pass.
