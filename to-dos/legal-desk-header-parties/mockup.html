· Is this the best we can do? The second pass

What the first draft had, and what changed after asking
  1. The first draft kept the payer's office under the name. On a GC account that reads as the building's address, and it is not. The line under the name is now the job's address, and the payer's city sits in the Contract cell. Every other number in the head is about the job too.
  2. The first draft drew four cells, a fourth for Click, so the row read as the lien chain. Cut: a box that never changes is noise, and the chain is already said by the Contract cell's general contractor, which is what makes Click a subcontractor for the lien clock.
  3. The first draft opened a second map. A street-map window won the first pass on the strength of the precinct line. Weighed again: no record carries a precinct today, the office's court map is not drawn, and when it is, its shapes can be laid on the county map the desk already has. The venue press now opens the desk's Map on the county with the case held, and the county card gains the two places and the rule lines. The window stays drawn above as V2 for the comparison.
  4. The owner's name as the record spells it. The appraisal district writes CP RETAIL HOLDINGS LLC in capitals, and the first draft cased it. Left as written: the lien affidavit names the owner the way the record does, and the desk should have one spelling, not two. The quiet line, in the desk's own words, says what the record is.
  5. The contract cell names the signer. The agreement's signer is already on the Paper tab's coverage (signer_printed_name, the co-signer when there is one), so the cell can say subcontract signed by R. Vance and not only signed. When no one signed, it says no contract, a sworn account, the words the Account tab's Basis column uses.
  6. The venue cell says two counties, in one line. The first draft said only the property's county. The packet's rule already gives both places and the printed packet already says so; when both are the same county the cell says where the work was done and where they live, which is its own fact.
  7. The venue sentence's firm words are not lost. No counties are colored yet, so the firm takes every account is the firm segment's hover title today and stays there; the amber states keep their doors on the firm segment and gain the same amber on the venue cell. Two cells can be amber for one gap; they open the same record.
  8. A sixth state was missing: a payer with no customer record. Added above; its door is the desk's existing Link a customer.
  9. Cut: a cell for the tenant or the brand (on no record the desk reads); a courthouse pin (the court map holds shapes, not courthouses, and a guessed address would be the one guessed thing on this desk); a fourth place for the firm's office (the firm covers counties, not a point); an inline fold under the strip for the map (it needs the height of the four question cards and pushes them off the screen on every press).
