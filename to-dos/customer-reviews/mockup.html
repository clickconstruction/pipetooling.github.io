2 The Google ask: the three ways, side by side

A · Everyone, after the direct review Recommended

Google's policy, in its own shape: the same ask in the same words to every customer who answers.

The five-star page leads with the Google ask. The lower page leads with what could we have done better and the office's call, then the same Google line under it.

The cost: a 2-star customer may post 2 stars. The counter: the office saw it first and calls the same day.

Build: one constant, GOOGLE_ASK_AT = 'everyone'.

B · Only at five stars, as asked

Google's content policy lists among what merchants may not do: “discourage or prohibit negative reviews, or selectively solicit positive reviews from customers.”

The page does what the owner described. The lower page has no Google line. The reports of what Google does when it finds this are of reviews taken down and listings suspended; the listing is the thing the reviews are for.

Build: the same constant set to 'five'. One line.

0 · The smallest version, this week

Policy-safe: it asks everyone.

No table, no page. A {google} blank filled from the same setting, and the Review asked step's starter line Would you leave us a review on Google? {google}, pressed from the strip above the reply box.

What it skips: seeing the review directly, and catching the unhappy customer before Google. Named as the fallback, not the plan.
