Equipment pre-use check: check it off, slide the levels, take the photos, then run the machine

Before a crew runs a machine, the phone walks them around it the way the owner's drill video does: a short list of Yes / No / Not here checks, one open at a time, then the levels as sliders (fuel, hydraulic oil, engine oil, coolant) and the hour meter typed off the dash, then four photos that file into the job's Drive folder, then one press that says the machine is safe to run. A No holds the machine and tells Dispatch. The sheet is the stage checklist sheet the crews already fill, with a levels row and a photo row added. Every frame is 375 px. The machines, the job and the people are made up.

1 Where it starts on a job: the Job Mode card, once you are here

The card already knows the job and, once Dispatch has put a machine on it, the machine. After I'm here a line under the job reads what is on site and whether it has been checked today. The check is asked, never forced: the big presses stay, and the line turns green when the check is done by anyone on the job.

A held machine. A No on a check that matters (a leak, a brake, a missing guard) holds the machine: the line goes red, Dispatch has the row in its inbox, and the line stays red until Dispatch replies or the office resolves the problem on the machine's record. A No that does not hold (a dim light, a loose step) files a problem and the machine still reads safe to run.

6 The machine's record: the fleet board already has the shape

People → Vehicles is a card per vehicle with a holder, a reading, its problems, maintenance tasks, a service log and a ledger. A machine is a card of a second kind on the same board: its reading is hours, its holder can be a job, and its check-ins are the pre-use checks. Nothing new to invent for who holds it, what is wrong with it or when it was serviced.

7 The two doors, weighed

The owner's two open questions, each as the ways considered. The green pick is the recommendation; the others say why not, or when.

Where and when it triggers for a job

  1. The Job Mode card after I'm here, as a line per machine on the jobrecommended — the crew is at the site and at the machine; the card already knows the job and, from Dispatch's hand-off, the machine; nothing is forced, and a second person sees the first one's check. Shown in frame 1.
  2. Before I'm here, between On my way and the arrivalno — the check is a walk around the machine, which is on site; asking it in the truck gets a check filled from memory.
  3. A stage checklist question of the kind "equipment check"not as the trigger — a stage's questions are answered once per stage; a pre-use check is once per machine per day. The stage checklist sheet is reused whole; a stage may still carry a question Was the excavator checked today? that reads the day's check and answers itself.
  4. Dispatch's assignment of a machine to a jobthe source, not the moment — the hand-off is what tells the card which machines to ask about; it is not where the crew does the check.
  5. The morning clock-inno — clock-in happens at the shop or in the truck, and the day may not run the machine at all.

Where it triggers for the equipment itself

  1. A machine is a card on the fleet board, with a label on the machine that opens its checkrecommended — the board already holds a holder, a reading with its history, problems, tasks, service, insurance, a ledger, and check-in questions with a cadence; a machine adds a kind, hours for miles, and a job as a holder. The label is the on-site box label's twin. Shown in frames 2 and 6.
  2. An Equipment register beside Vehiclesno — a second table with the same six things, and a second place for the office to look; if the board gets crowded, a Machines group beside Active and Inactive is the fix.
  3. The Materials on site ledgerno — a machine is not a part in a box; it is not counted or used up. Its label, its code route and its phone-first sheet are borrowed; its tables are not.
