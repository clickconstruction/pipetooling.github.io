How a tag works on a job today

Punch list #363, live since 15 November. It is on job notes: the Pipeline row's box, the job window, Job Mode and Quickfill. It is not on bid or estimate notes yet. Those were left for later on the card.

  1. Type @ or press the @ button. A list opens. The job's crew is on top, then the office.
  2. Pick a name. A tag is the name you picked. A name you only typed is plain words, so "arrive @ 8:45" tags nobody.
  3. Post. The name becomes a chip and the note reads waiting on Dana. It turns amber after two working days.
  4. The tagged person hears. A phone notice, held outside their Push me hours. A red count above Jobs. A Tagged job notes section at the top of the Pipeline. A Needs you line on the Dashboard for people with no Pipeline.
  5. They answer. Any note they type on that job clears the tag. Or they press No reply needed. Opening or reading clears nothing.
  6. The asker hears back. A notice, and the answer counts above Jobs until it has been on their screen under You asked.
Who the list offers

The office, and that job's crew. Never a sub, an estimator, a person who has left, a digital twin or an account in training mode.

How it is kept

A tag is a row of its own beside the note: who, by whom, when, how it ended, and when the asker saw that. Only the database's own functions write it.

Walked so far

From the sender's side, on 15 and 16 November. The tagged person's screens have not been walked yet.

How task notes work today

A task card keeps one thread: who made it, each done, reopen and sign-off, and every note. Anyone who can open the task can add a note. That is the people who have the task, the person who gave it, and the office. A task has no job of its own. A job rides on it only as a link.

Post✓ Complete

The gap: Luis's question reaches nobody. Dana reads it only if she happens to open this card. A note sends no notice and adds to no count.

What the numbers say

People tick tasks and almost never write on them. The card has the counts. A note that reaches nobody gives no reason to write one. So the tag is less an add-on here than the thing that makes a task note worth typing.

The hard cases, with a pick for each

The casePickWhy
Tagging someone who cannot open the task or its jobThe list does not offer them.A task is open to its own people and the office. The same rule as a job: nobody is tagged on something they cannot open. A task has no job column, so a job's crew cannot be offered by rule. To bring a teammate in, give them the task.
A private or HR-flavoured taskA tag never widens who can read.The list is only people who can already open that task. The office can open every task today, tagged or not. Nothing about a tag changes that.
A task that repeatsThe tag stays with the task, not the day.The note stays on the day it was written. The question still waits when Thursday's card comes up, and an answer on any day clears it.
A task already done, or waiting on reviewTagging still works. Nothing reopens.Notes can be added to a done task today. A tag is a question about the task, not a change to it. A sign-off does not clear a tag either.
Ticking the task done while a tag waits on youThe tick clears nothing.One rule everywhere: words or No reply needed. ✓ Post & complete with a typed note does clear it, since a note was typed.
The reviewer's Reopen with commentLeft as it is.It already tells the person, with the reason. It needs no @. The comment counts as a typed note, so it answers a tag that waited on the reviewer.
A task that is handed on or forwardedIts tags go with it.#369's Hand it on swaps who the task is for and keeps its notes, and the Checklist page's Forward becomes the same press. My Inbox still has an older Forward that copies the task and deletes it. Until that one moves too, its window says a question is open before it goes.
