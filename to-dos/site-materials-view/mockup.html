A site's materials on one screen

My recommendation: give a site one screen, read only. It opens from the Materials desk, from the Development page and from Go anywhere. Its top line says how fresh the record is. Under it you read the boxes, what is due a count, what cannot be found, what the material is worth and how much of it has a price. Then one row per part across the site's jobs, with its total and the boxes that hold it. Two names join only when their words are the same. Two names that are nearly the same ask you once. The first build needs no change to the database.

The short of it

Now

  • The desk is one row per job. A site with boxes under two jobs is two rows.
  • The search lists lines. It gives no total.
  • The whole site is a paper of about ten pages, four presses away.
  • The same fitting sits under four spellings as four parts.
  • Money is priced for what went missing. Nothing prices what is on hand.
  • Nothing says when the record was last touched.

My suggestion

  • One screen per site, one press from the desk.
  • How fresh the record is comes first.
  • One row per part with its total and its boxes.
  • The search adds up the rows it shows.
  • The same words join by themselves. Nearly the same asks you once.
  • The worth is shown with how much of the site has a price.

Nothing on the screen writes. A count and a correction stay on the job's page and the phone.

Her site, replayed read on the live app with every write refused · figures rounded

The design was run on the one site that has boxes before it was called the best. Five things came back, and each one changed the drawing.

The boxes
About 100, on 2 jobs

The desk shows the site as two rows. Almost every box holds one line.

The names
Nearly all were typed

About a hundred part names. Fewer than ten fold by their words alone.

The near twins
Dozens of pairs differ only in a size

A fold that guesses would join a 2 inch fitting with a 3 inch one. So nothing folds on a guess.

The record
Quiet for over a week

On hand is the first walk's count. So the screen opens on how fresh it is.

The money
1 piece in 20 has a price

A bare dollar total would mislead. So the worth always shows its cover.

One more thing the replay found. A count resets its line. If two spellings in one box became one part before the sums, one count would wipe the other's pieces and call them missing. So each spelling keeps its own line and its own count, and the row adds them up.

Your calls

Six calls. My pick is first on each.

1 · Where it lives

Recommended. One screen on the On site tab, with doors from the desk, the Development page and Go anywhere.

A seventh look on the Development page. A primary and a superintendent cannot open that page.

2 · Who sees it

Recommended. The owner, the office and a primary, with the money. A superintendent, for the jobs they are on, with no money.

The owner and the office only.

3 · How names fold

Recommended. The same words join by themselves. Nearly the same asks once, and the answer belongs to that site.

No fold at all. Or every answer applies to every site.

4 · The worth

Recommended. Show what is known, with its cover beside it.

Hide the figure until half the lines have a price.

5 · Places

Recommended. Keep a place per job. The screen joins places by name and names the jobs.

A place that belongs to the site. It needs a database change and nothing on the site asks for it yet.

6 · The order

Recommended. Build the screen. Look at it on the real site. Then offer names as people type, before the stickers walk.

Build the whole train at once.

Is this the best we can do? For seeing, yes. For the numbers behind it, not yet, and no screen can fix that alone. On hand is only as true as the last press, and on the real site the record went quiet for over a week. So the screen says how fresh it is before it says anything else.
