The Choose job sheet on the tally lists every job the viewer can see, number · name with the address under it, newest number first, four hundred rows deep. The owner's ask from his phone: a way to search, and the jobs you were on recently, at least the last three, with a way to see more. This page draws the sheet three ways, picks one, takes a second pass at it, and asks three questions.

Where "the jobs you were on" come from Your own clock-ins (clock_sessions, yours, not rejected or revoked), newest first, each job once. A viewer with no clock-ins, the office sorting from a desk, gets the jobs their card's purchases were last sorted to (the Transactions tab's own rows), and each row says so: Purchase sorted Oct 24 instead of Clocked in. A viewer with neither sees no Recent section at all, and the sheet reads as today plus the search. Search matches the way the app's job search does: the number with or without its letter (972, J972), the name, the address, the customer.

The second pass on Draft A after the first render

Decided while drawing

  • Search first, not focused. 48 px, the pill the On site tab's own search wears. A tap raises the keyboard and the sheet stays above it (the app's PhoneSheet does that; the build moves both pickers onto it).
  • The head stays put. Handle, title and search are a fixed head; only the lists scroll, so the search never scrolls away.
  • Every recent row says when (Clocked in today · Yesterday · Mon Oct 27). Show, not tell: nobody needs a sentence explaining the section.
  • Show 7 more carries its count, and ten is the ceiling. Past ten is what the search is for.
  • Headings count what matches while typing (Recent · 2 match · All jobs · 9 match), so a narrowed sheet never looks like a short list by accident.
  • No Recent section at all when the viewer has none. A sentence saying No recent jobs is a bare no-sentence, and empty here is simply "not yet".

Changed on the second look

  • The when-line names its source. For a viewer with no clock-ins the rows come from sorted purchases, and you were on would be a lie for the office. A clock-in row reads Clocked in today; a purchase row reads Purchase sorted Oct 24; the heading drops jobs you were on when the rows are purchases.
  • All jobs keeps every job, in today's order, with its count. The render of Draft C showed the cost of mixing: 972 twice in four rows with nothing between them. Under its own heading the repeat reads as what it is.
  • Draft B settled itself at 375 px. The third chip rendered as one digit. Chips are out.
  • One sheet for both tabs. Reading the code: Materials estimate and On site each draw their own Choose job today. They become one component, so neither falls behind.
  • Show my jobs only (the checkbox under the Materials estimate button) stays where it is and narrows All jobs as today; Recent ignores it, since those are your jobs by definition.

Three questions for the owner his yes before building

  1. Where Recent comes from. Your clock-ins first; with none, the jobs your card's purchases were last sorted to; with neither, no section. Should the jobs scheduled to you this week count too, or only where you actually were?
  2. The "when" line on each recent row (Clocked in today · Yesterday · Mon Oct 27, or Purchase sorted Oct 24). Keep it, or just the three rows under the Recent heading?
  3. All jobs keeps every job, so a recent one shows twice (once under Recent, once in its place). Fine, or take the recent ones out of All jobs?
