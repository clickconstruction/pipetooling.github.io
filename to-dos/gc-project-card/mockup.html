What makes it chaotic, said plainly. ① The head mixes who-and-where with every door to a window, as blue words in one wrap; on a job being built it is fifteen. ③ The verdict and the one thing to do next, the most important line, come third, and the summary numbers ⑤ come after the detail they sum up. ④ Two keys explain the colours in as much space as the colours take; the owner's own rule is that explanation leaves the page. ⑥ The stage's end sits in the middle of the card with a blue button that fights the Next button. ⑦⑧ Plans are two sentences with links inside them. ⑨ Each trade is said three times: a tile, a heading, then its companies, with nothing tying them. Everything is 13px, left-aligned, in one column with no headings, so the eye has nowhere to land.

Is this the best we can do?

What the card is for

The Board row above it already answers "which jobs need me": the ring, the days left, who to call, the price. The card is where the office works one job: sees why it is on Watch, does the next thing, calls the people, reads a scope, opens the plans. So the card should read top to bottom in the order those questions come, and every repeat of what the row said is noise.

Weighing A, B and C

ShapeWhat it winsWhy it lost, or did not
A · bandsKeeps the owner's approved order and every part in view; a heading a band; trade rows end the triple-say; keys fold. Smallest build.It kept the stage rail and the clock caption as their own bands, so the top of the card is still three lines before the calendar. It did not fix the head's facts being a run-on. It is most of the answer.
B · railThe doors finally have a home, with a fact on each; on a building job that is the only shape that holds ten windows without a wrap of blue words. The stage column is pure.Two columns are two reading orders, and the calendar has to take its compact size to fit 760px. On a laptop (under 760px of card) it collapses to A anyway. The rail's facts duplicate the bands they summarize. The doors-with-a-fact idea is worth keeping; the column is not.
C · tabsThe shortest card; nothing scrolls; the walkthrough already goes tab by tab.It hides the calendar, which the owner asked for as the visual at the top (2026-10-04), and hides the trades' holes behind a tab. A tab you have to press is a hole you did not see. The owner removes hidden state; he does not add it.

What D takes from each, and what it changes on its own

  • From A: the bands, the trade rows, the keys behind a link, the foot.
  • From B: the doors as grouped sets with a fact on each, and More ▾ for the rare and the building-stage windows, so the head is one line on every job.
  • From C: the numbers up with the verdict, and the days left as one of them.
  • On its own: the verdict band is first and tinted, so a Watch job looks different from an On track job before a word is read; the stage rail goes (said three times: the chip, the Board's section, the rail); the head's facts become one sentence with the customer and the architect, which the card never named.

What I am not sure of (the owner's calls)

  1. The stage rail. D drops it. If the rail is how a new assistant learns the four stages, keep it as the first band's grey caption (A shows that), one line, no dots.
  2. Where the stage's end lives. Call D (2026-10-03) put We sent our bid · We won this · We lost this "on the project's head" so the office can press them once the door opens. D moves them to the foot, plain. If they must stay high, they go into More ▾ under When the bid ends, never as a blue button beside Next.
  3. The keys. Behind a Key link (D), or gone for good once the rows say the words. I lean to the link for the first month, then take it out.
  4. The trade table's columns. Scope · Budget · Quotes · Asked · Next is my pick. Budget is the money team's (Our number's gate); for an assistant the column reads —. Say if Budget should not be on the row at all.
  5. The customer and the architect in the head. New on the card. They open the customer window, as on the Board row.
  6. The building stage. This mock-up is a bidding job. On a building job the verdict band carries the work and money bars the code already draws, and the trades' rows carry each trade's buyout dots. I would mock that card next, before any build, since it is the busiest.

The build, if D is a yes

Four small PRs, each shippable alone, oldest first: 1 the head (the facts line, the grouped doors, More ▾), 2 the verdict band (tinted, Next, the numbers moved up, the rail folded) inside GcStageHealth, 3 the trade table (one row a trade, the tile and the asks folded in, keys behind the link), 4 the plans band and the foot.
