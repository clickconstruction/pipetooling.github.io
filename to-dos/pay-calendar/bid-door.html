Should an estimator set the Pay calendar from a bid?

My pick: yes, in a narrower form. The estimator writes down the terms they read, on the bid, and says whose they are: this job's or the GC's on every job. The GC's go to the office now. This job's wait on the bid and follow the job when it is won. Either way the office makes them the calendar with one press, with the payer's record in front of them. The estimator never sees a payment.

The three forms, and what each costs

FormWhat the estimator doesMigration and gatesWhat the estimator newly readsThe risk
Write it down, the office sets itmy pick Types the terms on the bid and says whose they are. The office saves the calendar: now for a GC, after the win for a job. One small migration. One new column on the bid's own GC row, where the terms wait. The two money gates are not touched. No money and no calendar. Only what estimators wrote on bids, which they read already. One press of office work per GC or per won job. An estimator sees what was read for a GC, not what the office set.
The estimator sets it Saves the calendar from the bid. Sees the two days and the read-back, never the record. One migration. Two gates of the calendar's own, a new policy, and a refusal for the digital twins written into the save. Every payer's calendar: the days, the clause, who set it. A calendar moves the due day on every bill that payer has out, set by someone who sees none of those bills and cannot see the record that would warn them.
The estimator only sees it Reads a GC's calendar on the bid. Tells the office by hand when it is wrong. One migration. A read gate of the calendar's own and a new policy. Every payer's calendar: the days, the clause, who set it. The terms the estimator just read still have nowhere to go.

Why the two money gates cannot simply be widened. The gate that lets the office set a calendar also lets it record a customer's promise to pay, mark a bill as paid by claim and set the day a payer accepted a bill. The gate that reads a calendar also reads every promise and paid claim in the company. Adding estimators to either would hand them all of that, and the digital twins sign in as estimators. So any form where the estimator reads or saves a calendar needs gates of the calendar's own.
