The estimator's pay terms, on a won bid only

My pick: yes, only once the bid is won, and as one quiet line. An estimator who wins a bid already sends Dispatch a short draft of the job. Nothing shows on a bid that is not won. The estimator never sees a payment.

On a won bid, the office sets itmy pick Adds the terms to the job draft they already send, behind one small press, and says whose they are. The office checks them in New Job and they set with the job. None. The terms ride the draft's Dispatch row. The two money gates are not touched. Nothing. The estimator reads the contract while bidding and writes the terms down at the win, so they may open it twice.
On every bid, the office sets it the last drawing Writes the terms on the bid's GC card at any time. A job's terms wait for the win. One small migration. A column on the bid's own GC row for the terms to wait in. What estimators wrote on bids. Most bids are not won, so most of what is typed is never used. The bid documents' sample contract may not be the one that gets signed.
The estimator sets it Saves the calendar themselves. Sees the two days and the read-back, never the record. One migration. Two gates of the calendar's own, a new policy, and a refusal for the digital twins written into the save. Every payer's calendar: the days, the clause, who set it. A payer's calendar moves the due day on every bill that payer has out, set by someone who sees none of those bills and cannot see the record that would warn them.

Why the two money gates cannot simply be widened. The gate that lets the office set a calendar also lets it record a customer's promise to pay, mark a bill as paid by claim and set the day a payer accepted a bill. The gate that reads a calendar also reads every promise and paid claim in the company. Adding estimators to either would hand them all of that, and the digital twins sign in as estimators. So any form where the estimator reads or saves a calendar needs gates of the calendar's own.
