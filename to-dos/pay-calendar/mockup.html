Pay calendar

Some payers pay on a set day of the month. A bill in by the cutoff is paid on that day. One day late waits a whole extra month. This page shows one setting that teaches the app both dates. Today on this page is Thursday, Nov 12. Every name and number is made up.

The short of it 1 · The setting 2 · A billed row 3 · The forecast 4 · The cutoff What it covers Grace's calls

The short of it

My recommendation: build it, and build the cutoff first. Put a Pay calendar on the payer. A job can carry its own. A payer with none reads exactly as today.

Now

  • Every pay date is the bill date plus the payer's usual days.
  • A calendar payer's usual days swing from 12 to 68. The guess lands in the middle.
  • Nothing tells the office the last day to send a bill.

A bill can be past its contract pay day while the row says in 25 days.

My suggestion

  • The payer holds a cutoff and a pay day. Set once, every job of theirs reads it.
  • A billed row shows the contract's pay day, with their real record under it.
  • A bill not yet sent shows its cutoff, and what one day late costs.

Send by Nov 30 to be paid Dec 10. After that, Jan 10.

One honest finding. I tested this on the payments on record. A calendar was the better guess for 3 of 11 commercial payers. For those few it was far better. For a payer who simply pays fast it was worse. So a person turns it on for a payer. The app never sets it alone.

What I would not build. Business days and holidays. Two check runs a month. Net 30 as a setting, since the measured pace already answers it. A calendar the app sets by itself. A separate calendar page. Retainage, which waits on final acceptance and not on a month.

Grace's calls my pick first on each

  1. Build it? The setting, the pay day and the cutoff first, three small steps. Then look at what the cutoff did. Or the whole train of eight. Or not yet.
  2. Where it lives. On the payer, and a job can carry its own. Or on each contract only, which misses every job with no paper stored.
  3. Which date leads when their record disagrees. The contract's pay day leads and the record sits under it. Or the measured guess leads.
  4. Who may set it. The same people who record a date a customer names. Or the owner and developers only.
  5. How early the cutoff asks. Needs you from 7 days out and a Working row from 10. Or other numbers. Or no Needs you line.
  6. A calendar for a whole development. No. One press sets the other jobs there. Or yes, a development holds its own.
  7. The name. Pay calendar, with cutoff and pay day. Or your own words for it.

One thing only you have. Which payers to set first, and what each contract says.
