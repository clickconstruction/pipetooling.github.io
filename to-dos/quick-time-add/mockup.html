A call or an email, without clocking in

Office staff get called after hours. Nobody pauses a customer to clock in, and nobody clocks in for a four-minute email — so the time is worked and never recorded. This adds one small door on the clock: say how long, in fives, up to thirty minutes, say what it was, save. It becomes an ordinary hours entry that goes to approval like any other, marked so the approver can tell it from a punch.

1 · The door — only when you are not clocked in

While someone is clocked in the time is already counting, so the door is not there. When they are off the clock it sits under Clock In, quiet. Field roles never see it; neither does anyone on salary (their hours are made for them).

Why A. It is the gesture you described — +5, +5, type, done — and it cannot be fat-fingered into 30: every tap is small and the number is huge. The meter shows the ceiling before anyone hits it; at 30 the button goes quiet rather than erroring.

B · Pick a length

Add quick time

For a call or an email you handled off the clock. Up to 30 minutes at a time.

One tap for any length — faster for a 25-minute call, but six small targets in a row, and a slip between 10 and 15 is silent.

The trade. B is one tap instead of up to six. A is what you asked for and is harder to get wrong. If the office mostly logs 5s and 10s — which is what "a quick call" suggests — A costs one extra tap, once. Your call; the kernel underneath is the same.

3 · What it becomes — an ordinary hours entry, marked

Saving writes one finished clock session: it ends when you said it ended, starts that many minutes earlier, carries your sentence as its note, and is pinned to the Office job unless you chose another. It shows on My Time straight away and waits for approval like every other entry.
