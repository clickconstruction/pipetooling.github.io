A call or an email, without clocking in

Office staff get called after hours. Nobody pauses a customer to clock in, and nobody clocks in for a four-minute email — so the time is worked and never recorded. This adds one quiet link on the clock: ＋5, ＋5, a few words, Add 10 min. Up to thirty minutes, in fives. It becomes an ordinary hours entry that goes to approval like any other, marked so the approver can tell it from a punch.

1 · The door — a quiet link, only when you are off the clock

While someone is clocked in the time is already counting, so there is no door. Off the clock it is one small link under the clock row — not a second button competing with Clock In, and not a box that is on screen all day for something used now and then. Field roles never see it; neither does anyone whose hours come from a salary schedule.

Why it is built short. The words are typed with the keyboard up, which takes the bottom half of a phone. Everything — the length, the kind, the words, the button — has to fit in the half that is left, or the person scrolls a sheet to find Save. This does; the first draft did not.

The first draft, and why it was replaced (2026-09-20)
  • It was a form, not a gesture. Nine things on screen — a readout, −5, ＋5, a meter, a scale, the words, starter chips, For, Ended — for a ten-second job.
  • Four widgets said one number. The readout, the stepper, the six-segment meter and the 5–30 scale under it. Here the bar is all of them.
  • Two fields were dead weight. For cannot exist (quick adds are Office-only — a session on a real job moves its stage and its last work date), and Ended is "just now" nineteen times in twenty, so it is one line that opens only when tapped.
  • The door was a permanent dashed box under Clock In, on screen all day, and it stretched the Tally and Job Report squares beside it. Now it is a link.
  • The button did not say the number. Add 10 min is the confirmation — which also removes the only argument for a slower stepper over tappable cells, so the bar takes both.
  • It was never drawn with the keyboard up, which is the only state that matters on a phone.

3 · What it becomes — an ordinary hours entry, marked

Saving writes one finished clock session: it ends when you said it ended, starts that many minutes earlier, carries your sentence as its note, and sits on the Office job — the words say which customer or job it was about. It shows on My Time straight away and waits for approval like every other entry.

The honest part. This is self-reported time, so it is never hidden inside a punch: a chip on every row, the sentence they typed, and a weekly total beside their clocked hours. One number tells you whether it is being used as intended.
