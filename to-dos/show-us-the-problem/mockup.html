What the page never shows, and what guards it

A text can be forwarded. So the link is treated as a key that opens very little.

The worryWhat the design does
The link is forwarded or seen by someone elseThe page shows who asked and a count. It never shows a name, an address, a price, a job or a picture sent before. The worst a stranger can do is add a file, and the office sees who it came in on.
Someone guesses a codeTen letters, the estimate link's own alphabet and its guard: 20 wrong tries in an hour from one place and the door locks for that place.
A link left open foreverIt closes after 7 days, or when the office closes the look. A closed link says so and gives the phone number.
A huge or endless uploadA cap per link: 20 photos and 3 videos, each video 2 minutes at most. Only pictures and video are taken.
A weak signal, or noneEvery file is kept on the phone when it is picked and sends by itself when the signal is back. A video picks up at the piece it stopped on. A reply to the text with the photos is the way round.
Nobody looksIt lands on the thread as a customer's line, so Needs a reply, the badge and the push all fire. The thank-you promises only the office's own hours.
A price from photos that the visit changesThe estimate says it was priced from photos and that the tech confirms it at the home before work starts.

The owner's calls my pick first on each

  • 1. The name. First look for the record and Show us the problem on the customer's page. Or: Problem report, Look-ahead, your own word. Not case, which already means an HR file, a legal matter, a check that came back and a card dispute.
  • 2. Who sends the link. A person's press only. Or: the missed-call text back carries it too, with no person in between.
  • 3. What the page says about the customer. Nothing: who asked, and Click Plumbing. Or: greet them by first name.
  • 4. How much video. Ask for 10 to 20 seconds, take up to 2 minutes. Or: longer, or photos only at first.
  • 5. How long the link lives. 7 days and it takes more than one visit. Or: one day, or until the office closes it.
  • 6. What we promise back. A look within office hours, said in the hours the app holds. Or: a clock, such as within an hour.
  • 7. The price from photos. One price, with the line that the tech confirms it at the home. Or: two options to pick between, or never a price without a visit.
  • 8. The tech's part. The office asks one tech by name. Or: every tech sees a shared list and whoever is free answers.
  • 9. Where the files live. Answered: Google Drive, in Customer supplied photos. Left of it: how long the app keeps its own copy of a video for playing. A year. Or: until the job is paid, or for good.
  • 10. A caller who is not a customer yet. The look hangs on their number's thread and makes no customer until the office names them. Or: make the customer when the link goes.
  • 11. The folder's name. Customer supplied photos, holding video too. Or: Customer supplied files.
  • 12. A picture a customer texts in. Saved from a look, it goes to Customer supplied photos. Or: every texted picture saved to a job goes there from now on.
  • 13. When the new folder is made. When something first lands in it. Or: on every new job up front.

Before any code: try it by hand on the next five calls. Ask each caller to text a photo to the company number. It lands in their thread today. That shows whether callers will do it and whether the office can price from it, for nothing.
