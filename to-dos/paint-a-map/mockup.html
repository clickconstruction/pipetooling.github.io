The short of it

What a phone's browser can do

  • Follow a person who walks or drives with the screen on, and hold the screen on for them.
  • Place them within about 16 ft under open sky. That is plenty for a route, a gate or a meter.
  • Work with no cell signal. The line is kept on the phone and sent later.

What it cannot do

  • Follow a locked phone. No browser reads location with the screen off, on any phone. A phone in a pocket draws nothing.
  • Measure a length you will price. A walked 50 ft can read 35 or 65.
  • Find anyone indoors. The line ends at the door and the photo does the rest.

What I would build first

  • One thing, a painted map: lines, places with a photo, a name, a job.
  • Two ways to make a line: walk or drive it, or draw it by hand on the view from above.
  • One link, no sign-in, with a dot for where the reader is.

6 · Measuring, honestly what each way is good for

The bars are my reckoning from the published 16 ft figure for a phone under open sky. Each bar is drawn as a share of its own run. The one-day try on your own phones, step 0 of the plan, replaces them with measured numbers.

A 50 ft run, walked

Each end can be off by 10 to 15 ft, so it can read anywhere from about 35 to 65. Not a measurement.

A 50 ft run, drawn by hand on the view from above

Each press lands within a few feet when the end can be seen in the picture. Better, and still not a tape.

A 500 ft run, walked in the open

The same 15 to 25 ft at the ends is now 3 to 5 in a hundred. Fine for about how far. Beside buildings or under trees, double it. Not for a length that gets priced.

A 500 ft run, drawn by hand on the view from above

The same few feet at each end, now under 1 in a hundred. The better way to get a length from the app.

A wheel or a tape, typed onto the line

The number to price from. The line shows it in place of the phone's guess: 612 ft, measured with a wheel.

How the app says a length. Always about, rounded to the nearest 10 ft, with its give or take, and with how it was made: walked, driven, drawn by hand or measured. Never 638.4 ft. A typed length wins over every other.

The owner's calls

  1. Screen on. Is walk with the screen on, in your hand good enough to build? My pick: yes. A locked phone would take a native app.
  2. Measuring. May the app show a walked length at all? My pick: yes, as about, with its give or take, and a typed length wins.
  3. Try first. Step 0 on your own phones before the train? My pick: yes, it is one day.
  4. Who may paint. Anyone who may open the job? My pick: yes. Who may share a link: the office and the painter.
  5. The link's life. Ends by itself after 90 days, or lives until ended? My pick: 90 days.
  6. The painter's name on the shared page. My pick: no. The company's name only.
  7. The stop. Five minutes standing still pauses it, an hour ends it. Or other numbers.
  8. The map under it. The free maps the app draws today, or Google's, which bills past a monthly count. My pick: the free ones, with Directions opening Google Maps.
  9. Which copies first. The picture and the page with the link (my pick), the files after the count. Or the files sooner, if a general contractor or an engineer is asking today.
  10. Who may send a copy. A copy cannot be taken back. My pick: the same people who may share the link, with those words on the sheet.
  11. Filing the page. On a press (my pick), or by itself the first time a map is shared.
  12. The doors. Under the address on the job, the tech's job card and the development, plus the header's search (my pick). Or a header button as well.
  13. A sub's work order. May a painted way in show on a sub's page, which needs no sign-in? My pick: yes, later, and only a map the office has shared.
