Clock out: the bids of the day

Today an estimator types what they worked on before Complete clock out lights. The app already knows most of it. This page draws the clock-out as it is, three ways it could read the day by itself, and the one we would build.

Every bid, job number, time and count here is made up.

The day

Today, and three ways to change it

The sheet is the same window on a phone and on a desktop. Press the rows and buttons: they work.

Todaythe person types it
  • The note is required. One of our two estimators types a single word most days.
  • The sheet takes one job or one bid for the whole day.
Athe note writes itself
  • Smallest. No new table. It reads the bid history the app already keeps.
  • Weak. A correction is typing. Nothing downstream can count a note.
Ba list with the minutes
  • Good. One row a bid. A tick takes one off.
  • Weak. Minutes and change counts read like a stopwatch, and the app cannot stand behind them.
Cthe day as a strip
  • Good. The shape of the day shows at a glance.
  • Weak. The grey reads as time not worked. Most of it is work the app cannot see.

Is this the best we can do?

No, not yet. Each version gets one thing right and one thing wrong. The pick below keeps the right things.

What was weakWhat the pick does
A bid opened for two minutes sat beside one worked all morning.Two shelves. Worked on holds bids the person changed. Opened, nothing changed holds the rest, each with one Add press.
Exact minutes and change counts claim more than the app knows.Every row reads the same way: the size, then the tab, as 2 hours · Pricing or 5 minutes · Counts. The size is rounded, and one line under the list says so. Never a total against the clock.
The list could hold up clock out.Complete clock out is live the moment the list holds one bid. The note becomes optional.
Taking one off by mistake.Take off leaves the row in place, struck through, with Put back.
Work the app cannot see: a takeoff in CountTooling, a call, a set of plans on paper.Add a bid the app missed offers the bids on the person's board as one-press chips, then the search the sheet already has.
A day the app saw nothing.The sheet says so and offers the bids on the person's board to add. It asks for the note as it does today.
A bid that became a job.The row keeps the bid's name and shows the job number beside it.

What is still weak, said plainly. A press on Complete clock out does not prove the person read the list. Time in CountTooling stays unseen until that app sends its own beat. A salaried person has no clock-out, so their list sits on the Dashboard instead.
