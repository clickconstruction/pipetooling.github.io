Two boards glued together became one table. The page read the bids and the jobs as two copies of the Bid Board and the Pipeline. Now it reads the site piece by piece: each bid on the row with the job it became, and the plans and the open question on that row.
Ticket chrome left the record. A Questions on this bid card with pills, a reply box and two Ask buttons was a help desk inside a window. A question is now the fourth kind in the box the office already types into (Note · Text · Email · Ask), and reads back as a line in the record's log with its answer under it. That is the history the owner asked for.
The house line carries the site's pulse. Not 5 bids · 6 jobs, which never changes, but 1 question waiting · new plans Oct 24, which is why you would open the page.
Since you looked is a line in the feed, not a filter with an Everything older link. What is new sits above it, what you have seen sits under it, in one list.
Tabs that did nothing are gone. Five tabs over cards that all showed anyway. Now a jump strip, as Messages has, over one page.
The plans loop closes on the page. A set arrives, the page names the siblings on an older one, the leader asks, the estimator answers, and the answer reads back under the plans line and on the piece. Draft 2 stopped at the ask.

What a development is. One site with many bids and jobs on it, some of them at the same time. The app keeps it as a record on jobs (a named row, the house icon on the Pipeline, a filter, GC Review's By development) and, since v2.6019, on bids. This draft gives it a page of its own and keeps the questions a leader asks about it where the answer is read later: on the record. green outline = new

Pieces, not two boards The thing no board can show is the row: this bid became that job, on this set, with this waiting on it. The head says 9 since you looked. The plans loop, closed The newest set is in the head's strip. The Pieces table marks every older set. A check per piece in its own table is the stronger version, left for a later card.
Not in it yet The line stays above the lists with its count and one press, so a development fills up without 30 edits; the real example has 20 jobs and 13 bids in that state. The match is the members' own addresses, as PR 1's notInIt.ts already does it.
Who opens it The office set sees all of it. An estimator opens it from a bid and sees the pieces, the jobs' names and stages, the plans and the questions, with no money on the jobs (a definer read, PR 6). Primaries, superintendents, subs and helpers do not reach it.
The head's people The GC contact comes from the bids' GC contact, estimating from the bids' estimators, on site from the open job's crew. Three names tell a leader who to call before any table does.

4 · Kept, as the record's log
The ask and every reply are lines in the log of the record they were asked on, with the answer under the question, and in the site's feed. A bid's lines show on the job it became. Nothing is retyped into a note, because the question is the note, with a to and an answer.
One lane, read from three places: the inbox (the answerer), the record's log (anyone reading it later), the feed (the leader coming back to the site).
Why the request lanes and not a new table A question needs a to, an open state, an inbox someone watches, a push, an age, a thread and a close. estimator_requests and dispatch_requests have all seven, their notes tables carry the thread, and the people already watch them. A new table would be a fourth internal lane beside the robots' questions and the plans questions, with a fourth inbox nobody has yet.
Why not Messages Messages is one thread per outside person. The estimator is not a contact, and a question about a site has no customer to tie it to. Its slots are the wrong shape for an internal ask.
Why the box and not a sheet Draft 2 opened a sheet with three fields. The office already types into one box on the job and one on the bid; a fourth word on it is less to learn than a new window, and the log it writes into is where the answer is read.
What the lane gains development_id beside bid_id and job_ledger_id (still one reference per request), a read for the office of every request on a record (today the asker, the group and a dev read a request), and the record's log drawing requests beside notes.

5 · Is this the best we can do? the second pass

What draft 2 got wrong It drew the page as the Bid Board over the Pipeline, and the questions as a help desk: cards, pills, reply boxes, two buttons on every record, a sheet with three fields. The owner liked the idea and not the thing. The idea was everything in one place and a history; the thing was two lists and a ticket queue.
What the page is for A leader opens it with one of three questions: what moved since I looked, what is waiting on whom, and which pieces has the newest set not reached. The feed answers the first, the counts and the Waiting column the second, the head's strip and the Pieces table the third. Everything else on the page is the door to a record.
A question is history, not a ticket The owner's words were make it a history of the development or bid or job. A history is a log with the question in it and the answer under it, in the same list as the notes, written from the same box. The inbox is the answerer's view of the same row; the record never shows the inbox's chrome.
The house line earns its place A door that says 5 bids · 6 jobs is a label. A door that says 1 question waiting · new plans Oct 24 is the reason to go. It costs one read of the requests and the newest plan date, both of which the page reads anyway.
Still true from the first pass A record, not a tag (v2.1198 chose it, and the real site shows why). Dead on arrival without the address suggestion, so PR 1 shipped it and PR 3 keeps the page's line. Not Messages, not notes, not a new table. No money on the development itself, no scheduling, no robot.
What this draft does not solve A per-piece plans check (touched · not touched · repriced) is a small table of its own and would make the Plans column a fact instead of a reading of the estimator's prose; a later card. The feed's Their word line reads the customer thread's summary and is only as good as that summary. A piece that is one bid and two jobs (a rough-in and a top-out) takes two rows; a piece name of its own would be a new column on bids and jobs, not drawn.
What to ask the owner now Three things only this draft raises: whether a question should be the fourth kind in the box (drawn) or keep its own button; whether the Pieces table replaces the Bids and Jobs lists outright (drawn) or sits above them; and whether the house line's pulse is right, or the counts were. Everything else stands on the six calls of the first pass.
