What the page keeps today, and what it does not. The What changed card already draws a dated line for everything a record did: a bid sent, won or lost, new plans filed, a job's stage moves, bills and payments, the job's thread notes, the questions and their answers. Three things are missing. A note written by a person about the site or the customer has no home here: a job's notes live in its thread, a bid's in its own, the customer's terms in two fields on the customer. A plan set's history is one line per filing with nothing that says what it replaced. And an event no record caught, an email from the GC, a call from the super, a letter, cannot be written down at the time it happened: a thread note is stamped when it is typed, not when the email came in. The new thing is an entry: what happened, when it happened, about which piece, from what source, kept forever, written by a person in the app or by a robot through MCP. Three ways to put it on the page, then the pass.

A · One stream: the card becomes History, with a place to write at the top recommended

The What changed card keeps everything it draws and takes two more kinds of line: what people wrote (a job's notes, a bid's notes, a note on the customer, a note on the site) and what happened (an entry with its own time, source and piece). At the top a composer: What happened, About (the site, a job, a bid, the customer), When (now, or set back to the email's time), the Source chips, and Keep it. Under it the chips that narrow: by kind (Plans alone is the history of plans), by piece (JP1098 only, set from a Pieces row's history › door). An entry draws on its own tint so the eye tells what a person kept from what a record did. You last looked stays where it is; the search covers it all.

Is this the best we can do? the pass

What history isA dated line that never changes, kept by a person or a robot about something that happened, at the time it happened, about a piece or the site or the customer: an email, a call, a text, a visit, a letter, a decision. The What changed card already is the site's history for what the records did; the entries are the half it could not see.
The recommendation: AOne stream, since one question (what happened here, and when) wants one answer; the composer at the top where the eye goes for news; chips that narrow to a kind or a piece, so the history of plans and the history of one job are two presses, not two cards. B keeps the records clean at the price of two places; C reads well for one job and has no place for the site or the customer.
Where plans' history comes fromEvery filing is already a line (bid_changes with plans in it). The replaces set 1 of Oct 3 words come from the bid's earlier filing; 3 pieces moved to it from the jobs whose plan date is the new set's. Nothing new to store; one kernel in whatChanged.ts grows the line.
What the notes becomeA job's thread notes and a bid's notes stay where they are (the job's Activity box reads them) and are read into the stream as they are today. A note on the customer or on the site is an entry with source Note, so the two new homes cost no new table.
What this draft does not solveWhether a kept line can ever be struck (a wrong date, the wrong job): drawn here as never, with a second line that says so. Whether the email itself attaches to the entry, as the Legal desk's Evidence tab takes a file. Whether an entry about a job also shows in that job's Activity box. Whether a bid's twin may write.

The record

One table, read by the page and by MCP, written by both. The thread notes are not moved into it.

ColumnWhat it holds
development_idThe site it belongs to, always.
job_id · bid_id · customer_idThe piece it is about, at most one; all null means the site itself.
happened_atWhen it happened: the email's time, the call's time. Set by the writer; defaults to now.
sourceemail · call · text · on_site · letter · note, the badge and the verb.
bodyWhat happened, in the writer's words. Plain text; 4,000 characters is plenty.
author_user_id · via · agent_labelWho kept it; app or mcp; the key's name when a robot wrote it (the inbox robot).
created_atWhen it was kept, which may be days after it happened. Never updated; no updated_at.
link_url · file_pathOptional: a link, or a file in a bucket (the .eml), for the draft that attaches the email.

Through MCP

The dev seat's write pair, as every other write there: plan_history_entry(entry) replies with the dry run and a plan_hash; apply_history_entry(entry, plan_hash) writes when the world still matches, once per plan. Reading is the same stream the page draws, so a robot and a person never see two histories: get_history({ development_id | job_id | bid_id, since?, kinds? }) returns the lines, records and entries alike, newest first; read_rows('history_entries') stays for the raw table. A twin (an estimator robot) has no write verb for this unless the owner says so; its add_bid_note stays a bid note.

plan_history_entry{ development_id, about: { job_id | bid_id | customer_id }?, happened_at, source, body, link_url? }The line as it will read, the piece it resolves to, plan_hash.
