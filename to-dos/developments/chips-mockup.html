Is this the best we can do? the pass

Why a confirm and not a bare pressBoth writes move a row onto a board or a filter other people read: the Pipeline's house filter and this page's counts for the first, the Unsent/Working board and the clock quick picks for the second. The house rule for a write that lands somewhere else is a confirm window whose button carries the verb, and a toast with the way back. A bare press with Undo would do for the first; the second puts work back in front of estimators, so it asks.
What the press writes, and only thatPut it in sets the row's development_id, the same column Edit Bid's and Edit Job's picker and PR 1's Put it in line set; the same-master backstop still holds. Put it back on the board clears the two archive stamps, as the Bid window's own Back onto the Unsent / Working board does, and nothing else: no outcome, no sent date, no column move.
What the words should beThe chips are the spike's; the app's own word for the second state is Archived (the Bid Board's button), so the chip could read archived. The first could name the development, not in Harlow Ranch, which also makes the press read right. Both are one-word changes; the press is the same either way.
One at a time, and the counted pressA chip is one row. The not in it line's Put the 34 in is the counted press for all of them at once, PR 3's one transaction; the spike leaves that button a stub and makes the chip real, which is what was asked and what a walk needs. A counted put them all back on the board is not asked for and is left out: thirteen bids returning to the working board at once is a decision for the Bid Board.
What this draft does not solveA chip that is also a press is small on a phone; the row's card at 375 wide gives it room, but a finger may miss it, so the confirm window is also the guard against a slip. not in it on a row of its own says nothing about which development the press would put it in; the confirm names it.
