I like the idea of the pieces section, but they all fulfill different things. So bids should be turned into jobs, for example, and we should kind of see them paired together. How can I offer a tool to more easily connect bids and jobs?  · I realize if a bid and a job are connected I may need to separate them later. We should try and make that easy too.

What a pair is: the job came from this bid (jobs_ledger.bid_id, the same link Edit Job's Disconnect bid clears). One bid may take several jobs. Pairing writes that one column and nothing else; the job keeps its customer, address, development and budget copy, the bid keeps its outcome. What the real site taught: no bid there carries a value, the early jobs predate the bids, and nothing pairs today, so names are the whole signal and a suggestion is a sort, never a verdict. Three ways to put the tool on the Pieces card, then is this the best we can do.

A · Pair mode built in the spike

A press on the card's header, Pair bids with jobs, replaces the table with the site's unpaired bids on the left and unpaired jobs on the right. The likely pairs sit on top as rows with Pair and Not these, and a counted press takes the ones kept. Pick one on each side and the button names the pair. Done pairing brings the table back.

What it does wellThe whole site's unpaired pieces at once, both sides, which is the thing to see when 13 bids and 30 jobs never met. The suggestions are a strip, not a verdict. One shape at desk and phone (one list on a phone: pick a bid, then a job).
What it costsIt is a mode. The table he liked goes away while he pairs, and the paired rows he just made are not in front of him. Two presses to reach it, two to leave. The pick-then-press is a dance for a pair he can already see.
UnpairBack on the table, a small Unpair on the bid cell of a paired row; the toast offers Put it back. Fine as drawn; the question is only where pairing lives.

B · In the row recommended

No mode. The gap in the row is the tool: an unpaired job's Bid cell reads no bid here · came from…, an unpaired bid's Job cell reads no job yet · became…, and when the names say so the cell already holds the likely one with Pair. A paired row's bid cell has Unpair. One line above the table counts the likely pairs and carries the counted press, so the fast path is one press and the careful path never leaves the table.

What it does wellThe pairing happens where the gap is, in the table he liked, with the row's own facts beside it (the job's stage and crew, the bid's status and value). Pair and Unpair are the same cell, which is the second thing he asked for. No mode, nothing to leave. The likely ones are one press each or one counted press for all.
What it costsA list of 13 bids in a picker is long on a phone, so the picker is a small sheet with a search line, not a native select. There is no view of "what is left to pair" as a whole; the strip's count and the no bid here cells are that view, which is enough for one site and thin for 30 rows.
A second job for a bidThe picker's second section lists bids that already have a job, so a rough-in and a top-out can both come from one bid. The paired row shows → J902 · a second job so it never reads as an accident.

Is this the best we can do? the pass

What the tool is forTwo things he said: see them paired together, and separate them later, easily. Both are about the row. A mode (A) and a deck (C) take him away from the row to do the thing the row is for. B keeps him there.
The recommendation: B, with A's stripPairing in the row (came from… ▾, became… ▾, the likely one pre-filled with Pair, Unpair in the same cell with Put it back), and one line above the table that counts the likely pairs and carries the counted press, folding open to A's suggestion rows when he wants to read them one by one. The mode switch goes. The two-column picker survives only as the content of the cell's sheet.
What the real site changesFew shared words, no values, so the strip will usually say 1 likely pair or none, and most pairing is the picker. That makes the picker the thing to get right: the bids listed with number, name, status and value, a search line, the likely one first and marked, and It came from no bid as a way to stop the cell from asking again.
What stays from the buildThe kernel (words, value, dates), the three writes (pair, unpair, won as a counted press), Put it back, the Not these memory on the device. Only where the controls sit changes.
What this draft does not solveA job that came from two bids (an alternate accepted with the base) has one bid_id; the second bid shows nothing. A piece name of its own (finding 2 in SPIKE.md) would make the row read as one thing even before it is paired. Neither is drawn.
What to ask the ownerWhich one: A as built, B in the row (recommended), or C the deck. And whether a paired row should show the bid's value beside the job's revenue when they differ, since that gap is what a pair makes visible.
