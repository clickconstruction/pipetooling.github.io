The owner, on the strip
I think we should make it more clear that this is a review process and not a flow chart where things flow through it. How could you help me make it look like that so we can show but still tell a user in some ways.

Why it reads as a flow. The strip borrowed the Pipeline's stage bar whole: the arrows between segments, the colored dots, the lit segment with the bar under it. On the Pipeline those mean a job moves left to right. Here the four are sections of one page a leader reads top to bottom, and nothing moves between them. Three ways to draw that, then the pass.

B · A review of four looks, ticked as you go recommended

Each section is a numbered look: ① What changed ② Pieces ③ Plans ④ Questions. The number is lit while you are in that section and turns to a tick once you have scrolled past it, so the bar fills in as the review is done; the left cap keeps the score, Review · 2 of 4. No arrows: the order is the page's order, top to bottom, and the ticks say looked at, not moved on. A title on the cap tells the rest in one sentence for whoever hovers.

What it does wellIt shows the review happening: the ticks fill as the leader reads, the score says how far along, and the numbers say the order without an arrow. The cap's one word, Review, is the tell; its title is the longer tell for anyone who wants it.
What it costsA tick can be read as done in the sense of nothing left here; it means looked at this visit, which the cap's title says. The ticks live in the browser for this visit only, which is the point: a review is of today.
What staysPinned at the top, a press jumps to the section, the lit segment follows the scroll, the newest-set tail at the right, the counts with their nouns.

The same four, but the tick is a press: the leader marks each look done, and the cap reads Reviewed 2 of 4; the last tick offers Mark as looked for the feed in one press. The most explicit review, and one more thing to press.

Is this the best we can do? the pass

What the owner asked forShow but still tell. A shows by subtraction and tells nothing. C tells by making the leader do the telling. B shows the review as it happens and tells in one word on the cap, with the sentence a hover away.
The recommendation: BNumbered looks, ticks that fill as you scroll past, Review · n of 4 on the left cap, the newest-set tail kept on the right, no arrows, no stage dots. Counts gain their noun (0 new, 1 set) so a zero reads as a fact and not an empty stage.
What looked at meansA section counts as looked at once its heading has passed under the pinned bar on this visit. Jumping to a later section ticks the ones passed over, since the page scrolled through them. The score resets on the next visit; the feed's Mark as looked stays the thing that remembers.
What this draft does not solveA reader who never scrolls (a short site that fits on one screen) sees 1 of 4 while every section is in view; the rule could count a section as looked at once it has been fully on screen, which is a measure rather than a scroll test. Left for the PR.
