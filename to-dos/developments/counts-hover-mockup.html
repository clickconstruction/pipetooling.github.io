What each count holds. A count is a rule over the site's records, and the detail is the records the rule picked, grouped by it: Waiting is the open questions, who they are on and how old; Bids the bids by status (sent, not yet decided, archived) with value and estimator; Jobs the jobs by stage with revenue and last work; Bid, not yet decided the undecided bids with their prices, adding up to the figure; Open on bills the billed jobs and what each still has open, its revenue less what was paid. Each ends in a door to the board that holds them. An empty count still holds a sentence: what it would count, and the press that fills it. Three ways to show it, then the pass.

A · A card on hover, pinned on press recommended

Rest on a tile and a card drops under it: a head restating what the count is (6 bids on Harlow Ranch), the records grouped by the count's own rule, number · name · value · a muted fact each, at most eight rows then and N more, and a foot with the door to the board. It leaves when the pointer does, after a beat, so you can move into it and press a row. A press on the tile pins the card until Escape or a press elsewhere; on a phone, where nothing hovers, the press is the only way in and the card opens as a sheet under the tiles.

The recommendation: AIt answers the ask as asked, and the grouping by the count's own rule is the detail the page lacks. Pinned on press, a sheet on a phone, a beat before it leaves, Escape closes, eight rows then and N more, the board's door in the foot, a sentence on an empty count.
What the card holds, by countWaiting: the open questions, who they are on, age, asker. Bids: by status, value, estimator, the sent or archived day. Jobs: by stage, revenue, crew, last work, the paid ones as one folded line. Bid, not yet decided: the undecided bids with their prices, and the ones with no price marked not counted, so the figure adds up before his eyes. Open on bills: each billed job, what it still has open, billed and paid beside it.
