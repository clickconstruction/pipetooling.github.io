What the cell says about one job. Four readings stacked: how far along (the percent and its day, the bid value, the stage chips), where the money is (the bar: paid, billed, done but not billed, left; the legend with the percentages; Left on Job), this bill (paid and left on it), and the dates (billed, expected with Pays in ~N d, the lien deadline with the notice to approve, Ask for a date when the expected day is past, the last note). At site scale the first two readings add up across the jobs; the last two do not add, they list: one block per open bill, the tightest first. Three ways to place that, then the pass.

A · A Money look on the page recommended

A fifth card between Pieces and Plans, so the strip reads five looks: ① What changed ② Pieces ③ Money ④ Plans ⑤ Questions. Its head is the site's bar and legend, the percent done weighted by each job's value, the jobs by stage in place of the stage chips; under it the open bills in the cell's own dated shape, one block per bill, the tightest lien runway first, with the cell's presses (Approve the notice, Ask for a date) where the Pipeline has them. The foot is GC Review's statement for the development.

What it does wellThe cell's four readings, at the site's scale, in the cell's own order: how far, where the money is, which bills, their dates. A leader sees the one number that matters (Left on the site) and the one bill to chase, without opening the Pipeline. A look of its own means the strip's amber can say 1 bill past due.
What it costsA fifth look makes the strip Five looks (a word on the cap, a number on a chip). The percent done across jobs is a weighted average, which is a number nobody set; it must say so on hover. The presses (Approve the notice, Ask for a date) are the Pipeline's and must do the same thing here.
What it readsThe jobs' revenue, payments and percent (the page's rows plus pct_complete), the invoices (billed on, amount, paid against), the expected day from the payer's own pace (billedExpectedPay), the lien day from the last work month (lienByForJob) and the notices sent. All reads the Pipeline makes today, scoped to the site.

Is this the best we can do? the pass

What adds, and what listsThe percent and the money add across the jobs; the bills and their dates do not. A sums the first two and lists the rest, tightest first, which is what a leader does with the Pipeline by eye. B and C keep the per-job cell and leave the summing to the reader (C) or shrink the dates to numbers (B).
The recommendation: AA Money look between Pieces and Plans: the site's bar, legend and stage counts on top, the open bills in the cell's dated shape under it, the Pipeline's presses kept, GC Review's statement as the foot. The strip reads Five looks and the Money chip carries $N left and, in amber, N bills past due.
The percent doneA site has no percent of its own; the card's is each job's percent weighted by its value, the paid jobs at 100. The box says weighted by value on hover and never offers an input: the percent is set on the job.
The Open on bills tileIts hover card stays as built (the open bills as rows) and its foot gains Go to Money ›, so the tile and the look agree and the card is the short form of the look.
What this draft does not solveThe bids: a bid not yet decided is money the site may have, and the card shows it as one chip beside the stages, not on the bar. The lien notice state needs the Lien desk's reads (the notices sent per month), which the spike will stub as notice state: PR 2 if they are not in reach.
