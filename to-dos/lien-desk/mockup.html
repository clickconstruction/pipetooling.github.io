The Lien desk: from "a notice is due" to "it went out," with the leader's say-so on record

The forecast now knows which job-months are unpaid and when each month's lien notice closes. The next step is to own the send. This page proposes one modal with three entry points: a queue of every notice the law says is due, drafted by the office, approved by the leader (or sent on his word, noted), then handed to the lien tools and recorded. Draft A is the direct reading of the ask. The critique asks whether that is the best we can do. Refined B is what came out of it. Numbers are today's, from the live forecast.

What qualifies

A job with a GC on it (we are a sub), a month with approved clock sessions, money still open on the job, and no live § 53.056 notice naming that month. It appears 30 days before the month's deadline (the 15th of the 3rd month after; 2nd for residential), turns amber at 14 and red at 7, and stays a week past as missed so the loss is seen, not silent.

Who does what

Office (assistant, controller) drafts, fixes the data, sends, records the mail. Leader (master, dev) approves or holds, and can set a standing rule per GC. Bypass: the office may send on the leader's spoken word with a one-line note; the leader sees it as an FYI, not a decision.

Is this the best we can do?

What Draft A gets right

It puts the statutory duty in a queue with a deadline, where today it lives in a per-job window nobody opens until the money is already old. It keeps the leader in the loop with a record. It reuses the notice the Lien window already renders.

The bypass is honest about how a plumbing office runs: the leader says "send it" in the truck, and the app should not pretend otherwise.

Where it falls short

Per-item approval becomes a rubber stamp. Six today, a handful every month. Robert will click Approve all without reading. The click has to carry a decision, or not exist. A standing rule per GC ("always send to Loberg without asking") turns most items into an FYI and leaves the ask for the cases that need him: a live promise, a GC we have never noticed before, a hold he set last time.

The leader's pane shows the statute, not the relationship. His decision is "send routine paper, or call first". He needs the promise, the pay record, the GC's other open jobs, and whether this is the first notice we have ever sent them.

Readiness comes before drafting. The desk should refuse to send an unmailable notice to Robert and instead show the fix: the owner of record, one door away, with the county lookup.

Notices go out as a batch. Six approved notices are a mail run: one PDF, one tracking form. Recording them one job at a time is the part the office skips.

One notice can name several months. J650's June notice can carry July and August too, each still inside its window. The earliest month sets the deadline.

Fourteen days is too late to start. Certified mail and the owner lookup take days. Surface at 30.

The plan

Small PRs, each shippable, kernel first. The forecast's kernel from v2.3400 is the spine.

Data
job_lien_desk_itemsone row per (job, earliest month, kind) from drafted on; months text[], status drafted · awaiting_approval · approved · held · sent · missed, fields jsonb (the draft), drafted_by/at, approved_by/at, approval_mode leader · word · rule, word_note, held reason/until, sent_filing_id → job_lien_filings. "Due" items are derived, never stored.
customers.lien_notice_policyask (default) · send · hold + note; beside payment_terms; leader-write.
list_lien_notice_months()RPC for the Dashboard count: sub jobs with open money × approved-session months × not noticed × deadline within 30 days. Office-gated, SECURITY DEFINER, cheap.
RLSoffice reads/writes items; a trigger allows approved_by only for master/dev unless approval_mode = 'word' (then word_note required); both read-only appliers.
PR train
  1. PR 1 — the queue and the card. Kernel lienDesk.ts (derive due items from the v2.3400 work-months kernel + filings + policy; severity; readiness), RPC for the count, Dashboard keys lien-notice-draft / lien-notice-approve replacing the per-job card, deep link.
  2. PR 2 — the desk, office side. Migration (items + policy), the modal shell: piles, list, pane with readiness + months + evidence (reuse ForecastWorkMonthsPanel) + the notice preview (the Lien window's renderer, extracted), Save draft · Send to Robert · Robert said to send it; the three doors; the forecast's Send notice… routes here.
  3. PR 3 — the leader side. Your decisions with the why-line and context, Approve & next, Hold (promise · call first) with re-ask, standing rules + the Customer review column, the FYI list with pull-back.
  4. PR 4 — the run. Packet PDF (owner + GC copies, cover sheet), Record the run → job_lien_filings per notice with months_covered per month; the Lien window's notice tab shows desk state and records per month too.
  5. PR 5 — affidavits. The 4th-month window as a second item kind with the same flow (notarize · file · serve), and the hand-off line to the Legal desk when paper does not move the money.
Decisions for the owner
Lead time: 30 days before the deadline (proposed), or the day the month's bill goes past its expected pay date?
Default rule for a new GC: ask each time (proposed), or send without asking with an FYI?
The cover note: on by default (proposed), off, or per GC?
Bypass reach: assistant and controller (proposed), or controller only?
Held items: re-ask 3 days before the deadline (proposed) or 7?
GC entered as the customer with no GC set (J273, J258, J878): treat as sub jobs when the customer is a builder (proposed: a "who owns the site?" chip that sets the GC), or leave them direct?
