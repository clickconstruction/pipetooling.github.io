Why the grid, not a list

• Each fact once. A month's paper is a check in that paper's column; the paper's date, claim, jobs and saved copy sit in its header. Nothing is repeated in prose.
• This notice is a column like any other — its checks are the ticks the office already uses. Adding a month to the notice and reading which paper named it are the same gesture.
• The window is its own column, so the eye finds the dates: closed, mail by, not yet. The earliest open one is the bold date under the grid.
• A closed month is a row, not a banner — closed Sep 15 · not noted · Note it as missed — and a paper that named it after the window shows ✓ as information, which is what counsel's rule (v2.3745) makes of it.
• A by-hand paper (v2.3770) is a column exactly like a paper the run sent — the dots could not show it, since it has no desk item.
• Old months come back. The desk's read stops 30 days past a deadline; a month a paper or an item names is a row anyway.
• It is one job's slice of the Timeline tab's grid (v2.3768): the same facts, here for the job that is open.
