A held check: recorded the day it comes, counted the day the bank makes it good

A paper check is not money until the bank says so. Today the app counts it the moment someone types it. This plan lets the office say what a check is for the day it arrives, and counts it only when the bank posts it. A lien is released after that, never before. Every name and number here is made up.

  1. 1The door is the bank’s own row. When a check is deposited, the Dashboard says so and names the bills it fits. One press holds it for those bills. Nobody types the check.
  2. 2A held check counts nowhere. Its bills stay open, each marked with the check and the day the bank expects it. Chasing stops for those bills. The lien clocks keep running.
  3. 3The bank’s answer does the rest. When the bank posts it, the app counts it and tells the office which liens to release. When the bank fails it, nothing has to be undone.

Why not just record the payment

A check typed on a bill today counts at once. Only three things in the app wait for the bank.

Counts the moment it is typed
  • The bill reads Paid, and a job paid to zero moves to Paid in Full.
  • The customer’s page says You’re all paid up.
  • The sub’s page says Customer paid, and the office is nudged to queue their pay.
  • Paid in full emails go to the office.
  • Chasing, statements and the Collections mark stop.
  • The job leaves the lien lists and the year-to-sue watch.
  • Bills sent, the customer’s totals and their pay speed all count it.
Waits
  • The prompt for an unconditional waiver, seven days.
  • The close of a Stripe invoice, seven days from the day typed.
  • The Dashboard’s Money in line, which follows the bank.

The seven days run from the day typed, not from the bank. A check typed the day it is deposited closes its Stripe invoice a day before the bank expects to post it. Stripe never takes that back.

When a check fails

The bank can fail a check three ways. They are different problems, and the app’s returned-check work already tells the first from the second.

Three gaps in the last row need building. The Collections mark a job lost when it read paid does not come back. A sub told Customer paid is not told otherwise. A release made on the check is not named on the case.

  • 1She has to know to go to the Lien desk. The check reached the bank from her phone. The app already holds the bank’s row. It should come to her and say what the check fits.
  • 2It only works where a lien is filed. A check for an ordinary job has no such door, so the rule would hold for some checks and not others.
  • 3A second press can be forgotten. She said which bills on day one. A press on the day it clears adds a day’s delay and no new choice. If she is out, paid liens sit on record.
  • 4The day reads as a promise. It is the bank’s estimate. If the bank is a day late, the screen says cleared and the money is not there.
  • 5The old way still counts at once. Record payment on the same bill takes a check and marks it paid. Two rules for one check, and it can be counted twice.
  • 6Quiet where it must not be. If a held bill drops out of everything, the lien’s own clocks drop out with it. A check is not a reason to miss a filing day.

The second draw answers each. The door is the bank’s row, on the Dashboard, for any check. The bank’s posting counts it, with no second press. Every screen says the bank expects it and acts on the fact, never the date. The bill’s own payment press knows the check is held. Lien days stay in view beside the bank’s day.

    What must never happen

    The mistakeThe guard
    A lien is released on a check the bank has not made goodThe release reads the bank’s fact. It is offered only when the paying deposit has posted. A check typed the old way, with no deposit, waits its seven days as now.
    One check is counted twiceA held bill refuses a second check payment and says which check holds it. A held deposit is not offered to other bills in Accounts Receivable.
    A payer is chased for money the office holds a check forOne rule, this bill has a check held for it, read by the chase list, the statements and the reminders.
    A lien day is missed because a check came inA hold never takes a job off a lien list and never holds a lien paper by itself. The desk shows the lien’s day beside the bank’s day.
    A hold that never endsA check in the drawer asks for its deposit after three days. A check past the bank’s day reads late until the bank posts or fails it.
    A check comes back and nobody noticesThe hold ends itself. The Dashboard says so in red, the card’s badge turns red, the bills chase again, and the liens read on record.
    A check the bank could not read is treated as a bad checkThe app tells the two apart by the bank’s reason, as the returned-check work does today. An unread check goes back in the drawer with its hold. No fee, no mark on the payer.
    It was held for the wrong billsLet it go ends a hold any time before the bank posts. After that, a payment moves as it does today.
    The bank posts it, then takes it backThe returned-check case that exists today takes the payments off. The case names any release made on that check.

    The calls

    For the company’s owner
    1. What does cleared mean? Pick: the day the bank posts it. Most failed checks fail before that. The rest came back within six days after, so the release tab carries a caution for those days and does not block.
    2. Does the count need a press? Pick: no. The office chose the bills on day one. The bank’s posting counts the check, and the Dashboard says it did.
    3. Every check, or only where a lien waits? Pick: every check. One rule is easier to keep than two. A leader keeps Count it now with a reason, for a cashier’s check or a payer who is always good.
    4. Who may release before the bank posts? Pick: a leader only, with a typed reason.
    5. What does the payer’s page say? Pick: We have your check. It is clearing.
    6. A payer whose check came back before. Pick: their next check still counts when the bank posts it. The release waits the six days after, unless a leader releases sooner with a reason.
    For Taunya and Grace
    1. Where should the line appear first? Pick: Needs you on the Dashboard, and on the deposit’s row in Money in.
    2. What if a check fits two sets of bills? Pick: the line names both and opens the hold with nothing placed.
    3. Who is told when it clears? Pick: every office seat, with the liens to release in the same line.
    For counsel questions only
    1. The release is due ten days after a written request. What if the bank has not posted the check by then?
    2. May the release wait a further week after the bank posts, for a payer whose check has come back before?
    3. May the payer be told in writing that the release follows once the check clears?
