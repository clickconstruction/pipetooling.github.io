Is this the best we can do? draft 1, read against the game

Draft 1 had the idea and not the thing. Read next to the five screenshots, it got these wrong, and draft 2 moves each one.

Two headers, misaligned. The banner's nine tiles sat in their own grid, then a second header row with emoji, and the columns under it did not line up with the tiles. Now one header: the tiles are the column heads, the site's totals sit under them, every row lines up.
A sea of dashes. An open job showed nine member rows with six dashes each. Now a cell that is not the row's to fill is blank, and every member fills Cost.
The members did not roll up. Nothing in a member row added to the job's Cost, and Overhead had no row under it. Now the Cost column is the spine: a person's labor, a sub's sheets, a supplier's invoices, the card's charges and the overhead day share are each a row, and they sum to the job's Cost exactly.
No share at a glance. Who put in most took reading nine numbers. Now a bar under each member's Cost is their share of the job's cost.
Days summed over jobs was a number nobody uses. Now Days is distinct days with work at every level: a person's, a job's, the site's.
No order. Jobs sat in data order. Now rows sort by Earned, biggest first, and a press on a tile re-sorts, as the Money look's heads do.
One blue chip for every state. The game colours the chip by state; the Pipeline already colours every job by status. Now the chip wears the job's status colour, and the open job's chip wears a ring.
Earned hid its percent. The figure comes from the percent done the owner reads everywhere. Now the percent sits beside it, as the margin sits beside Profit.
Emoji tiles on pastel. They render differently on every device and read as toys. Now plain glyphs on solid green, red and amber tiles, the game's weight in the app's colours.
One margin for the whole site. A pass-through materials job dragged the site to 19%. Now the Profit tile says both: the blended margin and the margin on work alone.
Parts did not follow the rule. A part header showed three figures and could not open. Now every level is one rule: the site, a part, a job, a member each fold to three and open to nine and their children.
No dark render. The app has a dark theme and the game is dark. Now the board is drawn in both.

The board recommended

A dev or a controller reads it. Green is effort, red is what it cost, gold is the money. The tiles are the column heads with the site's totals under them. One job is open: its nine, then the rows that put something in, each with its share of the job's cost as a bar, summing to the job's Cost. The controller reading the page is Dana, so her office line is gold.

Earned follows the job's percent: the whole contract at 100%, the percent's share while working, half when no percent is on record yet (≈). Overhead is the day share from People → Overhead, from the day the pool began. A press on a tile sorts by it; the default is Earned, biggest first. Every cell's title names its source, as the Costs column's does today.

The four ways, weighed

A · By job, people under each. Picked.Answers the owner's sentence word for word: the jobs that make up the site, what each earned and cost, what kind of cost, and who. The banner is the site's P&L. Every number already exists for one job in Job Summary.
B · By part of the site. A chip on A.Answers which building paid. Same grid, one level more. Worth little until jobs are on parts, so a chip and not the default.
C · By cost kind as the squads. Rejected.Labor, Subs, Parts and Overhead as the rows, jobs under each. A Labor row would fill one column and leave eight blank, and a job would appear four times. The banner's nine totals already answer where the money went.
D · By time. A chip on A, and the Timeline view.What did this site cost us this month is a window on the same grid, as Job Summary already cuts it. The shape over time is the timeline's job, not a grid's.
