What is lifted from the game the pattern, not the look

One grid. The same nine columns mean the same thing at every level: the whole site in the banner, each job in its row, each person, sub or supplier under the job. A collapsed row shows the three that matter most. A press opens the rest. Your own row is gold.

The board recommended

A dev or a controller reads it. Green is effort, red is what it cost, gold is the money. One job is open: its nine, then the people, the sub, the two supply houses, the card, and the office line. The controller reading the page is Dana, so her office line is gold.

Earned follows the job's percent: the whole contract at 100%, the percent's share while working, half when no percent is on record yet (the ≈ flag). Overhead is the day share from People → Overhead, from the day the pool began.

The four ways, weighed

A · By job, people under each. Picked.Answers the owner's sentence word for word: the jobs that make up the site, what each earned and cost, what kind of cost, and who. The banner is the site's P&L. Every number already exists for one job in Job Summary.
B · By part of the site. A chip on A.Answers which building paid. Same grid, part header rows. Worth little until jobs are on parts, so a chip and not the default.
C · By cost kind as the squads. Rejected.Labor, Subs, Parts and Overhead as the rows, jobs under each. It breaks the one rule that makes the pattern work: a Labor row would fill one column and leave eight blank. The banner's nine totals already answer where the money went.
D · By time. A chip on A, and the Timeline tab.What did this site cost us this month is a window on the same grid: earned by hours in the window, labor in the window, the overhead days in the window, as Job Summary already cuts them. The shape over time is the timeline's job, not a grid's.
E · By person. A chip on A.The grid turned over. Cheap once A exists, since the kernel holds the same triples: job, contributor, measures. Earned per person wears ≈.
