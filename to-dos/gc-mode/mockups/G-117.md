# G-117: a trade tells us it will be late, before the day passes

Helper 4, branch `spike/g117` (from `origin/spike/gc-mode` at fca844a31), 2026-10-06. The mockup and
the plan come first, for the lead's go.

**The row** (`GANTT_FEATURES.md`): *A trade tells us from its portal that it will be late, with a new
day, before the day passes.* It serves **Chase**. Stands: Part (*Move the date*).

**What is there today.** A trade's portal has its own chart (G-110), *Your schedule on this job*.
When **we** move a bar and tell the trades, the trade answers *These dates work* or *I need
another day* (G-113). That ask reaches the move's row, Follow up and Needs you (owner's call 5).
*Your dates with Click* lets a trade move a day it promised us, its start day among them. That
move is a promise, though. It never reaches the chart, and the office's *Starting soon* card only
shows it as an amber note.

**The gap.** Nothing lets a trade say first, while there is still time: *we will finish Wed Oct 14,
not Fri Oct 9, because the membrane ships late.* The office learns it when the bar goes amber,
after the day.

**The loop this builds.**
1. The trade sends the new day from its own bar, with why.
2. The office sees it as a move to take or push back on. The chart shows it too.
3. **Take** saves an ordinary move. The trade's reason is the move's reason, and its words are the
   move's words. So Tell the trades, the customer's *What changed* and *Days lost by cause* all
   read it the way they read any move.
4. **Push back** sends the office's words back to the trade, and the trade answers.

Every example below is the made-up data as it stands. Today is Fri Oct 2. On Fair Oaks Shops,
Building D, Summit Roofing (Carla Nguyen) has *TPO membrane* Sep 21 to Oct 9, 50% done, and
*Roof curbs* Oct 5 to Oct 9, not started.

## 1. The trade's side: its portal

### Where

A job being built → **Your schedule on this job** (`GcPortalSchedule.tsx`, G-110) → under
**Your work**, on each of the company's own bars that is not finished. The door shows only under
*Your work*, never under *Before you* or *Waiting on you*.

### The door on a bar

```
YOUR WORK
TPO membrane                                   ▕██████████░░░░░░░░░░▏
Sep 21 – Oct 9 · 50%
[ We will be late ]                            ← a quiet button, one per unfinished bar
```

### The form: work under way asks for the finish

Pressing it opens the form under that bar, full width:

```
┌───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│ Tell Click the day you will finish.                                       │
│ It finishes [ 10/14/2026 ]  Wed Oct 14. That is 5 days after Fri Oct 9.   │
│ Why  ( Weather ) ( The trade before ) (•Materials ) ( Crew ) ( Other )    │
│ [ The membrane ships Oct 12. We finish two days after it lands.        ]  │
│                                                                           │
│ WHAT WAITS ON IT                                                          │
│ Your Sheet metal and flashing would start Thu Oct 15, 3 days later.       │
│ Rooftop units by Cool Breeze Mechanical would start Thu Oct 15,           │
│ 3 days later.                                                             │
│                                                                           │
│ Click decides. Your dates stay as they are until Click moves them.        │
│ [ Send it to Click ]   [ Not now ]                                        │
└───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

- Under way means it reported more than 0%, or it has an actual start. It keeps its start. The
  day picked is its new finish.
- **Send it to Click** stays off until the form is right. The line beside it says what is missing:
  *Pick the day.* · *Pick a day after Fri Oct 9.* · *Pick why.* · *Say what happened, in a
  sentence.* A sentence is at least 8 characters, the same rule as a move (`MOVE_NOTE_MIN`).
- **What waits on it** lists the bars that wait on this one directly and would move. It shows the
  same work as the chart's *Waiting on you*. It never shows a price or the job's finish. With
  nothing waiting, the heading is not drawn.
- **Why** offers the look-ahead's five reasons, which the portal already says in both languages.
  Each one is also a move's reason, so the office never has to translate it.

### The form: work not started asks for the start

```
│ Tell Click the day you can start.                                         │
│ It starts [ 10/07/2026 ]  Wed Oct 7. It would finish Sun Oct 11.          │
```

A bar not started moves whole and keeps its length. The weekly walk moves bars the same way.

### After sending: the bar's line, one state at a time

```
TPO membrane
Sep 21 – Oct 9 · 50%
(You said Wed Oct 14. Click has not answered yet.)  [ Change it ]          ← grey tag
```

- **Change it** opens the form with the day, the reason and the words already filled in. Sending
  it replaces the open notice, so the office sees only the newest one.
- **Click took it** (the bar now has the new dates):
  `(Click took your day, Wed Oct 14.)` in green, and the door is back for a later slip.
- **Click pushed back**:
  ```
  (Click needs Fri Oct 9.)                                                   ← red tag
  “We need the roof dry by Oct 9. Cool Breeze sets the rooftop units Oct 12.” Robert, Oct 2
  [ We will make Fri Oct 9 ]   [ We will be late ]
  ```
  After *We will make Fri Oct 9*: `(You said you will make Fri Oct 9.)` in green.
- **The bar moved another way** (the office dragged it, or the trade before pushed it): no tag.
  The chart shows the new dates, and the door is back.

### The portal's home: a to-do, only for a push back

> Click needs Fri Oct 9 on TPO membrane at Fair Oaks Shops, Building D. Say you will make it, or
> give another day.

It opens the job at the chart (`anchor: 'schedule'`). An open notice puts nothing on the trade's
home, because the trade is not the one who owes the next step.

### Spanish

Every new portal word has its Spanish, through `gcPortalI18n.ts` (G-119), for example *Vamos a
atrasarnos*, *Dígale a Click el día en que va a terminar.* and *Click necesita el vie 9 oct.*
`PORTAL_SPANISH.md` is redrawn with `portal-spanish-list.ts`, so the native speaker's list carries
them.

## 2. The office's side

### The Schedule tab: a new card between the chart and *Changes to the schedule*

It is drawn only when a notice is open or a push back is still before its day.

```
┌ Trades say they will be late (1) ─────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│ Each came from the trade's portal. Take the new day as a move, or push back.      │
│ Nothing moves until you do.                                                       │
├───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│ Summit Roofing · Roofing · TPO membrane  [Materials]   Sent today, 7 days before  │
│                                                         its finish.               │
│ Says it will finish Wed Oct 14, not Fri Oct 9. That is 5 days later.              │
│ “The membrane ships Oct 12. We finish two days after it lands.” Carla Nguyen      │
│ If you take it: 2 activities after it move out. The job still finishes Tue Dec 8. │
│ [ Take Wed Oct 14 ]   [ Push back ]   Call Carla                                  │
└───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

- The *Sent* words come in three forms: *Sent today, 7 days before its finish.* ·
  *Sent Fri Oct 2, on its finish day.* · *Sent today, 2 days after its finish passed.* A notice
  on work not started says *start* instead of *finish*. The ones that miss the day read red.
- *If you take it* comes from `planMove`, the window's own sentence. It turns red when the job's
  finish moves.
- **Call Carla** is a `tel:` link to the company's phone (`partnerReach`, `telHref`). Follow up
  has the same link.
- **Take Wed Oct 14** opens **Why it moved** (`GcMoveExplain`) the way *Put its days on the
  schedule* does for a change order (G-76). The dates come from the notice, *Materials* is
  picked, and the words are filled in:
  *Summit Roofing told us Fri Oct 2: The membrane ships Oct 12. We finish two days after it
  lands.* The office can change either one. **Save the move** saves an ordinary move that also
  carries `lateNoticeId`.
- **Push back** opens a short form inside the row:
  ```
  What you need from them
  [ We need the roof dry by Oct 9. Cool Breeze sets the rooftop units Oct 12.     ]
  It goes to Summit Roofing in its portal. TPO membrane keeps Fri Oct 9.
  [ Send it to Summit Roofing ]  [ Back ]          Say what you need, in a sentence.
  ```
- **After a push back**, the row stays on the card until its day passes:
  `Summit Roofing · TPO membrane  [pushed back]  We asked them to keep Fri Oct 9, today. No answer yet.`
  Once the trade answers, it reads *They will make Fri Oct 9, said today.* in green.

### On the chart: the trade's day, not on the dates yet

The bar gets an **amber dashed tail** from its finish to the trade's new finish. It is the same
idea as the change order's violet tail (G-76). The tail's title reads: *Summit Roofing says it
will finish Wed Oct 14: materials. Not on the dates yet.* The key gains *a trade's new day, not on
the dates yet*. Once the notice is taken, the bar itself has the dates and the tail is gone.

### What Take leaves behind

**Changes to the schedule** shows the move as any other, with one new line:

```
Fri Oct 2 · Robert   [Materials]                                         [Undo]
Roofing · TPO membrane moved from Sep 21 to Oct 9, to Sep 21 to Oct 14.
“Summit Roofing told us Fri Oct 2: The membrane ships Oct 12. We finish two days after it lands.”
2 after it moved out.
Summit Roofing asked for this Fri Oct 2, 7 days before its finish.            ← new
The trades have not been told.
```

**Tell the trades · 2** then shows two messages. Summit gets *TPO membrane: Sep 21 to Oct 14, not
Sep 21 to Oct 9.* and *Sheet metal and flashing: Oct 15 to Oct 24, not Oct 12 to Oct 21.* Cool
Breeze gets *Rooftop units: Oct 15 to Oct 26, not Oct 12 to Oct 23.* Both messages carry
*Why: materials: Summit Roofing told us Fri Oct 2: …*

**The customer's What changed** reads *Dry-in is 5 days later than planned, because of materials.
The finish holds.* It never names the company. *Days lost, by cause* puts the move at a trade's
door. **Undo** puts the dates back, and the notice is open on the card again.

These are the **Tell** half. They need no new code, because the move carries the reason.

### Follow up and Needs you (the Chase half)

They follow owner's call 5 for a trade's *another day*. An open notice is a reason on the company
until it is taken, pushed back, or the bar moves:

- **Follow up**, under Summit Roofing · Carla Nguyen: *Says TPO membrane will finish Wed Oct 14,
  not Fri Oct 9: “The membrane ships Oct 12. We finish two days after it lands.”* It reads amber,
  and red once the day it changes has passed with no answer from us. Its code is `late`.
- **Needs you on the dashboard**: the phrase *Summit Roofing says it will be late*.
- No new line goes on the ring card. A company that was not on Follow up for this job is counted
  in the board row's people to call, the same as an *another day* ask.

## 3. The rules

- **Who** may send one: the company awarded the bar's trade, on a job being built. It cannot send
  one on our own crew's bars, an inspection, an added activity or a finished bar. Finished means
  100% reported, or an actual finish.
- **Which day.** Under way (more than 0%, or an actual start) asks for the finish, and the start
  stays. Not started asks for the start, and the bar moves whole. The new day must be **after**
  the one it changes and **on or after today**. A sooner day is G-37's job.
- **Before the day passes.** The door shows while the day is still ahead, which is the point of
  the row. It also shows after the day, because a trade that is already late should still give a
  day. The record keeps when it was sent against that day: *7 days before its finish*, or
  *2 days after its finish passed*. That difference is what a trade's record can count later (see
  *Left out*).
- **Where a notice stands** is worked out each time it is read. Nothing is copied or cleaned up.
  The checks run in this order:
  1. **Replaced**: a newer notice from the same company on the same bar.
  2. **Taken**: a standing move carries its id. Undoing that move makes it open again.
  3. **Moved**: the bar's dates are no longer the ones it was sent against.
  4. **Pushed back**: the office answered with words. If the trade then said *We will make it*,
     it is **kept**.
  5. **Open**: anything else, which leaves it waiting on the office.

## 4. The record and the actions (`gcTypes.ts`, additions only)

```ts
/** A trade's word from its portal that it will be late (the Gantt, G-117): the new day, why, and the office's answer. */
export interface LateNotice {
  id: string                                   // 'late-1', newest first
  partnerId: string
  lineId: string
  on: string                                   // the day it was sent
  by: string                                   // the company's contact
  /** Under way: the day is a new finish. Not started: a new start, the bar moving whole. */
  started: boolean
  was: { start: string; finish: string }       // the bar's dates when it was sent
  to: { start: string; finish: string }        // the dates it asks for
  reason: LookAheadReason                      // weather · trade before · materials · crew · other
  note: string
  /** The office needs the day as drawn. */
  pushedBack?: { on: string; by: string; note: string }
  /** After a push back, the company said it will make the day. */
  kept?: { on: string }
}

ProjectSchedule.lateNotices?: LateNotice[]     // unset until the first one: the golden state does not move
ScheduleMove.lateNoticeId?: string             // the notice a move took, like changeOrderId

| { type: 'tradeSayLate'; projectId: string; partnerId: string; lineId: string; day: string; reason: LookAheadReason; note: string }
| { type: 'pushBackLateNotice'; projectId: string; noticeId: string; note: string; by: string }
| { type: 'tradeKeepDay'; projectId: string; partnerId: string; noticeId: string }
setScheduleActivity gains lateNoticeId?: string   // kept on the move only when that notice is open on that bar
```

The reducer adds three cases at the end of the switch. `setScheduleActivity` passes the checked id
to `moveRecord`, whose new last parameter is optional. Each case logs one line:
*Summit Roofing says TPO membrane on Fair Oaks Shops, Building D will finish Wed Oct 14, not Fri
Oct 9: materials.* · *Robert asked Summit Roofing to keep Fri Oct 9 on TPO membrane: “…”* ·
*Summit Roofing will make Fri Oct 9 on TPO membrane.* A bad input returns the same state.

## 8. Left out, and why

- **Nothing is sent.** In the real build a notice emails the job's project manager, and a push
  back emails the trade as a new portal message kind (`KIND_GROUP`). Here the portal's to-do and
  Needs you carry it, the same as the rest of the prototype.
- **The company that asked is still asked to confirm.** After Take and Tell the trades, Summit gets
  *Your dates moved* for the very day it asked for. The lead's call, 2026-10-06: leave it. It reads
  true, because the day did move, and changing who Tell the trades writes to would change a
  sentence in a shared kernel with tests on it. If it bothers the owner, it becomes its own row.
- **The trade's record (G-116).** A notice sent ahead of the day should count for the trade. The
  other side is a bar that went late with no word, and counting it reads the fixture's late bars.
  That moves the bench's golden snapshots, so it is the owner's call. The lead raises it with the
  other snapshot moves.
- **Take in the weekly walk.** A bar with an open notice could offer *Take Wed Oct 14, their day*
  beside *Take …, its pace*. The lead's call: a later round. This round carries only the fact line.
- **Take on Starting soon.** A start promise later than the bar's start could offer the same
  Take. That would turn *Move the date* into a move. The lead's call: a later round.
- **An unanswered push back on Follow up.** That is an unconfirmed date, G-116's open leg. It
  waits for the same call.
- **A sooner day.** That is G-37, Helper 1's row.
- **Our own crew.** It has no portal, and our superintendent moves our bars directly.
- **A tour stop.** The made-up data has no notice, so a stop would point at nothing. It waits
  for the walkthrough's next pass.

## Is this the best we can do?

Three ways it could be better, through the chart's three jobs:

1. **Chase: the trade sees what its day does before it sends.** *What waits on it* in the form
   says *Rooftop units by Cool Breeze Mechanical would start Thu Oct 15, 3 days later.* It shows
   only what the trade's chart already shows, with no price and no job finish.
   **Picked (it is drawn into section 1 above).** A trade that sees whose crew it holds up is the
   trade most likely to find the two days. It is one call to `planMove`, a kernel already tested.
2. **Chase: a push back gets an answer.** After Click pushes back, the trade can say *We will make
   Fri Oct 9* or send a new day. The office's card holds the row as *No answer yet* until it does.
   **Picked (drawn into sections 1 and 2).** Without it a push back ends in silence, and the office
   has to call to learn whether the day holds. It is one small action and one button.
3. **See: one door wherever the office meets the bar.** **Update the week** would list a bar with
   an open notice, even one starting more than a week out. The trade's word would be among its
   facts, with *Take Wed Oct 14, their day* beside *Take …, its pace*. *Starting soon*'s amber
   *That is 3 days after the schedule's start* would get the same Take for a start promise later
   than the bar. That turns today's *Move the date* into a move as well.
   **Partly picked: only the walk's fact line.** It is about ten lines, and it stops the walk from
   *keeping as drawn* a bar whose trade says otherwise, which is the one way this design can
   contradict itself. The Take buttons in the walk and on *Starting soon* are left. The card under
   the chart and Follow up already put every open notice in front of the office. Which bars the
   walk lists, and the start promise, are Building's rules, worth the lead's word first. They
   are the next piece if wanted. The lead's word, 2026-10-06: not this round. Both are in *Left
   out*.
