# G-141: Ask for the days

## The job

G-98 says whose door the late days lie at: *All 4 are the customer's: a change order for them
would save $2,000.* Then nothing happens. Asking for the days means writing up a change order by
hand on Bill the customer, with no cost, the days typed in, and words the customer will read.
The form refuses that order, since a change order with no cost cannot be saved. If one got through
on a trade's work, G-76 would draw its days as a tail on that trade's bar and offer to push the
bars a second time. Those days are on the chart already. They are the reason the job is late.

G-141 puts a press on G-98's line, *Ask for the days*. It drafts the time extension: the days, the
moves they come from, and the customer's words. Then it stops. Sending and signing go the way they
go today.

## What it reads

- **G-98's count** (`lateFinish`): the late days at the customer's door, at most the late days. A
  move counts there when it stands, its reason lies at the customer's door (`CAUSE_OF`), and it was
  not made by a signed change order (`changeOrderId`).
- **The moves themselves**: each standing move at the customer's door that put days on the finish
  (`finishFrom` to `finishTo`), with its bar, its days, its reason and our note.
- **The contract's day** (`substantialCompletionOn`) and the fee a day, if typed on Bill the customer
  (`ownerLateFinish.perDay`).
- **The customer's words**: G-96's `CUSTOMER_WHY`, the same words their What changed and G-98's
  customer lines use. Never a company, never the fee.

**The customer's portal → Change orders**, once sent, through today's card:

Today's card would say *+$0* and *It adds $0 to your price.* A time extension says the two lines

**Signed**: the contract's day moves 4 days, since `substantialCompletionOn` counts signed orders'
days, and G-98's line reads it as above. **The bars stay where they are.** G-76 skips the order, so
it draws no tail and offers no *Put its days on the schedule*. The customer's schedule says what
it says for any signed order: *Change order 1 added 4 days to your contract. Substantial completion
is now Tue Dec 15, 4 days past the Dec 11 you signed.* It is no line on the bill, since it has no
price.

## The kernel

`src/lib/gcMode/gcTimeExtension.ts`, pure, out of the barrel, with `gcTimeExtension.test.ts`.

```ts
export interface TimeExtensionMove { id: string; lineId: string; label: string; days: number; reason: ScheduleMoveReason; note: string; on: string; undoneOn: string | null }
export interface TimeExtensionAsk {
  days: number                        // the customer's late days not asked for yet: G-98's count, less what a live ask covers
  moves: TimeExtensionMove[]          // the standing moves at the customer's door they come from, not in an ask already
  reason: ChangeOrderReason           // 'owner', or 'plans' when every move is a change to the plans
  description: string                 // the customer's words, from CUSTOMER_WHY
  contract: { from: string; to: string }
  saves: number | null                // the days at the contract's fee. Null: none typed. Ours only
}
export function timeExtensionAsk(state: GcState, project: GcProject): TimeExtensionAsk | null   // null: nothing to ask for
export function isTimeExtension(co: ChangeOrder): boolean
export function timeExtensionLines(state: GcState, project: GcProject, co: ChangeOrder): string[] // its row's lines, ours
```

**The field**, on `ChangeOrder` (an addition): `daysOnChart?: string[]`, the standing moves whose
days it asks for. Set means its days are on the chart already. G-76 never puts them there again,
and G-98 stops counting those moves as the customer's once the order is signed.

**The action**, one, since `draftChangeOrder` refuses an order with no cost and has no place for the
field: `{ type: 'draftTimeExtension'; projectId: string }`. The reducer reads `timeExtensionAsk`
from the state itself, so a stale press, or a second one, drafts nothing. The draft has the next
number, cost and price 0, no trade (`packageId: null`), `days`, `daysOnChart`, and the reason and
words from the ask. The log reads *Drafted change order 1 on Fair Oaks Shops, Building D: 4 days of
time for the customer's late days. Nothing is sent.* The action goes in the golden test's `all` list
with `used.add('draftTimeExtension')`, played in `gcTimeExtension.test.ts` because it needs late
days first. No fixture change, and the golden test does not move.

**G-98** (`gcLateFinish.ts`, mine): a move in a signed time extension leaves the customer's count,
the way a move made by a signed order does. A draft or sent one keeps counting, since the days are
still late, and `split` says the ask stands. New fields: `asked` (the live time extensions and their
days) and `toAsk` (the days still to ask for). The press shows only when `toAsk` is over 0. Every
line G-98 pins today stays word for word, since none of its tests has a time extension.

**G-76** (`gcChangeOrderDays.ts`): `changeOrdersOnChart` skips an order with `daysOnChart`. One filter.
**The bill** (`gcOwnerBilling.ts`): the bill's lines skip it the same way, so there is no $0 line
with a *% done*. The pay application's change order summary is built from those lines, so it is
not listed there either. Its $0 moves no total.

## Left out, and why

- **Every day their moves cost, not only the late ones.** Trim's move put 5 days on the finish, but
  only 4 are late, so the ask is 4, the number on the line it sits on. Their What changed told them
  *The finish moves 5 days later*, and asking for 5 would match it. That is your call, and it is one
  line in the kernel.
- **Money with the days.** A time extension can also ask for the cost of the longer job, our general
  conditions a day. That is the owner's call. This asks for time only.
- **The weather's days.** Many contracts give weather days too, and G-58's log is the record. That
  is another row, and G-98 counts only the customer's door.
- **Editing or throwing away a draft.** No change order can be edited or thrown away today. That is
  the list's own gap, and not this row's.

## Is this the best we can do?

1. **See: the moves it asks for, marked where they live.** The move history would show *Asked in
   change order 1* on each move the ask covers. **Not picked:** the press goes away once asked, the
   order's row lists its moves, and an undone one says so there. A third place to read it is noise.
2. **Tell: the decision, and the day we asked for it.** When a decision the customer owes holds the
   moved work (the waits record, kind `decision`), the ask names it in their words. *A time extension
   for the restroom tile decision we asked you for on Sep 28.* The fixture's wait says exactly this:
   asked Sep 28, still open, holding Trim. **Picked:** a dated ask is the strongest case for days,
   and it is still their own decision, never a company. It falls back to `CUSTOMER_WHY`'s words when
   no decision holds the work, or when there is more than one, to stay under 20 words. It changes
   only the draft's description, which the portal card, the asks list and the weekly report read.
3. **Chase: what each unsigned day costs, on our side.** Documents' row for a sent time extension
   would say *saves $2,000 at the contract's fee* beside *Remind them*. **Not picked:** reminding is
   the existing path's, as you wrote it. The fee belongs on G-98's line, which already says it.

## Built (2026-10-06): what changed from this plan

- **The days are every day the customer's moves put on the finish** (the lead's call): 5 on the late
  job, not the 4 late. Pinned: the ask equals the sum of the standing customer-door moves' days on the
  finish, and the whose-days line's late count stays 4. G-98's money line reads the late days as it
  did. A move with the reason *change order* belongs to that order, so the ask leaves it out.
- **The rule is said on the order** (the lead's): *5 days, the days your decision moved the finish.*
  It sits under the description on our row and on the customer's portal card. *your decisions*, *the
  change to the plans* and both together read the same way.
- **G-98 carries the ask**: `lateFinish` returns `ask` (what the press drafts) and `asked` (the
  drafts and sent ones), so the press, the reducer and the line read one call. The plan's `toAsk` is
  `ask.days`.
- **The whose-days line says the days asked**: *change order 1 asks for 5, the days their moves put
  on the finish.* Saying *asks for them* would have hidden the fifth day. A move since reads *Their
  moves since then add 3 days, not asked for yet.*, and the press asks for that move alone.
- **The press says its days**: *It drafts a change order on Bill the customer for 5 days, the days
  their moves put on the finish.* The office sees the 5 before it presses. It is hidden in the
  what-if copy, whose reducer runs only moves.
- **The pick, built**: the decision is named when one decision the customer owes holds every moved
  bar, with the day we asked if there is one. Otherwise it is G-96's words, and a change to the plans
  makes the order a plan revision.
- **Undo** takes only the last move, so the undone-move test uses a job with Trim's move alone. Its
  row then says *Trim's move of Fri Oct 2 was undone Fri Oct 2.*, and the customer's count drops to
  none.
