# G-145: their dates to meet only, onto a running job

Helper 4, branch `spike/g145` (from `origin/spike/gc-mode` at 6b50cb035, the counts in), 2026-10-06.
The mockup and the plan come first, for the lead's go.

**The row.** G-145: *Their dates to meet only, onto a running job: a file's new milestones set beside
ours with the difference in days, nothing else moved (G-137 brings a whole schedule only before
Start or a walk).* It serves See and Chase, phase Later. It was G-137's third option, set aside then
because it writes onto a started schedule. The owner said yes to it as its own row.

**What is there today.**
- **G-137, Bring in their schedule**, reads Microsoft Project's XML (and P6's) and our three
  spreadsheets through `readScheduleFile`. It makes the job's whole first schedule, and its door is
  closed on a job past Start (`importRefusal`: *This job started Wed Jul 1. A new set of plans is the
  way to change its schedule now.*). On a running job, a customer's new dates are typed one by one
  in the Milestones card.
- **The dates to meet** are `schedule.milestones` (`ScheduleMilestone`: id, label, planned,
  packageId, metOn). Substantial completion is the contract's finish: it is due on its planned day
  plus the days signed change orders add (`substantialCompletionOn`), and G-98's late finish counts
  the projected finish against it.
- **The made-up job**, Fair Oaks D, today Fri Oct 2, started Jul 1. Its dates: Slab poured Aug 28
  (met Aug 27), Dry-in Sep 25 (late), Rough-in inspection Oct 13, Substantial completion Dec 11.
  The projected finish is Fri Dec 11, the contract's own day.

## 1. The door

On a job being built, in the **Milestones** card where the dates to meet are kept:

```
Milestones                                                  [ Bring in their dates… ]
Slab poured            Aug 28 · met Aug 27   …
Dry-in                 Sep 25                …
```

- It reads the same two files through G-137's reader and takes **only their dates**: the rows
  `readScheduleFile` marks as a date (a milestone in Project's file, a date to meet in our
  spreadsheet). Their activities are passed over and counted, never read further.
- It shows only on a job being built, with a schedule. Before Start, *Bring in their schedule*
  (G-137) is the door, and it brings their dates with everything else.
- **Closed while a what-if copy is open**, as G-137's door is: *A what-if copy is open. Keep it or
  throw it away first.* It never shows inside the copy.

## 2. The window: their dates beside ours, before anything is written

Cibolo Creek Partners' master schedule for Fair Oaks D, a made-up Project file that only the tests
and the browser check read (`gcTheirDatesSample.ts`, like G-137's Helotes file):

```
Their dates to meet                                                                  ✕
Only their dates to meet come in. Nothing else on the schedule moves, and nothing is sent.
[ Choose a file ]  cibolo-master.xml      From [ Cibolo Creek Partners ▾ ]
In the file: 6 dates. Its 9 activities are passed over.

   Their date              Their day      Ours                       Our day     Difference
[ ] Notice to proceed       Wed Jul 1     [ A new date to meet ▾ ]   —           That day has passed.
    Slab poured             Fri Aug 28    Slab poured                Fri Aug 28  Ours was met Thu Aug 27, so it stays.
[x] Dry-in                  Fri Oct 2     [ Dry-in ▾ ]               Fri Sep 25  7 days later
[x] Rough-in inspection     Fri Oct 16    [ Rough-in inspection ▾ ]  Tue Oct 13  3 days later
[ ] Substantial completion  Fri Dec 4     [ Substantial completion ▾ ] Fri Dec 11 7 days earlier
                            This is the contract's finish. With theirs, the projected
                            finish, Fri Dec 11, is 7 days past the contract.
[x] Grand opening           Fri Jan 15    [ A new date to meet ▾ ]   —           a new date

                                                       [ Cancel ]  [ Take 3 dates ]
```

- **Which of ours**: a select per date, *A new date to meet* or one of ours not met. It starts on
  ours with the same name (G-137's own rule, letters and digits, any case), else on a new date.
- **The difference** is their day against the day ours is due: *7 days later*, *3 days earlier*,
  *the same day*, or *a new date*.
- **What starts ticked**: a date that differs from ours, and a new date still ahead. Never ticked at
  first: one the same as ours, a new date already passed, and the contract's finish.
- **Never tickable**: one of ours already met (*Ours was met Thu Aug 27, so it stays.*).
- **The contract's finish** says plainly what taking it does, before it is ticked and after, in G-98's
  own words from the Projected finish measure: *is 7 days past the contract*, *leaves 7 days to
  spare*, *leaves no days to spare*. Only when the office ticks it does the contract's day change.
- **Days a change order added.** When signed change orders moved ours (`addedDays`), their day is
  read as the day it is due: *Ours counts 3 days by change order, so it is due Fri Dec 18 as
  theirs.* Its planned day becomes theirs less those days, so nothing counts them twice.
- **Take 3 dates** sends one action with what is ticked. With nothing ticked it is off and says
  *Tick a date to take it.*
- In a file with no dates: *This file has no dates to meet.* G-137's own refusals (not Project's XML,
  not our columns) read as they do there.

## 3. What it writes

```ts
// GcAction:
/** Their dates to meet from a file onto a job being built (G-145): each to one of ours, or a new one. Only the milestones change. */
| { type: 'takeTheirDates'; projectId: string; file: string; from: string; dates: { name: string; on: string; ours: string | null }[]; by: string }
```

- **Only `schedule.milestones`**, through the record that exists. One of ours takes its new planned
  day: their day, less the change orders' days on substantial completion. A new one is added with the
  job's own owner, not met. Its id is made the way the Milestones card makes one.
- **Refused whole** when the job is not being built, has no schedule or a what-if copy is open. It is
  also refused when one of its dates names one of ours that is met or not there, or has no day or no
  name.
- **The log names the file**: *Took 3 of Cibolo Creek Partners' dates to meet from
  cibolo-master.xml on Fair Oaks Shops, Building D.*
- **The golden test** gets `'takeTheirDates'` in `all`, with `used.add('takeTheirDates') // played
  in gcTheirDates.test.ts: their dates onto a running job (G-145)`. No fixture change, no snapshot
  moves.

## 4. What the chart and the customer read after

- **The chart's *Dates the job must meet*** row draws each date on its new day, with its state worked
  out as always (due, late, hit). Dry-in on Fri Oct 2 is due today, no longer late.
- **The contract's finish only if ticked.** The Projected finish measure, G-98's late lines, Bill the
  customer's finish risk and G-141's ask all read `substantialCompletionOn`. With Dec 4 ticked, the
  measure says *7 days past the contract*. Then G-98 says the money at the fee, if one is typed, and
  whose days they are, as it does for any contract day. Unticked, none of them change.
- **The customer and the architect** read the dates to meet as the job has them. Their portal's dates
  and its next date show the new days. Nothing tells them: no letter, no message. *What changed this
  week* reads moves, and there are none.
- **The printed chart and the export** (G-21, G-136) read the same milestones.

## 5. What it never does

- **Nothing moves.** No bar, no wait, no gap, no baseline, no move record, so no Tell the trades. The
  activities are the same objects after as before, and a test holds it.
- **Nothing is sent.** No letter (G-94), no portal message, no email.
- **No what-if.** The door is closed while a copy is open, and the action is not one a copy takes.
- **No dates while bidding, on a lost job or before Start.** G-137 is the door before Start.
- **No met date is changed.** Ours keeps the day it was met.

## 8. Left out, and why

- **Their activities' dates.** That is G-137 before Start, and a new set of plans after. A mid-job
  file changes only the dates to meet.
- **Keeping their file's dates as a record beside ours** after the window closes. The window shows
  them side by side. Once taken, the dates to meet are the record, as the lead asked.
- **Telling the customer that their dates came in.** Nothing is sent. They see the new days where
  they read their dates.
- **A date's owner trade.** A new date comes in as the job's own. The Milestones card sets a trade,
  as it does today.

## Is this the best we can do?

1. **Chase: what starts ticked does the office's first pass for it.** A file with twenty dates should
   not need twenty clicks, and it should never quietly take the contract's day. **Picked**: a date
   that differs from ours and a new one still ahead start ticked. The same day, a passed new date
   and the contract's finish start unticked, and a met one cannot be ticked.
2. **See: say what the contract's day does, before the tick.** Taking their substantial completion
   changes what every late count reads. **Picked**: the sentence under that row uses G-98's own words
   from the Projected finish measure, worked out on the dates as they would stand.
3. **Tell: a line in the customer's portal, *Your dates to meet now follow your file of Oct 2*.**
   **Not picked**: the lead's rule is that nothing is sent, and the portal already shows the days
   they gave. It can be a row of its own if the owner wants the record said.
