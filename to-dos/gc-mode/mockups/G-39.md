# G-39: one line, several bars

## What it is

A line of a statement of work is one bar: Pecan Valley's Lighting runs Sep 14 to Oct 23 at 40%.
In the field it is two jobs, the sales floor and then the back of house, and the office needs to
see them apart. The sales floor can be behind while the back of house has not started. Today the
one bar averages them away.

G-39 splits the line into **parts**. Each part has a name, its own dates and its own percent. The
line stays the line: its dates are the span of its parts, and its percent is its parts' percents
weighted by their share. So everything that reads the line reads it as today: the pay
application, the list, the paper, the forecast, the export, the portals' charts and the customer.

## Where the parts live

On the activity, as an addition:

```ts
/** One part of a split line (G-39): its days are counted from the line's start, so any move of the line carries it. */
export interface ActivityPart {
  id: string          // `${lineId}-p1`
  name: string        // "Sales floor"
  /** Its first day, in days from the line's start. 0: with the line. */
  from: number
  /** Its days. The part that ends last ends with the line, whatever this says. */
  days: number
  /** Its share of the line's work, in percent, set at the split from the days. The shares add up to 100. */
  share: number
  /** Percent done, as the trade or our crew reported it. */
  pct: number
  actualStart?: string
  actualFinish?: string
}
ScheduleActivity.parts?: ActivityPart[]   // unset: one bar, as today
```

**Days counted from the line's start** is the choice that keeps this small. Every way a bar moves
today changes only the line's start and finish: a drag, a push, a pull (G-37), days got back
(G-82), Undo, Redo and the what-if (G-81). With the parts counted from the start, all of them
carry the parts along with no change. The part that ends last ends with the line. So when the
walk gives the line a new finish, that part takes up the days.

**The line's percent is Σ part percent × share.** Each share is set at the split from the days
(Sales floor 26 of 40 days, 65%; Back of house 14, 35%). It is **kept** when a part moves later.
A slip does not make the work bigger, so it should not make that part count for more. The line's
number is worked out when a part is reported and stored on the line as today (`pctReported`, or
our crew's `pctByLine`). So billing still follows reports, never a schedule move.

**Splitting a reported line keeps its percent.** Each part starts at the line's percent, so the
weighted sum is the line's. The trade then reports each part. **Make it one bar again** takes the
parts away, and the line keeps its percent and its dates.

## The screens

### 1. The opened activity: a card under it, *Parts of this line*

Unsplit:

```
┌ PARTS OF THIS LINE ───────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│ Lighting is one bar. Split it when the work goes in parts, a floor │
│ or an area at a time.                          [Split into parts…] │
└───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

Split:

```
┌ PARTS OF THIS LINE ───────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│ Sales floor     Sep 14 to Oct 9    65% of the work   60% done      │
│ Back of house   Oct 10 to Oct 23   35% of the work    0% done      │
│ Lighting is 39% done, the parts by their share.                    │
│ [Change a part's dates…]                      [Make it one bar]     │
└───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

### 2. The split window: *Split Lighting into parts*

```
┌───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│ Split Lighting into parts                                      │
│ Each part gets its own bar. The line's dates and its 40% stay. │
│  Name            Starts       Finishes      Share of the work   │
│  [Sales floor ]  [Sep 14]     [Oct 9 ]      65%                 │
│  [Back of house] [Oct 10]     [Oct 23]      35%                 │
│  [+ Another part]                                                │
│ The first part starts Mon Sep 14 and the last ends Fri Oct 23,  │
│ as the line does. Move a part after the split to change that.   │
│                                     [Cancel]  [Split it]        │
└───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

Two parts to start, splitting the days in half, named *Part 1* and *Part 2*. The parts may overlap.
The shares follow the days and are shown, not typed. Refused, with the reason, when a name is
missing or doubled, a part has no days, there are fewer than two parts, or the first does not start
and the last does not end with the line. No reason is asked: no date moves.

### 3. The chart: a fold with a row for each part

The line's row stays as it is: the line's bar, its fill, its pill, its links. A caret before its
name folds the parts, the way a group folds:

```
▾ Lighting                  Pecan Valley Electric   [behind]  ████████░░░░░░░░░░░░
    Sales floor             60% · plan 73%          [behind]  ██████░░░░
    Back of house           not started             [starts Oct 10]      ░░░░░░
```

- A part's row has its bar at its dates, filled to its percent. Its pill reads done, behind, due
  today, or starts in, by its own dates, and the today line runs through it.
- Its hover card gives its name, the line's name, its dates and days, its percent against the plan
  by its own dates, and its share.
- **A part drags like a bar**, to move it or to pull an end, through **Why it moved** like any
  move.
- No links start or end at a part. The line's waits hold the whole: what waits on Lighting waits
  on its last part's finish, and Lighting waits on what it waited on.
- A split line opens unfolded. The fold is the person's, like a group's.
- The List view, the paper (G-21) and the export (G-136) draw the line as today.

### 4. A part moves: *Move Electrical · Lighting, Back of house*

The same window, reasons and sentence. It shows the part's dates before and after, then what the
line's span does and what that pushes:

```
  Back of house   Oct 10 to Oct 23  →  Sat Oct 17 to Fri Oct 30   (7 days later)
  Lighting now ends Fri Oct 30. Nothing after it moves. The job still finishes Tue Dec 8.
```

It saves as one move, `setScheduleActivity` with an optional `part`. The line's start and finish
become its parts' span, what follows is pushed as for any move, and the move keeps the parts before
and after (`ScheduleMove.parts`), so Undo and Redo put them back. Its row reads "Electrical ·
Lighting, Back of house moved from Oct 10 to Oct 23, to Oct 17 to Oct 30." Tell the trades names
the part: "Lighting, Back of house: Oct 17 to Oct 30, not Oct 10 to Oct 23."

### 5. Reporting

- **The trade's portal**: a split line's row shows the line's percent as text, with a row for each
  part under it, each with the percent picker the line has today. It uses the same words and the
  same rule: never below what is billed. A part's pick that would take the line under its billed
  percent is not offered. `tradeReportPart` sets the part, works the line's percent out, and sets
  real days the way a line's report does (G-55): a part's first report over 0% is its start, and
  100% is its finish. The line's own real start is its first part's, and its finish is when every
  part is at 100%.
- **Our own crew** (the Draws tab's crew card): the same, per part, for a split stage
  (`selfReportPart`).
- **The walk** reads the parts: under a split bar's facts, one line a part, such as "Sales floor:
  60%, plan 73%. Started Mon Sep 14." and "Back of house: not started, Sat Oct 10." Its question
  stays the line's finish.

## The tests

`gcSplitBars.test.ts`, on Fair Oaks D's Lighting:

1. Splitting keeps the line: the same dates, 40%, shares 65 and 35 from the days, each part at 40%.
   Refused when the parts do not start and end with the line, a name is missing or doubled, a part
   has no days, or there is one part.
2. Part reports: Sales floor 60% and Back of house 0% make Lighting 39%. A part's first report sets
   its real start, and its 100% its real finish. The line's real finish waits for every part. A pick
   under the billed percent is refused. `tradeReport` on the split line is refused.
3. A part moved later past the line's finish: Lighting ends Oct 30. What follows is pushed as for
   any move, the move keeps the parts before and after, and Undo and Redo put them back exactly.
4. A move of the whole line carries the parts: a drag, a push, a pull and the walk's new finish,
   with the last part taking up the change.
5. Make it one bar: the parts go, and the line keeps its percent and dates.
6. Nothing else changes: with a split line and no report, the pay application, the list, the
   paper's rows, the forecast and the export read exactly as before the split.
7. In a what-if copy (G-81), a part moved stays in the copy, and Keep carries the parts.
8. Every sentence passes `plainWordsFailures`.

`GcSplitBars.render.test.tsx`: the split window's refusals, the card, a part row on the chart with
its pill, the portal's part pickers, and Why it moved for a part.

## What I am leaving out, and why

- **Parts on the trade's own chart and the customer's view.** You asked that what reads the line
  keeps reading the line. The trade sees its parts where it reports them.
- **Waits between parts** (Back of house after Sales floor). The parts' dates say the order, and
  the line's waits hold the whole. Waits inside a line would put a second network under every bar.
- **Splitting an inspection or an added activity.** Neither has a percent to report.
- **Splitting in a what-if copy.** The copy moves parts that exist. A split is not a move, so it
  belongs to the real schedule.

## Is this the best we can do?

Three ways it could be better, one for each job:

1. **See: shares kept, not days counted each time.** You suggested weighting by days. I keep each
   part's share from the days at the split. **I pick it.** If shares were counted anew, a part that
   slipped would weigh more and move the line's percent with no report. Billing would then follow
   the schedule, not the work.
2. **Tell: the move names the part to the trade.** "Lighting, Back of house: Oct 17 to Oct 30" in
   Tell the trades, not the whole line's dates. **I pick it.** A trade told its line moved, when
   only its back-of-house crew must change, calls the office to ask which crew. It is one branch in
   `movedLines`.
3. **Chase: the call list names the part behind** ("Sales floor is 12 points behind"). **Not now.**
   The call list (G-115) reads the line's standing, which the parts already feed through the line's
   percent. A part-level reason is one more kind on a list Helper 3 owns. Better asked of them once
   parts are in.
