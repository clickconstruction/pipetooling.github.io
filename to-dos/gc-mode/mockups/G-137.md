# G-137: bring in a schedule a customer or the architect hands us

Helper 2, branch `spike/g137` (from `spike/gc-mode` at `aed868b10`, G-136 in), 2026-10-06. The row
in `GANTT_FEATURES.md`: *Import a schedule a customer or architect hands us* (See, Later; round
three, the last of my order). The shape is the lead's (2026-10-06). It reads the two files G-136
writes. It shows what it found before anything is written, with the trades guessed from the names
and left for the office to tick. It writes through the first draft's own path. This is the mockup
and the plan, for the lead's go before any code.

## What it is for

A customer who keeps a master schedule of their own hands us that schedule at award: "this is the
schedule you build to". That customer is an owner's rep, a GC we build under, or an owner with a
scheduler; on a small job it is often the architect acting for the owner. Today the office draws a
first draft from the stages, then moves bar after bar to their dates, one window at a time. Their
file already holds their dates and their waits. This brings them in with one press, onto our
trades' lines. The first schedule we Start from is then theirs wherever it names our work, and our
first draft everywhere else.

## Where it sits

On a job whose schedule is drawn but not started and never walked, the *Drawing the schedule* card
gets **Bring in their schedule instead**. That window adds one line: *It takes the place of the
schedule drawn now. The changes made to it are lost.*

Office, in buyout with no schedule: Studio Ocotillo, its architect, sent the owner's schedule from

in, Casework install on the Reception desk, and leaves the punch list out. The window then closes
on the chart, drawn like any first draft. The log says *Drew the schedule on Helotes Dental Office
from Studio Ocotillo's file, helotes-schedule.xml: 11 of their activities on our lines and
inspections, 4 dates to meet. 8 of our lines were drawn as the first draft draws them.* A wait on a
row left out is left out with it.

## What it reads

The two files G-136 writes, and the same files from the customer's own programs:

- **The project file** (`.xml`): Microsoft Project's XML (MSPDI), which Project saves with *Save as
  XML* and Primavera P6 exports as *Microsoft Project XML*. The importer reads each task's
  name, start, finish, milestone flag, outline level, summary flag and waits. It also reads the
  project's title and its minutes a day, the length of a gap. A summary task is a group: its name
  helps the trade guess, and it is never an activity. Project's own summary of the whole job (task
  0), a blank row and an inactive task are passed over. *Start no earlier than* a day before the
  task's start comes in as *Not before*, and *finish no later than* as *Must finish by*. The hold
  G-136 puts on every task, on its own start day, is passed over.
- **The spreadsheet** (`.csv`): any of the three header rows G-136 writes. These are our team's 16
  columns, the owner's *Group, Name, Start, Finish, Done %, Where it stands*, and the GC's *Stage,
  Activity, Start, Finish, Where it stands*. A customer who edits our file in Excel and sends it
  back changes the dates, so the reader takes `2026-10-09` and Excel's own `10/9/2026`. The
  byte-order mark is optional. On our team's copy, *Trade* and *Kind* settle the guess, and *Waits
  on* is read back from its names (`Slab on grade (Concrete) +3 days`).
- **Not read**: Project's `.mpp` and P6's `.xer` (their own formats, each needing a library the
  browser does not have), and a PDF of a chart. The window says to ask for *Save as XML*.

## What it found, before anything is written

- **Activities**: each task of theirs that is not a group or a date, with its dates and its days.
- **Waits**: the finish-to-start links between their activities, with the gap in days. A gap is
  read in working days (their minutes a day) or in elapsed days. The days are kept as written.
- **Dates**: their milestones. Each is ticked to come in as a date to meet. One with our first
  draft's name (*Dry-in*, *Rough-in inspection*, *Substantial completion*) takes the place of
  ours, and the window says so.
- **What it could not read**, each in a sentence, counted once:
  - a wait that starts or finishes with another task (the app's waits are finish to start);
  - a wait on a task outside the file;
  - a wait on a group;
  - a wait on a date (the app's waits run between work);
  - a gap given as a percent;
  - a task with no start or finish;
  - a spreadsheet *Waits on* name that matches no row, or two;
  - a file that is neither MSPDI nor one of our three spreadsheets.
- **Our lines not in it**: the job's lines no row lands on, by trade.

## The guess, left for the office to tick

Each activity of theirs gets a place, guessed in this order, with the reason shown beside it:

1. **Our own spreadsheet says**: *Kind* `inspection` is an inspection, `added activity` is the
   job's own, `date to meet` is a date, and a wait (`delivery`, `decision`, `permit`, `utility`) is
   *Not ours*. Waits are kept on the job's waits, never brought in from a file. *Trade* names the
   trade.
2. **Not ours by its name**: *by owner*, *by others*, *owner furnished* or *by tenant*. The dental
   equipment is the owner's, though *equipment* is one of HVAC's words.
3. **Inspections**: a name with *inspection*. One with *rough* goes on the rough-in inspection, one
   with *final* on the final inspection, any other on an inspection of its own.
4. **The trade**: a group or a name that holds one of the job's trades (*Plumbing*, *HVAC*). If
   none does, a word only one of the job's trades has. Those words are New Project's list of what
   each trade does (`TRADE_TEMPLATES`, its words and usual scope) and the job's own lines. So
   *ductwork* is HVAC and *casework* is Millwork. A word two trades share guesses neither, and the
   reason says so: *rough* is in Plumbing's lines and in Electrical's.
5. **The line**: inside that trade, the line whose name shares the most words with theirs (the
   first four letters of each word, as New Project matches sheets to lines). If none does, the
   trade's one line in the stage the first draft reads from the name (`lineStage`). Otherwise
   *Pick a line*.
6. **Nothing found**: *Pick where it goes*. The row stays out unless the office places it.

The choices for a row are each of the job's lines by trade, the rough-in inspection, the final
inspection, an inspection of its own, the job's own (an added activity, G-38) and *Not ours*. Two
of theirs may go on one line of ours: our bar then runs from the first start to the last finish,
and the window says *Rough in: 2 of theirs*.

## How it writes: the first draft's own path

One new action, `importSchedule`, makes the activities and their waits in one press. It is added to
the golden test's list. The reducer builds the schedule with one pure function,
`importedSchedule(project, kept)`, in five steps:

1. **The first draft**: `scheduleDraft(project, workStarts, project.rough?.days)`, exactly what
   *Draw a first draft* draws. That gives every line of every trade, the two inspections and the
   three dates to meet.
2. **Their lines**: a line with rows of theirs takes their dates, or the span of them, and their
   waits on what is on the schedule. The gap of each wait is kept, the largest when two collapse
   into one. A wait their own dates break is left out and listed. The same goes for their
   inspections and the job's own.
3. **Our lines not in it**: each keeps the first draft's waits and days, and starts the day after
   the last of what it waits on finishes, or on *Work starts*. Plumbing's Top out runs after their
   Rough in, as the first draft runs a crew's lines one after another.
4. **The inspections not in it**: placed the same way. The final inspection waits on every
   activity nothing else waits on, as the first draft does.
5. **The dates to meet**: theirs as ticked. Ours not taken by one of theirs are worked out again
   the first draft's way: dry-in on the last dry-in line, the rough-in inspection on its finish,
   substantial completion three days after the final inspection.

The result is a `ProjectSchedule` like any other: activities on lines, waits with gaps, inspections
and milestones, no baseline, no moves. There is no second shape. Nothing records that a file made
it except the log line. On a job we bid with a rough (G-45), the *Drawing the schedule* card then
reads it against the weeks we bid: *The first draft runs 15 weeks. We bid 14.*

**It changes nothing in the draft kernel.** It calls `scheduleDraft` as the first-draft card does,
with the rough's days when we bid one: the stage-days door G-45 opened, read and never changed. If
G-44 (templates, Helper 5) changes what the first draft draws, our lines not in a file follow it.
Its tests pin those lines against `scheduleDraft` itself, never against fixed dates, so the two can
land in either order.

## What it never does

- **Never onto a schedule already walked or started.** The reducer refuses a job past Start, one
  whose schedule has a walk or a recorded move, and one still bidding or lost. Fair Oaks D was
  started Jul 1. The made-up data records no walk for it, so the Start rule is what keeps it shut.
  A new set of plans is the door for changes after Start (G-76 and the sets).
- **Nothing to the trades or the customer.** No Tell the trades, no letter, no portal message. What
  their portals show is what a first draft shows.
- **No what-if.** It never writes into a what-if copy (G-81). While a copy is open the door is
  closed: keep it or throw it away first.
- **No percent, no actual days.** What is done is reported by the trades and the walk, never read
  from someone else's file. A file's *Done %*, *Really started* and *Really finished* are passed
  over, and the window says so once when the file has any.
- **No waits from outside.** Deliveries, decisions, permits and the utility stay on the job's waits
  (G-73 to G-75), entered by the office.

## The kernel

`src/lib/gcMode/gcScheduleImport.ts`, pure, tested, out of the barrel:

```ts
/** What a file holds: its rows, its dates and what it could not read. */
export function readScheduleFile(text: string, fileName: string): ScheduleFileReading
/** Each row's guessed place on this job, with the reason in words. */
export function guessPlaces(project: GcProject, reading: ScheduleFileReading): Map<string, ImportGuess>
/** The schedule the import makes (the reducer's one call). */
export function importedSchedule(project: GcProject, kept: ScheduleImport): { schedule: ProjectSchedule; notes: string[] }
/** Why an import is refused on this job, or null. */
export function importRefusal(project: GcProject): string | null
```

`gcTypes.ts` gets the action and its payload, additions only: `ScheduleImport`, which holds the
file's name, who handed it, *Work starts*, the kept rows with their places, dates and waits, and
the ticked dates. The MSPDI names (`MSPDI_NAMESPACE`) and the spreadsheet's headers
(`CSV_COLUMNS`) are read from `gcScheduleExport.ts`, so the writer and the reader cannot drift
apart.

## Left out, and why

- **Opening a real customer's file.** As with G-136, the reader is checked on our own files and on
  made-up ones in the schema's shape. The owner tries the first real one with a scheduler's copy.
- **Project's `.mpp`, P6's `.xer`, a PDF.** Each needs a reader the browser does not have. *Save as
  XML* is one click in both programs.
- **Any spreadsheet's columns.** Only G-136's three header rows are read, which is the lead's shape.
  Project's own Excel export has other columns. Reading it would be one more header row, and is
  the lead's call.
- **Bringing their dates onto a schedule we already run.** This is what a mid-job file usually wants
  (the owner moved the opening). It is the lead's rule that no import touches a schedule walked or
  started. Their new dates to meet are typed in the milestones editor today.

## Is this the best we can do?

Three ways to make it better, by the chart's jobs:

1. **See: each guess says why.** A trade guessed from their group, from a word, or from nothing
   reads the same in a select, so the office must check every row to find the weak ones. A *Why*
   cell beside each guess fixes that: *their group Plumbing*, *the word ductwork*, *our
   spreadsheet's Trade column*, *no trade in its name*. Rows with no place sort to the top. The
   office then reads only the rows that need it. **Picked**: it comes from the guess, which already
   knows its reason, and it is what makes "left for the office to tick" quick on 200 rows.
2. **Tell: their dates kept beside ours.** Their file could be kept as a named plan, like G-41's
   baselines. The chart would then show their dates under our bars after we move one, and the
   customer's *What changed* could say how far we are from their schedule. **Not picked**: the plan
   at Start is that record already. Until the office changes it, the imported schedule is their
   dates. A second plan is G-41's ground, and the owner's call.
3. **Chase: dates to meet only, onto a running job.** A mid-job file brings only their new
   milestones, set beside ours with the difference in days, and nothing else moves. **Not picked**:
   it is the most common later need, but it writes onto a walked schedule, which is the lead's rule
   against. Worth its own row.
