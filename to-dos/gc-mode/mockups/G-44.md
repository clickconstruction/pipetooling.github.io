# G-44: templates, a job's schedule shape for the next job like it

Helper 5, branch `spike/g44` (from `origin/spike/gc-mode` at fc62e7206), 2026-10-06. Round three,
the lead's assignment in place of Helper 2.

**State:** the lead said go with A on 2026-10-06. It is built as planned below, with the numbers the

**The row.** "Templates: save a job's schedule shape and start the next job like it from it." It
serves See.

**The job, as the lead put it.**

- **What a template holds:**
  - the stages and their lengths;
  - the activities per trade, with their days and their waits as offsets;
  - the places from G-83, if kept;
  - the parts from G-39, if any.

  It never holds dates, companies, percents or moves.
- **Where it is saved from:** a built job's Schedule tab, *Save as a template*, with a name and the
  job it came from.
- **Where it is used:** G-45's rough while bidding and the first draft at buyout. Both offer *Start
  from a template* beside the usual lengths. Both draw through the same draft kernel, so there is one
  path.
- **How it ages:** a job started from a template records which one, so the owner can see where each
  job came from.
- **What it never does:** nothing reaches the trades or the customer. No edit to a template changes a
  job already drawn.
- **The build:** a record on the state, actions in the golden list, no fixture change, and the golden
  test unmoved.

## What is there today

- **The first draft** is `scheduleDraft(project, start, stageDays?)` in `gcNewProject.ts`.
  - Every line of every trade goes in its stage, and a stage starts when the stage before it is done.
  - A trade's lines run one after another, sharing the stage's days.
  - It draws two inspections, the rough-in and the final.
  - G-45 added the optional `stageDays`, a record of days by stage key. The rough keeps it as
    `rough.days`, and `draftSchedule` takes it as `days`.
- **One job has a schedule:** Fair Oaks Shops, Building D, being built. It is 72% done, 30 activities,
  and 23 weeks from Mon Jul 6 to substantial completion on Fri Dec 11. It was drawn by hand: many lines
  run side by side across stages, and none of its waits has a gap set.
- **The others have no schedule:**
  - Boerne Retail Shell is bidding. It has nearly the same trades and line names as Fair Oaks D.
  - Helotes Dental Office is in buyout.
  - Stone Oak Pharmacy is being built, with no schedule drawn yet.

## What a template must carry: the finding that shaped the plan

A template that carried only days would not draw the next job like the last one. I tried each way on
the made-up data, with a throwaway copy of the kernel, before writing this. Boerne Retail Shell from
Mon Nov 2, 27 of its 31 lines named as on Fair Oaks D:

| What the template carries | Boerne | Fair Oaks D redrawn from its own template |
|---|---|---|
| Nothing: the usual lengths (today) | 14 weeks | n/a |
| Days only, run in the first draft's stage order | **42 weeks** | not tried |
| Days and waits, no offsets | 19 weeks | 17 weeks, not 23 |
| **Days, waits, and each line's offset after them** | **23 weeks** for its shell, **27** with its fire sprinkler | **23 weeks, every line on its own dates** |

- **Days alone run in the wrong order.** The first draft runs every stage, and every trade's lines,
  one after another. Fair Oaks D ran them side by side: Erection beside Joists and deck, Electrical's
  service during structure. Its days in the draft's order nearly double the job.
- **Waits without offsets lose the time between work.** A line often starts some days after the work
  it waits on, with no gap set. Fire alarm started 9 days after Lighting finished. Plumbing's trim
  started 47 days after the rough-in inspection.
- **With offsets, a template is the job's shape with no dates.** Each line keeps its days, the lines
  it waits on and how many days after them it started. Redrawn from its own first day, Fair Oaks D
  lands every line on its own dates. That makes a clean pin for the tests.

Boerne's fire sprinkler, which Fair Oaks D did not have, is drawn by the first draft's rules: the
mains after the roof, and the rough-in inspection after the mains. That brings the shell's 23 weeks to
27. The throwaway copy had let the inspection pass before the mains were in. The build keeps the
inspection after every rough-in.

So the lead's "the template's days as the optional argument G-45 added" becomes two optional inputs on
the same kernel:

- **The stage days** stay G-45's argument as they are.
- **The template's lines** come in one new optional argument. A record of days cannot hold waits.

It is still one path: the usual draft, the rough's stage days and a template are all drawn by
`scheduleDraft`. With neither argument, the draw is exactly today's. G-45's snapshot of every
fixture job's draw holds that.

## The rules

- **A template holds its job's shape, with no dates.** That is each line of each trade, and the two
  inspections the first draft draws, each with:
  - its trade and name;
  - its stage and its days;
  - the lines it waits on, by trade and name, with the office's gap on that wait (G-35) where one was
    set;
  - its offset: the days after the last of those finished that it started. With nothing to wait on,
    it is the days after the job's first day.

  It also holds the stages' spans for the card, the weeks to build there, and where it came from.
- **It holds no dates, companies, percents, actual dates or moves.** It takes the plan as it stands,
  with the moves already in its dates, and no move is copied. *Not before* and *must finish by* days
  are dates, so they stay with their job. So does a G-38 activity the office added, which names its
  own reason and who.
- **Drawing: a line covered by the template runs as it ran there.** A line is covered when the
  template has one of the same trade and name, whatever the case. It takes:
  - its days;
  - its waits, the ones this job has, with their gaps;
  - its offset after the last of them.

  A covered line whose waits this job has none of falls back to the stage rules, keeping its days.
- **Drawing: every other line is the first draft's own.** It goes in its stage, after the stage
  before it and its trade's line before it. It takes the stage days: the rough's, else the usual.
  The two inspections follow the template's when it has them, else the first draft's rule.
- **A draw takes a copy.**
  - The rough keeps the template's lines it drew with as `rough.like`, so every redraw reads the copy.
  - The first draft turns them into dates once.
  - Renaming a template, or setting it aside, never touches a job. A job keeps the name it was drawn
    with.
- **It is recorded where it is used.**
  - The rough keeps `rough.template`, and the schedule keeps `schedule.template`. Each is `{ id, name,
    on }`.
  - At award, the first draft drawn from a template's rough keeps the same record.
  - *Drawn from it* on the card is read from these records, so it cannot drift.
- **Nothing reaches the trades or the customer.** Templates live in `state.scheduleTemplates`. The
  drawn schedule is an ordinary first draft. The trades' and the customer's views never read
  `template` or `like`.
- **Only a job being built saves one**, as the lead said: "a built job". A template needs a schedule
  that has run, not a draft.
- **The places (G-83).** Helper 4 confirmed `ScheduleActivity.place?: string` is kept only when the
  office keeps it, never guessed into the record.
  - A template line keeps its line's kept place, tidied as G-83 keeps one.
  - The draw writes it on the same line of the new job as kept, since the office already gave it once.
    The lead chose that over a guess.
  - The office changes it there.
- **The parts (G-39).** A split line keeps its parts' names, first days from the line's start, days and
  shares, with no percent or actual dates. The new line is split the same way, with nothing done.

## The kernel: `src/lib/gcMode/gcScheduleTemplates.ts`

Pure, out of the barrel, with `gcScheduleTemplates.test.ts`.

```ts
/** Additions to gcTypes.ts. */
export interface TemplateLine {
  /** "Roofing". Empty for one of the job's two inspections. */
  trade: string
  /** "TPO membrane", or "Rough-in inspection". Another job's line of the same trade and name takes it, whatever the case. */
  label: string
  stage: string
  days: number
  /** The lines it waits on, each with the office's gap on that wait (G-35) when one was set. */
  after: { trade: string; label: string; gap?: number }[]
  /** Days after the last of those finished that it started. With nothing to wait on: days after the job's first day. */
  offset: number
}
export interface ScheduleTemplate {
  id: string
  name: string
  from: { projectId: string; name: string; donePct: number }
  on: string
  by: string
  lines: TemplateLine[]
  /** Each stage's span on that job, first start to last finish, for the card. */
  stages: { key: string; days: number }[]
  weeks: number
  /** Not offered for new jobs. The jobs drawn from it keep what they drew. */
  asideOn?: string
}
/** Which template a draw came from, by the name it had that day. */
export interface TemplateUse { id: string; name: string; on: string }

// RoughSchedule.template?: TemplateUse; RoughSchedule.like?: TemplateLine[]
// ProjectSchedule.template?: TemplateUse

/** The shape of a job being built, ready to save. Null: nothing drawn. */
export function templateShape(state: GcState, project: GcProject): Pick<ScheduleTemplate, 'from' | 'lines' | 'stages' | 'weeks'> | null
/** The templates offered for a new draw: not set aside, newest first. */
export function templatesOffered(state: GcState): ScheduleTemplate[]
```

**Kernel changes elsewhere, additions only:**

- **`scheduleDraft(project, start, stageDays?, like?)`** in `gcNewProject.ts` gets the fourth
  optional argument, `like?: TemplateLine[]`.
  - Each line looks for its covered line, by trade and name.
  - Lines of one stage are taken in wait order, so a covered line comes after the lines it waits on.
  - With no `like`, nothing runs differently.
- **`draftSchedule`** in `gcBuildingSchedule.ts` passes it through.
- **`roughDraw`** passes `rough.like`.

**Actions.** Three are new, and two existing ones take an optional field.

| Action | What it does | Refused when |
|---|---|---|
| `saveScheduleTemplate { projectId, name, by }` (new) | Saves `tpl-N` with `templateShape`. | No schedule, not being built, or a name problem. |
| `renameScheduleTemplate { templateId, name }` (new) | Renames it. | A name problem. |
| `setAsideScheduleTemplate { templateId, aside, by }` (new) | Sets it aside, or brings it back. | No such template. |
| `setRough { ..., templateId?: string \| null }` | A string copies that template's lines into `rough.like` and records it. `null` goes back to the stage days alone. Absent, a redraw keeps what the rough had. | The template is set aside or unknown. |
| `draftSchedule { ..., templateId? }` | Draws with the template's lines and records it. When the job's rough came from that template, it draws with the rough's copy. | The template is set aside or unknown, unless it is the rough's own. |

The three new actions go in the golden test's `all` list, each with `used.add(...)` pointing at
`gcScheduleTemplates.test.ts`. The walk plays none of them. No fixture change, so the golden test does
not move.

## Left out, and why

- **A template's own activities beyond the first draft's** stay with their job. That means an
  inspection only that job had, like Fair Oaks D's electrical service inspection, and G-38's added
  activities. The draw makes only what the first draft makes, so a new job never gains a bar it has
  no line for.
- **Editing a template's days.** A template is a job as it ran. To change it, draw a job from it,
  change that job, and save the job as a new template.
- **Matching lines by a near name**, like *Rough in* against *Rough-in*. Names are matched whole,
  with case ignored, so a template never takes a wrong line's days. The coverage line says what it
  covers.
- **A template from a job still in buyout.** Its schedule is a draft, not a job that ran.
- **The trades and the customer** never see a template, by the lead's rule.
- **B, days from what really happened** (G-55's actual dates): a small row once actual dates are
  recorded as a habit. Today few lines have them.
- **C, a template's track record**, how its jobs finished against the weeks it made: a row once jobs
  close. None drawn from a template can finish in the made-up data.

## Is this the best we can do?

**A. See the fit before drawing.** The *Start from a template* control says, before anything is
drawn, how many of this job's lines the template covers. It also says the weeks it would make, and
the weeks without it. Boerne: 27 of 31, 27 weeks against 14. Helotes: 8 of 17, 19 against 10.
A template from the wrong kind of job shows itself in two numbers. **I pick this.** It is two
kernel calls already planned, and it answers the question the owner has at that moment: is this job
like that one?

**B. Days from what really happened.** Use each line's actual days where G-55 recorded both its
actual start and finish, and the plan's days elsewhere. **Not now.** Fair Oaks D records actual dates
on few lines, so the template would be a patchwork. The plan as it stands already carries every move
the weekly walk made. It is a small row after this one, once actual dates are recorded as a habit.

**C. A template's track record.** On the card, show how the jobs drawn from it finished against the
weeks it made: *Drawn for 3 jobs. They finished 2 weeks later than drawn.* **Not now.** No job drawn
from a template can finish in the made-up data, so there is nothing to show. It is the natural next
row once jobs close.

So the build is the plan above with A. G-83's places and G-39's parts ride with the line, since both
landed during the build.
