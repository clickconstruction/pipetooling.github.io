# G-57: the projected finish with weather and crews

## The job

The Projected finish measure reads two things: the plan, and the pace of the work so far (G-56). The
later one wins. Neither looks ahead at what the daily log already says about this job. Rain has
stopped the roofers before and will again. A trade that had 5 a day now sends 3. G-57 adds a second
finish line under the measure: the same projection with a weather allowance and the crews on site
now. It never replaces the pace line, and it says in one sentence each why the two differ.

## What it reads

- **The pace projection that exists** (G-56, `projectedFinish`). The second line runs that same
  function on the schedule with each piece of work left stretched, so the later of plan and pace
  still wins, and it can never come before today's line.
- **Weather days the log already records** (G-58, `weatherLostDays`): the dates the log says the
  weather stopped work, and **which trades** it stopped. Outside is read from the log, never guessed
  from the trade, the way G-58 reads it. The fixture has one job with logs, Fair Oaks D, and no past
  jobs with logs. Past jobs' weather is the real build's.
- **Crew counts.** The log's people a day per trade, and G-142's told counts through
  `crewCountsNow(project)` once Helper 4's row merges. Until then the kernel takes them as its last
  argument, default none, the same slot G-98 left for G-117. After G-142, the default becomes the
  call.

## The two rules, named on the surface

- **Weather**: `WEATHER_DAYS_A_MONTH`. **From the log** once it covers a month: the days the weather
  stopped work, over the days the log spans, times 30. Until then, **our rule: 2 a month**, and the
  sentence says so. The allowance goes on the work left of each trade the log has seen stopped by the
  weather: its remaining days × the rate ÷ 30, rounded. A log with no weather days adds none, and says
  that too.
- **Crews**: `crewSoFar` is the trade's people a day on the days the log has it on site. `crewNow`
  is its told count for the week the work falls in (G-142), else its newest logged day in the last
  week. When the crew now is smaller, the trade's work left stretches by so far ÷ now: 2 against 3
  makes 9 days left into 14. **A bigger crew is not counted on** until the work shows it, so this line
  only ever adds risk. That matches G-82, where a second crew is an offer, not a fact. Our own crew is
  read the same way. A trade with nobody on site this week is said by name and not stretched, since
  nobody's pace cannot be divided.

The two sentences each say their own days: the finish with weather only and with crews only. The
first line says the two together, which need not be the sum. Nothing goes on the customer's side, in
the portal, the Friday report or the letter, until the owner says so.

## The tests

- On the fixture, the line and both sentences as above: the same day, our rule, three short trades
  covered.
- A month of log with 3 weather days on HVAC makes the rate the log's, 3 a month. A short HVAC crew
  this week stretches Test and balance. The finish moves, and each sentence's days are its own.
- A bigger crew adds nothing. Nobody on site is said by name and not stretched. A told count for a
  coming week, through the slot, beats the log for that week's work.
- No weather in the log: no allowance, and it says so.
- The line never comes before the pace line, and the pace line is unchanged.
- Every sentence passes plain words. The customer's portal, report and letter have none of it.
- Render: the block sits under the measure's words on the fixture.

## Left out, and why

- **Past jobs' weather and the season.** The fixture has one job with logs. A December allowance
  from a September log is a guess, so the rule says *until the log covers a month*.
- **The customer.** Not until the owner says so.
- **Planned crew per bar.** A bar has no crew. The crew so far is the plan's stand-in, and G-142's
  told count is the trade's own word.
- **Moving anything.** It is a reading. The walk and G-82's offers move bars.

## Is this the best we can do?

1. **See: where the days land.** The chart draws each stretch as a faint tail on the bars that carry
   it, like G-76's change-order tail, with the reason on its hover card. **Not picked:** the line and
   two sentences answer *how late and why*. Tails on twenty bars that their spare days absorb would be
   noise. Worth it only where a tail reaches the finish, and that is a second row.
2. **Chase: the short crew is a call.** A trade whose shrunk crew moves the second line joins
   G-115's call list: *Crews: 1 on site against 3 so far. At that, Test and balance finishes Dec
   18.* With Call and Follow up. **Picked:** a short crew is the one thing on this line someone can
   change by phone today. It reuses the call list's rows and sheet, one reason added.
3. **Tell: the contract, on the second line too.** *With weather and crews: Fri Dec 18, 7 days later,
   7 past the contract.* **Not picked:** G-98's lines already say the contract for the pace line, and
   a second contract count beside it would argue with it. Better once the owner decides which line
   the customer hears.
