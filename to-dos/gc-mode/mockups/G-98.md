# G-98: the late-finish days in the contract

## The job

The contract says when the job finishes, and the change orders the customer signs move that day.
Many contracts also charge a fee for each day past it. The pieces exist in three places, and nobody
puts them in one sentence:

- The Schedule tab's **Projected finish** measure says *3 days past the contract*. It says how many
  days change orders added, but not which orders.
- **Bill the customer** has the *Finish date* card. It holds the fee a day (`ownerLateFinish`, typed
  by the office) and the money at risk.
- The customer's portal says *We expect to finish Tue Dec 15, 3 days past the Dec 12 in your
  contract*. It lists each signed order's days, but never why the days are late.

G-98 writes the sentence. It says how many days past the contract, what they cost at the contract's
fee, which change orders moved the contract's day and which would, and whose door the late days are
at. The office sees it on the Schedule tab, and the customer reads it in their own words.

## Where it sits

- **Schedule tab → the Projected finish measure**, the strip's finish card. New lines under its
  existing words, on a job being built.
- **The customer's words**: under the finish in their portal's *Your schedule*, after *At a glance*'s
  finish on the Friday report, and after the contract lines in the schedule letter (G-94). It shows
  only when the finish runs past the contract.
- **Bill the customer → Finish date** (pick 2): the same split line and change orders, so the two
  tabs say one thing.
- **Not on the ring card, the board row or Needs you.** The ring card is in the golden walk's
  readings (`stageProgress`), so a line there moves every step's snapshot. That is the owner's call.

## The screen

The fixture's Fair Oaks D finishes on the contract's day, Fri Dec 11, with no change orders and no
fee typed. So on the fixture nothing new shows, and the measure keeps saying *no days to spare*. The
words below come from the prototype with three steps taken. Trim moves 7 days for the customer's
tile decision. Test and balance moves 9 days for rain. Change order 1 adds 1 day and is signed, and
change order 2 adds 2 days and is sent. The fee is $500 a day on Bill the customer.

```
┌ PROJECTED FINISH ─────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│ Tue Dec 15   [3 days past the contract]                       │
│ HVAC · Test and balance now finishes Sun Dec 13. The plan at  │
│ Start had Fri Dec 4. What comes after it moves the finish to  │
│ Tue Dec 15. The contract says substantial completion by       │
│ Dec 12, with 1 day by change order.                           │
│ ───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────── │
│ At $500 a day, the 3 days cost $1,500.                        │
│ All 3 are the customer's: a change order for them would save  │
│ $1,500.                                                       │
│ Change order 1 moved the contract 1 day, signed Fri Oct 2.    │
│ Change order 2 would move it 2 more days once they sign it.   │
└───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

The line under the rule, every case:

| When | The words |
|---|---|
| Late, a fee typed | *At $500 a day, the 3 days cost $1,500.* |
| Late, no fee | *No late fee is entered from the contract. It goes on Bill the customer.* |
| Some late days are the customer's | *4 of those days are the customer's: a change order for them would save $2,000.* Without a fee: *… would move the contract.* |
| All of them | *All 3 are the customer's: a change order for them would save $1,500.* |
| A signed order with days | *Change order 1 moved the contract 1 day, signed Fri Oct 2.* One line each. |
| A sent order with days | *Change order 2 would move it 2 more days once they sign it.* |
| On time, or early | The change order lines only. The chip already says *no days to spare* or *4 days to spare*. |

The customer's words, after their finish line, only when late:

```
We expect to finish Tue Dec 15, 3 days past the Dec 12 in your contract.   (as today)
Change order 1 added 1 day to your contract.                               (as today)
Those 3 days came from a decision we were waiting on from you.             (new)
```

With part of them theirs: *4 of those days came from a decision we were waiting on from you. We are
working to make up the other 3.* With none: *We are working to make up the days.* The words for why
are their portal's own (`CUSTOMER_WHY`): *a decision we were waiting on from you*, *a change to the
plans*. Never a company's name, never the fee.

## The rule

- **Late days** = the projected finish (`projectedFinish`, G-56) less the contract's day
  (`substantialCompletionOn`, with signed orders' days). These are the same two `ownerFinishRisk`
  reads, so the Schedule tab, Bill the customer and the portal count one number.
- **The money** is the late days times the fee a day the office typed (`ownerLateFinish.perDay`). No
  fee, no money: say so. The fixture has none, which is right. The prototype can type one on Bill the
  customer, so no fixture change is needed to see it.
- **The customer's days** are the finish days of the standing moves whose reason lays at the
  customer's door (`CAUSE_OF`, G-96: the customer, the plans), less any move that put a signed change
  order's days on the chart (`changeOrderId`), since the contract already has those. They are capped
  at the late days. They do not lower the money at risk: until the customer signs a change order for
  them, every late day counts. The sentence says what that order would save.
- **Change orders**: signed with days moved the contract (*moved*), sent with days would move it
  (*would move*). A draft or a declined order is not named.

## The kernel

`src/lib/gcMode/gcLateFinish.ts`, pure, out of the barrel, with `gcLateFinish.test.ts`.

```ts
export interface LateFinish {
  /** Days the projected finish runs past the contract's day. 0: on time or early. Null: no schedule, or no substantial completion. */
  late: number | null
  /** Days to spare before the contract's day. 0 when late. */
  spare: number
  /** The late days whose reason lays at the customer's door, capped at `late`: a change order to ask for. */
  customers: number
  /** The fee a day typed from the contract (Bill the customer). Null: none. */
  perDay: number | null
  /** late × perDay. Null without a fee. */
  atRisk: number | null
  /** Signed orders with days (moved the contract), then sent ones (would move it). */
  orders: { number: number; days: number; state: 'signed' | 'sent'; on: string | null }[]
  /** The office's lines, for the Projected finish measure and Bill the customer. */
  words: string[]
  /** The customer's lines, after their finish. Empty unless late. */
  customerWords: string[]
}
export function lateFinish(state: GcState, project: GcProject): LateFinish
```

## The tests

- `gcLateFinish.test.ts`. The fixture: on time, no words, no customer words. The late job above: the
  lines word for word, in English. The customer's days are capped at the late days, and a part-theirs
  job gets its own pair of lines. Without a fee there is no money, and the line says where it goes. A
  move that put a signed order's days on the chart is not counted as the customer's. A draft or
  declined order is not named. Every line passes `plainWordsFailures`.
- The Friday report and the letter, on the late job: the customer's lines sit after the finish. The
  existing tests do not move: their exact lines are the on-time fixture's, and the new lines show
  only when late.
- A render test of the Schedule tab on the late job: the measure has the lines. A render test of the
  customer's schedule: the new line is under the finish.
- The golden test does not move: nothing it reads changes, no action, no fixture.

## Left out, and why

- **The ring card, the board row, Needs you**: they move the golden walk. That is the owner's call.
- **Which days the contract excuses**: the weather's, a strike's. That is the contract's words, and
  the real build reads them. Here only the customer's door counts as theirs. The *Days lost, by
  cause* card under the chart still has every door.
- **The money for the customer**: never. They read days and why.
- **Who sees the dollars**: anyone in the prototype. In the real build the money line follows
  *Money* (the owner and the controller, question 9), as Bill the customer does.

## Is this the best we can do?

Three ways it could be better, one for each job:

1. **See: whose late days they are.** It says how many late days are the customer's, and what a
   change order for them would save. A bare *3 days past the contract* reads as all ours. **Picked:**
   it is the line that turns a penalty into an ask, and G-96 already lays each move at a door.
2. **Tell: one sentence on both tabs.** Bill the customer's *Finish date* card says the same split and
   change orders as the Schedule tab. Today the Schedule tab counts the days and Bill the customer
   counts the money. If only one learns whose days they are, the two disagree on what to do.
   **Picked:** a line on a card that exists, from the same kernel.
3. **Chase: Ask for the days.** A button drafts the time extension change order with the customer's
   days. **Not picked:** G-76 would read a signed order's days as not on the dates yet and offer to
   push the bars again. Those days are on the chart already, from the moves. It needs a field on the
   change order and a guard in G-76's kernel, which are the lead's to place. I recommend it next.

## Built (2026-10-06): what changed from this plan

- **One call, pinned.** `lateFinish` carries Owner Billing's `ownerFinishRisk` as `risk`. The
  Projected finish measure's chip reads `risk.past`, the same value it worked out before. Bill the
  customer's card calls `lateFinish` and reads its `risk`. The customer's lines come from the same
  call. A test counts the days each surface shows on the fixture, the late job and after one more
  move, and they move together. `customerStanding`, the portal's own finish line, is pinned equal in
  the same test. It cannot call the kernel, because the kernel reads its `CUSTOMER_WHY`.
- **On time with a fee typed**, the measure says what a day past would cost: *Each day past Fri Dec
  11 costs $500.* Checked live after typing $500 on Bill the customer.
- **Pick 3 is G-140** at the end of section F, New and Later, naming the field on the change order
  and the guard in G-76's `changeOrdersOnChart`.
