and the plan come first, for the lead's go.

**The row** (`GANTT_FEATURES.md`): *The billing forecast follows the schedule: what we expect to
bill each month, as the bars move.* It serves **Tell**. Stands: Part (Owner Billing's forecast).

**What is there today.** Owner Billing's forecast is the Money tab's **The next 6 weeks**
(`cashAhead` in `gcOwnerBillingAhead.ts`). It is cash by the week, across every job. Of our bills
it counts what is sent, plus one expected bill per job: the draft, `ownerPayApp`. That draft
bills only the work reported **today**. It never looks past the next bill day, and it never reads
a bar. On Fair Oaks D the draft for Sun Oct 25 asks **$98,566**. The schedule has the job 92%
done by Oct 25, which is a bill of about **$368,744**. Nothing after October is forecast at all.

**The gap.** Nobody can see what we will bill in November or December. Nobody can see how moving
a bar moves that money. The customer, whose cash it is, sees only *The next bill comes Sun Oct
25*.

Every number below is the made-up data as it stands. Today is Fri Oct 2. Fair Oaks Shops,
Building D is worth $1,488,762 to Cibolo Creek Partners, and $860,695 has been asked for so far.
I worked them out with the real kernels in a scratch script before writing this.

## 1. How a bar becomes money

**One rule moves the work.** Each bar keeps the percent its trade has reported. What is left is
spread evenly over the days from tomorrow, or its start if later, to its finish. By a bill day the
bar is done to that point. On or after its finish it is 100%. A bar already past its finish counts
as done by the next bill day.

Our own crew's stages move the same way. A signed change order's work runs to the finish of the
bar its days landed on, or else its trade's last bar.

**The bill is computed the way the draft computes it.** For each bill day, the kernel makes a
copy of the job with every line at its projected percent. It runs the same `ownerPayApp` the draft
uses on that copy. So the trade's share of what the customer signed for, our costs and fee
following the trades, the retainage step, and the floor of what was already billed all come out
the same. The bill counts as sent, and the next month starts from it. The months run until the
schedule's last bar is done. No billing math is written twice.

On Fair Oaks D, as the schedule stands:

| Bill day | Done by then | We bill |
|---|---|---|
| Sun Oct 25 | 92% | $368,744 |
| Wed Nov 25 | 97% | $70,405 |
| Fri Dec 25 | 100% | $40,043 |
| With the final bill | | $148,876 they hold |

$860,695 asked so far, plus the three bills and what they hold, comes to the price, $1,488,762.
Each figure is rounded here, which puts the sum a dollar over. The kernel's own numbers add up
exactly.

**A move moves money.** If we take Summit Roofing's late notice (G-117, TPO membrane to Wed Oct
14), the rooftop units it pushes finish Mon Oct 26. **$6,601 of the Oct 25 bill moves to Nov
25.** Moving Site lighting a week later moves **$15,401** the same way.

## 4. A move says what it does to the bills

- **Why it moved** (`GcMoveExplain`), under what the move pushes and does to the finish:
  *Billing: $6,601 of the Oct 25 bill moves to Nov 25.* When it touches more than two months, or
  adds one, it lists each: *Oct 25 goes down $6,601. Nov 25 goes up $6,600.* When it moves no
  money, nothing is drawn.
- **Changes to the schedule**, on the move's row: *Billing: it moved $6,601 of the Oct 25 bill to
  Nov 25.* This works out the dates without that one move. It is drawn only while every bar the
  move touched still sits where the move left it, the same test Undo uses. Otherwise it says
  nothing, rather than a wrong number.

## 6. What the made-up data does not carry

A true forecast would want these. None is invented. Each is a stated rule, said on the screen
where it matters.

- **How work spreads inside a bar.** There is no curve or crew loading per line, so it is even
  over the bar's days.
- **The architect's cuts.** A bill counts as certified in full.
- **Materials stored ahead of the work.** There is no stored balance in the months. Stored
  materials count once they are work in place.
- **The day the customer releases retainage.** The contract's terms after acceptance are not
  in the data, so it shows *with the final bill* and no date.
- **A trade with no signed statement of work.** Its bars carry no customer dollars yet. If any
  part of the price is not on a bar, the card says *$N of the price is not on the schedule yet*.
- **The bill day.** It is Owner Billing's default, the 25th (`OWNER_BILL_DAY`, HANDOFF call 6).
  The forecast follows whatever it is.

It is pure, out of the barrel, and tested in `gcBillingForecast.test.ts`. It reads the schedule,

| `weekBillingShift(state, project)` | the job | what this week's standing moves changed, each reverted newest first |

## Is this the best we can do?

1. **See: a month's trades.** ▸ on a month lists the trades whose work it bills, from the same
   projected bill's lines, with our costs and fee spread in. *Roofing $100,000 · Electrical
   $98,600 · HVAC $93,600* is why October is big.
   **Picked (drawn into section 2).** It answers the first question anyone asks of a big month.
   It costs nothing, because the projected bill already has the lines.
2. **See: the cash weeks follow the bars too.** *The next 6 weeks* would count Oct 25 as the
   schedule's $368,744, not the draft's $98,566, a $270,178 gap in the week the money comes.
   **Not picked.** The trades' next draws, the out side, would have to follow the same bars in
   the same change, or the weeks would read rosier than they are. That changes `cashAhead`'s
   numbers and its tests in Owner Billing's kernel. It is a changed sentence, not an addition,
   so it is the lead's call. It would be one option on `cashAhead`, off by default, if you want it.
3. **Tell: the bars' pace instead of their drawn days.** TPO membrane's pace says Wed Oct 14
   while its bar says Fri Oct 9.
   **Not picked.** Two sets of dates would argue, the call the customer's *What changed* made too.
   The weekly walk is what moves a bar to its pace, and the forecast follows the bar the moment it
   does.
