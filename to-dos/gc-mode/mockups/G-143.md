# G-143: a trade's site-finish lines after the dry-in gate, not after its trims

Helper 2, branch `spike/g143` (from `spike/gc-mode` at `6b50cb035`), 2026-10-06. The row in
`GANTT_FEATURES.md`, raised in round three, is G-143: *In the first draft, a trade's site-finish
lines wait on the dry-in gate and their own earlier site lines, not on the trade's trims.* It is
New, Later, and the owner said yes in round four. This is the mockup and the plan, for the lead's go
before any code. The rule was tried in a scratch copy to measure what moves; nothing is committed
but this file.

## What is wrong today

The first draft runs a trade's lines in stage order, one after another, so a crew does its lines in
turn. The stages are listed in build order, and Site finish is listed after Trim. Yet it waits only
on dry-in, as its own comment says: "Site finish waits only on dry-in, so paving runs beside the
work inside".

That holds for Sitework and Concrete, whose lines before their site lines all come before dry-in.
It breaks for Electrical, whose Site lighting comes after its trims in the list. On Boerne Retail
Shell, drawn from Mon Nov 2, Site lighting waits on Fire alarm and runs Sun Jan 24 to Tue Feb 2,
after every trim. The parking lot lights become the job's last work and set its finish.

## The rule

In `scheduleDraft`, and in its copy for a new set of plans, `scheduleSetLines`:

- **Which lines count as site finish**: unchanged. They are the first draft's own stage words
  (`lineStage`): site lighting, sidewalk, striping, paving, drive-through, parking, planting,
  irrigation, sod, seed and landscaping, and every Landscaping line.
- **The gate they wait on**: unchanged. It is dry-in, or the nearest stage before it the job has
  work in. Pad B has no roof, so it waits on its slab.
- **What they wait on within their own trade**: this is the change.
  - Today a line waits on its trade's line before it in list order.
  - With the change, it waits on its trade's line before it among its own stage and the stages that
    stage comes after, along `after`. Call that the stage's chain.
  - Every stage but two waits on the stage listed just before it, so for them the chain is every
    stage listed before, and nothing changes.
  - Site finish waits on dry-in, so its chain is dry-in, structure, slab, underground, foundations
    and site prep. A site-finish line waits on its trade's earlier site-finish line, or else on its
    trade's last line before dry-in. It never waits on the trade's framing, rough-in, close-in,
    finishes or trims.
  - Closeout's chain skips site finish the same way, so a closeout line never waits on a site line.
  - One small function, `stageChain(key)`, serves both places. A set's new site line is then placed
    the way the first draft places it.

Trade by trade:

- **Electrical**: Site lighting waits only on the gate, the roof's four dry-in lines. On Boerne it
  runs Tue Dec 29 to Thu Jan 7, beside the rough-ins and trims, as paving does.
- **Sitework**: Paving still waits on Utilities, which is on its chain, and on the gate. Striping
  still waits on Paving. Unchanged.
- **Concrete**: Sidewalks and curbs still waits on Slab on grade and on the gate. Unchanged.
- **Landscaping**: its lines one after another from the gate. Unchanged; no made-up job has it.

## Left out, and why

- **Moving Site finish up the stage list, to just after dry-in.** It would read simpler. But the list
  is also the order every stage list is drawn in: the rough's rows, the customer's stages, the paper.
  So the customer would read Site finish before Framing, and many more readings would move. The
  chain changes only who waits on whom.
- **Site lighting waiting on the electrical service.** Lights need power to be tested, so the
  service and gear before them is a fair wait. But it is one line's special case in a draft that has
  none, and the office adds the wait in one window.

## Is this the best we can do?

Three ways to make it better, by the chart's jobs:

1. **See: one rule for every stage, the trade's line before it along its stage's chain.** It is
   today's rule wherever a stage waits on the one listed before it. It is the right rule where one
   does not, site finish and closeout. **Picked**: one bar moves on two made-up jobs, the golden
   walk is untouched, and a set places new work by the same rule.
2. **See: Site finish moved up the list.** **Not picked**, for the reason under *Left out*: it moves
   the order every stage list draws in.
3. **Chase: an inside crew and a site crew per trade.** The office could say a trade has two crews,
   and each would get a queue of its own. **Not picked**: the schedule has no crews (G-84's people
   count reads the bars), so it is a new record and the owner's call, worth its own row if crews
   ever come in.
