**The job, as the lead put it.** The trade's start reminder reads the same list the office's bar
reads, so a master agreement or a W-9 is never missing from one side and present on the other. It
goes out in the trade's language, with the Spanish list regenerated by its script.

  steps: award, master agreement, insurance, W-9 and statement of work. Insurance is read on the day
  the work starts, and a statement of work signed on older plans does not count. The bar's note and

  submittals not approved, insurance that runs out before the first day, and a statement of work
  not signed. It has no master agreement and no W-9, and it never says a statement of work is only
  drafted or is on older plans.
- **They disagree on a real case.** On Helotes started anyway, on Tue Oct 6, Kendall Air's 14-day
  reminder says only *Your statement of work is not signed yet.* Its bar waits on *a signed master
  agreement and a signed statement of work*. The statement of work is only drafted, so the trade is
  told to sign a paper it has not been sent.

## How it is built

- **`startNeeds` reads `startGaps`.** Its submittals stay as they are. Then each gap the office's
  bar has, on the same first day, is mapped to its sentence in the trade's language. The mapping is
  a `Record<StartGapKind, …>`, so a gap kind added to the office's list and not to the trade's fails
  the typecheck. That is the "never one side without the other", held by the compiler.
- **The words come from the gap, not a second reading.** Insurance *ran out* and *runs out* are
  told apart by the date against today, as the office's gap does. A statement of work's state comes
  from the gap's kind and the paper's status.
- **An import cycle broken first.** `gcNotReady.ts` imports `START_REMINDER_DAYS` from
  `gcStartReminders.ts` for its *late* day, and `gcStartReminders.ts` would now import
  `gcNotReady.ts`. A top-level constant read across that cycle is undefined on load. So
  `NOT_READY_LATE_DAYS` becomes its own `3`, with a test that it equals the last of
  `START_REMINDER_DAYS`.
- **The Spanish list** is regenerated with `portal-spanish-list.ts` and committed.

## Left out, and why

- **The office's words.** G-77's bar and block already read the list, so they do not change.
- **A reminder for a trade already on site.** G-114 stops once the crew is on the log or work is
  reported. G-138 is the office's word for a trade at work with its insurance run out. A second
  message to the trade is not this row (G-138's C).
- **Submittals into the office's gap list.** They are holds on the chart, with their own record and
  pill. Folding them into papers would put one fact in two places on the office side.

## Is this the best we can do?

**A. Chase: a button beside each line in the trade's portal.** The reminder could carry *Sign it* and
*Send it*, so the message is also the way to do it. **Not picked, because it is already there.** A
message's lines are the email's paragraphs, as plain text (`PortalMessage.lines`). Every message
carries the link to the company's portal home, whose to-dos already list the master agreement, the
insurance certificate and the W-9 (`todoMsa`, `todoCoi`, `todoW9`), each with its door. A button per
line would be a new message shape for a door the trade lands beside anyway.

**B. Tell: say when a paper came in.** The 3-day reminder could say *Your master agreement is
signed, thank you.* for a gap that closed since the 14-day one. **Not now.** It means keeping what
the first reminder said. Reminders are derived, not stored, on purpose (G-114's second look).

**C. See: show the office the trade's words.** The opened bar's block could say "The trade's reminder
says: …". **Not now.** It is the same list now, so the office's own words already say it.

So the build is the plan above, with none of the three: the one list is the whole of the row.
