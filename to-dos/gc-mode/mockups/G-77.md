# G-77: a trade not ready to start, said on its bar

Helper 5, branch `spike/g77` (from `origin/spike/gc-mode` at fca844a31), 2026-10-06.

**The row.** "A trade not ready to start: no signed statement of work, insurance run out. The
bar says so before its start day." Serves See and Chase. Phase 4, *Tie in what holds the work*.
Stands: Part (Get started).

**The job, as the lead put it.** The bar itself says, before the start day, that the trade is not
ready and why, and the office can act from there. A not-ready bar reads like a held bar. It does
not invent a second style.

## What is there today

- **Get started** (`startChecklist` in `gcStart.ts`) is the office's definition of ready. Each
  trade has five steps in order: awarded, master agreement, insurance, W-9, statement of work. It
  has a next-step sentence. Insurance is judged on today, not on the day the work starts.
- **Start reminders** (`startNeeds` in `gcStartReminders.ts`, G-114) are the trade's half. At 14
  and 3 days out its portal lists submittals not approved, insurance that runs out before its
  first day, and an unsigned statement of work.
- **Held bars** (G-16) are one look for every hold: amber stripes, the *held* pill and a note.
  The note reads *waits on …* and turns red when the hold is late. The *Held* filter and the
  group's *N held* chip count them, and the walk lists them. The Schedule tab builds the holds
  in `holdsOf`, from RFIs, then submittals, then waits. The customer's views pass no holds.
- **The papers' next steps** (`paperStep` in `gcPaperSend.ts`): each missing paper in a company's
  window has one button: *Send to sign*, *Remind them* or *Ask for it*. It opens the send with a
  due day, writes the promise and puts the paper on Follow up.

What is missing: none of this reaches the chart. On Fair Oaks D today, Pecan Valley Electric's
insurance ran out Sep 15. Its *Site lighting* starts Mon Oct 19 and reads *37 spare days* in grey.

## The kernel: `src/lib/gcMode/gcNotReady.ts`

Pure, out of the barrel, with `gcNotReady.test.ts`.

**Inputs:** `GcState` and the `GcProject`, using its schedule, packages, awards and statements of
work; the partners' master agreement, insurance date and W-9; the plan sets; the promises; and
`today`. Insurance looks 30 days ahead, the same `INSURANCE_ASK_DAYS` as the renewal ask. A gap is
late at 3 days, the last of `START_REMINDER_DAYS`.

**Outputs:**

```ts
export type StartGapKind = 'award' | 'msa' | 'insurance' | 'w9' | 'sow'

export interface StartGap {
  kind: StartGapKind
  /** Get started's column: "Insurance", "Statement of work". */
  label: string
  /** The block's line: "Insurance ran out Tue Sep 15." Insurance is read on the day the work starts. */
  line: string
  /** On the bar, alone: "current insurance, theirs ran out Sep 15". */
  barWords: string
  /** On the bar, in a list: "current insurance". */
  noun: string
  /** The company window's paper: 'msa' | 'insurance' | 'w9' | 'sow-<package>'. Null: an award. */
  doc: string | null
}

/** What a trade still needs before it starts work on `on`, in Get started's order. Empty: ready. */
export function startGaps(state: GcState, project: GcProject, pkg: TradePackage, on: string): StartGap[]

export interface NotReadyBar {
  lineId: string
  pkg: TradePackage
  partner: Partner | null
  start: string
  gaps: StartGap[]
  /** It starts within 3 days, or its day passed with nothing reported. */
  late: boolean
}

/** Every bar on a job being built that has not started, on a trade not ready for it. */
export function notReadyBars(state: GcState, project: GcProject): NotReadyBar[]

/** The bar's words after "waits on": one gap with its state, or several by name. */
export function notReadyWords(gaps: StartGap[]): string

/** The chart's holds with the not-ready bars added (kind 'paperwork'). A hold already there stays, after the papers. */
export function withNotReady(holds: Map<string, GanttHold>, state: GcState, project: GcProject): Map<string, GanttHold>

/** The opened activity's block: its title, each gap with its button, promise and hint, its last line. Null: nothing to say. */
export function notReadyBlock(state: GcState, project: GcProject, lineId: string): NotReadyBlock | null
```

**The rules:**

- **A bar counts** when the job is being built and the bar belongs to a trade. Inspections, our own
  crew's stages and added activities never count. The bar must not be done, have no real start
  recorded and have nothing reported. A trade on site still counts for its bars that have not
  started.
- **Ready is Get started's five steps.** Award, master agreement, W-9 and statement of work are read
  as Get started reads them, so the block and Get started's table never disagree. Signed on earlier
  plans is not ready, as on Get started.
- **Insurance is read on the bar's own start**, the one thing Get started cannot do. None on file is
  a gap. One that ran out is a gap. One that runs out before this starts is a gap once it is
  within 30 days, the day the renewal ask begins. Further out it is not a gap yet: most
  certificates are renewed in the usual run, and a bar striped five months early would be noise.
- **Late** is a start within 3 days, or a start that passed.

**No new action.** The button opens the company window's send, which plays `sendPaper` as it does
today. The golden test does not move: no fixture data, no reducer case and no reading it pins
changes.

  already chases the same papers on their own: insurance renewals and *papers we wait on*.
- **The trade's portal.** It already hears through the start reminders (G-114) and each paper's
  send.
- **A list of every not-ready trade.** That is G-115's call list (Helper 3), *each company's …
  held work*. If it reads the chart's holds, it carries these with nothing more.
- **The customer and the architect** never see a company's papers. Their views pass no holds.
- **The drafting stage.** See *Where it sits*.

## Is this the best we can do?

Three ways it could be better, by the chart's three jobs.

**A. Chase: one press to the send, not two.** The button names the paper's own next step and
opens the send itself (`send: true` on the company opener), the way Get started's *Send to sign*
opens the customer's. Without it the button can only open the company at the paper, and the
person presses its *Ask for it* again. **I pick this.** It is three small additions in the
Board's files, and it makes *the office can act from there* literal: from the bar, two presses
to an ask on its way with a day it is due. It needs your OK because it touches `GcMode.tsx`.

**B. See: the running bars too.** Pecan Valley has been on site since Sep 21 with insurance that ran
out Sep 15. Its *Lighting* and *Panels and feeders* bars are under way, so they are not waiting
to start and stay unstriped. A red note on a running bar would say *theirs ran out Sep 15* with
no stripes, since the work goes on. **Not now.** It goes past the row's *before its start day*,
and it means a new note in `GcGantt`'s `barNote`, a changed function in a shared file. The draws
already lock until a current certificate is in. I would offer it to the owner as its own row.
It is the bigger risk on a real job.

**C. Tell: one list for both sides.** The trade's reminder names submittals, insurance and the
statement of work. The bar names Get started's five steps, so a master agreement or W-9 can
hold a bar while the trade's reminder says it is all set. Making `startNeeds` read this kernel
would make the trade hear what the office sees, in its language. **Not now.** It rewrites G-114's
sentences and adds lines to the native speaker's Spanish list. Instead, test 7 holds that the
reminder never names a paper the bar does not, so the gap can only be the master agreement or
the W-9. I would raise it with the owner alongside B.

So the build is the plan above with A.
