# G-45: a rough schedule while bidding, for our bid's weeks to build

Helper 2, branch `spike/g45`, 2026-10-06. The row in `GANTT_FEATURES.md`: *A schedule while bidding:
a rough one from the stages, for our bid's "weeks to build"* (Tell, Later; round three). The mockup
and the plan, for the lead's go before any code. **Built 2026-10-06** on the lead's go with the pick;
`GANTT_PLAN.md` → *Later, a schedule while bidding, as built* says how it stands. One change from this
plan, the lead's lock: the weeks are kept as they went when the bid goes in (or at award), so the
record cannot drift from the proposal.

## What it is for

A customer comparing bids asks two things: how much, and how long. Our number answers the first from
the trades' quotes. Nothing answers the second: the bid has no duration anywhere, and the schedule is
drawn only after we win. So "how long" is a guess said on the phone. A rough schedule from the stages
of the job, drawn while we bid, makes the weeks come from a chart the office can stand behind, and the
same lengths seed the first draft when we win.

## The four decisions

1. **Where it sits while bidding: the Schedule tab, with the word "rough".** Every project already
   shows the Schedule tab, and on a job with no schedule it offers *Draw a first draft*. On a job
   still bidding, the tab opens instead on **A rough schedule for our bid**. Our number shows the
   weeks beside the price. No second tab, no card elsewhere: the place to look at dates is the
   Schedule tab at every stage.
2. **What draws it: the first draft's own kernel.** `scheduleDraft` (`gcNewProject.ts`, the one
   behind *Draw a first draft*) already draws from each trade's scope lines when it has no statement
   of work yet, which is every trade while we bid. Its lengths are the stages' usual days
   (`SCHEDULE_STAGES`). The rough adds one thing: the office can change a stage's days for this job.
   That is one optional argument to `scheduleDraft`, the job's stage days, each falling back to the
   usual one, so every caller today draws exactly what it draws now.
3. **What it feeds: one count, `roughWeeks(project)`, never a stored number.** The weeks run from the
   first day of work to substantial completion on the rough draw, rounded up to whole weeks. Our
   number shows them as *Weeks to build* with a sentence for the proposal, and *We sent our bid*'s
   log line names them. When our bid is sent, the rough locks: what went to the customer stays
   what it was, the way a sent bill keeps what it went with.
4. **At award: kept, not turned into the schedule; the first draft starts from it.** The rough is
   drawn on the trades' scope lines. Once we win, each trade's statement of work brings its own
   schedule-of-values lines, the lines it reports and bills on, and those are what the schedule's
   bars must be. So the rough is never copied into the schedule. It stays on the job as the record
   of the weeks we bid. *Draw a first draft* in buyout starts from the rough's start day and its
   stage lengths, and once drawn, the tab says the first draft's weeks against the bid's: *The first
   draft runs 15 weeks. We bid 14.*

**Nothing reaches the trades or the customer.** The rough lives in its own field on the job
(`project.rough`: the start day, the stage days, who drew it, when), never in `project.schedule`,
which every trade's and customer's view reads. Its chart has no Print or PDF and nothing on it moves.

(or *The first draft runs the 14 weeks we bid.*). Once building, the measures take over: the projected
finish against the contract's day.

## The kernel and the record

`src/lib/gcMode/gcRoughSchedule.ts`, pure, tested, out of the barrel:

```ts
/** The rough schedule's draw: the first draft's kernel on the trades' scope lines, with the job's stage days. Null: none drawn. */
export function roughDraw(project: GcProject): ProjectSchedule | null
/** Weeks to build on the rough draw: first day of work to substantial completion, rounded up. Null: none drawn. */
export function roughWeeks(project: GcProject): { weeks: number; days: number; start: string; finish: string } | null
/** The stages the job has, in build order, with the days the rough uses (the job's, else the usual). */
export function roughStages(project: GcProject): { key: string; label: string; days: number; usual: number; start: string | null; finish: string | null }[]
/** "We will build Boerne Retail Shell in 14 weeks from the day work starts." Null: none drawn. */
export function proposalWeeksWords(project: GcProject): string | null
/** On the first draft, against the bid: "The first draft runs 15 weeks. We bid 14." Null: no rough, or no first draft. */
export function firstDraftAgainstBid(project: GcProject): string | null
```

- `gcTypes.ts`, additions: `RoughSchedule { start; days: Record<string, number>; by; on }`, `rough?` on
  `GcProject`, a new action `setRough { projectId, start, days, by }`, and an optional `days` on
  `draftSchedule`.
- `gcReducer.ts`, additions: `setRough` draws or redraws, only while bidding, not lost and before the
  bid is sent, with a log line; `draftSchedule` passes `days` through; `markBidSent`'s line adds the
  weeks when there is a rough.
- `gcNewProject.ts`: `scheduleDraft(project, start, days?)`, the optional stage days, falling back to
  the usual; `gcBuildingSchedule.ts`'s `draftSchedule` passes them.
- The golden test: `setRough` in its `all` list with a `used.add` naming `gcRoughSchedule.test.ts`.
  No fixture change, no new step: no snapshot moves.

## Left out, and why

- **Moving single bars on the rough.** The rough is stages and their days. Bars are the first draft's,
  on the trades' real lines.
- **The full Gantt for the rough.** Its activities are scope lines that will be replaced; the stage rows
  say what a bid needs.
- **Sending anything to the customer.** The app sends nothing while we bid; the proposal sentence is
  the office's to use.

## Is this the best we can do?

Three ways to make it better, by the chart's jobs:

1. **Tell: general conditions from the weeks.** Most of general conditions is time: the
   superintendent, the trailer, temporary power. A weekly rate times the rough's weeks would make a
   longer schedule raise the price by itself. **Not picked**: it ties the price to the schedule, a
   change to how we price every bid, which is the owner's call. The weeks sit beside the general
   conditions so the office sees both.
2. **See: jobs like it.** A line such as *Jobs like it took about N weeks* beside our 14 would catch a
   rough that is too hopeful. **Not picked**: the past jobs the prototype keeps (`gcScopeBook.ts`) carry
   scope lines, not durations; the real build reads closed jobs' schedules.
3. **Chase: the bid against the first draft.** Once we win, say whether the first draft keeps the
   weeks we bid: *The first draft runs 15 weeks. We bid 14.* **Picked**: it is the one place the bid's
   promise meets the real plan, before the customer signs a contract day, and it costs one sentence
   from the two counts the rows already make.
