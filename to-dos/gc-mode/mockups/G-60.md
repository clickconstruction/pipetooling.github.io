**The row.** "The daily log and the chart disagree: a trade on site with no bar that week, or a bar
with nobody on site." Serves See. Phase 4. It stands Part: the walk puts the log's days on site
beside what the trade reported, and the verify list does too.

**The job, as the lead put it.** The gap is the chart itself and the week as a whole. One case is a
week where the log has a company on site and the chart has no bar for it. The other is a bar
running with nobody from that company in the log. Each is said where someone looks, with what to do
about it. The chart keeps its one look for a bar: the note, the hover card and a card, no new bar
style. There is no fixture change and no snapshot move.

## What is there today

- **The daily log** (`gcBuildingLog.ts`) is one log a day. Each log lists the crews by trade with
  their workers (`crews: { packageId, workers }`), the delays with whose and why, and whether the
  weather stopped work. It knows a trade, not a bar. `onSite` gives a trade's days and worker-days.
  `onSiteWords` gives the walk's sentence. *This week* on the Daily log tab is Monday to Friday.
- **The walk** (`gcScheduleWalk.ts`) shows the log's week beside each bar it lists: *The daily log
  has them on site Mon, Tue and Thu, 14 worker-days.* or *Not on site in the 3 days logged that
  week.* It lists only bars, so a trade with no bar never comes up. It is walked once a week.
- **Weather days** (G-58, `lostDaysByLine`) are already read off the same log: a day the weather
  stopped a trade is a lost day on its bars.
- **The chart's note** (`barNote` in `GcGantt.tsx`) is the one thing worth saying beside a bar. In
  order, it says a failed inspection, a hold, a change order's tail, or how far it moved.

On Fair Oaks D today the log and the chart agree. Every crew logged this week has a bar running,
and every running bar's trade is on site. So nothing new shows on the made-up data. The browser
recipe below writes the logs that disagree.

## The two cases, read the same way the walk reads the week

**This week** is Monday to today, on the days with a log. That is the walk's week and the Daily log
tab's.

**A bar runs on a day** when the day is inside its planned dates. It also runs when it is not done
and its finish has passed, since the work is still going. And it runs inside its real dates, once
the walk or the opened activity recorded them (G-55).

1. **On site, no bar.** On a logged day, a trade's crew is on site and none of its bars runs. One
   day is enough: a crew on site is a fact.
2. **A bar running, nobody on site.** A trade's bars that are not done run on 2 or more logged
   weekdays, and its crew is on none of them. Some days do not count. A day the log says the
   weather stopped the site, the job or that trade is G-58's weather, read the same way. A bar
   that is held is explained by its hold. One day off is ordinary, so it takes two. A crew there
   on some days and not others is the walk's pace, not this row.

Our own crew counts like any trade. Inspections and added activities have no crew in the log, so
they never count.

## The kernel: `src/lib/gcMode/gcLogVsChart.ts`

Pure, out of the barrel, with `gcLogVsChart.test.ts`.

**Inputs:** `GcState`, the `GcProject` (its daily logs, schedule, packages and awards), and the
chart's holds (`Map<lineId, GanttHold>`, optional), so a held bar is explained by its hold. It reads
`onSite` the way the walk does, and the log's weather the way G-58 does: a stopped site, or a
weather delay for the job or the trade. On a storm day a crew that never came is explained too,
not only the crews logged.

**Outputs:**

```ts
export type LogChartKind = 'absent' | 'noBar'

export interface LogChartGap {
  kind: LogChartKind
  pkg: TradePackage
  /** "Summit Roofing", or "Our own crew". */
  company: string
  /** The company to open. Null: our own crew. */
  partnerId: string | null
  /** absent: the logged weekdays its bars ran without it. noBar: the days it was on site with none of its bars running. */
  days: string[]
  /** noBar: its worker-days on those days. */
  workerDays: number
  /** absent: its bars running this week, not done and not held. */
  running: { lineId: string; name: string; finish: string }[]
  /** noBar: its next bar that has not started, for "It started early". */
  next: { lineId: string; name: string; start: string } | null
  /** noBar: its last bar, when all its work finished before these days. */
  last: { lineId: string; name: string; finish: string } | null
  /** The card's sentences, and what to do. */
  words: string
  todo: string
}

/** This week's disagreements, the trades not on site first, then the ones on site with no bar. */
export function logChartGaps(state: GcState, project: GcProject, holds?: Map<string, GanttHold>): LogChartGap[]

/** By bar: the note beside it and the hover card's line. Absent: each running bar. On site, no bar: the next bar. */
export function logChartNotes(gaps: LogChartGap[]): Map<string, { note: string; words: string }>

/** Weekdays logged without the crew before a running bar says so. */
export const LOG_ABSENT_DAYS = 2
```

**No new action.** *It started* plays `setActualDates`, which is in the golden test already.

## Left out, and why

- **The board row, the ring card, Follow up and Needs you.** A *log and chart disagree* count there
  moves the golden walk's snapshots, so it is the owner's call.
- **A crew there some days and not others.** That is the walk's pace (*At this pace it finishes
  …*), and the walk already shows the log's days beside it.
- **Weekend logs for a bar with nobody on site.** A quiet Saturday is never news. A crew on site on
  a Saturday with no bar is still said.
- **Missing logs.** *No daily log for Wed Sep 30* is the Daily log tab's own warning. A day with
  no log says nothing either way.
- **The List view.** It has no notes. The card is the list.
- **B, a Follow up call from a row.** The sheet belongs to the page, and G-115's call list is where a call belongs.
- **C, a one-press move with the log's reason.** The log gives a reason but not a day, and the editor and the walk ask for the day.

## Is this the best we can do?

Three ways it could be better, by the chart's three jobs.

**A. See, where the log is written.** The Schedule tab is where the office looks. But the person who
knows why a trade was on site is the one who just wrote the log. The card's *on site, no bar* rows on
the Daily log tab, under *This week*, ask the question at that moment. *It started Fri Oct 2* answers
it there in one press. **I pick this.** It is one line in `GcBuildingLog.tsx` reusing the card. The
*not on site* rows stay on the Schedule tab, which holds the chart's holds that explain them.

**B. Chase: the call, from the row.** A trade not on site wants a call. The card could open the Follow
up sheet on the company with the reason filled in. **Not now.** The sheet belongs to the page, so
the Schedule tab has no way in yet. The company's name already opens its window, with the phone and
the Activity to log the call. I would make it one row of G-115 (Helper 3's call list) rather than a
second door.

**C. Tell: one press to move with the log's reason.** When the log gave a reason for the absence, the
move could fill itself in, the way the walk's *Add the N lost days* does for weather. **Not now.** The
log gives a reason but not a day. *Panel boards are two weeks out* is a sentence, not a date. The
editor and the walk already ask for the day. If the log grows a *back on* day, this is a small
follow-up.

So the build is the plan above with A.
