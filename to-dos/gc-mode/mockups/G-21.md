# G-21: Print or PDF, the chart as it is shown

Helper 2, branch `spike/g21`, 2026-10-06. The row in `GANTT_FEATURES.md`: *Print and PDF of the chart
as it is filtered, on one or more landscape pages* (Tell, Phase 3). The mockup and the plan, for the
lead's go before any code.

## What it is for

The owner's ask for the Gantt: "an accurate look at the project … and be able to communicate that to
the owner and follow up on the trades." The chart is the best picture of a job the app has, and it
lives only on a screen that scrolls. Three people want it on paper:

- **The superintendent**: on the trailer wall, and in hand at the Monday meeting with the trades
  (See, Chase).
- **The office**: as a PDF on an email to the customer or the architect (Tell).
- **Whoever runs the meeting with the customer and the architect**: the chart on the table, no
  laptop (See, Tell).

The only print on the Schedule tab today is the letter card's *Print or PDF* (G-94), and it prints
words, not the chart.

## The decision

**Print or PDF** on the chart's toolbar opens a window, **Print the chart**. It says what will print,
asks who it is for, and shows the pages small. Its own **Print or PDF** opens the pages in the
browser's print dialog, where *Save as PDF* is one of the printers.

The pages are a document of their own, drawn from the same bars the chart draws (`ganttBars`,
`ganttFilter`, `ganttGroups`), not the screen sent to a printer. The chart on screen scrolls
sideways, draws only the rows in view (G-135), sticks its names to the left and keeps its facts in
hover cards. A browser printing it would cut it at the page's edge and drop the rows not drawn. The
document is drawn in points on letter landscape pages, one SVG a page, so it comes out the same on
every printer and in every PDF reader. It is light by construction, whatever the app's theme.

This is how the app prints elsewhere: a pure kernel writes the page as HTML, and
`printHtmlInNewWindow` (`src/lib/bidDocuments/htmlDoc.ts`) opens it and calls print. The Lien grid
(`lienTimelineBook.ts`) and the procurement log (`procurementLog.ts`) are letter landscape the same
way, with `@page { size: letter landscape }`.

**"As it is filtered" means as you have it.** The filters, the grouping, the folds and *Hide the
links* carry over to paper. The zoom does not: the paper picks its own scale so the whole span fits
the page's width.

## Where the press sits

- **The Schedule tab → the chart's card → the toolbar's first row**, at the right end after
  *Today*, *Hide the links* and *Open all*: a quiet button **Print or PDF**, drawn like *Today*.
  Its title: *Opens the chart as you see it on landscape pages, to print or save as a PDF.*
- **In both views.** On *List* it still prints the chart. The list is how a phone reads the chart;
  the paper is the chart.
- **Off when nothing passes the filters**, titled *Nothing passes these filters, so there is nothing

  - The customer, when they read stages (an owner, as on Fair Oaks D): *Cibolo Creek Partners
    reads the stages of the job, as in their portal. Their copy names no company and shows no spare
    days. The filters and folds do not change it.*

    chart as you see it. It names no company and shows no spare days.* Grouped by company, one more:

- **As it will print**: a frame with the document in it, scaled to the window's width. It scrolls
  through the pages. It redraws when *Who it is for* changes.
- **Cancel** (quiet) and **Print or PDF** (primary). Print or PDF closes the window.

On a phone the window is a sheet from the bottom, like *Why it moved*.

The ASCII's marks: `█` done, or the done part of a bar under way; `▒` the part left; `░` not
started; `/` held; `:` an inspection; `▬` a group as one bar; `[ ]` the red edge; `●` asked or
ordered, `◆` expected, `!` the day the work needs it; `◂ ▸` a bar cut at the page's edge; `|` today.

How paper draws each:

- **The bars** look as on screen: the chart's saturated status colors and the light theme's fills.
  Paper adds patterns so a black and white printer keeps the meaning: held is striped, an
  inspection dashed, a change order's days hatched, a weather day dark striped. Every standing is
  also a word in *Where it stands*.
- **The left columns** replace the hover card: *Activity*, *Dates*, *Done*, *Plan*, *Where it
  stands*. *Done* and *Plan* are blank on inspections and added activities, which report no
  percent, and both columns drop while the schedule is being drawn.
- **A note beside a bar** uses the screen's words (`barNote`): what holds it, a failed inspection's
  days, change-order days not on the dates yet, days later or sooner than at Start. A note that would
  run off the page goes to the left of its bar.
- **A thin line under** a moved bar is the plan at Start. **A thin green line over** a bar is the days
  it really ran (G-55).
- **The dates the job must meet**, staggered on two lines as on screen, on every page. **What the
  work waits on**, on page 1 only, as it sits on top of the screen.
- **Today** is a blue line down the page, with *today* in the head.
- **Weekends** are tinted only when a day is 6 points wide or more, as in the look-ahead. Holidays
  keep the screen's small amber mark in the head.
- **Links** are drawn when the chart draws them: red on the tight chain, grey otherwise, and only
  between two bars on the same page.

## The kernel

`src/lib/gcMode/gcGanttPrint.ts`: pure, tested, out of the barrel. It reads `gcGantt.ts`. Only the
window reads it.

```ts
export type GanttPrintFor = 'team' | 'customer'

/** What the tab knows that the chart does not: the job's words and the customer's picture. */
export interface GanttPrintJob {
  name: string                // "Fair Oaks Shops, Building D"
  place: string               // "7920 Fair Oaks Pkwy, Fair Oaks Ranch"
  company: string             // GC_COMPANY.name
  by: string                  // who prints, for the foot
  finishWords: string         // projectedFinish's why, then the contract's day, as the Measures card says them
  doneWords: string           // "72% of the work is done. We planned 76% by today."
  customer: {
    name: string
    everyBar: boolean         // customerMaySeeEveryBar (call 3)
    finishWords: string       // customerStanding().finishWords
    stages: CustomerStage[]   // customerStages
    milestones: MilestoneRow[]
    changes: string[]         // customerChanges
    asks: string[]            // customerAsks().map((a) => a.words)
  }
}

/** The chart as the person has it, read from GcGantt's own state. */
export interface GanttPrintInput {
  bars: GanttBar[]            // every bar: ganttBars' output, GcGantt's `all`
  filters: GanttFilters
  by: GanttGroupBy
  folded: ReadonlySet<string>
  links: boolean              // false when Hide the links is pressed
  milestones: MilestoneRow[]
  waits: WaitRow[]
  lost: Map<string, LostDay[]>
  today: string
  building: boolean
  for: GanttPrintFor
  job: GanttPrintJob
}

/** One row on paper: a group heading, a bar, the waits' heading and rows, or a customer's stage. */
export type GanttPrintRow =
  | { kind: 'group'; key: string; title: string; sub: string; start: string; finish: string; pct: number | null; stands: string; folded: boolean; continued: boolean }
  | { kind: 'bar'; bar: GanttBar; stands: string; note: { words: string; red: boolean } | null; cut: { start: boolean; finish: boolean } }
  | { kind: 'waitsHead' }
  | { kind: 'wait'; row: WaitRow; who: string | null }
  | { kind: 'stage'; stage: CustomerStage }

export interface GanttPrintAxis {
  first: string                // a Monday
  last: string
  ptPerDay: number
  months: { label: string; at: number; days: number }[]
  ticks: { label: string; at: number; strong: boolean }[]
  marked: { at: number; weekend: boolean; holiday: string | null }[]
  weekends: boolean            // tinted only at 6 points a day or more
}

export interface GanttPrint {
  title: string                // the document's title, which Chrome offers as the PDF's name
  head: string[]               // page 1's sentences
  running: string              // the line atop pages 2 and after
  shows: string[]              // the window's sentences: the pages, what it shows, the folds, the lines, who it is for
  columns: ('activity' | 'dates' | 'done' | 'plan' | 'stands')[]
  axis: GanttPrintAxis
  pages: GanttPrintRow[][]
  links: { from: string; to: string; critical: boolean; page: number }[]
  key: string[][]              // the marks each page uses, in the key's order
  lists: { title: string; lines: string[] }[]   // the customer's What changed and What we need
  foot: string                 // "Printed Fri Oct 2, 2026 by Robert Douglas, Click Construction."
}

export function ganttPrint(input: GanttPrintInput): GanttPrint
export function ganttPrintHtml(print: GanttPrint): string
```

### Which rows print, under which filter

The rows are the chart's, picked by the chart's own kernel, so paper and screen cannot disagree.

| On | Prints |
|---|---|
| Nothing | every bar |
| 5 or fewer spare days | the tight bars (`b.tight`) |
| Late or behind | late, behind (due today counts) and failed |
| Held | the bars something holds |
| Next 3 weeks | under way, or starting within 21 days; with pick 2, the look-ahead window |
| Moved since Start | moved and not done |
| Two or more | the bars that pass every one, as on screen |

- **Grouped** by the chart's grouping, in its order (`ganttGroups(shown, by)`). A group prints only
  with a bar that passes, and its one bar spans those bars.
- **A folded group** prints as its one bar, with no rows under it.
- **The dates to meet** print on every page. **What the work waits on** prints on page 1, whatever
  the filters, as on screen.
- **The axis** is `ganttAxis` over the shown bars instead of all of them: their dates and plan at
  Start, the dates to meet and today, from the Monday a week before to two weeks after. So a filtered
  print draws close.
- **The scale** is the page's width over the days. At 10 points a day or more every day is marked
  (the look-ahead); at 2 or more, every Monday (Fair Oaks D draws 2.4); under 2, the 1st and the
  15th (a job longer than about eight months).
- **The customer's copy** for an owner ignores the filters and folds: the stages are their picture.

### How rows split across pages

- **Page 1**: the head, the column heads and months, the dates to meet, the waits, then rows.
  **Pages 2 and after**: the running line, the heads and months, the dates to meet, then rows. Every
  page ends with its key and *Page n of N*.
- A bar row is 13 points and a group heading 15. A page takes rows while they fit.
- **A group heading never ends a page.** It moves to the next page with its first row.
- **A group cut by a page** repeats its heading at the top of the next one, with *continued* after
  its title.
- **A link** prints when both its bars are on the same page.

## Left out, and why

- **A long job's weeks tiled across pages.** The paper fits the whole span to its width. Past about
  eight months its marks become the 1st and 15th. Tiles mean taping pages on the wall; worth it when
  a real job needs it.
- **A paper size choice.** A letter PDF prints on 11 by 17 with the dialog's *Fit to page*.
- **A line from a bar on one page to a bar on another.** It would need a stub and a page number.
- **One trade's own sheet.** The trade's portal chart (G-110) already is that, in its language. A
  one-company paper waits on the one-company filter (G-13).
- **A record that a print was made.** It changes nothing on the job. A schedule sent to the customer
  and kept as sent is G-94.
- **The zoom.** Paper picks its own scale.
- **A tour stop.** The walkthrough's chart stop could name the button, but `gcTour.ts` is shared and
  its stops are counted by other tests. It waits for the lead's word.
- **Dark mode.** The paper is light in both themes.

## Is this the best we can do?

Three ways to make it better, one for each of the chart's jobs:

1. **Tell: a copy for the customer.** Our chart names every company and shows the spare days and the
   red chain. The plan's firmest rule is that the customer never sees a company's name or spare
   days, and the first PDF the office emails from a plain print would break it. **Who it is for**
   gives them their own picture: the stages for an owner, every bar without names for a GC or an
   owner's rep (call 3), with what changed and what we need from them under it. **Picked.** Telling
   the customer is the row's own job, and the words and the stages already exist in the portal's
   kernels. It costs a switch in the window and one branch in the kernel.
2. **Chase: the three-week look-ahead sheet.** The paper printed most on a job is the look-ahead for
   the Monday trade meeting. With *Next 3 weeks* on, the page runs from last week to three weeks
   out, a day at a time, so each trade's days read straight off the page. **Picked.** It is one rule
   in the kernel's span, and it makes the filter mean on paper what it means on the job.
3. **See: a wall copy.** 11 by 17, or a long job's weeks tiled across pages, for the trailer wall.
   **Not picked.** A letter PDF already prints on 11 by 17 with *Fit to page*, and tiles help only
   jobs longer than about eight months. It is better built when a real job asks for it.

If the lead drops pick 1, the window goes with it: **Print or PDF** on the toolbar prints our team's
copy at once.
