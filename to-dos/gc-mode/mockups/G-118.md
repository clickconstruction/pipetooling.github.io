# G-118: the superintendent's morning list

Helper 5, branch `spike/g118` (from `origin/spike/gc-mode` at 2332f0019), 2026-10-06.

**The row.** "The superintendent's morning list from the chart: who should be on site today and
what they are doing." Serves See and Chase. Phase 4. It stands Part: the daily log.

**The job, as the lead put it.** The list is the chart read for one day. It has every bar running
that day by trade and company: what the activity is, its state (behind, held and why, not ready, a
late notice open), and who is expected. It also has the inspections and deliveries due that day. It
goes on the Daily log tab above *This week*, today by default, with the day pickable, so yesterday's
list can sit beside yesterday's log. The superintendent sees who is missing by mid-morning and has
the company's name to call. It is a pure kernel, with no fixture change and no snapshot move. Where
the fixture carries no count, it shows what the log has and says so.

## What is there today

- **The Daily log tab** is where the superintendent already is each morning. Today's log is on top,
  started from the day before's crews. Then *This week*, whose day cards pick the day. Then G-60's
  *on site, no bar* rows.
- **The chart's rows, holds and waits.** `scheduleMeasures` gives every bar with its percent, and
  `ganttBars` its standing. The chart's holds are the submittals, RFIs and waits (`holdsOf`) plus
  G-77's papers (`withNotReady`). `waitRows` has the deliveries, decisions, permits and the
  utility with their expected days. G-117's `openLateNotices` has a trade's own word that it will
  be late.
- **A bar runs on a day** by G-60's rule (`runsOn` in `gcLogVsChart.ts`): inside its planned dates,
  past its finish while not done, or inside its real dates.
- **No trade reports a crew count.** The only count is the daily log's workers by trade. So
  *expected* is the log's last count for that company, and the list says so.
- **A company's phone** comes from its record through `partnerReach`. It is made up on the
  prototype until the record has one, and it is a `tel:` link.

**The words:**

- **Header:** *Who should be on site · {Fri Oct 2}*. Under it: *{5} companies on {6} activities,
  and {an inspection}.* With nothing running: *Nothing on the chart runs this day.*
- **Each company:** its name (it opens the company's window), its trade, and its contact's first name
  and phone as a call link. Our own crew has no phone. The line under the name has one of these
  forms:
  - *Last on the log {Thu Oct 1} with {3}.*
  - *Not on the daily log before.*
  - *On the log for {Thu Oct 1} with {3}.*, or *On today's log with {3}.*, in green.
  - *Not on the log for {Thu Oct 1}. Last on it {Tue Sep 29} with {5}.*, or *Not on today's log …*,
    in red.
- **Each bar:** the activity's name, where the day sits in it (*first day*, *last day*, *day 12 of
  19*, *2 days past its finish*), its percent done, and the chart's own pill. The pill reads
  *behind*, *ahead*, *due today*, or *3 days late* in the chart's words.
  - A hold says *waits on {submittal 07 62 00-01}*, the chart's note.
  - An open late notice from G-117 says *says it will finish {Mon Oct 12}*.
  - Not ready from G-77 is a hold of kind paperwork, so it reads like one: *waits on current
    insurance, theirs ran out Sep 15*.
- **Inspections** running that day: *{Electrical service inspection}, the city.* If it failed before:
  *It is seen again today. It failed {Mon Sep 28}: {the note}. That was {Pecan Valley Electric}'s work.*
- **Expected today:** each open wait expected that day, *{The transformer}, from {CPS Energy}.*
- **The foot:** *No log for today yet. Each company's count comes in when it is written.* Once
  written, nothing.

**Picking the day.** The list reads the tab's own day. The *This week* cards pick it, and the
caught-up days pick it, so a picked day shows its list and its log one under the other. The header
adds *‹ {Thu}* for the day before, and *{Sat} ›* when the day is not today, so Monday can reach
Friday. It goes no earlier than the day work started.

Every sentence passes `plainWordsFailures`. The bar lines are labels, not sentences.

## The kernel: `src/lib/gcMode/gcMorningList.ts`

Pure, out of the barrel, with `gcMorningList.test.ts`.

**Inputs:** `GcState`, the `GcProject`, the chart's holds, and the day (default `state.today`).

**Outputs:**

```ts
export interface MorningBar {
  lineId: string
  /** "TPO membrane". */
  name: string
  /** "first day", "last day", "day 12 of 19", "2 days past its finish". */
  dayWords: string
  pct: number
  /** The chart's pill in its own words, then its hold or a late notice. */
  flags: { words: string; tone: 'red' | 'amber' | 'green' | 'grey' }[]
  held: boolean
}

export interface MorningCompany {
  pkg: TradePackage
  company: string
  partnerId: string | null
  /** Its contact's first name and phone. Null: our own crew. */
  call: { first: string; phone: string } | null
  bars: MorningBar[]
  /** The log's last count before the day. Null: never on the log before. */
  lastOnLog: { on: string; workers: number } | null
  /** The day's own log, once written: its count, or 0 when written without them. Null: no log that day. */
  onTheDay: number | null
  /** The line under the name. */
  logWords: string
  /** Not on the day's written log: called first, in red. */
  missing: boolean
}

export interface MorningList {
  day: string
  expected: MorningCompany[]
  /** Every bar it has that day is held. */
  heldOff: MorningCompany[]
  inspections: { lineId: string; words: string }[]
  /** Waits expected that day: "The transformer, from CPS Energy." */
  arriving: string[]
  logWritten: boolean
  /** The header's line and the foot's, in plain words. */
  summary: string
  foot: string | null
}

export function morningList(state: GcState, project: GcProject, holds: Map<string, GanttHold>, day?: string): MorningList
```

**The rules:**

- **Which bars:** a trade's bar that runs that day (G-60's `runsOn`), not done unless its real finish
  is on or after that day. Inspections are listed apart. Added activities have no company to
  expect, so they are left out.
- **Order:** the missing first, then the job's trades in order. Each company's bars go in chart
  order.
- **Expected:** a company with at least one bar running that is not held. Held ones go under
  *Held, not expected*.
- **The count:** the log's latest crew for the company before that day. *Before*, so the list reads
  the same before and after the day's log is written. The day's own log only answers *on it or not*.
- **No new action, no fixture change.** The golden test does not move.

## Left out, and why

- **A crew count from the trade.** The model has none, so the list shows the log's last count and
  says so. See C.
- **Milestones due that day.** They are the office's dates to meet, on the chart and in the walk, not
  a crew to expect.
- **The customer's decisions due that day.** That is office work, already on What we need from you.
  The list keeps the waits that arrive or work on site: deliveries, the utility, a permit.
- **Pecan Valley's insurance on its running bars.** It is still G-138, the owner's call. The list
  shows it only where the chart's holds do, on bars not started.
- **The board, Follow up and Needs you.** These are untouched: no snapshot moves.

## Is this the best we can do?

Three ways it could be better, by the chart's three jobs.

**A. Chase: the missing first, in red, once the log says so.** A list in trade order makes the
superintendent find the gap. Ordering by *not on the day's log* first puts the call at the top with
the phone beside it. Yesterday's list read against yesterday's log is then the morning's call sheet.
**I pick this.** It is a sort and a line in the kernel, and it is what *who is missing* means.

**B. See and Chase: tick them in as they arrive.** A *Here* tick beside each company could put it on
today's log with its usual count. Then *missing* is true by 9 a.m., not only once the log is
written. **Not now.** It changes how the log gets written: today it starts from the day before's
crews in one form. A half-written log saved at 7 a.m. is a new habit for the superintendent. That
is the owner's call, and it would be its own row.

**C. Tell: the trade's own count.** The trade could say in its portal, with its weekly look-ahead
marks, how many it sends. Then the count is their promise, not the log's habit, and a short crew is
news. **Not now.** It is a new answer in the portal in two languages, with lines for the native
speaker's Spanish list. Until then the log's count, said as the log's, is honest.

So the build is the plan above with A, and the holds moved into one kernel if you say yes.
