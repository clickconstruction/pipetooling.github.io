Each company can only say yes or no to our scope lines. The trade has one list of Known exclusions, set by us. Anything else a company leaves out ends up in a free-text note that the totals never read.

After: each company's exclusions, side by side and in the price

Each quote carries its own list of exclusions. Every exclusion any company names gets a row, so everyone is compared on the same things. A company that left it out gets a cost to cover it, just like a scope line. A trade's Known exclusions show as expected and cost nothing.

With what each one leaves out covered, Tri-County is lowest, by $650. Lonestar's quote is lowest as sent, by $8,100. What it leaves out adds $13,250.

How it works, in short

  • ModelA quote gets a list of exclusions: a name from a shared list for the trade (or typed), and optionally a unit price ("rock at $38/cy"). The names are matched across companies, so "permits", "permit fees" and "Permits and fees" are one row.
  • Compare quotesA Their exclusions section, one row per exclusion any company named. Each cell says excluded, included, not said, or expected when it's one of the trade's Known exclusions. An excluded cell gets cover, a cost that goes into All in exactly as a left-out scope line does, so the lowest number reflects it. not said gets Ask them, which uses the ask thread. A warning names a flip, like "Tri-County is lowest once exclusions are covered".
  • PortalThe quote form gets What your quote leaves out: the trade's common exclusions as ticks, plus typed ones, in both languages. The office can enter the same list from an emailed quote.
  • Per companyEach company keeps a record across its quotes ("Permits and fees, 5 of 6"). It shows on Trade partners and the company's card, and can pre-ask "please include permits" in the next invitation.
  • The bookThe shared list of exclusion names grows from what companies write, beside the scope book, so a trade's ticks get better over time.
