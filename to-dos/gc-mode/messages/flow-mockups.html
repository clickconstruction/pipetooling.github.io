The flow panel, three ways, and where a flow is edited

A second side bar at the right of a thread, folded by default, that shows the person's journey with us and where they are in it: what is done, what is on now, whose move it is, and what comes next. A trade partner's journey is onboarding once, then cycles (asked to quote, awarded, draws); a customer's is the bid, the contract, the build, and pay application cycles; an architect's is questions, submittals and certificates. Same data in every draft: Greg Paulk of Hillside Excavation on the Boerne bid, and Dr. Priya Raman on the Helotes project.

A · The stepper: one line down, cycles as rows

A vertical list of steps with a dot each: green done, orange now, gray next. A cycle shows as a dashed row inside its step, one pill per round, so "asked to quote" reads as three rounds with the current one lit. Each step says whose move it is and opens its surface.

B · The chart: boxes and arrows in two lanes

A real flow chart, small: an Onboarding lane that runs once, then a Per job lane with the loops drawn as dashed boxes, a decision drawn as a slanted box. The current box is orange; done boxes are green; the loop shows which round is running.

The journey as phases that fold open, each with its items and who is on the hook for each, us or them, in the court's colors. Less a picture, more a to-do list that both sides can read. The counts on each phase say how far along it is.

Where a flow is edited: Settings → Flows

One page per kind of person. A flow is a list of steps; each step names what finishes it (a fact the app already keeps, picked from a list), whose move it is, and when to nudge. Cycles are steps marked repeats with what starts a round. The panel draws whatever is here, so changing the journey is editing this list, not code. Shown: the trade partner's flow.

"Finished when" is picked from facts the app keeps today (the ask, the papers, the award, the statement of work, the draws, the waivers), never typed. A step the owner adds that the app cannot see becomes a tick the team sets by hand.

What it changes elsewhere: the same list can drive the nudges Follow up sends and the ring on the Board, so one edit moves all three. That is the reason to build the flow as data, not as a drawing.

My pick: B for the panel, with C's "us / them" tags on the current box, and the editor as drawn. B is the picture you asked for and keeps cycles as loops; the tags carry the court into it. A is the cheaper first cut if the chart is too much to draw at 300 px. Whatever the panel looks like, the editor is the part that pays: the journey becomes a list the office owns.
