Bill day · a summary at the top, three ways

The page today opens with a strip of five sections, six calendar cards, a seven-line gate list and then the tables. All of it is true and none of it says, in one look, where the money is and what to do today. These three put one picture at the top and fold the rest. 4. Is this the best we can do?

  • Why a bar and not a pie. The question is "how much is still out", and a bar reads left to right the way the sections do below it: not in, sent back, waiting, approved. The same colors tint the section bars, so the picture and the page agree.
  • Dollars on the bar, counts in the legend. Counts alone hide that the three not in are half the money. Dollars alone hide that it is three trades, not one.
  • The three things are not a new list. They are the first three open gates, in the gate list's order, with the gate's own press. When a gate closes, the next one steps up. Nothing to maintain.
  • Fold, do not delete. The six calendar cards hold words people rely on (the reminder day, Wendi's list, who certifies). They fold behind "the dates in full"; the gate list folds behind the ring. Both open with one press and remember the choice per computer.
  • Left out on purpose. A forecast line (it has its own tab), last period's numbers (one word on the timeline is enough), a per-trade mini-chart (the tables are that).
  • Not sure of. Whether "To the owner" should show the expected number or the asked number before the draws are approved; drawn as expected, with "if all come in as expected" on hover. Whether the picture belongs on the Helotes job too, where there is one trade and nothing to stack; probably a single-row bar and the same three things.
