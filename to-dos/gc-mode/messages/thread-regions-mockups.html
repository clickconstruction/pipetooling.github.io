A's tinted well for the conversation, B's pinned bands with their shadows. The three regions read apart by colour and by behaviour: the grey band is the thing that moves, the white bands are the things that stay. The message cards stand white on grey, the quick replies panel turns white so it belongs to the composer band, not to the well.

Is this the best we can do? The proposal

D, built in two steps so each can be judged alone: the well first (A), which changes only colour and costs nothing; then the pinned bands (B) on top, which change how the thread scrolls.

Three things weighed against the first draft:

  1. The pinned header is tall. With the jobs rail, the court line and the stamps, it takes a third of a laptop screen before the first message. So in B and D the header pins in two parts: the name row with its corner stays fixed, and the rest, the facts, the court line, the toolbar, the jobs, scrolls away with the conversation and comes back when you scroll up. The composer always pins whole. The mock-ups above pin the whole header; the build would pin the name row only.
  2. The thread's own scroll must not fight the page's. The list and the flow panel already stick to the top of the window; a thread that scrolls inside its card means two scrollbars at a wide window. The build gives the card the window's height (the thread column fills the viewport under the app header, as the people list does), so the page itself no longer scrolls on Messages and only the conversation does.
  3. The well's grey is the page's grey. At a wide window the well would sit grey on a grey page with a white card edge between, which is faint. The build uses a shade of its own, a touch lighter than the page's, so the card still reads as a card and the well as a band inside it; the mock-ups use the page's grey, which is the worst case.

If the pinned bands feel like too much for the first cut, A alone is one line of CSS and the right first step either way.
