What changed from the first draft. A cascade across the top, because bill day is a chain and not a day: last period's payment, the trades' deadline, our checking days, bill day, the owner's pay-by, the trades' pay day. Gates under it: the seven things that must be true before the application goes, each with its blocker named and one press to clear it; these are the Money page's amber notes made into a list with buttons, so there is one list and not two. Expected on every row, from the schedule's forecast, so "still to come" is a number and a trade asking for far more than the schedule expects stands out. Changes beside waivers, since an unsigned change order billed early is the mistake this page exists to catch. The lines opened under a draw that waits on us: claimed, we see, the schedule, and this draw's money at what we see, with the photo and the invoice beside the line; Approve reads the corrected amount. Ask all three in one press, and the app's own reminder three days before the cut-off, named on the cascade. A trade's habit in the small words: late once before, never late. Closing out as its own group for the final application and the retainage release. The Draws window still writes the draw.

Why this is the piece that matters most. The office cannot know whether level 1 rough-in is 90% or 75% from a desk; the superintendent can, by walking it. Today that walk is a conversation and a guess. The phone page is the day's claims as a list, with the last draw and the schedule's number as the eyes' calibration, and one tap per line that becomes we see on the draw. The checking days on the cascade are this walk.

It is the daily log's own list on a bill-day week, not a new place: the daily log already carries crews on site and photos; on the days between the cut-off and bill day it carries the claims to check.

Is this the best we can do? What the second pass found

  1. The first draft was a list; the job is a chain with gates. The office's question on bill day is "can it go, and what stops it", not "who is in". The gates answer it in one glance and every blocker has its press. The trade rows become the detail under the gates.
  2. "Expected" turns chasing into judgment. A trade asking for $27,900 when the schedule expects $24,200 is a line to walk, not a draw to approve. The forecast exists already; it was not on the trade side.
  3. The check is the work, and it happens on site. Nothing in the first draft said how "we see 75" gets known. The superintendent's phone list is the answer, and it is the daily log, not a new page.
  4. Changes and waivers are gates, not columns. Billing an unsigned change and paying without an unconditional waiver are the two expensive mistakes; a column is read past, a gate is not.
  5. It is half of a screen that exists. The Money page's bill day, amber notes and forecast are the owner's half; building beside it would make two bill days. The two halves share the cascade and the total line.
  6. Dropped from the first draft: the "days coming up" strip (the Forecast tab is that, and better); the Bill day nav tab (it is the Money page's); the job as the only unit (every job, filtered).
  7. Still the owner's: the cut-off as a setting or fixed; whether the app may send the cut-off reminder itself; whether the superintendent's taps approve a line by themselves when they match the claim (lean no: the office approves, the walk informs).
