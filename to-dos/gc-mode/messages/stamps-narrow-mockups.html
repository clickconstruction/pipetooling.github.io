The pick, and is this the best we can do?

C. The folded header has one job for these two: say who wrote last and how long ago. A filled pill answers the first before any word is read, and the +N answers the second. It also drops the one thing nobody could read, the signed difference. B is the honest minimum if the pills feel like one shape too many; D is where to go if the calendar earns its place on the page rather than on the card.

Three things weighed:

  1. The colors must not mean "court". The court pill is orange for ours and green for theirs. The stamp pills use the calendar's pair, green for them and blue for us, so a green pill means "they wrote last", never "their turn"; the court pill beside it still says whose turn.
  2. One form or two. A says the same thing wide and folded at the cost of a row. C makes the folded form its own thing, which the folded header already is (the state pill, the strip). The wide block stays as it is.
  3. The between-mark had a reader. The owner asked for it to see the two stamps' distance. The +N on the older pill gives the same distance when the newer one is today, which it is whenever the row matters; when neither is today the card's two full lines carry both counts.
