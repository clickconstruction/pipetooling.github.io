Papers on this job · two shapes

Boerne Retail Shell, a bid due Thu Oct 8. Today the section lists every paper of every company in one long table, the done rows as loud as the one that ran out, and the heading says nothing. What the office wants from it: is anything on this job at risk, what must be in before bid day, and who do I chase. Two shapes, with the same four pieces.

1. The pieces

PieceWhat it does
1The count in the titlePapers on this job · 1 lapsed · 2 late · 3 waiting, each a pill that filters the list to those rows. Green all in when there is nothing.
2Before bid dayA strip of what the job's next day needs: for a bid, every carried trade's insurance current and every quote in; for a project, the waivers and the certificate before bill day. Each line with its press.
3Companies in trouble first, done foldedA company reads as one header line with its own counts; its at-risk and waiting papers show, its done ones fold behind 4 done ›. A company with nothing wrong is one line.
4One state languageEvery paper's state is one pill: done with its day, waiting with its days, lapsed with why, runs out in amber thirty days before it lapses, later for one not due yet. Today's prose becomes the hover.

Companies as rows, the papers as columns in the flow's order, a dot per cell. The whole job's paper health in one look, and a column that is mostly empty says what everyone still owes. Best on a job with many trades; thin on a job with two.

4. Is this the best we can do?

  • A wins on the press. Every bad row carries its own ask, and the strip at the top is the three things to do before Thursday in the order that matters. B shows more at once but every press is a hover away.
  • B is the view to keep for the big job. Stone Oak with twelve trades and three paper columns each is a grid, not a list. Drawn as a second tab on the section, List · Grid, the list first.
  • "Runs out Nov 1" is the piece that earns its keep. The certificate that lapses on a Wednesday was fine on Tuesday; thirty days of amber is what stops the next Hillside.
  • The strip reads the next day, not every day. On a bid it is bid day; on a project, bill day; on a closed job, nothing, and the strip does not draw. The rules are the gates the Money page already keeps, read per paper.
  • Left out: a paper-by-paper timeline, the sent and signed emails inline (Open reaches them), a per-job count of Spanish pages.
  • Not sure of: whether a company with nothing wrong should fold to one line, as drawn, or show its done rows gray. Folded keeps a twelve-trade job to twelve lines; the hover on 5 done lists them.

Pick one and I build it into the job page; the pills in the title and the strip come with either.
