The owner's rule for this rail (2026-10-11): the day matters most for many of these conversations, so the bid day or the stage stays on every chip, in a slot of its own that never shrinks either. The day's tone says how close it is: plain when far, Oct 8 · 6 d within a week, today, and Sep 30 · late once it has gone by. The demo's today is Fri Oct 2.

The pick, and is this the best we can do?

A. One chip, one width, the day always in the same column so the eye runs down the rail and reads Oct 8, Oct 22, building without hunting. Its tone does the counting. B says the same in words and costs the height; C drops the name, which a phone cannot get back.

Three things weighed:

  1. The day's words per kind. A bid's slot is its bid day, then sent once our bid went; a GC project's is its stage and, once set, its start date (starts Oct 19), since that is the day a customer asks about; a Pipeline job's is the Stages board's word with its day (billed Oct 1). Each is a fact the app keeps, never typed; the real build reads it from the Board, the schedule and the Pipeline. What goes in the slot for each stage is the owner's to confirm, and the hover card says the full sentence.
  2. The slot is a fixed width, so long words are cut. "Oct 8 · 6 d" fits; "collections" fits; "buying out" fits; a stage with a day, "starts Oct 19", is the longest and sets the slot's width. The chip is 296 px for it; at the folded header the strip scrolls and chips keep their width.
  3. GC projects need a number. gc_projects keys on a projects row, which has project_number, hand-typed and unique on the Projects page. The real build would read it into GC mode and have New project claim the next free one; until the owner decides, a project with none reads GC in the green badge.
