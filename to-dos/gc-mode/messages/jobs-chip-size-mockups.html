One size for the job chips

Greg Paulk's rail today: each chip is as wide as its words (the job, the trade, the day or stage), so three jobs make three sizes. Across the app a compact job label is the number first, then the name, and only the name shrinks: the header search ("J927 · name"), the schedule ("J1064 · name"), dispatch, the clock, the sub labor sheet and the checklist's job chips all do it, with the number in a small badge that never shrinks and the name cut with an ellipsis (ledgerDisplayPrefixes.ts, ChecklistTitleWithLinks.tsx, LienJobNumber.tsx). The pick, and is this the best we can do?

A. It is the rule the rest of the app follows, so a chip here reads like a search result or a schedule bar, and the job number becomes something the office can say on the phone. 240 px at a wide window; in the folded header the strip scrolls and the chips keep their width.

Three things weighed:

  1. GC projects have no number today. gc_projects keys on a projects row, and that row has project_number, hand-typed on the Projects page and checked unique. The real build would read it into GcProject (one more column in gcIo.ts's select) and have New project claim the next free one, so every GC project carries a number from its first day. Until the owner decides, the chip of a project with no number reads GC in the green badge.
  2. The trade leaves the chip. It is the same on every chip of a trade partner and already on the header and each line. The one case it told something apart, a company quoting two trades on one job, gets two chips reading the same; then the chip keeps "· Sitework" after the name, the exception rather than the rule.
  3. Plain J and B, not JP and BP. The app uses the per-trade prefix where no trade pill is near and the plain letter beside one. The chip's badge is colored by kind, not by trade, so the plain letter is right here, as in the header search.
