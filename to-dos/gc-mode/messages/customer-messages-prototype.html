How the window reads

Orange is the customer's.
A line Dana got or sent, pressed on her page, or the app sent her, is orange, with a deeper bar when it came from her. Most of this feed is ours, so the few lines she saw are the ones to find at a glance. The GC thread is the other way around, mostly her words, so there the team's notes carry the color. The box turns orange the moment what you write goes to her, so a note and a text never look alike.
The step Dana is on comes first.
Her flow is the residential one, and the job is Working, so the Working lines lead. Then the rest of her flow, then anyone's. In each group the lines you use most come first, then the team's. That is the GC strip's rule, unchanged.
Blanks are filled when you press, never typed.
{first}, {me}, {address}, {job} come from the job and the sign-in. {minutes} from the drive, {window}, {back day} and {back time} from the schedule, {link} from her page. A blank the app does not know, {thing} or {found}, stays lit in the box and Send waits for it.
A phone mark says which lines a tech gets.
Lines for customers marked on the phone are the five on Job Mode, the step's first. The office orders them in the editor's Phone tab. A tech sees no other line.
Spanish beside English.
A line carries both. Dana's record says English, so she gets English. A line with no Spanish reads English to a Spanish speaker, and the editor marks it no es so the office can fill it.

Job Mode, the tech's phone. One new press on the job card, Message Dana. It opens the customer lines marked on the phone in the editor, the step's first, with nothing to type. A tap shows the text as it will go, with the blanks filled from the drive, the job and the clock. Change a line in the editor on screen 1 and it changes here.

Why a short list and not a keyboard

The complaint was silence, not wording. Five or six presses a tech can make with one thumb, between the truck and the door, get sent. A keyboard does not. The lines are the team's quick replies marked for the phone, so the office edits them in one place and every tech's phone changes.

What fills the blanks

The minutes come from the drive to the address. The window and the back day come from the schedule. The address and the first name come from the job. The tech's name comes from the sign-in. A line with a blank only the tech knows, like what was found, asks for that one thing and nothing else, and the same words go on the status report.

Who gets the reply

Dana's answer comes back to the tech who texted until the end of the day, then to the office. Outside hours the app answers once that the office opens at 7:30 and puts her on Needs a reply. The office sees every line either way.

The door

Everything in Messages today is behind the office team. A tech's door is narrower: the threads of customers on jobs they are dispatched to or clocked on, this week and last, with no money lines and no office-only notes. A tech reads the strip and never the editor.

Dana's side. One number and one mailbox for everything from Click Plumbing, whoever pressed Send. A request that needs something from her is a text plus a press on her portal page, never a text alone.

The owner, 2026-10-13.

What only you can decide before a helper draws the screens. Each one has my lean under it. Answer in the chat, in order or out of it.

  1. The tech's door. Which customers' threads can a tech read and write? All customers, or the ones on jobs they are dispatched to or clocked on?
    Dispatched or clocked on, this week and last. No money lines, no office-only notes. The office sees everything.
  2. Free text for techs. Quick replies only, or a keyboard too?
    Quick replies at first. "Something else" is a note to the office that Rosa sends. Open the keyboard to named techs later.
  3. Who edits the phone's lines. The office team, as every quick reply today, or a tech lead too?
    The office team. A tech who wants a line asks in "Something else" and the office adds it; the editor's history says who.
  4. Who answers a reply. The tech who texted, the office, or both?
    The tech until 5 PM, then the office. After hours the app answers once and the line waits on Needs a reply.
  5. Numbers and boxes. One Quo number for everything, or one for customers and one for trades? office@ for all customer mail, bids@ for the estimators' quotes?
    Two numbers, two boxes, one inbox. The customer number is the office line people already have.
  6. Where a line lives. On the job window alone, or the customer's whole thread with other jobs' lines muted?
    The whole thread, other jobs muted. A customer with two jobs is one conversation.
  7. Which reminders start on. The app can text the estimate reminder, the bill reminder and the review ask by itself.
    Estimate and bill on from day one. The review ask after you read ten real threads.
  8. The seventeen integrations. Decided 2026-10-13 on the mockups: sixteen Add, history No. Candidates and subs went against the lean, so Messages has seven kinds of contact, customer · trade partner · architect · lead · supply house · candidate · sub, four of them with no flow and two off Follow up by default.
    Build the kinds as one change to the contact record, not seven; the lead's re-point to a customer is the same move as Who is this?'s fold.
  9. Order of building.
    Finish the thread window on GC projects, make it the customer window everywhere, then the job window's chip and box, then Job Mode's press, then the residential flow and its reminders.

What the quick replies kernel gains for this

Steps per flow, not per kind.
Today a line's step comes from one flow per kind of person. A customer may be on the GC customer flow or the residential one, so the strip takes the person's flow and the editor groups customer lines by flow, then step.
Blanks.
A line may hold named blanks. A fill table per send names what the app knows (the person, the job, the drive, the schedule, the link). fillLine returns the text and the blanks left, and the box refuses to send with one left.
The phone mark and its order.
A line for customers may be marked for the phone with a place in the phone's order. phoneLines(lines, stepId) gives the tech's list, the step's first, at most six.
Spanish.
A line carries text_es. The strip picks by the person's language and falls back to English, as the portal words do. A change to either language is a version.

What the three share

People, not tickets.
A row is a person, and their whole thread opens in the middle. Nothing is a ticket with a status; the person is Open, Snoozed or Done, as cut 2 built it.
Three sorts and no more.
Who is this? for a sender nobody has named, with the box or number it came in on setting the first guess. Which job? for a person with two or more active jobs and no tie, where the default already took it. Who has it? for a conversation nobody is on, where the rule gives it out and the pill changes it.
The areas are filters.
Customers, Trade partners, Architects, Leads and Subs filter one list; Supply houses and Candidates sit under one more pill and never count on Follow up (the seven kinds, decided 2026-10-13). A GC customer sits with the trade partners' work because her flow is the GC one, whichever box she wrote to.
The thread is the same window everywhere.
From the job window, from a customer's name, from Job Mode and from here, it is cut 1's window at its URL. The page only decides what sits beside it.
