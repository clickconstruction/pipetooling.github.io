Why five lines and not a keyboard

The complaint was silence, not wording. Five presses a tech can make with one thumb, between the truck and the door, get sent. A keyboard does not. The lines are the team's quick replies for the customer kind on the step the job is on, so the office edits them in one place and every tech's phone changes.

What fills the blanks

The minutes come from the drive to the address. The window and the back day come from the schedule. The address and the first name come from the job. The tech's name comes from the sign-in. "Here is what we found" is the one line with a blank the tech fills, and it is the same words that go on the status report.

Who gets the reply

Dana's answer comes back to the tech who texted until the end of the day, then to the office. Outside hours the app answers once that the office opens at 7:30 and puts her on Needs a reply. The office sees every line either way.

The door

Everything in Messages today is behind the office team. A tech's door is narrower: the threads of customers on jobs they are dispatched to or clocked on, this week and last, with no money lines and no office-only notes. That door is the one new piece of access this needs.

Dana's side. One number and one mailbox for everything from Click Plumbing, whoever pressed Send. A request that needs something from her is a text plus a press on her portal page, never a text alone.

What only you can decide before a helper draws the screens. Each one has my lean under it. Answer in the chat, in order or out of it.

  1. The tech's door. Which customers' threads can a tech read and write? All customers, or the ones on jobs they are dispatched to or clocked on?
    Dispatched or clocked on, this week and last. No money lines, no office-only notes. The office sees everything.
  2. Free text for techs. Quick replies only, or a keyboard too?
    Quick replies at first. "Something else" is a note to the office that Rosa sends. Open the keyboard to named techs later.
  3. Who answers a reply. The tech who texted, the office, or both?
    The tech until 5 PM, then the office. After hours the app answers once and the line waits on Needs a reply.
  4. Numbers and boxes. One Quo number for everything, or one for customers and one for trades? office@ for all customer mail, bids@ for the estimators' quotes?
    Two numbers, two boxes, one inbox. The customer number is the office line people already have.
  5. Where a line lives. On the job window alone, or the customer's whole thread with other jobs' lines muted?
    The whole thread, other jobs muted. A customer with two jobs is one conversation.
  6. Which reminders start on. The app can text the estimate reminder, the bill reminder and the review ask by itself.
    Estimate and bill on from day one. The review ask after you read ten real threads.
  7. Order of building.
    Finish the thread window on GC projects, make it the customer window everywhere, then the job window's chip and box, then Job Mode's press, then the residential flow and its reminders.
