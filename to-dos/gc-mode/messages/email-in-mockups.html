Messages, cut 6: email in, the bids@ reader and the files that arrive

Every email that reaches bids@ lands in the sender's thread, the way a text from the company number does in cut 5. A reader on the server (gmail-sync, run every few minutes) takes each new email once, finds whose address it is, ties it to a job when its subject names one, keeps a copy of each small file and keeps a link as a link, then puts a label on it in Gmail. It never deletes or archives. A file that reads as a paper a step waits on is offered in the thread, never filed on its own. What follows is each screen as it would look, the rules the reader keeps, the cut list, and what I need the lead to settle.

The reader adds a label to each email it took, and nothing else changes: nothing is archived, marked read, moved or deleted. The weekly newsletter has no label, because the reader left it out.

What the reader keeps to

  • Once each. An email is one line, claimed by its Gmail id, so a second run or two runs at once write it once. The reader keeps Gmail's history id to start the next run where the last one stopped, and falls back to a search by the label when Gmail has dropped that history.
  • Whose it is. The sender's address, matched by its key (lower case, trimmed), finds the contact through contact_for_channel, as cut 1 built it. An address two people share goes to the one it last carried a message for.
  • Left out. Mail with an unsubscribe line or marked bulk, no-reply senders, Gmail's Promotions, Social, Updates and Forums, spam and trash, and anything our own app sent, which the thread already shows as a sent copy. These stay in Gmail with no label.
  • Files. Each file up to the size we keep is copied into the private message-attachments bucket. The office reads it there, and only the server writes it. A small inline picture, a signature's logo, is left out. A link to a file host (Drive, Dropbox, Box, WeTransfer, OneDrive) in the body is kept as a link.
  • The job. A bid's, a GC project's or a Pipeline job's number in the subject ties the line to that job. With no number, an exact name among the person's own jobs does. Otherwise the line takes their star, and with no star it sits on the person.
  • Who sees it. The office team, as every line. Nothing reaches a portal. The emails we sent stay the office's read, so an estimator sees none of them (the known gap, not widened).
  • A switch. The reader does nothing until a dev turns it on (gmail_sync_on_v1), so the first run is watched.

The cut list

  1. Kernels (pure, tested): src/lib/emailIn/. gmailMessage.ts turns Gmail's message into an email: the headers, the plain text (or the HTML read as text), the files and the inline pictures. forwarded.ts reads the first sender, time and subject out of a forwarded block. leftOut.ts holds the bulk rules above. jobInSubject.ts finds the job number in a subject. paperGuess.ts reads a file's name and kind into a W-9 or a certificate, or nothing. fileLinks.ts finds the links to file hosts. Fixtures use only made-up .example addresses.
  2. SQL (one migration, claimed from main): messages.gmail_id with a unique index (whole, not partial, as quo_id's is, so the write's ON CONFLICT can use it) and gmail_thread_id for cut 3's replies. message_attachments holds the message, kind (file or link), name, mime, size, our bucket path or the link, the paper guess, and whether it was filed or turned down. The private message-attachments bucket gets its storage policies: the office team reads, the service role writes. email_place_address is quo_place_number's twin for an address. email_write_line writes once by the Gmail id. gmail_sync_state holds the history id, the last run and the last error. The switch is off. A cron runs it every few minutes, as gc_office_notices's does. The house fences, the doc, ACCESS_CONTROL, and a bed.
  3. The edge function gmail-sync: the token from _shared/driveUpload.ts's path, given the Gmail scopes (one optional argument, which leaves every Drive caller as it is), as GMAIL_IMPERSONATE_USER. X-Cron-Secret, the switch, the history since the last run, each email through the kernels' copy in _shared, the line, the files, the label, and the history id saved last. Its EDGE_FUNCTIONS.md section, the dev-mcp catalog line (as #5452 did) and config.toml's verify_jwt = false. Secrets by name only.
  4. The screens: the chips under a line, the window's Attachments with Open, the paper offer and its press into the company window, a forwarded line's words, and whatever question 6 decides for Save to Drive. The help guide's section, the release note, and the docs.

What I need the lead to settle

  1. Replies go to people's own inboxes today. The GC senders (gc-trade-email, gc-customer-email) send from the app's address with the Reply-To set to the PM's or the sender's own address. So a trade's or a customer's answer lands in a person's inbox, never in bids@, unless they forward it. Should our sends add bids@ to the Reply-To (Resend takes two), so every answer reaches the reader? That is a change to cuts 3 and 4's senders, and it is the owner's call. Without it, the forwarded copy (screen 4) is the only way their answers arrive.
  2. Which mail. The Inbox only, or the Inbox and Sent? Sent would bring the team's own replies written in Gmail into the threads as lines from us. Spam and trash are left out either way.
  3. The first run. Only new mail from the day it is turned on, or the last 30 days so the threads start full?
  4. The size we keep. Every file up to 25 MB (Gmail's own cap on an email), leaving out inline pictures under 30 KB?
  5. Who reads the files. The office team, gc_office_team(), the same as the lines they hang on, estimators included. Or is_office_staff(), the sent copies' readers?
  6. Save to Drive. In this cut, with a small function that copies our copy into the job's Drive folder through driveUpload.ts (Plans, or Team only for a quote or a photo)? Or with cut 7's Drive handler, showing Open alone until then?
  7. The papers. A trade partner's W-9 and certificate only for now, offered as screen 3 shows? A customer's papers later?
  8. The label and the pace. The label In ClickTooling, and a run every 3 minutes all day?
  9. A dev's view of the reader. Is gmail_sync_state through the dev-mcp catalog enough to watch it (the last run, its count and its last error)? Or should a line on Messages show it to a dev?

Is this the best we can do? Three ways it could be better, and the pick

  1. A push instead of a poll. Gmail's watch through Pub/Sub would land an email in seconds, not minutes. But it needs a Pub/Sub topic, a public endpoint and a watch renewed every week. The lead chose the poll, and three minutes is fast enough for an office.
  2. Reading inside the PDF. A plan set's title block and a certificate's dates read from the file itself would tie more jobs and fill the dates for the office. That means reading PDFs on the server. It is a later step, once the name-and-subject rules show how often they miss.
  3. Every answer through bids@. Question 1. If our sends add bids@ to the Reply-To, the forwarded copy becomes the rare case instead of the rule.

The pick: the poll, the name-and-subject rules, and the offer that never files on its own, built as the four PRs above. Question 1 goes to the owner before cut 3's sender is final.
