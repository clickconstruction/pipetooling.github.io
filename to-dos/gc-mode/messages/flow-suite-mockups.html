The flow suite: what a step is, how the office edits it, and how it reaches the papers

Today the panel draws a person's journey and Settings → Flows renames its steps. This mock-up draws the rest in five pieces, in the order a person meets them: the step box gets a paper mark; a press on a step opens its card; the card sends or asks for the step's paper through the paper send the company window already has; the editor chooses what finishes a step from the facts the app keeps; and a step's nudge becomes a Follow up row. One rule under all five, from the plan: a step is finished by a fact the app already keeps, never by a typed word. A sweep of GC mode found a stamp for nearly every step drawn (the list is in the plan); the four it has not got are named at the end.

Finished when is the step's facts, each read live with a mark, its date and where the copy lives, and one button that moves it: Open for a paper on file, Ask for it for one they owe (it fills the reply box with the step's quick reply, the way the strip does, with their portal link on it), Send it for one we owe (the paper send the company window already has, so the sent copy is filed and the thread shows it).

By hand, without a typed word. A paper handed to us in the office is filed as a paper, File a certificate we were handed: a file or a photo, a date, and who filed it. That is a fact the app keeps, so the step finishes the same way. There is no "mark it done" that writes nothing; the plan's rule holds.

After this lets the office get ahead by one step: send the MSA while the insurance is still coming. The step card never sends two steps ahead.

How they got here is the step's history in words, each line a stamp with its person and day. A done step's card reads the same way, with its paper on top and Open beside it.

What the office can change: a step's name, its place in the lane (the ⋮⋮ handle), whose move it is, the nudge (a count of days and a channel, or at their promised day), whether it repeats and per what, which papers it turns on, and which facts finish it, joined with and or, on a fork, or. A step can be added between two or removed; removing one that people are on shows them in the preview and they land on the next step, since the facts decide. What it cannot change: the facts themselves, which are a list in the app with the column each reads (the sweep's table in the plan); and the lanes, Once, Per job, Any time, which are the shape of the data. Who: the office edits (the owner and the assistants, as Settings is); everyone reads. History is the quick replies window's: every change with its person and day, a Removed tab, and Put it back.

A step's nudge fires when the step is on now, the move is theirs, and the thread has been quiet that many days; a promised day beats the count, as the loop's at their promised day says. The row says the step, what we are waiting on and the channel the flow asked for, with a one-press switch to another. Answering, or the fact turning true, clears it. Hold is the owed strip's day chips. The Board's ring keeps its own three stages until its lists and the flows agree; that is a later cut, named in the plan.

Is this the best we can do?

Five places it could be otherwise, weighed:

  1. Could the step box stay a picture, with everything in the editor? No. The question the panel raises is "what do I do about it", and that answer is per person, not per flow: Tom's insurance, Greg's late quote. The card is where the per-person answer lives; the editor is where the shape lives. Two places, each one thing.
  2. Could "Finished when" be typed, so the office can say anything? It is the plan's one rule and this mock-up leans on it harder: the preview column, the paper marks, the Follow up rows and the Documents' Finishes column all work only because a fact is a read. A typed fact is a to-do list, and the app has those. The cost is that a fact the app does not keep cannot be a step until it does; the four gaps are listed next.
  3. Could the papers be a step of their own instead of a column? The W-9 and the insurance are two facts of one step, Papers in, as the owner drew it; MSA and SOW are one paper each. A papers column names what a step turns on without adding boxes, and the Documents side reads it back. Keep the column.
  4. Is a new table needed? One small one, for what the office edits: the step's name, order, whose move, nudge, repeats, papers and the ids of its facts, plus the history. The facts are a list in code with the column each reads, and the state is a read over the stamps, as the person-journey kernel the app already has does for its five journeys. The flow is that kernel's list, made editable, not a sixth list.
  5. Does one flow per kind hold? For now. A trade whose flow differs (a supplier with no MSA) is the open question the plan carries; the editor's answer, when it comes, is a step marked skip for a kind of company, not a second flow.

The four facts the app does not keep yet, each a small column, named so the real build counts them: a trade's first portal visit (today "Portal opened" is their press on Got it, and a per-ask opened mark); a lost mark on a quote we did not carry (today only inferred); the day a customer said yes (today only the stage moves); and the warranty year's end (computed from acceptance plus a year, no column). "Start date sent" exists already as the schedule letter's send.

The pick: build pieces 1, 2 and 3 in the prototype next (the marks, the card, the editor with the picker and the preview), then 4 and 5 once the owner has pressed on them. The plan's section names the order and the stamps.
