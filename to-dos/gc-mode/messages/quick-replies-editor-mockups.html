The quick replies window: every line, how much it is used, and who changed it

C's groups need each line to say who it is for and, if it belongs to a step, which. This window is where anyone sets that, sees the whole list at once with how often each line is used, and reads a line's history. Opened from Edit on the strip. Two states: the list, and one line opened.

Pressing a row opens it in place: the words and the two tags are fields, Save keeps the change, and the old words go to the history rather than away. The history is every version with what changed, as a strike-through and a highlight, who did it and when, plus the use count by person and the last use. Remove this line… asks first and keeps the line in the history as removed, so a removed line can be brought back; whether removal is allowed at all is on the plan's open list.

3 · Adding one · the same row, empty, with the tags guessed from the words

The words go in first; the app guesses who it is for and the step from them (W-9, insurance read as a trade partner's papers) and shows the guess with the word guessed until the person confirms or changes it. Just for me keeps it off the team's list.

Is this the best we can do?

Three things weighed:

  1. Counts the strip does not print. The table shows use; the strip only orders by it. Numbers on the strip would turn a reply into a score.
  2. History is a table, not a diff viewer. One row a version, the changed words struck and highlighted, is enough for lines of one sentence. The real build stores each version as a row (`quick_reply_versions`: line, words, for, step, by, at), and the use count as presses (`quick_reply_uses`: line, by, at, contact), which also gives last used on Bill Sorrell.
  3. One window, two jobs. Seeing the list and editing a line are the same window, so nobody learns two. The cost is a long window on a phone; the tabs and the search keep it short, and a line opens in place rather than in a second window.
