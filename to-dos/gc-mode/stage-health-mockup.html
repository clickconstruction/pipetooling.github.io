2 · Is this the best we can do?

No. It counts steps. It does not say how the stage is going.

  • A count of steps is not health.

    14 of 25 with 6 days left is trouble. 14 of 25 with 20 days left is fine. Health is progress measured against the clock, and the ring has no clock.

  • The steps do not weigh the same.

    Sending our bid is one step, the same as one W-9. A trade with no quote at all is one step, the same as a quote that needs confirming.

  • It hides who is in the way.

    Every tab on this page is organized by trade. A glance at the top should show the trades: which are fine, which need us, which are a hole.

  • It says nothing about what to do next.

    The office opens a project to act. The top of the page should name the one thing to do now, and take you there.

  • Building health is buried.

    Fair Oaks D is 72% done where 76% was planned, and dry-in finished 7 days late. All of that sits in the ring card's small "Also" list. Money is missing too: the work in place is ahead of what we have billed.

Two counts disagree today. Boerne's header says trades 4 of 8 covered. The ring says 3 of 8 have a quote we'll use. The difference is Roofing: Summit's quote has a line with no cost (roof curbs). The header counts it, the ring does not. The new design uses one rule, so one page cannot give two answers.

Buying out has no clock. Helotes has no start date, so nothing can say it is behind. The fix is in the design below: ask for a target start date when we mark a bid won.

4 · Asked again: is this the best we can do?

Closer. Three things decided on the way, and three left for you.

The tiles are the same objects in every stage.

An early draft used a different chart per stage. Tiles that stay the trades while the stage changes what they show make the job feel like one story: quotes, then papers, then work.

A verdict in words, not a score.

A 0 to 100 health score was considered and dropped. It hides why. On track, Watch and Behind each come with the reason in one sentence, and the rule is written down.

The ring stays on the board.

The board row keeps the ring for a quick scan across jobs. The project page gets the strip, and the ring's card on the board uses the same counts, so the numbers match everywhere.

5 · The plan, if you like it

How it would be built in the prototype

  1. One rule for "a trade has a number".

    A quote with a line that has no cost does not count. The header, the ring and the strip all read it.

  2. The health kernel, tested.

    One calculation per stage gives the verdict, its reason, the next step and the tab it opens, the clock's marks, the tiles and the numbers. It builds on what the ring, Get started, the schedule and Bill the customer already count.

  3. The strip in the project header.

    It takes the place of today's numbers on the right. On a phone it shows the verdict, the next step and the tiles. The header is the Board lane's, so we build it together.

  4. A target start date when we win.

    "We won this" asks for one, so buying out has a clock from day one.

  5. The walkthrough learns the strip.

    "Walk me through this job" gets a stop on it.

What I need from you

The verdict rules.

The drafts are under each stage above. Change any threshold you want: 7 days, 2 days, a slip of 7 days.

Money in the Building strip.

Show "billed against work in place" to the owner and the controller only, as on the Money tab? Or leave money off the project page?

A target start date at "We won this".

Should it be required, or asked with a skip?
