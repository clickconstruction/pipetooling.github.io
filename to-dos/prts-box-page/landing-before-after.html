The box page a scan opens: before and after

The owner, 2026-11-10, after his first cage labels came off the Brother PT-D610BT and an iPhone scanned one: should the page the scan opens be improved. His test cage had one line, so most of the screen under it was empty. The page already shows the right things. What it lacks is the label's own look at the top, a plain word on whether the count can be trusted, the search within reach, and a sign-in press that says who it is for.

Drawn 2026-11-10 from BoxPublicPage.tsx, the box-read function and its kernel as they stand on main. Every cage, part, count, code and number is made up. The dotted line on a phone is about where an iPhone's first screen ends. The phones stay light in both themes, as the page does.

What is weak

The top does not look like the label. The label in the hand reads a stencil in a box and eight characters. The page reads a heading, and never shows the code.

The count's day is a date to work out. "Sunday, Nov 8" does not say whether the numbers can be trusted. The app already has the rule: a cage is due a count after 14 days.

The blue press is for the fewest people. "Sign in to change a count" tells a helper nothing about what follows, and tells a subcontractor to do something they cannot.

The search is a press away. "It is not in this one" is the most likely next thought, and it costs a tap and a new screen before the first letter.

A part that ran out vanishes. The page drops every line at zero, so a person hunts the cage for valves that the app knows are gone.

Nobody says whose cage it is in a sentence, or how to reach them.

Found on the way: on a retired cage's page the Find a part on this site press cannot work. The function answers "retired" before it reaches the search, so the press ends in an error. Read from the code, not walked on a phone.

4Where each piece comes from

What box-read answers today is one object: the code, the stencil, the kind, the place, the where-note, the lines with their maker and count, the day of the last count and the newest photo. The page draws all of it except the code. Nothing else is being held back by the page.

Not offered, because it breaks the page's other rule, that nothing signed out can write: a form on the page that reports a part missing. The text to the office does that job through a door that already exists.

Why B. It answers the three questions in the order a person at the cage asks them. Is this the right cage: the tile and the code match the label in the hand. Is my part here and can I trust the number: the list, the verdict, None left. What do I do if not: search the site, text the office, or sign in once. A stranger still learns nothing the label on the cage does not already say.

What B costs. The office's number goes on a page anyone with a label can open. It is the number already on every bill. A line marked "could not be found" tells a stranger that parts go missing from this cage, which is the one piece of B worth the owner's second thought.

!The owner's calls

  1. Build B, as two PRs with A first? My pick: yes. PR 1 changes no function and can ship the same day.
  2. Which press is blue, Find or Sign in? My pick: Find. Everyone can use it, and a crew member signs in once and never sees this page again.
  3. Do the sign-in's new words read right: "Sign in to record a use or a count"? My pick: yes, with "On Click's crew?" above it and the line that it is once per phone.
  4. Which lines at zero show as None left? My pick: the ones emptied since the cage's last count. A count clears them, so an old cage does not grow a tail.
  5. Does "could not be found" show to someone signed out? My pick: yes. It stops a helper hunting for valves the crew already looked for. Say no and the line just reads 10.
  6. A way to say something is wrong with no sign-in: a text to the office, a form, or nothing? My pick: the text, to the number the bill page shows. No form.
  7. The photo: a band 150 tall with a tap to see it whole, or as tall as today? My pick: the band. It buys one more line on the first screen, and at the cage the list is what the person cannot see.
  8. A retired cage's page: drop the Find press, or let a retired label search its old site? My pick: drop it. It is broken today, and a retired label may be anywhere.
