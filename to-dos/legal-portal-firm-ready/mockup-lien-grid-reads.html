Is this the best we can do?

  • The address under the job, not a column. The job cell is the row's name; the address is where it is. One cell, two lines, and the table loses its widest column. The print keeps the memo's twelve columns, which counsel asked for by name.
  • Property in words. Commercial; Residential with Homestead or No homestead beneath. Homestead only matters on a residential property, so a commercial row carries one word. A kind the office has not entered reads Commercial followed by the red ?, since commercial dates are what the grid shows for it.
  • The total first. Counsel reads the dollars before the months. The months wrap under it, as many lines as they need.
  • Missed months left off, not struck through. A closed window is nothing counsel can act on. The row still shows the month as unpaid, so nothing is hidden about the money; only the dead deadline goes. A job with every window closed shows a dash with its reason on hover, never a red ?, which means a fact the office has not entered.
  • A rail, not a select. Every GC is in view with its count and its dollars, largest first, so the firm sees where the money is before it clicks. All GCs leads; a job with no GC (contracted with the owner) gets its own row at the end, which the select never offered. The rail's numbers follow the Something due / All switch, so they always foot with the grid beside them. It stretches to the grid's height, and past about ten GCs a find box filters the names.
  • Not done: sorting the grid by the rail's order, or a per-GC print button. The grid keeps the book's order (next date, then balance), and Print the grid prints what is in view, so a per-GC print is one click on the rail then the button.
