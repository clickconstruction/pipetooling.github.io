The Lien grid, by court

The owner, 2026-10-07: break the Lien grid down by court as well, so a lawyer can easily pick out the cases they want. Today the rail beside the grid lists GCs. This adds a second way to read the same rail: Courts, county by county, each justice precinct with its jobs and dollars, the jobs over the $20,000 justice limit on a line of their own, and the jobs whose county the office has not entered at the foot. The jobs below are made up, in the sample matter's style.

  • One rail, two ways to read it. GCs stays as it is. Courts lists each county with its total, then its justice precincts in number order, each with its jobs and dollars. The counts follow Due in 30 days / Upcoming, so they foot with the grid.
  • Over the limit never sits under a precinct. A justice court hears $20,000 or less, so a bigger job reads Over $20,000 · county court under its county, in red, the way its chip already does.

Is this the best we can do?

  • First draft: a Court filter above the table. Rejected. A select hides the counts and dollars, which is what the GC rail was built to show. The same rail, read by court, keeps one way of choosing.
  • First draft: every precinct under its county, the big jobs too. Revised. A job over $20,000 cannot be filed in a justice court, so listing it under a precinct would mislead the very lawyer choosing by court. It gets its own red line per county.
  • First draft: filter only. Revised. Clicking through seven courts to see the book is work. All courts now bands the rows by court with each band's total, and the print carries the same bands.
  • GC and court together? Not now. One lens at a time keeps the rail short and the summary line honest. A lawyer choosing by court rarely needs the GC, and the GC rail is one press away.
  • Not done. Each justice court's address, phone and filing fee: that needs a court directory the office keeps, and would make a good next step for the matter's Where to file too. Remembering a lawyer's last pick between visits.
