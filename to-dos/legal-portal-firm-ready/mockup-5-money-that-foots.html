Money that foots

The Account tab's statement and the matter card's demand, as the firm reads them. The first pair is a harder copy of the sample matter: job 1042 carries an agreed write-down from $18,400 to $15,900 and a linked check; job 1189 has a $6,000 Stripe bill and a $1,500 ACH payment recorded on the job with no bill attached. The firm has entered a $450 fee and a $350 filing cost. The office applied a $3,000 recovery, and its Mark applied wrote the firm's 33% as a $990 cost. The sample's payment has no date because the fixture spells the column payment_date. That is item 9's (the consistent sample matter); this item leaves the fixture alone.

Is this the best we can do?

  • Should an unlinked payment just say "unapplied" instead? No. The app already has the owner's rule for money on no bill (work on no bill first, then the oldest bill, v2.3592 and v2.4534). The customer portal and the bill tab use it. A legal statement that ignores a payment the debtor can show a receipt for overstates the claim. The packet now uses the same rule, so the office desk, the firm and the customer's own page agree.
  • What if the rows still cannot meet the balance? The kernel names the known causes as rows of their own: work on no bill that a payment covered, and a bill marked paid with no payment recorded. Anything else becomes one row per job that says the records do not explain the difference. The office sees it on the desk before the firm does. So the column always reaches the Balance, by construction, and nothing is hidden.
  • Should the write-down sit directly under its invoice, out of date order? No. A statement of account runs by date and a running balance only reads in date order. The write-down carries the job number, so the pair is easy to match.
  • Drop the contingency row from the matter entirely? No. The office books it as a cost on its own side. The firm should still see what it earned, on its own line, outside the demand.
  • One more thing found while drawing it: the write-down used to make the shared payment rule think the written-down part was work on no bill, so a payment with no bill would have paid that phantom first. The packet hands the rule the job total less the write-downs.
