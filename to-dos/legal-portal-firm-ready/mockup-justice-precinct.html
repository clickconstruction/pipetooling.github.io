Which justice court each case would fall under

The owner, 2026-10-06: the overwhelming number of cases are below twenty thousand dollars and between San Antonio, Guadalupe County, Austin and San Marcos … I will almost certainly be taking many of these to the small claims courts run by precincts and justices of the peace … show on the legal portal which precinct in which county these cases would fall under.

What the law gives us to work with

  • The justice court hears a claim up to $20,000 (Gov't Code § 27.031), interest and costs aside, attorney's fees included. The app already applies this cap in the demand letter and the Lien desk.
  • Venue is a county and a precinct (TRCP 502.4). The defendant may be sued where the defendant resides, where the contract was to be performed, or where the incident occurred. For a plumbing bill the contract was performed at the job site, so the job's own address gives one proper venue, and the payer's address gives another. The lawyer picks; the portal shows both.
  • A justice court cannot foreclose a lien on land (§ 27.031(b)). The debt suit can go to the precinct court; a § 53.158 foreclosure goes to district court in the property's county. The portal has to say so beside the precinct, or a lien job reads as a small-claims case.
  • Precincts are drawn on county maps. Bexar, Travis and Hays publish their justice precinct boundaries as live map layers the app can ask with a point, the same way it already asks the state's parcel layer for the owner of record. Comal publishes map data on its open-data portal; Guadalupe and Williamson publish maps to read by eye. Where there is no layer the office enters the precinct from the county's map once, and it stays on the property record.

Is this the best we can do?

  • Show both venues, never pick one. A first draft picked the job site's precinct and called it the court. TRCP 502.4 gives the lawyer a choice, and the defendant's precinct is sometimes the better one (a GC in San Antonio, work in Blanco). The block names both with the rule and leaves the choice to counsel.
  • Say what a justice court cannot do. Without the lien line, a $4,800 job with a lien window open reads as a small-claims case; the foreclosure is a district-court suit. One line beside the precinct keeps the firm from filing in the wrong building.
  • The county's own map, dated, and the lawyer confirms. Precinct lines moved in 2021–2023 redistricting and will move again. The lookup reads the county's live layer and the source line carries the date; the words say confirm with the clerk. A hand-typed precinct says entered by the office, so the firm knows which it is reading.
  • A point on a map can sit on a line. The geocoder's point for a rural address can land a few hundred feet off. Where the point falls within 100 m of a precinct boundary the lookup writes both precincts (2 or 3 — on the line) rather than one wrong answer, and the record asks the office to settle it.
  • Bexar's places. Each Bexar precinct has two courts (Place 1 and Place 2); either may be filed in. The block says Precinct 3, Place 1 or 2 rather than inventing a rule.
  • The precinct lives on the property record, not the matter. It is a fact about an address, like the county and the parcel; it serves every job at that address and every future matter, and the office edits it in one place.
  • Court addresses come after the precinct. PR 1 seeds the six counties' courts from their own pages; anything not verified against a county page stays out, since a wrong courthouse address costs the firm a trip. The county page link is always there.
  • Not done now. E-filing links and the constable's fee schedule wait for the firm's answer on what it needs at intake (#85, items 12–15).
