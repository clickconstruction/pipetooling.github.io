Is this the best we can do?

  • Documents first, narrative second. A narrative that names exhibits the firm cannot open is a tease. Build the shelf, then the story that points at it.
  • One line on what it shows, required. A file named by its export helps nobody. The desk will not save a document without a title and the one line, and the portal shows the line beside the title.
  • Hold, like entries. The office may keep a document back with a reason — its own aging report is context, not an exhibit — and the firm sees only the count, exactly as with held entries. The share-everything default of item 29 stands.
  • The narrative is labelled, dated and signed. A band on the tab and on the print says it is the office's account, by whom, when. Counsel reads it as a brief from the client, which is what it is. It can be rewritten; the portal shows only the current one, the desk keeps who last saved it.
  • Narrative first in the tab order. The first draft put it last. A lawyer opening a new matter wants the story before the ledger; the ledger is one tab away.
  • Markdown, not a rich editor. The office already writes markdown in the help guides, the app already renders it, and a session can write it through MCP. Tables, bold, lists; no raw HTML, no images (those are documents).
  • Emails as documents, not as entries. A thread saved as a PDF or .eml is one document with one line on what it shows; turning each email into a record-of-contact entry would bury the firm. The record of contact stays what the office logged.
  • MCP writes in the plan-and-apply shape, like cost batches and HR entries: a dry run with a hash, then the write, as the dev, logged. Size cap 10 MB a file; PDF, image, .eml and .txt only.
  • Mockups stay on the sample matter. A first cut of this page carried a live matter's names and figures out of the owner's mailbox into the repo; it was stopped, rightly. The evidence itself goes on the matter, through the door this plan builds, never into a file that is committed.
  • Not done. Previews inside the portal (the firm opens the file), versions of a document (remove and add), and drafting the narrative from the record by one click on the desk — the MCP verb is that door for now.
