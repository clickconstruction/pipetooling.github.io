The Lien grid's rows, stripped

The grid itself does not change: by the owner's call (2026-10-05, and before it on punch list #41) the firm sees every billed job with money open and a lien month, as the office's book holds it. What changes is what travels behind each row. Today the function sends whole table rows; the grid draws twelve columns of dates and dollars and the rest rides along unseen.

The select names the columns, and shapeLienBookForCounsel (a shared kernel, tested) strips the rows again before they are sent, so a widened select later cannot leak by itself. The page's What you cannot do stops promising that the firm sees nothing of unreleased accounts; it says what the grid is.

Is this the best we can do?

  • Why not referred matters only? Asked again on 2026-10-05 and the answer was the whole book, stripped: the grid is counsel's early look at where a lien is about to be lost, and a matter is referred after that point. The firm reads dates and dollars; the office's words stay home.
  • Why keep the GC's notice rule? It is a standing rule, not a thing anyone said, and the book's held state depends on it. Without it the grid would show a held month as due.
  • Why shape twice? A column list in a select is a promise only until someone adds a column. The kernel is the contract and the test holds it.
  • What this does not do: the matter path's own `select('*')` on filings, demand letters and contacts is item 23; the lien book was the one visible to a firm with no matters at all.
