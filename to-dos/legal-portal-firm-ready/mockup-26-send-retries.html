Is this the best we can do?

  • A jsonb ledger on the event, or a new table of sends? The jsonb column. The ledger lives and dies with its event, needs no new RLS or training-mode fences, and the person's standing (the line we show) is two plain columns on the person. A table would earn its keep only for a send history, which the filed copies in sent_documents already are.
  • How many tries? Twelve, one per tick: an hour. Long enough to ride out a mail-service blip; short enough that a dead address is called dead the same hour. The person's line stays until an email gets through, so a given-up event is still visible.
  • Who gets a retried event? Only the people who were to hear it when it happened. The ledger is frozen on the first tick, so someone who confirms during the retry hour is not sent an old event (the v2.3325 rule).
  • What if the function is deployed before the migration? It notices the missing column and stamps after one pass, as before, so it never re-sends every five minutes. The portal reads * so it never breaks on the new columns.
  • Why rotate the stop link at all? Only when the person turns emails back on: then a stop link in an old, perhaps forwarded, email no longer pauses them again. Otherwise it never changes, and every email's link works.
  • Should the firm see the mail service's error text? No. The office does, for diagnosis. The firm gets what to do: stop the wrong address and add the right one. (Refined after the first draw: the line first said only “add the right one”, which left the bad address being tried on every event.)
  • Bounces after acceptance? Out of scope: this catches a send the mail service refuses. A bounce reported later by Resend's webhook would need its own pass.
