Is this the best we can do?

  • Second look (the owner asked). Ask for Robin or Casey in place of a list of full names, since that is what the person answering will say; what each number is for in place of at any time, which promised what the office may not keep; both numbers in one format; a word on what the controller decides; and the same line on the printed packet, where a lawyer holding paper had no number at all.
  • A strip, not a tab. A lawyer reaching for a phone should not have to find a page. The strip sits on every view, under the letterhead and above the tabs, and stays small: one line on a computer, four on a phone.
  • Two numbers, not a list. The office number reaches whichever assistant is in; the controller's number is the one that answers when the office does not. The assistants are named by first name so the lawyer knows who to ask for, but they share the office line rather than each carrying a mobile on the firm's page.
  • Where the names come from. The function reads the active users with the assistant role (names) and the controller role (name and phone) from the users table, so a new assistant or a changed number shows on the firm's next open with nothing to edit; the particulars block was the other home, but it is still 0 of 8 filled and names change with staff. If the controller has no phone on file the strip shows the office number alone, and the Legal desk's firm window says the line is missing. The office number is the portal letterhead's company phone. This needs a legal-portal redeploy; the sample portal draws the same strip from the fixture.
  • Numbers are tel: links, so on a phone one tap dials. On a computer they are plain copper text.
  • Not done: email addresses. The firm already writes to the office through a matter's question box, and that lands in the record. A loose email would not.
