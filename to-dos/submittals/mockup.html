Is this the best we can do?

What Draft A gets right

One table with both columns, statuses derived from model numbers, a revision that exists before it is sent, the house asked from the row, the GC's redlines as rows. Rev 2's Mail Drop and Steven's Sep 14 email could not happen on Draft A.

Where it falls short

It starts too late. A tab after Cover Letter documents alternates that were created weeks earlier, at the moment a quote line was picked. The eleven rows on SpaceX that differ from the schedule (eight alternates, a superseded model, an equal, a design change) were all decided on the Pricing compare, and nothing on that screen said so. On SpaceX the picks came after the win; on a bid priced from quotes they come before the price goes out, and the screen is just as silent. The fix belongs where the decision is made: a Specified column on the compare, a status chip the moment a pick differs from it. Wendi would have seen "8 alternates" the day she picked NWS, not heard about them from Steven on Sep 14.

A submittal is not a document, it is the state of the product decisions. Draft A makes a package the artifact. The truer object is one row per tag that lives from the fixture schedule to the purchase order: specified → quoted → picked → priced → sent (in lieu of) → won → submitted → approved → ordered. The "Submittals" tab is a lens on those rows at one moment, the PDF a render of the lens. Switching from Moore to NWS is then a re-pick, and the revision diff writes itself.

The reason belongs to the pick, not to the house. Draft A asks Kim why, on the desk and on the house's page; Kim can only say what NWS stocks. "Josam over JR Smith because one drainage package" and "50-gallon because the 40 is four weeks out" are Wendi's reasons at pick time. One chip when she taps a price that differs from spec. So Draft B changes the house's page: the "why yours instead?" question goes, and it asks only what the house knows — the sheet, the lead time, whether the specified model is still made.

Lead time is the schedule question, and the app never asks it. Caleb's real worry was fixtures on site in two to three weeks. Quotes carry a good-until date and no lead time. Add it to the quote line and the vendor page; then the Stage Plan can say "DWH-1 lands Oct 10, Top-out is Oct 2" without anyone doing arithmetic.

Disclose at bid time or carry the risk. The tough situation you described is priced in silently today. If the cover letter carries a short "in lieu of the schedule" block, the GC accepts the alternates when they accept the number, and a rejected alternate later is a change order, not our cost. That is a policy call, and it is the biggest lever on this page.

Where it still goes wrong

The specified column is only as good as the schedule read. The robot can read most schedules; a hand paste is the fallback, and both need a person to confirm before a bid goes out on it. "Equal" is the engineer's word, not ours: every alternate stays "proposed" until the GC page says approved. Some GCs review in their own system (Procore and the like): the PDF export stays first-class, and their decisions get typed back onto the rows by us. Old redlines in old PDFs: the robot can lift Bluebeam comments into row decisions, and will sometimes misfile one; show its work.

What robots back-fill

Read the fixture schedule off the plans (the twin substrate already locates it). Split a vendor submittal PDF by tag and file the pages. Draft reasons from supersession notices and "limited inventory" marks on the sheets. Turn a redlined PDF's annotations into row decisions on the older revision. Ask, never decide: each lands as a suggestion with its source page.

So: Draft B

Keep the desk and the GC's page from Draft A; the house's page loses its "why". Move the beginning of the story onto the Pricing compare and the Cover Letter, make the row the object, and let the tab be the lens. Next artboard.

Second pass · the portal

Is this the best we can do?

What the portal draft gets right

It is the page the GC already opens for bills and stage dates, not a fifth link. The submittal reads in the portal's own voice, decisions land on rows with a name, and the review is one card under the job it belongs to.

Where it falls short

Nobody knows it is there. The portal is pulled, never pushed: a customer opens it when a bill's footer sends them. A submittal revision that sits unread for six days is the SpaceX thread again. Every revision needs to go out as an email or text with the link and the three-line summary, the way the bid room link goes out, and the office needs to see opened Sep 16 by Logan the way it sees room views.

The portal has five asks and no list. Request a visit, ask us to bid, need other dates, ask the office about a bill, and now decide on a submittal, each in its own corner. The customer has no answer to "what does Click need from me?" We built that list for ourselves and called it Needs You. The GC deserves the mirror: a We need you strip at the top of the portal with every open ask on their jobs, and nothing else until there is one.

A decision is half a conversation. Logan says hold 1.0 gpf; Wendi answers Rev 4, TET2UB31, 3 weeks; Caleb asks about carriers. Today that exchange lives in an email thread the office loses. The app already keeps customer contacts, bid notes and inbox requests; the portal should show one conversation per job, both sides, with the office replying from the inbox it already works. Every decision is then an entry in that thread, not a form result.

Not every customer is a GC. A homeowner's portal is a statement and a stage card. A submittal card, review columns and tag numbers are noise there. The card appears only when the job carries a package, and the words stay plain: the products we will install, and the ones that differ from your plans.

Some GCs review in their own system. Structura may run submittals through Procore or a PM's inbox, and no portal changes that. So the PDF stays first-class, the decisions summary is copyable, the portal takes their redlined PDF back in a drop zone, and the office can type their decisions onto the rows from the email they sent instead.

Where it still goes wrong

Links travel. The page is forwarded to an engineer, then to someone at SpaceX; the name typed on the decision is whoever typed it. Fine for a submittal, and each forward should be a scoped link with its own opened record, the way the portal already mints separate views. Two reviewers disagree. The last decision wins and the earlier one stays in the thread with its name. A reply is only as good as the office's inbox habit. The conversation works if the estimator inbox is worked daily; the Needs You card for a GC message waiting is what keeps it honest.

What robots back-fill here

Draft the reply to a GC decision from the row's own facts (the resubmit product, its lead time). Lift decisions out of a redlined PDF the GC drops back. Write the revision email's three-line summary. Never send, never decide.

So: Draft C

Keep the submittal line and its review card as drawn. Add the We need you strip at the top of the portal, one conversation per job under the submittal line, a push when a revision lands, and a way in for the GC who reviews in their own system. Next artboard.
