Design · how we got here

Why it changed

The owner's observation, 2026-09-15

It is the customer who reviews the drafts, and the customer should not see what the GC is doing

The plan put review inside the customer portal with the GC as the reviewer, because the SpaceX thread ran through Structura. Two things are wrong with that. The person who decides on products is the customer — whoever the plans belong to — and the portal is a statement: bills, balances, stage dates, the GC's business. A reviewer must never see money, and a reviewer must not need a portal.

So

A submittal review is a room, not a portal card — the Bid Room's shape. One link per reviewer, one purpose, nothing else on the page: the rows, the cut sheets, a decision per row, a note, one conversation about the products. The office names the reviewers per submittal (the customer's contacts, or typed); a GC's person can be named the same way on the jobs where the GC decides. The customer portal does not change. Nothing about money, suppliers or the GC ever reaches the room.

What this keeps from the plan: the rows and the package (stages 1–3, built), the shares and events tables, the typed name and email on a decision (decision 6), the token page and its stale-revision rule, the four Needs You cards, the won question. What it drops: the portal line and card (5a), the We need you strip (5b), the portal message kind (5c). What it moves: the conversation onto the room.

Second pass · 2026-09-15

Is this the best we can do?

What the room gets right

The reviewer is whoever decides on products, named by the office. No money, no suppliers, no GC on the page. A link per person with its own opened stamp and its own decisions. The customer portal is untouched. The Bid Room already proved the shape.

Where it falls short

It still asks the customer to read a table. Draft A's page was a 22-row table with three columns of model numbers. A customer's real question is smaller: which rows differ from my plans, and why? So the room leads with the rows that differ (three cards, each saying why in one sentence), folds the nineteen that match under one line, and offers approve all 19 as marked. The table is the PDF's job.

"Alternate" is our word. The chips (superseded · equal · alternate · design change · accessory) are the estimator's vocabulary. The customer needs two: as your plans specify and differs — and for a differing row, the why in plain words. The room translates; the tab keeps the chips.

Links travel further than intended.
