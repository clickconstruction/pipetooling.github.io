## The ask, in the owner's words

> An estimator takes a page off of plans and sends them to a supply house who gives them quotes back, and then we bid the job and send in our cover letter, and if we win the job then we ask the business we got those quotes from for submittals that we share with the client. This is less than ideal and can put us in tough situations sometimes. … Maybe we should create infrastructure that could make the capture and presentation of submittals easier for all parties, and in a way robots could back fill, and maybe we could even show in a portal for our customers to come see and leave feedback.

The case that prompted it: SpaceX BA-02N (B375 → J1007), September 2026. The first submittal was built from Moore Supply's quote and sent Aug 28; Structura redlined ten substitutions inside the PDF on Sep 1; a revised set built from National Wholesale's quote went out Sep 10 by iCloud Mail Drop with no copy in the office; on Sep 14 the GC asked for a list of everything not on the schedule and why alternates were submitted at all. The table Wendi needed (specified · submitted · reason · lead time) was rebuilt by hand from the plans and the NWS quote: eight alternates, a superseded model, an "equal", one design change (a 1.28 gpf flush valve against a 1.0 gpf spec), one valve missing from the quote, and four accessories the schedule leaves to the contractor.

## The decision

**A submittal is not a document; it is the state of the product decisions.** One row per fixture tag lives from the plan's fixture schedule to the purchase order: specified → quoted → picked → priced → sent (in lieu of) → won → submitted → approved → ordered. The new **Submittals** tab (after Cover Letter, as sketched) is a lens on those rows at one moment; the package PDF is a render of the lens; the customer portal shows the same rows to the GC and takes decisions back.

Draft A (a desk after Cover Letter, a house cut-sheet page, a GC review page) was drawn first and kept; the critique moved the *start* of the story onto the Pricing compare (a **Specified** column, a status chip the moment a pick differs from spec, the reason asked at the pick, lead time on the quote line) and the house's page lost its "why yours instead?" question — the house knows availability and lead time, not our reasons. Draft C put the review inside the portal the GC already opens, added a **We need you** strip (the customer's Needs You), one **conversation per job**, and a **push** when a revision lands. Rejected: a job-side tab as the home (the products were chosen on the bid); a separate submittal link when the customer has a portal (the portal is the home, the token page is the fallback for a GC without one); verified identity for reviewers (typed name and email, like the bid room's signer — right for a submittal, not for a lien waiver).

### Owner decisions (answered 2026-09-14)

1. Home: **the Submittals tab lives on the bid**, read by the job and the portal.
2. Cover letter: **no submittal or alternates block at this time** — "it just confuses the customer". The priced alternate-versions block is unrelated and stays. Revisit only if a GC rejects an accepted alternate after the win (carried to `owner-decisions-pending.md` when v1 ships).
3. Cut sheets: **Wendi uploads them** (drop the vendor's PDF on the tab, attach per row). The house's own upload page is not v1.
4. Lead time: **Wendi sets it** on the row. Long term the owner wants to **ask a supply house in writing, where they can respond** — and only on the cut-sheet ask, after the win, never on the quote page. That written ask (a request lane + the house's page) is the deferred Stage 3b below, owner-gated; the quote page does not change.
5. Push: **wait for them to open the portal.** No revision email or text from the app; the office tells the GC its own way. The portal (or the token page for a GC without one) is the door; opened stamps still show the office whether they looked.
6. Reviewer identity: **a typed name and email on the decisions.**
7. The We need you strip: **every portal, drawn only when something waits.**

**New (v1)**

Tables (all RLS'd on the bid's access — `can_access_bid_for_pricing(bid_id)` for staff reads/writes; token-credentialed edge functions for outsiders; every CREATE TABLE ends with both appliers):

- `bid_specified_products` — `bid_id`, `tag`, `fixture` (the count-row name it maps to, nullable), `manufacturer`, `model`, `description`, `source` (`pasted · robot · typed`), `confirmed_by`, `confirmed_at`. UNIQUE (bid_id, tag).
- `bid_quote_lines` + `alternate_reason_kind` (`lead_time · discontinued · in_stock · equal · cost · other`), `alternate_reason_note`, `lead_time_days` (typed by the estimator in v1; the house fills it only through the deferred written ask). (`pick_reason` / `pick_source` stay the robot's *why this house* — different fact.)
- `bid_submittals` — `bid_id`, `job_ledger_id` (nullable; set when the job is opened from the bid), `title`, `rev_number`, `status` (`draft · shared · reviewed · superseded`), `note`, `package_path`, `shared_at`, `created_by`. UNIQUE (bid_id, rev_number).
- `bid_submittals` also carries `source_files jsonb` — the vendor PDFs dropped on the revision (`path`, `house_id`, `pages`), so a row's sheet is a file + page list, not a copy.
- `bid_submittal_items` — `submittal_id`, `tag`, `sequence_order`, specified (`manufacturer · model · description`), submitted (`manufacturer · model · label`), `supply_house_id`, `source_quote_line_id`, `status` (`as_specified · superseded · equal · alternate · design_change · missing · accessory`), `reason_kind`, `reason_note`, `lead_time_days`, `sheet_file` (index into `source_files`) + `sheet_pages int[]`, `sheet_source` (`estimator · robot`, and `house` once stage 3b exists), `carried_from_item_id`, `review_decision` (`approved · revise · rejected`, nullable), `review_note`, `reviewed_by_name`, `reviewed_by_email`, `reviewed_at`.
- `bid_submittal_shares` — the GC's link: `submittal_id`, `customer_id`, `token`, `via` (`portal · link`), `shared_at`, `shared_by`, `opened_at`, `open_count`, `decided_at`, `closed_at`; `bid_submittal_events` (`share_id`, `event_type` `view · decided · reply · file_dropped`, `metadata`, `occurred_at`). No recipient email and no Resend id: nothing is sent (decision 5).
- *(deferred, stage 3b)* `bid_submittal_requests` — the written ask per house: `submittal_id`, `supply_house_id`, `contact_id`, `sent_email`, `token`, `status`, `resend_email_id`, `viewed_at`, `received_at`, `needed_by`, `last_reminded_at`, `reminder_count`.
- `job_portal_messages` — the conversation: `job_ledger_id`, `bid_id`, `author_kind` (`office · customer · robot`), `author_user_id`, `author_name`, `author_email`, `body`, `ref_kind` (`submittal · submittal_item · stage · bill`, nullable), `ref_id`, `created_at`. Realtime not needed: the office reads it through an inbox row.
- Storage bucket `bid-submittals` (private): `<bid_id>/<submittal_id>/<file_index>.pdf` for each dropped vendor file (trimmed to the pages on rows at Done or at Share), `<bid_id>/<submittal_id>/package-rev<N>.pdf` for the render. ## The plan — six stages, fifteen PRs in v1 (plus the deferred written ask), a live gate at the end of every stage

Migrations start with `SET lock_timeout = '3s';`, number from `origin/main`, push only after merge with `bash scripts/db-push.sh`, then `npm run gen-types:linked` as its own chore PR.
