## The ask, in the owner's words

> An estimator takes a page off of plans and sends them to a supply house who gives them quotes back, and then we bid the job and send in our cover letter, and if we win the job then we ask the business we got those quotes from for submittals that we share with the client. This is less than ideal and can put us in tough situations sometimes. … Maybe we should create infrastructure that could make the capture and presentation of submittals easier for all parties, and in a way robots could back fill, and maybe we could even show in a portal for our customers to come see and leave feedback.

The case that prompted it: SpaceX BA-02N (B375 → J1007), September 2026. The first submittal was built from Moore Supply's quote and sent Aug 28; Structura redlined ten substitutions inside the PDF on Sep 1; a revised set built from National Wholesale's quote went out Sep 10 by iCloud Mail Drop with no copy in the office; on Sep 14 the GC asked for a list of everything not on the schedule and why alternates were submitted at all. The table Wendi needed (specified · submitted · reason · lead time) was rebuilt by hand from the plans and the NWS quote: eight alternates, a superseded model, an "equal", one design change (a 1.28 gpf flush valve against a 1.0 gpf spec), one valve missing from the quote, and four accessories the schedule leaves to the contractor.

## The decision

**A submittal is not a document; it is the state of the product decisions.** One row per fixture tag lives from the plan's fixture schedule to the purchase order: specified → quoted → picked → priced → sent (in lieu of) → won → submitted → approved → ordered. The new **Submittals** tab (after Cover Letter, as sketched) is a lens on those rows at one moment; the package PDF is a render of the lens; the customer portal shows the same rows to the GC and takes decisions back.

**Redrawn 2026-09-15 (decisions 8–11):** the review moved out of the customer portal into a room of its own, with the customer's architect or designer as the named reviewer and the GC watching. Draft A (a desk after Cover Letter, a house cut-sheet page, a GC review page) was drawn first and kept; the critique moved the *start* of the story onto the Pricing compare (a **Specified** column, a status chip the moment a pick differs from spec, the reason asked at the pick, lead time on the quote line) and the house's page lost its "why yours instead?" question — the house knows availability and lead time, not our reasons. Draft C put the review inside the portal the GC already opens, added a **We need you** strip (the customer's Needs You), one **conversation per job**, and a **push** when a revision lands. Rejected: a job-side tab as the home (the products were chosen on the bid); a separate submittal link when the customer has a portal (the portal is the home, the token page is the fallback for a GC without one); verified identity for reviewers (typed name and email, like the bid room's signer — right for a submittal, not for a lien waiver).

### Owner decisions (answered 2026-09-14)

1. Home: **the Submittals tab lives on the bid**, read by the job and the portal.
2. Cover letter: **no submittal or alternates block at this time** — "it just confuses the customer". The priced alternate-versions block is unrelated and stays. Revisit only if a GC rejects an accepted alternate after the win (carried to `owner-decisions-pending.md` when v1 ships).
3. Cut sheets: **Wendi uploads them** (drop the vendor's PDF on the tab, attach per row). The house's own upload page is not v1.
4. Lead time: **Wendi sets it** on the row. Long term the owner wants to **ask a supply house in writing, where they can respond** — and only on the cut-sheet ask, after the win, never on the quote page. That written ask (a request lane + the house's page) is the deferred Stage 3b below, owner-gated; the quote page does not change.
5. Push: **wait for them to open the portal.** No revision email or text from the app; the office tells the GC its own way. The portal (or the token page for a GC without one) is the door; opened stamps still show the office whether they looked.
6. Reviewer identity: **a typed name and email on the decisions.**
7. The We need you strip: **every portal, drawn only when something waits.**

### Owner decisions (2026-09-15) — who reviews, and where

8. **The reviewer is the customer's architect or designer**, who works for the customer and is often separate from the GC. We are paid by the GC, and the GC wants to facilitate that relationship. We usually meet the reviewer in the GC's email chain after the win. So **nobody is named by default**: Share takes a name and email from that chain (people seen before on the same GC customer are offered), each person gets their own link and record, and the person's **role** (architect · owner's rep · designer · builder) sets the room's words.
9. **The review is a room, not a portal card** — the Bid Room's shape: one link per person, one purpose, and **nothing about money** (no bills, balances, stage dates, prices, quotes or supply-house names) on the page. The customer portal does not change; decisions 5 and 7 as they applied to the portal are withdrawn.
10. **The GC's contact is a watcher**: sees everything on the room, decides nothing, may ask a question. **The GC's portal shows nothing about the review** — not even a one-line status.
12. **One room link per bid; people identify themselves when they act** (owner, 2026-09-15, "I like your entire plan for stage four"). We send the link to the GC and the GC forwards it wherever it needs to go. Anyone with it can open the room and read everything; *just looking* asks nothing. Approve / Revise / Reject or a note opens the identify sheet (name · email · *I am* architect · owner's rep · designer · builder / GC · other), which creates the person's record on the bid, saves the action, and rewrites the address to their personal link ("Reviewing as Dana Whitfield · not you?"). A forwarded personal link asks the next person to say who they are. The office may pre-name people on Share (a personal link, and the room recognises their email if they arrive through the forward) but never has to. **Anyone who identifies may decide until the office marks them watching**; the office pre-marks the GC's PM as watching. The later decision on a row stands, the earlier stays on the thread with its name. The office closes the room when the products are approved; a closed link reads *this review is closed* and the PDF stays downloadable. The link lives on the bid's Submittals tab (a **Share** button beside New revision; after the first share, a *Room link · copy · opened N times* line above the revision chips and the people under it); the Bid Board row's chip (4b) jumps there; the job never hosts it.
11. Still the owner's call: whether the app may send the link (off by default; the office copies each link into the email chain), and whether a design-change card asks whose call it is (with an architect as the usual reviewer, the card states the changed value and leaves the call to them).

## The plan — six stages, fifteen PRs in v1 (plus the deferred written ask), a live gate at the end of every stage

Every PR ships alone (branch → PR → auto-merge): `npm run claim` for the version, one release note + one `docs/recent-features/` fragment, the help guide with the surface, a `docs/migrations/` fragment per migration, an `EDGE_FUNCTIONS.md` section per function, `npm run typecheck && npm run lint && npm test && npm run build` and the theme check green before the PR. Migrations start with `SET lock_timeout = '3s';`, number from `origin/main`, push only after merge with `bash scripts/db-push.sh`, then `npm run gen-types:linked` as its own chore PR. Functions deploy before the push when a deployed function would select a column the push adds (`bash scripts/deploy-functions.sh <name>`), and the client goes first when an old client would misread. After every push: `npm run check:migration-drift`; after every deploy: `npm run check:edge-drift`.

**Stage gate:** no stage starts until the previous one has been used on a real bid by Wendi and the owner has seen it. The gate for each stage is written under it.
