Is this the best we can do?

What the second look changed
  1. The step “?” marks stay, on open steps only. The first draft folded all eight into the one help button. That loses the best thing about them: each one starts the walkthrough at its own step. Hiding them on folded steps removes the clutter and keeps that.
  2. A folded step is one line, and an open step drops its summary. The first draft left every explanation and every summary printed. Reading the live page showed where the repeats come from: step 1 is open, so its summary and its two cards say the same thing an inch apart.
  3. Help is a labelled button with three choices spelled out, not a bare “?”. Words on this page went in yesterday at your request, so it must stay easy to find. It is the second line of a three line menu.
  4. “Choose from the takeoff” lives once, on the takeoff card where the button already is. The first draft kept it as a link in the header.
Looked at and left out
  • Dropping the explanations for good. They are the plain-words teaching of the page. They stay on whatever step is open.
  • Moving “See what the GC sees” into Help. It is an action she uses before sharing, so it keeps its own button.
  • Shortening the Next line. Its wording shipped two days ago to fix finding 2. It gets a week of use first.
What changes
Two header lines become one. The title’s “?”, Words on this page and the strip’s walkthrough button become one Help button. A folded step shows its summary and no explanation. An open step shows its explanation and no summary.
Size
Medium. Many small moves in the tab’s header and the step frame. The walkthrough’s stops point at some of these lines, so each stop is checked after.
What it costs
Walk me through it and Words on this page are one click further away after the first visit.

Is this the best we can do?

What the second look changed
  1. Two fixed shapes, not columns that come and go. The first draft hid any column that was empty. Then typing one reason on one row would make a column appear and the whole table jump. Now the shape changes only when the bid gains a schedule, which is something she does on purpose.
  2. Each part is its own line with its answer in the next cell. The first draft lined the answers up beside the parts with line breaks. They drift apart as soon as a long part name wraps.
  3. “No answer yet” is said once per row when nothing on the row is answered. The first draft printed it beside every part.
  4. Their answer keeps its own button. The first draft put it under ⋯. On her road she types answers from an email, so it is the button she presses most.
  5. The counts became filters. Step 3 already says “6 still need a cut sheet”. Pressing that number now shows the six. This is an addition, and it can be left out without hurting the rest.
Looked at and left out
  • Cards on a desktop. A table lets her run her eye down the cut sheet column. Cards are for the phone only.
  • Making the answer cell itself open Their answer. A cell that is secretly a button is hard to find.
  • Dropping the fixture’s takeoff name under the tag. When the tag was typed by hand it is the only link back to the takeoff.
What changes
The table draws one of two shapes. Parts become lines with their own answer. Split, Part of… and × move under ⋯. The house and the file name are said once when every row shares them. On a phone each fixture is a card.
Size
Medium. It is the rows table’s own file since yesterday’s split. The printed cover table and the GC’s page do not change.
What it costs
Split and Take off are one click further away. A bid with two supply houses still prints the house on each row.

Is this the best we can do?

What the second look changed
  1. The window gets wider. The first draft put a six column table inside the same 760 pixels. That leaves about 220 pixels for a part name, and these names run to 70 letters. At 940 the name gets about 400 pixels and the columns still fit.
  2. Parts are grouped by who sees them. The first draft kept a control on every part and nothing else. Grouping answers the question the window’s own header asks, “3 the GC sees · 5 order only”, without counting switches. The up and down arrows only show where order matters, on the GC’s group.
  3. The rejected part is marked where it sits. Not in the first draft at all. Today the window says “1 rejected” at the bottom and leaves her to remember which.
  4. An empty lead time says “add”. Seeing the live window showed the grey “lead: 3 wk” example on every part. Step 8 says 43 parts have no lead time, and this is where they are typed.
  5. Two leftovers go. The Lead time heading over a sentence, and the stray “Proposed” beside Cancel.
Looked at and left out
  • Editing parts right in the rows table with no window. Eight parts with five fields each need the room.
  • Folding the order-only parts away. She changes them as often as the others, and one line each is cheap.
  • A “same house for all” shortcut. Step 8 already has “Tick the 43” for filling many parts at once.
What changes
The title reads “Edit LAV-1”. The tag has a label. Parts are one line each under headings, in three groups. Proposed joins the status choices and is lit. A row added by hand opens titled “Add a row”, and Save waits until it has a tag or a part.
Size
Medium. One window and the parts list inside it.
What it costs
“Shown to” is a small menu instead of three always-visible choices, so changing it is two clicks, not one.
