7 · The second look is this the best we can do?

Why not B

  • It stands on the bank balance being the whole bank. That question has been open since Oct 10. A first screen that cries wolf teaches a person to stop looking.
  • Most of what the eight weeks expect is late money the page assumes will land. The loudest number would be the softest one.
  • It never looks past eight weeks. A thin pipeline of sold work would not show until the cash had already dried up.
  • In a good month it has nothing to say. What is worth keeping from it is one rule, and the pick keeps it.

Why not C

  • A picture shows where. It does not say what to do, who does it, or whether it worked. It still needs A under it.
  • Two of its five sections cannot show a pile, and it says so on its face.
  • On a phone it fills the first screen and the words beside it do the work. As a strip it costs one line and keeps its point.
  • It is the costliest to build well and the easiest to get wrong. The strip is the cheap part of it.

What could still be wrong with the pick

  • The call is what has gone unused, and the pick makes it the page. The bet is that it went unused because it was buried, dev only, and reached no one. Naming a person today writes a word in a box. If the call still goes unpressed after four Mondays on the first screen, the ritual is the problem and the page should fall back to three numbers and one list.
  • "Earned last week" is new. The page leads with this week so far, which is nearly empty on the Monday the call is made. The last full pay week is the fairer number. It is a change to a figure the owner chose.
  • The October choices. He turned down a three stop walk as the opening, and the four usual fixes under the dial. The pick is one screen and not a walk. The fixes and the dial leave the first page together.
  • Two moves on a phone. A third fits at a desk. A fourth is a list again.
  • A budget, held by a test. The first screen's words come from one kernel. A unit test can hold a phone's first screen to 150 words and 24 numbers, the way the plain words test holds a guide.

What I would cut even if nothing else changed

  1. The Daily profit rate card. It reads approved hours only, so it shows a loss beside a headline that shows a gain. The Oct 10 redraw named this and the card stayed.
  2. Four of the five lists. The piles on you, the desk's moves, This week and Fix the reading say the same six things. One list, each row saying what it moves.
  3. The truth check as its own panel. It explains the net position line. It belongs inside that line's Break it down.
  4. The bills and receipts lists on the page. At two phone screens they are the longest block, under everything. Show 14 days. Keep the rest behind the chart.
  5. The owner box on every move. A typed name reaches no one. Fill it from who did that work, and let a tap change it.
  6. The Monday sheet as a second view. It is three screens on a phone and says "one screen". A first screen that fits needs no sheet.
