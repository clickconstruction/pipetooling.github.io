Your week: one progress signal a tech can watch move

The app already records what shows a tech getting better: the clock, the jobs, the customer's stars, the callbacks. All of it stops at the office. This draws one card on the tech's home that shows the one signal the office picked for them, how far it moved this week, and the customer's words by name. Compared to themselves, never to each other.

The first idea: hours, jobs and stars as tiles, with a progress bar under them. It reads like the office's dashboard shrunk to a phone.

Why it lost. Four numbers, and none of them is the one. The article's whole point is one signal, held long enough to feel it move. Hours and jobs are activity, not progress. A busy week and a good week look the same.

The streak is a trap. One callback sends 23 back to 0. A year of clean work erased by one return teaches people to hide callbacks. Draft 2 counts the last 30 jobs instead, so one return costs one.

The stars average hides the words. 4.9 means nothing next to "Marcus explained everything." The words are the reward. The number is a summary for the office.

Three lines at most. The signal and where it stands. The week's move. The customer's words, only on a week a customer wrote some. Jordan had none this week, so there is no empty quote box.

Hatched hours. Hours waiting for approval show hatched beside the approved ones and count in the week's move. A slow approval week never freezes the bar. They turn solid once approved.

No signal yet. Until the office picks one, a tech has no Your week card. The person’s People desk shows the picker with Pick the one number they will see move.

A flat week. When nothing moved, the pill turns grey and says Same as last week. It never shows a red arrow on a tech's own screen. The dots still show the climb.

6 The six calls

  1. Low stars. Do 1 to 3 star answers reach the tech? Recommended: no, they stay with the office.
  2. The crew rating trend. Ever shown to the rated person? Recommended: not in this train.
  3. A callback's confirm. Count only after It was a callback (recommended), or count every inferred pair unless someone presses Not a callback?
  4. License targets. Which licenses and hour counts per trade, and who types the hours before the clock.
  5. The raise ladder. What earns each pay step. Its own card once written.
  6. Who picks each person's signal. Recommended: the office, on the People desk, with a role default.

Is this the best we can do? The second pass

Written after looking at the first render of draft 2, at the pane's width and in one column.

  • Jordan's bar did not move. 41 hours is half a percent of 8,000, so the big bar looked the same every week, which is the article's exact problem. The dots under it were a second long view. Swapped for a short green bar to the next 500 hour mark: 450 of 500, visibly close.
  • The lead's arrow read backwards. A green ▲ beside 12 hours under asked whether up was good. Now a ✓, a pill short enough for one line, and a note under the dots that the dashed line is the target.
  • The dots had no time. Added 8 weeks ago and This week under them.
  • The callback press leaned one way. It was a callback was the red, solid button, so the office's easiest press was the verdict against a tech. Both buttons now weigh the same.
  • The charts pushed past the phone. They were wider than the card and shoved Clock out off the edge. They now fit the card.
  • No signal yet. The first render had no answer for a tech the office has not set up. Added: no card until a signal is picked.
  • What stays. One signal per person, the customer's words by first name, the rolling count over a streak, no leaderboard. Nothing in the render argued against them.
