The Physical inbox card: a card that decides, not a list that lingers

Quickfill's Physical inbox station asks Can I get to the bottom of the pile? and gives the assistant one box to type what is still in the tray. Six months of marks show the same seven words retyped every morning. In GTD's words that is a status, not a decision: nothing in the box has a next action, so nothing leaves. Three ways to redraw the card are below with made-up papers. The pick is A: each paper typed once, one press per paper, and the only count the paper nobody has decided on. The presses here work.

0Today, as the station draws it, and what its history says

The box is the only control. The count is the number of lines typed this visit, so the station reads 0 open beside a seven-line note, and the Roller never lists it among the stations with work. The Mark refuses an empty box, so an empty tray, GTD's goal, is the one state the card cannot record.

AThe clarify deck: each paper typed once, one press per paper · the pick

GTD's inbox step, drawn as a card. A paper that takes two minutes is done, never typed. Anything else is typed once with the day it came. Its presses are the six answers GTD gives a paper: Done, Trash, File it (where), Task (an owner and a day, through the header's Task window), Ask (dispatch or estimating), Waiting for (whom, until when), Someday. One more press, Comes back, makes the paper a routine, a repeating task in the Checklist, so the kind stops arriving in the tray. A paper leaves the deck on any press. The count is the paper nobody has decided on, with its age.

Where the seven words go, once
  • Tolls → a routine, Pay and sort the toll bills · every Friday. No screen holds a toll bill today.
  • Insurance cards → a routine on the plan's renewal. The plan and its renewal day are on People → Vehicles.
  • Renewals (registrations) → a routine on the 1st. A truck has no registration day in the app yet.
  • Certificates (licenses, OSSF, medgas, TDLR) → a routine on the 1st, 60 days ahead. A sub's license has an expiry on People → Subs. Our own have no dated home.
  • Extra phones → one task, or Someday. The lines are Settings → Company phone seats. The handsets are a drawer.
  • Lawsuit stuff → File it, the Legal desk's matter. Its next steps are the desk's planned steps.
  • Demand letters → File it, the letter's record on the Lien desk. Returned mail goes on the letter.

This sort takes twenty minutes and happens whichever card is picked. It is what empties the box. The card's job is to keep it empty.

BThe list with dates: the box becomes lines that carry over, each with its age

The cheapest honest change. The last mark's note is split into lines and pre-filled, each with the day it first appeared in the history and a ✕. A line gets a Task door. The count is the lines carried, and the oldest age sits in the header. No table: the ages come from walking the mark events back.

Where B falls short
  • It keeps the premise that the box holds kinds. GTD's inbox holds things. A kind never leaves.
  • The ages are honest but idle: tolls · 191 d is true and tells nobody what to press.
  • No place for waiting for or someday, the two answers that make a paper leave without finishing it.
  • The Mark still needs the list, so an empty tray still cannot be recorded.
  • The history improves only by the oldest age. The count stays a count of words.

CRetire the station: the tray stays paper, the app keeps the tasks

GTD does not need the inbox in software. The tray is the capture tool, and what matters is that every paper becomes a task in a trusted list, gets filed, or gets tossed. The trusted list here is the Checklist. The Assistant Dailys station already has a physical inbox box among the day's duties, the header has the three doors on every page, and the Checklist's Review board shows every open task by person with its age. So remove the station, set the four routines up once, and let the Dailys row say the tray was looked at.

?Is A the best we can do?

  • Every press is a decision. There is no box that takes a status. A paper that is in the deck has not been decided, and the card says so with its age.
  • Two-minute papers are never typed. GTD's own rule, and the deck's intro line says it. The deck sees a few papers a week once the routines exist, and most marks read 0 undecided. That is the goal, not a problem.
  • The seven words get homes, from the card. Comes back makes the routine and lists it under the deck, so the kinds that lived in the note have a visible place with a next day and an owner.
  • Waiting for and Someday exist. The Checklist has neither. The deck holds both as folds, out of the count, read on the Mark.
  • The history turns honest with no new column. The mark's Open items becomes the undecided count. The oldest age and the day's decisions are extra facts for the Mark history redraw to draw.
  • Left out, on purpose: guessing a routine from repeated titles, a scan or photo of the paper, a Todo Tooling bridge. Comes back is a press, not a guess. A photo can come later if the owner wants the paper itself. Handing a paper to the owner's own GTD inbox is his call, below.
  • The real risk: typing. If a paper is typed as a kind again, tolls, the deck is B. The intro line, the Comes back press and the owner's one-time sort are the three things against it.
