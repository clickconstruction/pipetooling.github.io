GC mode, the schedule builder: today and the shapes weighed, 2026-10-27

The owner, as a sub on another GC's job, saw that GC's schedule as one list of 50 rows in 8 phases. Each row had a start, an end, a day count, a type and an assignee. The job ran 20 weeks. A quick-lube shell is a job that repeats, so he wants to make a schedule like that once and put it on the next job. Every name and date here is made up. The critique and the owner's calls are in README.md beside this file.

A template is made one way: a job being built saves its schedule. It keeps the trades' lines and the two inspections. It drops the job's own work, the milestones, the phases and any line this job's scope book lacks. Its lines never change once saved. There is no editor, no kind, and no paste.

What the draft draws
One bar a lineEvery line of every trade's scope book or schedule of values. Nothing else.
Thirteen stagesSite prep to Closeout, fixed. A line's stage is read from the words in its name.
Two inspectionsRough-in and final, drawn by the app.
Three milestonesDry-in, the rough-in inspection and substantial completion.
Every dayEvery day is a working day. The chart marks weekends and six holidays and works them.

So the template is a timing overlay on the lines a job already has. It cannot add a row the scope book lacks. The office's own rows, permits, submittals, long-lead orders, a Notice to proceed, have no place in it. The phases are the thirteen fixed stages, not names the office gives.

The owner's calls, each with the recommendation the mock-up draws. The table in README.md holds them.
  1. Phases or flat. Phases, named by the office in the template. The thirteen stages stay as the fallback for a schedule drawn with no template.
  2. Who may make and change a template. The schedule team, the office and any GC project manager, the same people who move a bar. A save makes a new version.
  3. What a row is assigned to. A trade, Us or Office in the template. The company shows on the job once the trade is awarded. Not a person.
  4. Working days or every day. A template's days count Monday to Friday, with a switch per template to count every day. The chart keeps the 2026-10-05 ruling and works any day.
  5. What the trade portal shows. The phases strip and the dates to meet, above the bars it shows today. Never another company's bars.
  6. Where the builder lives. A Templates pill on GC projects, beside Customers. Not Settings.
  7. A row whose trade the job has no package for. It lands with No company yet, a door to Trade partners. The scope book is not changed by a template.
