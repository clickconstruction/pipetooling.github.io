What the price is made of: material, markup, tax, equipment, labor

One card on the Pricing Workbench that puts cost, price and profit side by side for material, tax, equipment and labor, and says plainly when labor is only what is left. A kind on each row so equipment stops being a guess, and the estimator's own tags adding up to the price. Second pass of 2026-10-19. Made-up bid throughout: Ridgeway Builders, Oak Hollow Apartments Building C (B2210), 48 units, priced at $186,400.

0 Before any card: the crew rate is the bid's rate until someone types one

The labor book already fills a bid's hours the first time Labor or Takeoffs opens. The rate never fills: it stays blank, labor costs $0, and the Workbench calls the bid uncosted. The crew rate card on the Labor tab already computes the number (the last 90 days' field wage × the burden factor) and only shows it. The smallest change with the most effect: the card's number is the bid's rate until a person types one, and every reader says which it is. Recommended first (PR 0, the owner's D5).

1 The card on the Workbench: cost, price and profit, line by line

Wendi's rule is the Price column. Material sold at +40%, tax on it, equipment on its own line, and the rest called labor. The Cost column is what the Workbench already knows, and the hours decide whether labor's cost is a number or a blank. The +40% is one company number (today's schedule-of-values factor, 1.5, becomes the material markup; the owner set it to 1.4) and the chip on the line overrides it for this bid. Driving, travel, permits and subs have no price of their own: Wendi's rule leaves them inside labor, so the card does too, and says so.

3 Equipment is a kind on the row, not a guess from its name

Today the composition bar guesses a row's kind from its words (rental, equipment, permit → other). A row keeps its kind instead: Fixture · Pipe · Fitting · Equipment · Allowance, the app's guess shown dashed until someone sets it. Equipment rows are the card's Equipment line and leave Material alone.

4 Tags are the estimator's own, and they add up: one card, two views
