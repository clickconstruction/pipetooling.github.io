Materials on site: cages, boxes and counts

Big jobs leave fittings, pipe and fixtures on site in stencilled cages and boxes. Today the app records when a part was delivered and nothing after that: not which cage it is in, how many are left, who took some, or why a number changed. My recommendation: a ledger of movements, never a typed-over quantity. Every number on a container is the sum of presses that each name what happened, so update with a reason is the only way to change one, and the loss report falls out of the same rows. The field gets a third tab on the Job Parts Tally, On site, one press from the Job Mode card. The office gets a page per job with a door in the Job window, the Pipeline's ⋯ tools and the bid's Procure step. Photos of a count are V2 through the Drive path the owner chose, and engraved parts are V3 on the same table.

Made-up data: job JP1042, Ridgeway Builders, Oak Hollow Apartments Building C, the crew Mateo Ruiz and Jordan Pike, the office's Carla Nguyen, Pecan Supply. Frames are fixed at 375 and 320 (the phone) and 1,280 (the desk); the page itself reads at 320 and up in both themes. Nothing here is built.

1The idea: a ledger of movements, never a typed-over number Recommended

Nobody types a quantity into a box. A press names what happened to some of a part in a container, and the number on the screen is the sum. Delivered adds. Used, Can't find, Moved out and Returned take away. A Count is a point in time that corrects the sum and leaves the difference on record. Because every change carries a reason, the owner's second ask, report when they are missing, is the same rows read a second way, with no second screen to keep.

Where does a crew type or tap more than today's tally for the same act?Nowhere. Today a request for more is a job pick, a typed fixture name, a typed search, a tap on the part, the arrows and Send to Office: six taps and two typed words.
