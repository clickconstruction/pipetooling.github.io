Accounts Receivable, Refreshed

The modal works: it reads who paid you, sweeps exact matches, and guards against counting a check twice. The screen just doesn't show that it does. Two directions below keep every behaviour and change what the eye lands on.

What the screenshot is telling us

  • The chrome explains instead of summarising. A three-line instruction paragraph and a dev-only disclosure row sit above the work. Nothing says how many deposits are waiting or how much money is unapplied.
  • The list has no state. A deposit row says amount, kind, date, name, "rem." — but not whether the modal already found its bill, or whether the money is probably recorded already. The smartest thing the modal does is invisible until you click.
  • The right pane leads with the least useful field. "Posted · Kind · Memo" take the top third; the memo box is the largest element on the screen and is optional. The number that matters, remaining to allocate, is a grey band.
  • Allocations don't read as rows. Target on the left, amount floating above the line on the right, the segmented control above both, "Add Additional Allocation" as a full-size button. One allocation looks like a form; three look like a mess.
  • Apply doesn't say what it will do. The footer is Cancel / Apply with no amount and no target.

Option A — same two rooms, better furniture

Keep the list-left, deposit-right muscle memory. Move the summary into the header, put state on every row, let the matches lead the right pane, and make the allocation a ledger line. Shown with the deposit from the screenshot, which has no open bill because its payment was recorded by hand already — the guard the guide calls "Link it instead".

Recommendation: Option A, and steal one thing from B — the Apply & next button in the footer, so a clerk can clear the pile without going back to the list.

The changes that carry the most, in order

  • Header summary replaces the paragraph. "4 deposits to match · $9,115.42 unapplied." The Stripe rule moves to the bill that carries the tag; the dev filter and Mark move under ⋯. The three-line explainer lives in the help guide it was copied from.
  • State on every deposit row. 1 exact match · 2 bills = deposit · probably recorded · pick by hand — the modal already computes all four; the row just never said. "Applied & returned" becomes a link at the foot of the list instead of a checkbox at the top.
  • The deposit header is the payer, not the metadata. Name first, then amount · kind · date · memo on one line, then a remaining meter that fills green as allocations are added. The memo box collapses to "Add a note".
  • Matches read as a list, not chips. Amount · job · address · age · the tag, one per line, the exact match tinted green and first. The same data the chips carry, in a shape the eye can compare.
  • Allocations are ledger rows. Kind · target · amount · ×, one line each, "+ Split across another bill" underneath. The amount box sits on the row it belongs to.
  • The footer says what Apply does. "Applies $250.00 to J992 · Done Right Foundation. The job goes to Paid." Apply carries the amount; Apply & next clears the pile.

How it would ship

Client only, no migration, no edge function. The component is 2,497 lines, so each PR also lifts a piece out — the playbook's pattern — and the restyle is what pays for the extraction.

  1. Header and list.

    Summary strip, ⋯ menu (Mark · Mercury filter), the To match · All segment, state chips on rows, Applied & returned as a foot link. Extracts ArDepositList + a small kernel arDepositRowState.ts that names a row's state from the match data the modal already has (tests: the four states).

  2. Deposit header and allocations.

    Payer-first header, the remaining meter, memo folded to "Add a note", allocation rows with kind · target · amount · × and the split link. Extracts ArDepositHeader and ArAllocationRows; the amount/target coupling (allocationAmountStrForTargetChange) stays in the kernel it is in.

  3. Matches and the guard.

    The matched-payer chips become the bill list (exact match first, Stripe tag on the row, the combo suggestion as one row that fills two lines); the "already recorded" steer becomes the amber card with its two buttons. Extracts ArPayerMatches. Existing render tests (BankPaymentsModalCombo, …LinkGuard) keep passing by role and text.

  4. The footer.

    The sentence, Apply with the amount, Apply & next (select the next To-match deposit after a successful apply; the Stripe auto-close Retry panel stays where it is).

Is this the best we can do?

What it keeps. Every behaviour: payer matching, the sweep, the combo suggestion, the link guard, Mark returned, the dev filter, Stripe-hosted confirmation, the Retry panel. Nothing new is computed except a row-state label from data already on the row.

What it risks. A restyle of a 2,497-line component is where regressions hide. The four PRs are ordered so each is visually checkable on its own and each extraction is covered by the three render suites that already exist plus one new kernel test. Merge them one at a time; do not stack.

What it leaves. The sweep review panel (the overlay) and the Stripe Retry panel are not redrawn here; they are rare screens and can follow the same tokens later. The Mercury filter modal (dev) is untouched.

The honest alternative. If the office never scans the pile and always works top-down, Option B is the better tool and the queue is worth the relearning. Worth one question to whoever does this on Monday mornings.
