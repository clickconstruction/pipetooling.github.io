The second look: is this the best that can be done?

The first drawing was rendered at desktop and phone width, in light and dark. Then it was looked at again. It had four windows. This page is the second drawing.

  1. The Who signs? window is gone. The first drawing asked Who is it with? and then Who signs? in a window of its own. That made four windows where today has three. The search now finds the people at a company, so one pick names both. The signer's name and title sit in the email window the office already uses.
  2. A company with no one on file needs no extra step. Most supply houses and customers have no named person yet. In the first drawing that meant opening Someone else every time. Now the name is typed once in the email window, and one tick keeps the person with the company.
  3. The windows are drawn at their real width. The first render squeezed four windows into a row. They are now two across, at the width the app opens them.

Three things were weighed and kept as first drawn.

  1. One chip, Outside companies. A chip each for customers, supply houses and trade companies would be three chips for three lines. The tag on each line says the kind.
  2. No "Add a new company" in the window. It would make a party that is on no page of the app. The window points to Customers, Supply houses and People, where a record has its other facts.
  3. The line says who signs, in words. A paper with a business needs the human beside it, so the second line reads signed by Dana Ruiz, Project manager.

Changed by the owner's call 3. The second drawing gave a sub one line on their People row, Signs for. A worker who signs for the boss's company did not fit it. He chose a company record of its own with its workers under it, and the People screen above is redrawn for that. Whether that record is the trade company GC mode already keeps is call 7 on the card.
